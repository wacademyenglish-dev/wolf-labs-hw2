Interactive Reading · DET Prep · Lesson 5 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the Interactive Reading question: one text and several tasks about it (complete the sentences, complete the passage with the missing sentence, highlight the answer, identify the idea, title the passage). The key skill is reading signposts: however, no longer, as a result, in other words.

The story: A text about how city libraries changed (invented, general facts). Then a short story in pieces to put in order.

About 38 min: I · The Text (10) · II · Five Tasks (10) · III · The Library Dilemma (7) · IV · The Story in Order (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “When did you last go to a library?” Then: “What do you think a library offers today, apart from books?”

In this question, students lose time reading the text again for each task. Train one careful first reading for meaning, then answer the tasks, going back only to find a specific line.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

however = contrast · no longer = a change · as a result = a consequence · in other words = the same idea again · main idea vs. detail · a title covers the whole text

THE TEXT

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

FIVE TASKS

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

THE STORY IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to point at the line in the text for every answer: the habit is more important than the score today.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

choosing a title that covers only one paragraph, lend vs. borrow, reading the text again for every task. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE LIBRARY DILEMMA (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the five tasks, the student uses the text to decide. Three dilemmas: what the city should build with its money, whether the library should end the silence rule, and where the student takes their own online test. For each, the student chooses and says why; wait five seconds in silence, then take the other side and use the PUSH line. Recast quietly while they talk and push the signposts of the lesson (however, as a result, in other words, no longer); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · The Text.
  • Focus: II · Five Tasks and V · I Can.
  • Out loud: III · The Library Dilemma and IV · The Story in Order.
