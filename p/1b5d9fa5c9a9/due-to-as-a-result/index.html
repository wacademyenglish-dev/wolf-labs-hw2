Due to, As a Result · The Drift Map

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: January 1992.

The grammar: The Rule: What comes after the linker? A noun, or a whole sentence?

Short class (about 50 min): I You and the Sea · III The Explainer · V The Drift Map · VI The Rule · VII The Deep Conveyor · X The Drift Lab Jury · XI End of the Shift. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 64 min.

Open with: “When did you last see the ocean? Where were you, and what was the water like?”

January 1992. A winter storm hits a cargo ship in the North Pacific, and thousands of plastic bath toys fall into the sea. Over the next years they wash up in Alaska, go around a giant gyre, and some get trapped in the Arctic ice. The student watches a short explainer about ocean currents, then works the night shift at the Drift Lab in Sitka: first they rebuild the ducks’ journey on the drift map, then they run the deep conveyor. The grammar: due to / owing to / because of + a noun (the cause), because + a sentence, As a result, / Consequently, at the start of a new sentence (the effect), and , which led to + a noun or -ing for the next link. On screen, the North Pacific gyre with six stations and a yellow duck that moves on and fades as the years go by; then the deep conveyor, a loop of eight stretches that light up.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: a four-minute explainer, sealed until you press play, in four chapters that stop by themselves: the lost ducks, the wind and the spin of the Earth, the deep currents, and why it matters for climate. The chapter times are estimates; if a chapter cuts mid-sentence, let it play a few more seconds. After the video (scene III, step 3): ONE screen. The video stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again), with the drift map from the lesson under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines from the explainer. Every right answer lights a station and the duck moves on. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, June asks one last question (“Why is my old duck almost white?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud with due to, as a result or which led to.

THE TRAP

Portuguese lets a whole sentence follow the words for due to and because of, so students say Due to the wind was strong. In English those take a noun; a full sentence needs because. The Portuguese word for consequently can glue two sentences with a comma; in English, consequently and as a result start a new sentence. Also: as consequence (no article), owning to, led to + a full sentence.

THE DRIFT MAP AND THE CONVEYOR

Scene V, before the rule: because and so are fine; collect what comes out. Scene V is the pressure: read the fact and the linker on the card, and the student builds the sentence with THAT linker. Help only with the noun: the sun is strong → the strong sun.

THE FACTS

The duck spill is real. The Drift Lab and June the beachcomber are invented. The science is kept simple: wind moves surface water; the Earth’s spin bends currents into gyres; cold, salty water sinks; the deep loop takes about a thousand years.

The words (II): storm, currents, tides, patterns and salty, before the video, in hear it in the video and turn the cards. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE DRIFT LAB JURY (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

End of the night shift: the student is the jury in three cases about the 1992 ducks (is the shipping company guilty, should the toy company pay for the clean-up, can June keep the duck she found). Each case has three pieces of evidence. The student chooses a verdict and explains the chain with due to, as a result and which led to. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
