Since March · Keys and a Contract

I live here for ten years is built from correct pieces. The shape is what is wrong, and a student can’t doubt a shape alone. Apartment 4B: two months without rent, and the landlady is at the door with the key. The student investigates, and every clue is a sentence that started in the past and hasn’t stopped. The screen is a contract with a ruler: since pins the start, for stretches the length, both end at NOW.

THE MISTAKE COMES FIRST

Scene I: let the student say I live here since…. Don’t correct it; it comes back at the end. He has lived here is a sentence about today, not a past tense.

THE RULER · THE CALENDAR

Scene IV: point at NOW every time. Scene VII: every crossed day has a date, and a date closes the door: past.

SLOW CONTRADICTIONS

Scene VIII: one sentence, pause, the other, pause, then decide. The last pair is the whole lesson.

GREEN, YELLOW, RED (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the rule, your student wants a room in the building and judges three situations: a flatmate’s dog, a neighbour’s piano, the landlady’s key. There is no right answer: let them choose a colour, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Push for since and for in every answer. Recast quietly (I have lived, not I live here since); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I At the Door, V The Fridge, VII Green, Yellow, Red, IX The Room Interview.
  • Focus: II Two Kinds of Fact, III I Live Here Since…, IV The Ruler, VI The Rule, VIII The Contradiction.
  • Write: X, their own answers to the landlady. It goes to their My notes.
