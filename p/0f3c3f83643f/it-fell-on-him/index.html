It Fell On Him · Coyote vs. ACME · The ACME Catalog

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Seventy years of falling rocks, and now a court.

The grammar: Me, Him, Them: After the verb: me, you, him, her, it, us, them.

The class (about 52 min): I The Desert · III The Trailer · IV Your ACME Top 3 · V Line by Line · VI Me, Him, Them · VII The ACME Catalog · VIII Make the Gag · IX One Minute. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “Every ACME product hurts the coyote. In the trailer, what falls on him? Guess.”

Coyote vs. ACME (2026) mixes real actors and cartoons: Wile E. Coyote buys ACME products to catch the Road Runner, every product goes wrong and hurts him, so he takes ACME to court. Will Forte plays his lawyer, John Cena the lawyer for ACME. Every joke in the film is something happening TO somebody: the anvil falls on him, the rock misses him. The grammar: object pronouns (me, you, him, her, it, us, them) after the verb and after on / to / at / with.

A note for the teacher: the trailer has no captions on YouTube now, so the five frames on the film strip are estimated, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and The ACME Box (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; A1: short answers are fine. 2 · The ACME Box: five things come out of the box, one at a time: the desert, the bird, the box, the anvil, the court. With each, the student answers a short question (who wants it? what falls on him?). At the end they tell the story in three sentences. The object pronouns are in the questions; don’t correct them yet.

The words (II): product, fault, blame, case and bigger, before the video, in turn the cards and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, One Minute): the student picks a topic (a cartoon they love, their favorite thing at home, a person they call every day) and talks for one minute with me, him, her, it, us, them. Then the teacher. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a cartoon canyon at dusk, with an anvil on a cliff. I: the film (the story, the people). III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a ruler, “who wins in court?” (ACME wins ↔ the coyote wins); the student taps and says “I think … wins”. Watch: which word did you hear? Five lines from the trailer (Corporation or Company, fault or fall, cases or cakes, bucks or books, nothing or something), each with a ▶ time before 0:33 or after 0:49: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: true or lie? Five sentences about the trailer; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Me, Him, Them: six sentences, each with one wrong pronoun (The anvil falls on he); the student says the right one, then taps Check and the right words appear. VI, The ACME Catalog: a catalog page with six products (anvil, rocket skates, bird seed, spring shoes, portable hole, a lawyer’s badge). Each gag has a gap; the student says the whole sentence out loud, taps Check, and the right sentence appears; the product gets a DELIVERED stamp and the joke. The rule board is in the corner. VII, Make the Gag: six words (me, him, her, it, us, them); the student tells a gag about the coyote and a new ACME product and taps each word when they use it. The rule board is in the corner. VIII: one minute.

THE COMMON ERRORS

The anvil falls on he, on his, he loves they, help I, listen to we, she asks they / their a question, I believe to you, and it for two things.

YOUR ACME TOP 3 (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer: every ACME product goes wrong for the coyote. Now your student picks: a rocket, a giant magnet or a big umbrella. They tap them from number one to number three and say it with the almost-ready sentence (I want it / I don’t need it / it helps me). There is no right answer. Show your order first if they need a model, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Desert, IV Your ACME Top 3, VII The ACME Catalog and VIII Make the Gag.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Me, Him, Them.
  • Write: IX One Minute.
