Assuming That · C1 Unit 7 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on precise condition words: assuming (that), given that (a fact), supposing (imagine), providing / as long as (only on this condition), in the event of + noun (formal), otherwise, and in case (precaution, NOT if).

The story: Nadia (invented) emails her team three weeks before Riverside Fest, an open-air music festival: permit, toilets, rain, storm, a backup sound engineer.

About 54 min: I · Your Plans (6) · II · The Plan (10) · III · How Sure Are You? (9) · IV · Say It Precisely (7) · V · The Committee (8) · VI · The Planning Board (14).

Open with: “What are you planning for your next holiday, and what could change the plan?”

Each word carries a level of certainty. The biggest trap: Portuguese “caso” translated as in case. Spend time on the umbrella pair in the comparison. The festival email shows all the words in one real plan; the board makes the student plan with them.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

assuming (that) · given that (a fact) · supposing…? · providing / as long as · in the event of + noun · in case (precaution) × if

YOUR PLANS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE PLAN

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

HOW SURE ARE YOU?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

SAY IT PRECISELY

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THE PLANNING BOARD

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

call me in case you need help (for if), given that it rains tomorrow (not a fact), in the event of it rains, providing we will pay, supposing it will rain, other wise. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE COMMITTEE (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, your student sits on the Riverside Fest committee and votes on three proposals (free entry for kids, no plastic cups, moving indoors in case of rain): Yes, Propose a change or No, justified with the precise condition words. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. In The last word, start the 45 seconds and let them persuade you; then argue back. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
