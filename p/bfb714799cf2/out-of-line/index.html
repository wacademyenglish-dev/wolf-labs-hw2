Out of Line · The Spotlight

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Put a negative word first (never, rarely, not only, hardly) and English flips the helper in front of the subject, like a question: Never have I seen a room go quiet like that.

The grammar: The Spotlight: a farewell speech at the pulpit. The negative word steps into the spotlight. Then the helper jumps before the subject.

Short class (about 52 min): I Never Before · II The Spotlight · III Nothing to Move · IV The IF Disappears · V Where the Weight Falls · VI Two Halves · VIII One Minute at the Pulpit · X Step Down. Skip if you’re short on time: VII Earned or Showing Off? and IX Out of Order (marked EXTRA on your screen). On the speaking pages the student speaks first and taps Check on their own screen. Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “What was it, and where?”

Put a negative word first (never, rarely, not only, hardly) and English flips the helper in front of the subject, like a question: Never have I seen a room go quiet like that. No helper? Add do / does / did: Rarely does she call. The IF of a third conditional can disappear too: Had I known … Students say Never I have seen. The form moves the weight to the front; it belongs in speeches and strong writing, and it costs something every time. On screen, the stage from the cover is the reward: when the student finishes the rule in scene II, the words on the stage reorder themselves (the negative word steps into the spotlight, the helper jumps).

THE PAGES

I Never Before: the student taps what it was (a place, a storm…), then three questions, one by one: they answer out loud and tap Check (an example and a follow-up appear). Don’t correct yet: write the strong sentence exactly.

II The Spotlight: a farewell speech on a dark paper. The student reads it out loud and taps every inversion (never have I seen, rarely have we had, hardly had we finished, not only did you finish, never will I forget; every word can be tapped, only the five count). Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line. When it is ready, the stage on top shows the inverted sentence, and the rule stays in the corner of III, IV, VI, VIII and IX.

III Nothing to Move: five lines with one wrong word (she calls, she finished, I eat, did we realized, did he knew). The student says each line right (do / does / did + plain verb), then taps Check.

IV The IF Disappears: four sentences with IF. The student says them without IF (Had I known…), then taps Check.

V Where the Weight Falls: play A and B first; the questions open after both are played. Three taps: which one puts the heavy word first, where the weight falls in B, what the form is for. Then they say both sentences themselves.

VI Two Halves: five first halves and five second halves. Tap a first half, then its second half (hardly / scarcely … when, no sooner … than). When all are matched, they say each whole sentence out loud.

VII Earned or Showing Off? (extra): play the award speech and the story for a friend. Four lines: true or lie? If it is a lie, the student says the truth.

VIII One Minute at the Pulpit: the speech in three parts (opening, middle, close). The student says each part out loud, taps Check, and the part joins the speech while the costume meter goes up (two inversions are earned, three are a costume). The middle part stays plain.

IX Out of Order (extra): six sentences, three out of order. Tap a sentence, then its box (out of order / in line). When all are in place, the three fixes appear: the student says them.

THE RECORDINGS

Each with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens after listening. Scene V: the same sentence twice; listen for where the weight falls. Scene VII: an award speech and a story about getting home. The second speaker says “had I got” (British); American usually says “gotten”. The form is correct in both; the question is whether it was worth it.

THE SPEECH

Scene VIII: one minute, two inversions. The costume meter would go red at three: the middle line should be plain, so the strong lines land.

BEHIND THE PULPIT (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after their one-minute speech, your student faces three real moments behind the pulpit: saying yes or no to a farewell speech, the blank in the middle, and the truth for a friend who used too many inversions. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two inversions themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while: this lesson has many Focus scenes in the middle.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Never Before, VIII One Minute at the Pulpit (no longer extra), IX Behind the Pulpit.
  • Listen: V Where the Weight Falls, VII Earned or Showing Off?
  • Focus: II The Spotlight, III Nothing to Move, IV The IF Disappears, VI Two Halves.
  • Write: X, their “never before” at the pulpit and plainly. It goes to their My notes.
