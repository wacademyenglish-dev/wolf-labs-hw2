With All Due Respect · C1 Unit 5 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on disagreeing diplomatically: I take your point, but…; I hear what you’re saying. However, …; I see it slightly differently; I’m not sure I’d go that far; Correct me if I’m wrong, but…; Perhaps we could…; softeners (a bit, slightly, might).

The story: The residents’ meeting at Green Towers (invented building): should the garden become a pool or a vegetable garden? Mr. Costa wants a pool, Ms. Lee wants the garden they voted for, Dr. Rao worries about money. The student is the mediator.

About 49 min: I · The Meeting (10) · II · Direct or Diplomatic? (9) · III · Cool It Down (7) · IV · The Arena (8) · V · The Mediator (15).

Open with: “Think of a meeting or a family dinner where people disagreed. How did it go?”

Brazilian professionals often sound too direct in English (“I don’t agree”, “You are wrong”) or too soft. Today is about the middle: recognize, make the difference small, propose. Point out that “With all due respect” is often aggressive in real life. In the mission you are three stubborn neighbors: be difficult, so the student has to be diplomatic.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I take your point, but… · I hear what you’re saying. However, … · I see it slightly differently · I’m not sure I’d go that far · Correct me if I’m wrong, but… · softeners: a bit, slightly, perhaps, might

THE MEETING

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

DIRECT OR DIPLOMATIC?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

COOL IT DOWN

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THE MEDIATOR

You are each neighbor, and you are stubborn. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student answers each neighbor diplomatically. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

you are wrong, I don’t agree with you (flat), with all due respect (used aggressively), I agree with you, but… (when you don’t), that is not true, your idea is bad. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE ARENA (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, your student picks a side on a new building rule (no music after 10 PM, even on Saturdays) and defends it for one minute with the diplomatic language from the lesson; then tap Swap sides and they defend the other side. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then interrupt them bluntly with the PUSH line (only you see it): they must disagree without “You’re wrong”. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · The Meeting.
  • Focus: II · Direct or Diplomatic? and III · Cool It Down.
  • Out loud: IV · The Arena and V · The Mediator.
