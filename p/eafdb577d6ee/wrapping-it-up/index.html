Wrapping It Up · The Long Table

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Christmas Eve at Grandma Edith’s: twelve people at one long table, and everybody talks at once.

The grammar: The Rule: Don’t repeat. Keep one small word.

Short class (about 54 min): I Your Holidays · II The Crackers · III The Rule · IV The Tense Decides · V The Wreath · VI Aunt Ruth on Speaker · VII Leaving Early · VIII Your Own Table. On the speaking pages the student speaks first and taps Check on their own screen. Full lesson: about 54 min.

Open with: “What is the biggest family night of the year for you?”

Christmas Eve at Grandma Edith’s: twelve people at one long table, and everybody talks at once. A full answer to every question sounds like a robot (Yes, I am staying for dessert). Real people cut it short and keep one small word: so / not (I think so, I hope not, I’m afraid not), the auxiliary (So do I, Neither can I, Nor have I, the echo She did?), to (I’d love to) and one / ones. Many students answer I think yes, I also don’t, I’d love, the red, or Is it? for every piece of news. On screen: the long table from above (whoever speaks lights up, YOU in candle gold), and the scissors that cut Leo’s long answer until only the small word stays.

THE PAGES

II The Crackers: six crackers. The student reads the relative’s question and answers short, out loud (before tapping). Then Check: the short answer appears and the scissors cut Leo’s long answer down to the small word (am, so, so again, to, can, not).

III The Rule: the table talk on a dark paper. The student reads it out loud and taps the small word that holds each answer (so, not, do, can, to, one; every word can be tapped, only the six count). Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line. The rule stays in the corner of IV, VI and VII.

IV The Tense Decides: six answers with a wrong helper (Nor do I, Neither do I, So do I, She does?, You do?). The student says the answer right (the helper comes from their sentence: have, did, am, did, can, will), then taps Check.

V The Wreath: six things the relatives say, six short answers. Tap what they say, then its answer; every right pair lights the wreath. Then the student says each pair out loud, fast.

VI Aunt Ruth on Speaker: four calls. The student reads Aunt Ruth’s line, answers short and adds one detail, taps Check (an example appears), then asks her back (the question is on the screen). You answer as Aunt Ruth: the line in brackets is yours.

THE TENSE DECIDES

When no helper is visible in their sentence (Mia got engaged), the helper is still there: present do, past did. In IV the wrong helper is underlined: ask “Which helper is in their sentence?”

A NOTE

A live A2 lesson already teaches too / either / So do I. This one is the B2 version: so / not, every auxiliary, echo questions, to and one.

LEAVING EARLY (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

A negotiation with a secret limit. It’s ten o’clock on Christmas Eve; the student wants to leave for a party and you are Grandma Edith, who wants everyone at the table until midnight. Ask her four questions: the student answers short first (I’m afraid not, I suppose so, I’d love to, the small one), then negotiates. Your limit is on your screen only: you accept 11:00 if they help with the dishes and promise to come for lunch. Wait five seconds in silence before you give in, and use the PUSH line to make it harder. Close the deal on the screen. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Holidays, II The Crackers, V The Wreath, VI Aunt Ruth on Speaker (you are Aunt Ruth), VII Leaving Early (you are Grandma Edith).
  • Focus: III The Rule, IV The Tense Decides.
  • Write: VIII, three lines the student’s family always says, with short answers. They go to their My notes.
