If I Hadn’t Missed It · B2 Unit 3 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new B2 lesson on the third conditional (If I hadn’t missed the train, I wouldn’t have met her) and mixed conditionals (past → now: I wouldn’t be here now; now → past: If I were braver, I would have…).

The story: Ten years ago Lena (invented) missed the 7:42 train by eight seconds, sat next to Inês, an architect, at the station café, heard about a job, moved to Porto and became an architect who lives by the sea.

About 37 min: I · Eight Seconds (10) · II · The Council Vote (8) · III · The Other Life (9) · IV · Your Sliding Doors (10).

Open with: “Can you think of a small accident that changed something in your life?”

A “sliding doors” story makes the chain natural. The student needs two things: the form (had + past participle / would have + past participle) and the time logic (past result or present result). The conversation is personal: let the student tell their own chain.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

If + had + past participle · would have + past participle (past result) · would + verb (present result: mixed) · If I were…, I would have… (now → past) · negatives: hadn’t, wouldn’t have · the chain: one if leads to another

EIGHT SECONDS

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

THE OTHER LIFE

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

YOUR SLIDING DOORS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

If I wouldn’t have missed (If I hadn’t missed), I would met (I would have met), If I didn’t miss it, I wouldn’t meet her (third: hadn’t missed / wouldn’t have met), I wouldn’t have been here now (mixed: I wouldn’t be here now), If I had knew (had known). B2: one correction per answer is enough.

THE COUNCIL VOTE (II) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Lena’s town has three proposals (close the station café, hire only through public ads, phone-free tables). Your student votes Yes, No or Propose a change, and says why with third and mixed conditionals (If the café hadn’t been there, Lena wouldn’t be…). There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Eight Seconds.
  • Focus: III · The Other Life.
  • Out loud: II · The Council Vote and IV · Your Sliding Doors.
