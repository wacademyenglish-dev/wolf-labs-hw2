They Might Have Left by Boat · The Search Chart

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Two island mysteries.

The grammar: The Rule: How sure are you? Modal + have + past participle.

Short class (about 49 min): I You and the Unknown · III The Lost Colony · V Kestrel Rock · VI The Rule · VII The Signal Flags · IX Nora’s Choices · XI The Last Page of the Log. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, X (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 63 min.

Open with: “Have you ever been to an island? Where was it, and what was it like at night?”

Two island mysteries. The real one: in 1590, the governor came back to the English colony on Roanoke Island and found it empty, with one word carved on a tree. The invented one: Kestrel Rock, a tiny island off Maine, where a bird researcher, Nora Vance, disappeared on a foggy night in October. The student reads the clues and speculates about the past: She must have left in a hurry. She might have seen a boat. She can’t have swum nine miles. The answer comes at the end: nobody died. On screen, a search chart of the island with six numbered buoys that light up as the student builds each theory, and three signal flags (CERTAIN, POSSIBLE, IMPOSSIBLE) where each piece of evidence is raised.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: an explainer (about three minutes) on the lost colony, sealed in four chapters. Near the end the narrator uses three modals of the past in one minute: may have been, could have referred, might have assimilated. Before the video the student picks a reaction as the governor who finds the island empty. In each chapter she taps True or Lie on three or four lines, and every line has a ▶ time that plays the moment again.

THE TRAP

For Brazilian students one verb frame covers three English meanings: the conclusion (must have left), the past obligation (had to leave) and the impossible (can’t have left). So they say she had to leave in a hurry for a conclusion, she mustn’t have swum for impossible, and put the simple past after have. Every correction on screen names one of these. Scene V: she says her theory, builds the sentence from the clue and taps Check. Scene VI: the three kinds of guess, then six broken lines: she finds the broken part and says the fix (Check shows it). Scene VII: how sure are you? Tap the flag. Scene IX: Right or Wrong.

KEEP IT A PUZZLE

The ending is happy: Nora and two fishermen are found safe on Gannet Island. Keep the tone of a puzzle, never of a crime.

The words (II): settlement, disappeared, hostile, voyage and clue, before the video, in hear it in the video and mystery meaning. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

NORA’S CHOICES (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the Coast Guard says everybody is safe, the student judges three moments of Nora’s night: what they would do at 1:50 AM, whether leaving with no coat and no note was brave or careless, and what the station should do about its rule. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (must have, might have, could have, can’t have); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
