A Ten-Hour Flight · The Boarding Pass

Tess Rivera writes a travel blog, The Carry-On Diaries. Her posts are long and honest, and almost nobody reads them. Her editor has one note: your titles are too long. So the student helps Tess turn her worst and best trips into short headlines: a flight that took ten hours becomes a ten-hour flight. A compound adjective packs a whole description into one word with a hyphen, and it goes before the noun. The number part never takes -s. On screen, boarding passes with a perforated stub: when the short headline is right, the stub tears off and shows the compound in big letters. And the blog, where the headlines go live one by one.

THE BOARDING PASSES

Scene II, before the rule: the student reads Tess’s long note out loud, talks about it, then says it as ONE short sentence. Tap exactly what you heard. Most will say a ten-hours flight or a flight of ten hours.

THE TRAP

Portuguese puts the description after the noun, with a preposition, and keeps the plural. English turns it into one adjective, before the noun, singular. The same student who says ten-hours would never say reds cars: show them that link.

HYPHENS

Before the noun, the compound takes a hyphen: a well-known hotel. After a verb, the hyphen often goes and the plural comes back: the hotel is well known · the flight was ten hours long. The lesson keeps the focus on the form before the noun.

THE BLOG AND THE PODCAST

Scene IV is the pressure: you read each reader’s comment, the student answers at once as Tess, and the post goes live. Scene VI: you are the podcast host; Tess answers from memory, then asks back. This lesson has no recording: your voice is the audio.

THE ARENA (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Tess’s best trip started with a lost bag. Your student picks a side, the well-planned trip or the last-minute one, and defends it for one minute with compound adjectives; then they swap and defend the other side. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
