The Essay · C1 Unit 10 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on the argumentative essay: thesis (This essay will argue that…), topic sentences (Firstly…, A further argument is that…), evidence (For instance…, According to…), the other side and refutation (It is often argued that… However, this view overlooks…; While it is true that…), and the conclusion (On balance…, In conclusion… — no new ideas).

The story: A model C1 essay (invented) answers: should university be free for everyone? Then a student’s first draft about homework, full of typical mistakes.

About 51 min: I · The Model Essay (11) · II · Opinion or Argument? (9) · III · Find the Mistakes (7) · IV · The Story in Order (7) · V · The Arena (7) · VI · Your Essay Plan (10).

Open with: “Should university be free for everyone? What’s your first reaction?”

The same skills as the talk, now in writing. Show the skeleton of the model essay; the order game rebuilds it; the student’s draft with mistakes shows the typical problems (double opinion, vague evidence, dismissing the other side, new ideas in the conclusion). The plan at the end can become the content of the final talk, or homework as a full 250-word essay.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

This essay will argue that… · Firstly, … / A further argument is that… · For instance, … / According to… · It is often argued that… However, this view overlooks… · While it is true that…, … · On balance, … / In conclusion, …

THE MODEL ESSAY

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

OPINION OR ARGUMENT?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

FIND THE MISTAKES

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

THE STORY IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

YOUR ESSAY PLAN

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

in my opinion, I think that, in conclusion, to conclude, some people say…, but they are wrong, vague evidence (many things got better), a new idea in the conclusion, a chatty question as the first line. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE ARENA (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the skeleton game, your student argues the essay question out loud: pick a side, one minute, with the essay phrases (It is often argued that… However, this view overlooks…; While it is true that…; On balance…). Then tap Swap sides and they defend the other side: this is the counterargument and the refutation, spoken. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder, and recast quietly while they talk. At the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The arguments can feed the essay plan in VI.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
