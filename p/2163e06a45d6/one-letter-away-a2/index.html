One Letter Away · The Chat

Tom wrote I’m boring to his friend Ana. He meant he was bored. One letter changed what Ana read. Portuguese marks the difference with the verb; English keeps BE the same, so only the ending does the job: -ED is the person who feels it, -ING is the thing that causes it. The screen is a chat: the student picks the ending, and Ana answers, or gets confused.

THE SLOT DECIDES

Person who feels it: -ED. Thing that causes it: -ING. My teacher is interesting is correct: people can cause feelings too.

QUIET ENDINGS

Scene V: five recordings, checked against the audio. -ed and -ing are fast and quiet: play twice, then the 0.75× button. The transcript opens after.

GREEN, YELLOW, RED (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VI is now a traffic light: three Sunday invitations (a three-hour film, a beach bus at 6 AM, a party with strangers). Your student picks green, yellow or red and says why: the plan is -ing, they are -ed. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). If they say I’m boring, smile and ask: Are you, or is the party? Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
