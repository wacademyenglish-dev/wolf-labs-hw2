If Only · Toy Story 5 · The Attic Box

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Toys who only wish to be played with.

The grammar: I Wish, If Only: I wish she played with me. If only she hadn’t left.

Short class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and The Attic Box · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V Hot Takes · VI I Wish, If Only · VII The Attic Box · IX The Debate. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 57 min.

Open with: “You know the toys now. In the trailer, what does Woody wish? Guess.”

Toy Story 5 (2026), Disney and Pixar. A package arrives for Bonnie: Lilypad, a talking tablet. Screen time starts, play time shrinks, and Woody finds more abandoned toys every day. Toys are for play, someone says, but tech is for everything. The grammar: wish / if only, with the tense one step back: wish + past for now (I wish she played with me, I wish I were new), wish + had + participle for what is over (if only she hadn’t left me), wish + would for another person’s annoying habit (I wish that tablet would stop), wish + could for an ability, and hope for what can still happen.

A note for the teacher: a family film. Lines checked against the official captions (24/09), told in our own words. The toys on screen are simple drawings, not characters from the film.

Warm-up and The Attic Box (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Attic Box: five things come out of a box in the attic, one at a time: Andy’s room, Bonnie, the tablet, the attic, the last question. With each, the student says what the toys wish (they wish she played…, if only she hadn’t…, they wish the tablet would…). At the end, they say what they hope happens to the toys.

The words (II): play, ruin, listening, peace and happy, before the video, in turn the cards and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Debate): “Children today should play with toys, not tablets.” The student chooses a side, the teacher takes the other one, with I wish / If only / I hope. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is the attic with the open box. I: Warm-up and The Attic Box. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: guess what arrives for Bonnie, with four clues, one at a time. Watch: Which word did you hear? and Who says it?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: two sliders (agree or disagree), and the student says why. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Hot Takes: four statements, a scale from 1 to 5; the student defends their number. VI: a note from the old bear, on old paper: the student reads it out loud, taps the wishes, then finishes the rule. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Attic Box: four toys speak (the bear, the robot, the dinosaur, the rag doll). For each, the student picks a way to answer (cheer up, be honest, make a plan…) and the talk appears: they read their part out loud, and you read the toy’s. Every answer brings a toy back to the edge of the box, with its wish on a tag. At four, the light from the little window falls on the whole box. VIII, Your Wishes: spin for a question, thirty seconds to answer. IX: the debate.

THE COMMON ERRORS

I wish she plays, I wish she will play, I wish I am / I would be new, if only she didn’t leave me (it’s over: hadn’t left), if only she wouldn’t have left, I wish I can fly, would stops, I wish she comes back tomorrow (hope), I hope she came back tomorrow, I wish she would still need us.

HOT TAKES (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, four hot takes from the world of the trailer (giving old toys away, animated films are for adults, five films is too many, a device that is always listening). For each one, the student taps a number from 1 to 5 and defends it with I wish / if only / I hope; you take the opposite side. There is no right answer, and the final debate (toy or tablet) is not repeated here. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (I wish she plays, if only she didn’t leave, I wish I would); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes: two statements are enough if time is short.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Attic Box, V Hot Takes, VII The Attic Box, VIII Your Wishes and IX The Debate.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI I Wish, If Only.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
