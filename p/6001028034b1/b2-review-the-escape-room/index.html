The Escape Room · B2 Unit 4 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 4 (Wishes and what might have been). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: An escape room in the cabin of Captain Hale, who vanished in 1890. The board game is the room: every square is a clue (the made bed, the missing lifeboat, the diary). Your student speculates about the past. Suggested truth: the first mate betrayed him and Hale escaped by boat to the island.

About 47 min: I · Speed Round (6) · II · The Clues (15) · III · The Broken Clock (7) · IV · Memory (8) · V · Would You Rather? (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Sixty seconds. Ready? Start the clock.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

wish / if only + past (now) · wish / if only + past perfect (the past) · should have, shouldn’t have · must have, can’t have · might / may / could have

SPEED ROUND

A 60-second speed round. Tap Start the clock, then Right or Pass for each item; the answer is on your screen. At the end of class, play again and beat the score.

THE CLUES

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

MEMORY

A memory game. The student opens two cards; when they match, the student makes a sentence with the pair (example under Show answer).

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I wish I have, I wish I didn’t say it (for hadn’t said), he must left, it mustn’t have been him (for can’t have been), if only I would have known, he can have taken the boat (for could have). B2: one correction per answer is enough.

THE BROKEN CLOCK (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

The student escaped, but the old clock from the clues is broken, and you are the owner of the escape room. Ask the four questions; the student answers in their role with must have, can’t have, I wish and shouldn’t have. Your secret limit (only you see it): you accept half the price, or a five-star review and a video. Don’t give in fast: wait five seconds in silence after each answer and use the PUSH line. Then close the deal on the screen. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the review has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · Speed Round and VI · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · The Clues, III · The Broken Clock and V · Would You Rather?.
  • Listen: IV · Memory.
