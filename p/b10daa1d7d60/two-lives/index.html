Two Lives · The Scale

Two lives, and hardly a better in the whole hour. Your student compares a big city and a small town, home and office, before and now, with as … as, not as … as, just as, twice / half as … as, the same as and different from. The screen is a brass scale: equal sentences keep it level; not as … as sends down the side that has more.

AS OPENS, AS CLOSES

The most common slip is as good than. When it happens, just say: AS opened it…

THE NEGATIVE IS THE USEFUL ONE

not as big as says less without sounding rude. In scene IV the hard part is the side: the side with more goes down, and it’s the OTHER one.

THE DEBATE

Scene IX: your student picks city or country, and you take the other side for real. Push back with the same shapes.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

as expensive than, the same that, different of, twice more expensive than, so expensive as. You will also hear different than in American speech; teach from.

THE JURY (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene V is now a jury: Bia and a job in the capital, Leo and the office, Dani and Rafa between the beach and the city. The numbers are the evidence. Your student chooses a verdict and says why with as … as, twice / half as, not as … as. Wait five seconds in silence before they speak, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The city-or-country debate stays in IX.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Two Lives, V The Jury, IX City or Country?
  • Listen: II Three Voices, VII The Study, VIII Two Strangers (the transcript opens after they listen).
  • Focus: III Six Shapes, IV The Scale, VI One Wrong Word.
  • Write: X, their two lives. It goes to their My notes.
