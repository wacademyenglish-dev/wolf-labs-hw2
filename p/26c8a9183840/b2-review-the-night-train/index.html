The Night Train · B2 Unit 9 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 9 (More elaborate sentences). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: On the night train to the coast, a diamond necklace disappeared from the safe at 3:10 AM. Your student is the inspector. You are three passengers: the pianist, the young lawyer and the guard. (The answer: the guard hid it in the pianist’s flower box and opened a window to confuse everyone.)

About 40 min: I · Speed Round (6) · II · Carriage 7 (15) · III · The Arena (8) · IV · In the Fog (8) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Sixty seconds. Ready? Start the clock.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

must have, can’t have, might have · participle clauses: having done, not knowing · cleft sentences: it was… who, what I need is · inversion: never have I, not only did · had I known

SPEED ROUND

A 60-second speed round. Tap Start the clock, then Right or Pass for each item; the answer is on your screen. At the end of class, play again and beat the score.

CARRIAGE 7

You are each passenger. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student sums up the case with participle clauses, clefts and inversion. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

IN THE FOG

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

having finish, never I have seen, had I known, I would locked, it was the guard which found it, what I need it is a holiday, not only he saw it. B2: one correction per answer is enough.

THE ARENA (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the case is closed, your student decides who is to blame: only the guard, or the train company that gave him the only key. They pick a side and defend it for one minute with Unit 9 sentences (Having…, It was… who…, Not only did…, Had they…), then tap Swap sides and defend the other one. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · Speed Round and V · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · Carriage 7, III · The Arena and IV · In the Fog.
