What Is Everybody Doing? · Spirited Away · The Bathhouse Windows

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A girl in a car, a tunnel, and a town for spirits.

The grammar: Right Now: Right now: am, is, are + -ing.

The class (about 49 min): I The Spirit Town · III The Trailer · IV Would You Rather? · V Scene by Scene · VI Right Now · VII The Bathhouse Windows · VIII Right Now, Out There · IX The Interview. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the bathhouse now. In the trailer, what is Chihiro doing? Guess.”

Spirited Away (2001) is the Studio Ghibli film by Hayao Miyazaki that won the Oscar for the best animated film. Chihiro, ten, is moving to a new town; her family walks through a tunnel and finds a town for spirits, her parents eat the spirits’ food and turn into pigs, and she works in a huge bathhouse to save them. The trailer is the official English one (GKIDS). The grammar: present continuous for right now: am / is / are + -ing (she is working, they are eating), questions with is / are first (Is she crying?) and isn’t / aren’t for no.

A note for the teacher: the five marks follow the timing of YouTube’s automatic captions; each moment is told in our own words. The trailer itself has almost no present continuous: the student describes what they SEE, which is exactly what the tense is for.

Warm-up and The Spirit Town (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). A1: short answers are fine. 2 · The Spirit Town: five pictures open one at a time: the car, the tunnel, the food, the bathhouse, the bridge. With each, the student says what is happening, with is / are + -ing. At the end, they tell the story in three sentences.

The words (II): building, entrance, afraid, help and forget, before the video, in match the columns and mixed letters. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Interview): the teacher is Chihiro on her first night at the bathhouse, and the student interviews her about right now. Then swap: the student is themselves on a first day at a new school or job. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is the red bathhouse at night with lanterns and steam. I: the film (Ghibli, the girl, the story). III: the trailer in three steps. Before: four clues about a girl, one at a time (“who is she?”); the student says it when they know. Watch: Which word did you hear? Five lines, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: True or lie? Five sentences, each with a ▶; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Right Now: a letter from Chihiro to her parents on a dark card. The student taps the words with am, is or are + -ing, then finishes the rule board. The board stays in the corner on the next pages. VI, The Bathhouse Windows: the bathhouse front with six dark windows (Chihiro, her parents, the spirits, Haku, a question, Yubaba). For each window the student reads who and what, says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the window lights up and the right sentence appears. VII, Right Now, Out There: six names (your mother, your best friend, your city, your pet, the weather, you and the teacher). The student says a sentence for each one and taps the name. VIII: the interview.

THE COMMON ERRORS

Chihiro walking, is walk, her parents is, Haku are, takeing, cleanning, Chihiro is crying?, Does she crying?, she doesn’t sleeping, she not sleeping.

WOULD YOU RATHER? (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer: your student is in the spirit town. Three quick choices, two options each: the free food (eat or wait), the white dragon (ride it or watch it), and a Saturday night (sleeping or going out). Each option has an almost-ready sentence with I’m …ing / I’m not …ing. There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Spirit Town, IV Would You Rather?, VII The Bathhouse Windows and VIII Right Now, Out There.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Right Now.
  • Write: IX The Interview.
