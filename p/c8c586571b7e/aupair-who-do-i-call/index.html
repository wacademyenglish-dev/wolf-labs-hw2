Who Do I Call? · Au Pair · Lesson 15 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the safety network: the local coordinator (first call for any family problem), the agency, the State Department help line (1-866-283-9090, jvisas@state.gov), 911 for emergencies, the National Human Trafficking Hotline (1-888-373-7888, text 233733) and the Brazilian consulate. Rematch (changing families) is done through the agency. Plus: how to make a clear 911 call.

The story: InterExchange coordinators explain their work (video). Eight situations to match with the right call. A 911 call in order. The student’s contact list.

About 41 min: I · Your Local Coordinator (8) · II · Who Do I Call? (12) · III · A 911 Call (8) · IV · My Contacts (9) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “If something went wrong tonight in the USA, who would you call?” Most students don’t know: that is the lesson.

Numbers checked on 15/10/2026: State Department help line on j1visa.state.gov; trafficking hotline on humantraffickinghotline.org (24/7). Check them again before each new group. The consulate number depends on the region: the student finds it on the Itamaraty website. Program rule: the local coordinator contacts the family and the au pair within 48 hours of arrival and meets them in person within two weeks.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

911, what is your emergency? · my coordinator · the agency · 1-866-283-9090 · 1-888-373-7888 · rematch

YOUR LOCAL COORDINATOR

InterExchange, “Au Pair USA Local Coordinators”, only 1:33–3:05 (captions checked on 15/10/2026).

WHO DO I CALL?

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

A 911 CALL

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

MY CONTACTS

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Homework that matters: the student saves every number in her phone AND writes the house address on paper.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

calling the parents before 911 in a medical emergency, not knowing the address of the house, thinking rematch is running away. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE SATURDAY DEAL (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after “Who do I call?”, the student practices the first step of the network: talking to the family. You are Jen, the host mom; Emma has soccer every Saturday at 8. Ask your four questions; your secret limit (only you see it) is Friday afternoon off, and the dad can take Emma two Saturdays a month. Then close the deal together. Any deal is fine if it keeps a day and a half off every week and one full weekend a month. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line. Recast quietly while they talk (I understand, but …; Could we …?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
