A Little Gravy, a Few Beans · Thanksgiving for Twelve

The day before Thanksgiving. The student is cooking for twelve guests, and the shopping list is a disaster: a lot of what you don’t need, not much of what you do. The student fixes the list, then serves a round table of twelve plates while the guests ask for more. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

Count it (beans, guests): a few, many, not many, How many? Can’t count it (gravy, rice, time): a little, much, not much, How much? a lot of works for both and lives in positive sentences. The mistakes: a few gravy, a little beans, a few of potatoes, much beans, I have much time, There is a few, a lot green beans.

THANKSGIVING

An American holiday on the fourth Thursday of November: family, turkey, and a lot of food. If the student doesn’t celebrate it, ask about a similar holiday at home.

THE BUDGET (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the table and the quantity cards, your student has only $100 left for the dinner and ranks five things. There is no right answer: let them order the list, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Then open your own order and agree on one top 3 together. Push for a lot of / a few / a little / not much in every reason. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Holidays, II The Shopping List (before the rule: tap the version you heard), IV Twelve Plates (you play each guest), V A Lot, a Little, VI The Phone Call (you are Josie; the big question closes it), VII The Budget (they rank, then you show your order).
  • Focus: III The Rule.
  • Write: VIII, their own party list, with the red wavy line for count / can’t count, How many rice, a few of, a lot green, much in a positive sentence, There is a few. It goes to their My notes.

No audio or video in this lesson: your voice is every guest’s.
