The Noir Novel · C1 Unit 4 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 4 (Inversion). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: A crime writer (invented) is finishing chapter twelve: Detective Vale, a foggy harbour, a ship that has already left. The student orders the scene, fixes the draft and chooses endings, all with inversion.

About 40 min: I · Bingo (8) · II · The Story in Order (7) · III · The Writer’s Top Five (7) · IV · Find the Mistakes (7) · V · Choose the Ending (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Nine squares. Pick one, answer, and make a line.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

no sooner… than, hardly… when, not only · had I known, should you, were it not for · not until, not once, nowhere · little did, at no time, under no circumstances · only then, only after · so … was … that, such was … that

BINGO

A 3×3 card of quick tasks. The student picks a square and does it out loud; tap Done. Three in a line (across, down or diagonal) is a BINGO. Keep going for a second line if there is time.

THE STORY IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

FIND THE MISTAKES

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

CHOOSE THE ENDING

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

no sooner she had arrived, little she knew, not until midnight she found, so thick the fog was, if had she known, hardly had I sat down than (for when). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE WRITER’S TOP FIVE (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After chapter 12 is in order, your student ranks what makes a crime novel impossible to put down (twist, detective, villain, atmosphere, pace) and explains the top one and the last one with inversion (Never have I…, Not only does… but…, Only when… do I…, Rarely does…). Wait five seconds in silence before they explain, then tap Show my teacher’s order, defend yours, and agree on one top 3 together. Use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · Bingo and IV · Find the Mistakes and VI · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · The Story in Order and III · The Writer’s Top Five and V · Choose the Ending.
