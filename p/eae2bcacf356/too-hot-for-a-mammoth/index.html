Too Hot for a Mammoth · Ice Age: Boiling Point · The Ice Bridge

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Twenty-five years of ice, and now it is too hot.

The grammar: Too Hot: Too means a problem. I am cold, not I have cold.

The class (about 54 min): I The Ice · II The Teaser · III Would You Rather? · IV Scene by Scene · V Too Hot · VI The Ice Bridge · VII Too Hot, Too Cold · VIII The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the herd now. In the teaser, is it too hot or too cold for Manny? Guess.”

Ice Age: Boiling Point (February 2027) is the sixth Ice Age film. Manny the mammoth, Sid the sloth and Diego the saber-toothed cat live in a world of ice; a volcano explodes, the ice breaks, and they fall into a lost world of lava and dinosaurs. The grammar: the weather with it’s (it’s hot), how you feel with be (I am cold, I am hungry), too + adjective for a problem (the lava is too hot), too + adjective + to + verb (too thin to walk on), and very for a good thing.

A note for the teacher: the teaser has only images and shouts (checked 26/09), so the five frames are estimated from what you see, and each moment is told in our own words. A1 rule: no portraits; the herd are names.

Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). A1: short answers are fine. 2 · Two Truths and a Lie: three rounds about the animals (Manny, Scrat, the lost world). The student reads the three sentences, says which one is the lie, then taps. In the last round, they say two truths and one lie about themselves, and you guess.

The ending (VIII, The Hot Seat): the student sits in the hot seat as Sid in the world of lava; the teacher asks five questions (hot or cold, too hot to touch, too big, hungry, too dangerous). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an animated ice valley at dusk with a volcano far away. I: the film (the herd, the problem). II: the teaser in three steps. Before: four clues, one at a time (very big, two long tusks, a world of ice and snow, now it is too hot); the student taps Next clue and guesses it is Manny. Watch: four questions (How is the first world? What happens to the ice? How is the lost world? Someone shouts “Look ___!”), each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that part. The teaser has only images and shouts. After: two rulers: is the lost world good for Manny (too hot for him / good for him), and are you cold or hot right now. III: five frames on the film strip. IV, Too Hot: six sentences with a gap (The cake is ___ good!); the student taps the sentence, then the box: very (a good thing) or too (a problem). When all six are in the right box, the rule board appears and stays in the corner. V, The Ice Bridge: a bridge of six blocks over the lava, with a thermometer: the lava, the weather, Sid, the ice, Manny, Diego. Each block has one sentence; the student taps True or Lie, and if it is a lie, says the right sentence. A right answer freezes the block and the temperature goes down. VI, Too Hot, Too Cold: Sid asks “Are you hot too?” and the student picks an answer (three reactions, each with a short talk), then flips four cards (the city in summer, the coffee, the old shoes, the bedroom) and says a sentence with too or very. VII: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

too much hot, Is hot today, I have cold / I’m with cold, I have hunger, too thin for walk, very big to go (too), cold too to swim, the cake is too good (very), the lava too hot.

WOULD YOU RATHER? (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the teaser: three quick choices from the film, two options each. The ice world or the hot jungle, a pet dinosaur (big or small), and soup (very hot or just warm). Each option has an almost-ready sentence with too / very + adjective and too hot to … There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Ice, III Would You Rather?, VI The Ice Bridge and VII Too Hot, Too Cold.
  • Listen: II The Teaser and IV Scene by Scene.
  • Focus: V Too Hot.
  • Write: VIII The Hot Seat.
