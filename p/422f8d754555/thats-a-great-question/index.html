That’s a Great Question · C1 Unit 10 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on Q&A: buying time (Let me think about that for a second), checking (If I understand you correctly…), not knowing (I don’t have the figures with me, but…), hostile questions (I can see why you’d think that. However…), off-topic questions (That’s a bit outside the scope…), parking (Can I come back to that at the end?) and closing (Does that answer your question?).

The story: Dr. Oko (invented) gave a talk about bees in the city. Now four people ask questions: an exact number, a worried mother, a student with a giant question, and a man who thinks it’s a waste of money.

About 49 min: I · The Q&A (10) · II · Panic or Professional? (9) · III · Keep Calm (7) · IV · The Press Conference (8) · V · Questions from the Floor (15).

Open with: “What’s the scariest part of a presentation: the talk or the questions?”

Students fear the Q&A more than the talk. Show that not knowing is fine: what matters is the next sentence. On the board, ask about the student’s OWN talk topic (from the previous lessons), and play the hostile, the expert, the never-ending questioner. Stop the student if they say “good question” every time.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Let me think about that for a second · If I understand you correctly, you’re asking… · I don’t have the figures with me, but… · I can see why you’d think that. However, … · That’s a bit outside the scope of today’s talk · Can I come back to that at the end? / Does that answer your question?

THE Q&A

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

PANIC OR PROFESSIONAL?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

KEEP CALM

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

QUESTIONS FROM THE FLOOR

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I don’t know. (dry), good question (every time), you are wrong (to the audience), inventing a precise number, that’s not my topic, um… uh… (instead of asking for a second). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE PRESS CONFERENCE (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the board, your student becomes Dr. Oko: a swarm of bees closed the city park for an hour this morning (nobody was hurt), and you are a journalist who wants a headline. Your secret (only you see it): you want her to say “It was our mistake.” Ask the five questions one by one, wait five seconds in silence, and let them buy time, check, admit what they don’t know and park the hard question. Use the PUSH line to interrupt once. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · The Q&A.
  • Focus: II · Panic or Professional? and III · Keep Calm.
  • Out loud: IV · The Press Conference and V · Questions from the Floor.
