Instead of Deciding · The -ING Gate

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: After a preposition, English turns a verb into a noun: -ING.

The grammar: The Gate: after a preposition, only a noun gets through. -ING is how a verb becomes one.

Short class (about 47 min): I Instead Of · II Who Could Have Told You · III The Gate · IV Two Kinds of TO · V Six Voices · VI The Evening Plan · IX What Gets Through. Skip if you’re short on time: VII and VIII About You (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 60 min.

Open with: “What were you supposed to decide?”

After a preposition, English turns a verb into a noun: -ING. I scrolled instead of deciding. She left without saying goodbye. Portuguese puts the infinitive after every preposition, so students say instead of decide and without to know. The trap: some TOs are prepositions too: look forward to seeing, be used to getting up. The test: can IT follow the TO? On screen, a gate: the verb arrives as a block, and only gets through with an -ING tag. Later, an evening clock from 7 to 10 PM lights up hour by hour.

NOT THERAPY

This hour is about the grammar of habits, not about health or mood. If the talk goes there, bring it back to objects and times.

THE GATE

Scene III: Nina’s note to herself. The student reads it out loud and taps every verb that comes right after a preposition (eight of them). Every word can be tapped; “looking” in “really looking forward” is the trap: it is not after a preposition. Then the student completes the rule by choosing inside it. From scene IV to VI the rule stays pinned in the corner.

Scene IV: six sentences with TO. The student says each one from the clue (a verb, or -ING?), taps Check, and the IT test lights up: “I’m used to it” works, so -ING; “I want to it” doesn’t, so a verb. Then they ask you the question under the answer.

THE RECORDINGS

Scene V: the six voices on one sheet, each with play, pause and 0.75×; a voice’s transcript appears after it plays to the end. The student builds the question to that person from the clue, says it, taps Check, then asks it. You answer in character and let them ask one follow-up.

THE EVENING

Scene VI: for each hour the student says the plan from the clue, taps Check, and the clock lights up. Then ask the question under the plan. Push once: “What will stop you from doing it?”

THE PHONE JURY (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the evening plan, your student is the jury in three small phone cases, with the evidence on screen. There is no right verdict: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the verdict harder. Every reason goes through the gate (instead of …ing, without …ing, be used to …ing). Keep it about objects and times, not health or mood. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word is one minute on phones at dinner, then you take the other side.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Instead Of, II Who Could Have Told You, VI The Evening Plan, VII The Phone Jury, VIII About You.
  • Listen: V Six Voices.
  • Focus: III The Gate, IV Two Kinds of TO.
  • Write: IX, their sentence from scene I and tonight’s plan. It goes to their My notes.
