No Pressure · Harry Potter · The Letters

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A boy under the stairs, a pile of letters, and a school that expects everything.

The grammar: Want Him to Go: They expect him to win. They don’t want him to fail.

The class (about 53 min): I Warm-up and Put It in Order · III The Teaser · IV Line by Line · V Your First Day · VI Want Him to Go · VII The Letters · VIII Your Letters · IX The Debate. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Harry now. In the teaser, what does everybody expect him to do? Guess.”

Harry Potter is the new HBO series (2026) from J.K. Rowling’s books, one book per season. Harry lives with an aunt and uncle who treat him badly; on his eleventh birthday, letters arrive: he is a wizard, and Hogwarts wants him to come. At the school a magic hat puts every first-year in a house, and everybody expects something from Harry. No pressure. The grammar: want / expect / need / would like / ask / tell + person + to + verb (they expect him to win, Ron asked Harry to sit), the object pronoun (want him to), and the negative (doesn’t want him to go, told him not to worry).

A note for the teacher: the teaser is calm and magical; the actors speak British English, and the lesson is written in American English. The times follow its captions (23/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Put It in Order (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Put It in Order: four cards from the start of Harry’s story, shuffled. The student taps them in order, saying why (“First…, then…”); then the cards show the real order, and the student tells the whole story in their words. The structure is already in the cards (they want him to…, they tell him not to…).

The words (II): ceremony, future, pressure, friendship and talent, before the video, in memory and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Debate): “A school should decide where students belong.” The student picks a side; the teacher takes the other one. Three reasons on each side and three helping phrases. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is the great hall with floating candles. I: Warm-up and Put It in Order. III: the teaser in three steps. Before: a role-play on cards: you are Harry, eleven; your aunt doesn’t want you to read the letter, Hogwarts wants you to come, and the student tells the aunt what they want. A button reveals the twist (a hundred letters fly out of the fireplace). Watch: five sentences, each with one wrong word (Did you hate the summer reading?; I hope you to work hard); each has a ▶ time: tap it, hear the line, and say the right word; Check shows the fixes. After: who says it? The student matches four people (the professor, Hermione, a teacher, Harry) with four lines from the teaser. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Your First Day: the student ranks five things they need people to do on a first day somewhere new, then compares with your order. VI, Want Him to Go: a letter to Harry on dark paper. The student taps what people want, expect, ask and tell (six places), then finishes the rule board. The board stays in the corner on the next page. VII, The Letters: six sealed envelopes on a table above a small fireplace, each with its own wax seal. Each letter has its words (Hogwarts · want · Harry · come to the school): the student builds the sentence, says it, taps Check, and the right sentence appears while the envelope opens with who sent it (Hogwarts, his aunt, the professor, Ron, the teachers, Dumbledore). VIII, Your Letters: two letters; the teacher picks A (from your family) or B (from your job). IX: the debate.

THE COMMON ERRORS

wants that Harry comes, wants Harry come, want he to, expects Harry working, asked to Harry, told to Harry, asked that he, wants him not to read (doesn’t want him to read), told him to not afraid.

YOUR FIRST DAY (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student has seen Harry arrive in a world where everybody expects something from him. Now it is their own first day somewhere new (a school, a job, a city): they put five things in order, from what they need people to do most to least (explain the rules, invite me to lunch, ask about my life, show me where everything is, leave me alone for a day), and defend the first and the last with need / want / would like + person + to. Then tap to show your order and compare. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their real first day. Recast quietly while they talk (I need that you show me, want me go, want he to); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Put It in Order, V Your First Day, VII The Letters, VIII Your Letters and IX The Debate.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Teaser and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Want Him to Go.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
