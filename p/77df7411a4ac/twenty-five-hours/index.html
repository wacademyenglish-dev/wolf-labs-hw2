Twenty-Five Hours · The Station Clock

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: The student is the station chief.

The grammar: How the Clock Talks: Four ways to say the time, and one word you never need.

Short class (about 47 min): I Yesterday · II Read the Face · III How the Clock Talks · IV Set the Clock · V The Departures Board · VI Day Train or Night Train · VIII Good for You? · IX Your Perfect Day. Skip if you’re short on time: VII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 60 min.

Open with: “What time did you wake up yesterday?”

The student is the station chief. You call the trains; they turn the hands of a real clock with their finger, then give the departures board its words, row by row. At the end, someone’s “perfect day” adds up to twenty-five hours, and the student writes their own, with times in words. No timer, no points. Scene II: the student matches six clocks to their words, and only then plays the station voice. Scene III: sort six times into the four ways to say them, then find the broken part of four lines (the rule stays in the corner).

THE GRAMMAR

o’clock when the long hand is on 12: eight o’clock, never eight hours (hours = how long). half past + the hour that already happened: 8:30 = half past eight (Portuguese “oito e meia” pushes eight and a half). quarter past / quarter to: right side, left side; to names the hour that is coming (8:45 = quarter to nine). AM after midnight, PM after noon.

GOOD FOR YOU? (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Three times from your student’s own life, and they choose a color: green Great, yellow OK, once, red No way! (a beach train at 5:45 AM, dinner at 10:30 PM, English class at 7:00 AM). There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) for a yes/no follow-up. Each round ends with your student asking you back: What time do you…? Recast mistakes quietly (times in words: quarter to six); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud — I Yesterday, VIII Good for You? (three times: green, yellow or red; then the student asks you back).
  • Listen — II Read the Face (the station voice checks), IV Set the Clock (YOU read the departure out loud; only you see the words), VI Day Train or Night Train.
  • Focus — III How the Clock Talks, V The Departures Board, VII Twenty-Five Hours (lunch sits on top of work: the extra hour).
  • Write — IX Your Perfect Day, with the red wavy line for eight hours, eight and a half, half nine and times in numbers (7:30). It goes to their My notes.

SET THE CLOCK

The student drags the hands (the long hand moves in five-minute steps and carries the hour with it past 12). The small buttons do the same for anyone who prefers to tap. After the six trains, swap: you drag the hands and the student says the time; Show answer gives the words of whatever the clock shows.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

The clock moves on both screens when the student lets go of a hand. You judge the speech: “Answered · show the follow-up”. G opens this guide.
