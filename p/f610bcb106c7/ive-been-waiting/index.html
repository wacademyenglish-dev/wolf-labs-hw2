I’ve Been Waiting · B1 Unit 2 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new B1 lesson on present perfect continuous (I’ve been waiting) with for and since, How long have you been…?, the contrast with present perfect simple for results (I’ve had five cups), and state verbs (I’ve known).

The story: Gate 14, 1:30 a.m. The 9 p.m. flight to Lisbon (invented airline) is delayed. Marta messages her brother; then the student talks to Dona Rosa (knitting), Ken (a musician playing for the gate) and Dr. Silva (answering emails).

About 49 min: I · Messages from Gate 14 (9) · II · How Long? (10) · III · Three Passengers (15) · IV · One Free Seat (7) · V · Your Long Waits (8).

Open with: “What is the longest time you have waited for something? Where?”

This is the classic trap for Brazilians: “estou esperando há três horas” becomes “I wait / I am waiting for three hours”. Make the student feel the time line: started in the past, still going on. In the mission, be a little dramatic as the tired passengers.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

have / has been + -ing · for + length · since + start · How long have you been…? · simple for results: I’ve had five · state verbs: I’ve known

MESSAGES FROM GATE 14

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

HOW LONG?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THREE PASSENGERS

You are each passenger. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student tells the airline about each passenger. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

YOUR LONG WAITS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I wait since nine (I’ve been waiting since nine), since three hours (for three hours), How long are you waiting? (How long have you been waiting?), I’ve been knowing (I’ve known), I am here since (I’ve been here since). B1: one correction per answer is enough.

ONE FREE SEAT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the three passengers, the airline has one seat on an earlier flight via Porto. The student puts Dona Rosa, Ken, Dr. Silva and themselves in order and explains the first and the last with has been + -ing, for and since. Then tap “Show my teacher’s order” (yours puts Ken first) and agree on one order together. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Messages from Gate 14.
  • Focus: II · How Long?.
  • Out loud: III · Three Passengers, IV · One Free Seat and V · Your Long Waits.
