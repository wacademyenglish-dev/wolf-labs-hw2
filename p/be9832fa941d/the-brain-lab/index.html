The Brain Lab · The Scan

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A lesson about what a second language does to the brain, with a short video and two real tests.

The grammar: The Dial: Not just “faster”. How MUCH faster?

Short class (about 43 min): I The Language Passport · II The Stroop Test · IV The Video · VI The Dial · VII Which Bilingual Are You? · X Your Brain Report. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 57 min.

Open with: “Which language do you dream in? Always the same one?”

A lesson about what a second language does to the brain, with a short video and two real tests. Your student is the volunteer in a language lab. You are the researcher: you ask, you start the tests, you read the results. Every scene lights up one region of the brain on the screen. The grammar is how MUCH more: I was a lot slower in English. Switching is far harder. The trap: very faster, more easier. The big question: is it ever too late to learn a language?

THE PASSPORT

Scene I is only talk. The last question is a trap: if your student says very harder, tap it and don’t correct. The report gives it back at the end.

THE TESTS ARE REAL

Scenes II and VIII: press Start, then tap ANSWERED after each word or card; the screen measures the time. In the Stroop test only you see the ink’s name. In the switch test, tap MISTAKE whenever your student uses the wrong language. Your student only speaks.

THE VIDEO

Scene IV: a short video about the bilingual brain, sealed until you press play, in four chapters; each stops by itself. The questions are open. If a chapter stops mid-sentence, adjust it. After the four chapters, the last step is ONE screen: the video stays on the left, with the brain scan under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines from the video (tap a time to hear that moment again). Every right answer lights one region of the brain. On a Lie the student says the true line, with a degree (much, far, a lot, a bit). When all six are done, the researcher asks one last question (“Is a bilingual brain very different?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud.

THE DIAL

Scene VI: your student reads the numbers and says the sentence; you tap the degree you heard. If you hear very + comparative or more + -er, tap it: the screen explains.

The words (III): bilingual, subtitles, proficient, accent and advantages, before the video, in match the columns and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE COUNCIL (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the switch test, your student sits on the lab council and votes on three proposals: Yes, No, or Propose a change. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. Recast quietly while they talk (“very faster” → “much faster”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Language Passport, II The Stroop Test, VII Which Bilingual Are You?, VIII The Switch Test and IX The Council.
  • Listen: III The Words, IV The Video and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Dial.
  • Write: X Your Brain Report.
