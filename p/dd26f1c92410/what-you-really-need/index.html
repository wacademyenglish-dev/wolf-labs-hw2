What You Really Need · The Advice Hour

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: It’s 11 PM and your student is the host of The Advice Hour, a late-night radio show.

The grammar: cleft sentences for advice: What you need is… · All you have to do is + base verb · The problem is that…

Short class (about 47 min): I The Dial · II The Words · III The Lines Are Open · IV Solomon’s Paradox · VI The Mixing Desk · VII Tonight’s Letter · X Sign-off. Skip if you’re short on time: V, VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “Who do you call when you have a problem? Why that person?”

It’s 11 PM and your student is the host of The Advice Hour, a late-night radio show. First they turn the dial: four shows, four questions about their own life. Then the lines open: five listeners call with problems (a job in another city, a friend who owes money, a dad who is never home, a daughter who wants to paint, a man who just retired), and the host gives advice on air. Then the video explains why that was so easy: Solomon’s Paradox, we reason more wisely about other people’s lives than our own. At the end, one more letter arrives, and it’s the student’s own, written in the third person. The host reads it on air, answers it like a stranger’s letter, and signs off with three lines of advice for themselves. The goal: the student leaves thinking about their own life.

The grammar: cleft sentences for advice, the important part goes last. What you need is + noun (What you need is a break); What you need to do is / All you have to do is + base verb (All you have to do is ask); What you want is + to + verb (What you really want is to paint); The thing (that) you should change is…; The problem is that…. This is a review lesson for the cleft sentences in It Wasn’t the Time: that lesson focuses on facts and focus (It was the conversation that…); this one uses clefts to give advice.

THE VIDEO

“I FIXED My Whole Life In 1 Night” (Henry Grey Earls, 2:26, we use 0:00–2:14). Sealed until you press play, in four chapters; each stops by itself. The last line of the video is an all-cleft: “Sometimes all you need to do is step outside of yourself…”. The video also opens with one: “All it takes is one simple technique.” Point to both after the video. After the video (page IV, step 3): ONE screen. The video stays on the left, with the five radio lines under it; on the right, five True or Lie lines from the video, written as cleft sentences (tap the time to hear that moment again). Every right answer lights a line. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all five are done, a caller asks one last question (“What do you do?”): the student taps an answer and says the advice out loud.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

Portuguese builds these sentences with a pronoun + “what” (“the what”, “all the what”), so word for word the student says all what you need and the thing what. After “do … is” Portuguese uses the infinitive, which the student turns into -ing (what you need to do is calling). “The problem is that” gets an extra subject (the problem it’s that). And because “what” starts questions, the student adds do: What do you need is a holiday.

THE DIAL, THE LINES AND THE LETTER

Scene I is only conversation: tap a station, ask the question, then answer it yourself. Scene III comes before the rule: any good advice counts; tap the version you heard, listening to “do … is + base verb”, “all you”, and no extra it. Scene VII: let the student write alone, in the third person (“A listener, 38, from…”). If they write “I”, point to the five callers. Scene VIII: the student reads the letter on air and answers it, one step at a time. The last question is the one from the video. Take your time there: it is the heart of the lesson.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

All what you need is…, the thing what…, what you need to do is calling, the problem it’s that…, What do you need is…, What you want is paint.

THE HOT SEAT (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Tonight the host is the guest. You are Dana Cole, a journalist writing about Solomon’s paradox from the video; your student stays the host of The Advice Hour and answers four questions in the role, with a cleft (What most callers need is…, All I have to do is…). Ask, wait five seconds in silence, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) at the end. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

  • Listen: II The Words, IV Solomon’s Paradox and V Line by Line.
