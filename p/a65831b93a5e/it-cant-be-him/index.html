It Can’t Be Him · The Batman · The Riddler’s Cards

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A rainy city, a killer who leaves cards, and a detective in a mask.

The grammar: Must, Might, Can’t: It must be him. It might be him. It can’t be him.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and The Evidence · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V The Jury · VI Must, Might, Can’t · VII The Riddler’s Cards · VIII Your Deductions · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the case now. In the trailer, who might the Riddler be? Guess.”

The Batman (2022), with Robert Pattinson: more a detective story than a superhero film. Somebody is killing the people who run Gotham and leaves a card for the Batman at every crime scene, with a riddle. Lieutenant Gordon brings the masked man to the crime scenes because he is the only one who reads the riddles. The grammar: modals of deduction: must be (sure yes, about 90%), might / may / could be (maybe, 50%), can’t be (sure no, 0%). No to after the modal, always be, and mustn’t be is not deduction (it means “not allowed”).

A note for the teacher: a PG-13 trailer, tense from start to finish (guns drawn, a fight, fire, a car chase). Nothing graphic. Lines checked against the official subtitles; the chase (1:46 to 2:15) is ESTIMATED, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and The Evidence Bag (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Evidence Bag: Five pieces of evidence open one at a time: a card with a question mark, a desk full of notes, a board with red strings, a police station, a rainy street. With each one, the student deduces out loud (it must be…, it might be…, it can’t be…) and changes their story if they need to. At the end, a short text says whose case it is, and the student tells the whole story. Don’t correct the modal yet: let them use it, the rule comes later.

The words (II): long, care, worse, different and under, before the video, in mystery meaning and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is the Batman, after the third card; the teacher is Gordon and asks five questions about the case (who the Riddler is, could it be a cop, Selina, why he writes to the Batman, where he might go next). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a rainy Gotham street with red lights. I: Warm-up and The Evidence. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a wheel of suspects (Gordon, Selina, the mayor…): the student says who might be the Riddler and who can’t be. Watch: Which word did you hear? Five lines, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: True or lie? Five sentences, each with a ▶; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Jury: a new card on Gordon’s desk; the student reads the evidence and gives a verdict with must / might / can’t, in two rounds. VI, Must, Might, Can’t: a note from the Riddler on a dark card. The student taps the words that show how sure he is, then finishes the rule board (must / might / can’t, and never “to be”). The board stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Riddler’s Cards: six cards face down on the table. For each clue the student reads it, says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the card turns over with a stamp (MUST 90%, MIGHT / COULD 50%, CAN’T 0%) and the right sentence appears. VIII: five strange things in daily life (the neighbor’s lights, an unknown number, a box at the door…): the student matches each with a deduction and says why. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

must to be, might is, must being, mustn’t be Gordon (can’t be), the killer can be a cop (might / could: a maybe about now), don’t can, Who he could be?, and the wrong level for the clue (must when there is only one small clue).

THE JURY (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student is the jury in a small new case: this morning a green card with a riddle was on Gordon’s desk, inside the police station (nobody is hurt). Round 1: read three pieces of evidence and say who left the card (the night cleaner, the reporter, a City Hall worker). Round 2: some officers think the Batman himself might be the Riddler: verdict? The student answers with must / might / can’t + be and gives the reason from the evidence. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to add a new fact that makes the verdict harder. Recast quietly while they talk (must to be, can be for maybe, mustn’t be for can’t be); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Evidence, V The Jury, VII The Riddler’s Cards, VIII Your Deductions and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Must, Might, Can’t.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
