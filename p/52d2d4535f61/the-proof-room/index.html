The Proof Room · The Six Padlocks

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: an escape room. Every lock needs the right key.

The grammar: could = years (a skill) · was able to / managed to = one moment (you did it; managed to when it was hard) · couldn’t = both.

The lesson (about 61 min): I Warm-up · II The Jar · III The Four Keys · IV The Escape Call (listening) · V The Players’ Council · VI The Six Locks · VII The Trap · VIII The Interview · IX Your Escape Story · X Your Three Sentences.

Open with: “Could you swim when you were six?”

“I could open the jar last night” is broken English. Make it negative, “I couldn’t open the jar last night”, and it’s perfect. That’s why nobody ever corrected it. One question decides it: years (a skill, a childhood) take could; one moment (you did it, once) takes was able to, or managed to when it was hard. In Portuguese: conseguia (sempre) = could · consegui (uma vez) = managed to / was able to.

I · WARM-UP

Three questions about their life. Write down the third answer word for word: most students say “I could” for one moment (“Last year I could pass my test”). Don’t correct it. It comes back in X.

II · THE JAR

Four pictures, one at a time: the student reads each line out loud. At the end, “I could open it” is crossed out and “I managed to open it” appears. Ask the one question: years, or one moment? Show answer explains it. The Portuguese bridge is there for the click; you don’t need to read it.

III · THE FOUR KEYS

All four rules are open on the screen, with the time ruler (a long bar is years, a short mark is one moment). Tap a key: the student makes one sentence about their life with it. Four keys, four sentences.

IV · THE ESCAPE CALL (LISTENING)

A 1-minute phone call: Mia (American) and Tom (British), who just got out of an escape room. 1) Listen once for the story (three questions; the transcript opens only after they listen). 2) Line by line: each line has its own play button; the student says which key Tom or Mia used, then taps. 0.75× slows it down. 3) Two talk questions about them.

V · YEARS OR ONE MOMENT?

Six pairs. The four keys stay on screen as a legend. The student says why before tapping.

VI · THE SIX LOCKS

Locks 2 and 5 open with two keys (was able to / managed to): ask what changes. Every open lock brings a quick question about their life with the same key. Don’t skip them: that’s the speaking.

VII · THE TRAP

Three of five are fine as they are; the two that break are positive. If they’re stuck, cover the NOT and read it again.

VIII · THE INTERVIEW

Question cards with the four keys. First the student interviews you (answer for real); then swap. After every answer, “When exactly?”: it decides the key.

IX · YOUR ESCAPE STORY

A real problem they solved, in six steps. After each step, tap to read the push-back. Listen for I could solve it about one moment: that is the mistake.

X · YOUR THREE SENTENCES

Read back the warm-up answers you wrote down. The student rewrites them with the right keys. It goes to their My notes.

THE PLAYERS’ COUNCIL (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Tom’s call, the escape room owner wants three new rules, and your student votes: yes, no, or propose a change. There is no right answer. Let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then vote against them and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (one moment: managed to / was able to, not “could”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up, V The Players’ Council, VI The Six Locks, VIII The Interview, IX Your Escape Story.
  • Listen: IV The Escape Call.
  • Focus: II The Jar, III The Four Keys, VII The Trap.
  • Write: X, the three warm-up answers again, with the right keys.
