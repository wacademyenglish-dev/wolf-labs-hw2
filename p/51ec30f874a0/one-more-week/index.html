One More Week · The Hourglass

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: The past form here is not about the past: it’s about tomorrow, and about somebody else.

The grammar: Who Is Doing It?: You: I’d rather + plain verb. Someone else: I’d rather you + past form.

Short class (about 47 min): I Somebody Near You · II Who Is Doing It? · III Three Steps Back · IV It’s Time · V Room to Say No · VI One More Week · X The Door You Leave Open. Skip if you’re short on time: VII, VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 67 min.

Open with: “Something you’d rather people didn’t do on the bus.”

The past form here is not about the past: it’s about tomorrow, and about somebody else. I’d rather you stayed (another person, past form). I’d rather stay (me, plain verb). It’s time we left (it should already be happening). Already done: I’d rather you hadn’t said that. Students say I’d rather you go and It’s time we go. The screen is an hourglass: negotiate with the right form and it turns, and Friday the 10th becomes Friday the 17th.

THE PAST ISN’T PAST

Scene II: the student reads Anna’s fridge note out loud, taps the verbs and finishes the rule (it stays on the screen). Say it once (“stayed” here is about tonight).

THE RECORDINGS

Nine recordings from two flatmates, Anna and Mark. One says “invite people round”: that’s British for “invite people over”. Each plays with pause and 0.75×; the line appears only after listening. Scene V line 1 can be heard both ways: let your student defend it.

THE DEADLINE

Scene VI: you are the boss. The boss lines are on your screen. Start the 90-second clock, push back once or twice, and let the student negotiate. Each time the student uses one of the four tools they tap it, and the hourglass turns.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I’d rather you go, It’s time we go, I’d rather to stay, I’d rather you didn’t said, It’s time for we…. Scene IV: match each situation to its “It’s time” sentence, then say the pairs out loud. Scene VII: Right or Wrong on six sentences; if it is wrong, the student says the fix.

THE HOT SEAT (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Mark has moved out. Your student is Anna, in the hot seat; you are Sam, who wants the room. Ask the four questions and let them answer with I’d rather you + past and It’s time. Your secret (only you see it): Sam plays the drums, and you say it only if Anna asks about hobbies. Then they decide on screen if Sam gets the room. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence before each reply, then use the PUSH line to make the choice harder. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (watch for “I’d rather you go”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Somebody Near You, VI One More Week, VIII The Hot Seat, IX Your Sentence, Again.
  • Listen: III Three Steps Back, V Room to Say No.
  • Focus: II Who Is Doing It?, IV It’s Time, VII I’d Rather You Go.
  • Write: X, their own wishes. It goes to their My notes.
