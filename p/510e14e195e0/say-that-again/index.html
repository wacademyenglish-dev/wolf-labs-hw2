Say That Again · The Static Radio

This hour doesn’t make the student understand more English. It gives them something to say when they don’t. Nine recordings at real speed. The screen is a radio with static: the covered word only clears when the student asks the right way: Sorry? · Could you say that again? · Could you speak more slowly? · Do you mean…? · How do you spell that? · What does … mean?

NOT UNDERSTANDING IS NORMAL

Say it out loud at the start. What isn’t survivable is nodding and saying yes. Do you mean…? is the most useful one: it gives back what they caught.

THE AUDIO

Nine recordings, checked against the voices on 06/10: the transcripts now show what is really said (contractions, “Lovely!”, “Good afternoon.”). The slow border recording starts at “…the purpose of your visit” and adds “Why are you here?”. Don’t translate the audio: if they need it slower, they ask, in English.

THE FAST TALKER

Scene VIII: you read each message fast, on purpose. Don’t slow down until they ask properly. Tap each way out you hear.

THE JURY (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the static, your student judges three travelers who didn’t understand: Lucas who says “What?”, Ana who says “yes, yes”, Marco stuck at the border. Read the evidence, let them choose a verdict, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Every verdict ends with what the traveler should say, using the ways out of scene III. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
