The Crit · English for Designers · Lesson 7 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: giving feedback in a critique: what, why, how; specific positives (What’s working really well is…); softening (a bit, might, I wonder if…, You could try…, What if…?); balancing positive and negative.

The story: An NN/g video about critique, a colleague’s bad feedback on a fictional restaurant website, then a crit where the teacher is the designer.

About 45 min: I · Three Tips for a Crit (8) · II · Fix the Feedback (10) · III · The Studio Council (7) · IV · Your Turn in the Crit (16) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “In your team, how do people give feedback? Direct? Polite? Silent?”

In the crit (cadeira), show the student any real website, poster or room photo you choose (not your own work, to avoid pressure), or describe a fictional gym home page. React a bit defensively once, so the student practices staying kind.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

What kind of feedback are you looking for? · What’s working really well is… · The … is a bit… · Users might… · You could try… / What if…? · I wonder if…

THREE TIPS FOR A CRIT

Nielsen Norman Group, 3 tips for effective design critique feedback (0:28–3:15).

FIX THE FEEDBACK

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

YOUR TURN IN THE CRIT

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to bring a design (their own or someone else’s) to receive feedback in the next lesson.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I don’t like (with no reason), You must change (You could try…), It is a little bit ugly (It feels a bit heavy), I wonder if it has not enough contrast (I wonder if it has enough contrast). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE STUDIO COUNCIL (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the crit role-play, a council vote. A fictional studio proposes three new rules for its Friday crit (juniors speak first; no “I like it” without what, why, how; feedback only by chat). Your student votes Yes, Propose a change or No, and says why. Wait five seconds in silence, don’t vote first, and use the PUSH line to make the vote harder. There is no right answer. Recast quietly while they talk (What’s working is …, I wonder if …, It might …, You could try …); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
