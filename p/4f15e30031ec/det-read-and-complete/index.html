Read and Complete · DET Prep · Lesson 4 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the Read and Complete question: a paragraph with many words cut in half, about three minutes for all of them. The first sentence is complete. The student learns a four-step method and practices with two timed paragraphs.

The story: No story: the student is the candidate. Method → two timed paragraphs → checking a candidate’s answers for mistakes.

About 40 min: I · The Method (8) · II · Two Paragraphs (14) · III · A Quiet Saturday (7) · IV · Find the Mistakes (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Three minutes, ten gaps: where do you start?” Most students say “the first gap”. The method says: read everything first.

This question uses American spelling. Grammar endings (-s, -ed, -er, plural) are where most points are lost: train the student to check them in the last 30 seconds.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

read the whole paragraph first · think grammar: -s, -ed, -ing, plural · small words first · check spelling at the end · the first sentence gives the topic · American spelling

THE METHOD

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

TWO PARAGRAPHS

The gaps: the student types only the missing letters (the box moves to the next gap by itself). With a clock, the text stays blurred until you start it. Check paints right and wrong and shows the full word. Spelling counts.

FIND THE MISTAKES

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to time the reading step: about 20 seconds is the goal.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

peoples, withought, more hard, do a mistake, allso, helps the brain stores. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

A QUIET SATURDAY (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the two paragraphs, a real negotiation. The test is at home, with a camera, and nobody can be in the room. Your student needs Saturday 9 to 11; you are Leo, the flatmate whose band rehearses in the living room at ten. Your secret limit (only you see it) is softer: the band can start at 11:30 or play in the drummer’s garage, but you want a favor back. Ask the four questions in order, insist a little, wait five seconds in silence after each, and don’t give your limit first. Then close the deal on screen and use the PUSH line. Recast quietly while they talk (It would be easier if…, What if you start later?, First… Then…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
