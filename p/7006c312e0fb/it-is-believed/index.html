It Is Believed · Disclosure Day · The Redacted File

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Eighty years of “it is said”, and one stolen file.

The grammar: It Is Believed: It is believed that we are not alone.

The class (about 53 min): I Warm-up and The Secret File · III The Trailer · IV Line by Line · V Your Call · VI It Is Believed · VII The Redacted File · VIII What People Say · IX The Interview. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the history now. In the trailer, what is the stolen file believed to contain?”

Disclosure Day (2026), Steven Spielberg. A man steals a secret file, proof that we are not alone; the truth has been hidden for 79 years, and nobody official will say “we know”. So the news is careful, and the passive hides who is talking. The grammar: passive reporting, with two doors: It is said / believed / thought / known that + clause, or subject + is said / believed + to + verb (to be for a state, to have + participle for the past). Never both doors at once.

A note for the teacher: tense, not graphic. Lines checked against the official subtitles (24/09), told in our own words. Lesson 298 uses the same film (the world clocks); this one is the paper.

Warm-up and The Secret File (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Secret File: five documents come out of a secret file, one at a time: Roswell 1947, Project Blue Book, the Navy videos, the stolen file, the TV news. With each, the student reports what is said or believed, like a careful journalist. At the end, they say what the file is believed to prove.

The words (II): inclined, universe, extraordinary, accept and disclosure, before the video, in mystery meaning and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Interview): the teacher is the TV reporter from Kansas City. The student asks five questions (the file, the thief, the visitors, the President, what was reported about her). Then swap: the student is a government spokesperson. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a dark government archive. I: Warm-up and The Secret File. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: six sentences (The Earth goes around the Sun; There is life on other planets…); the student taps the sentence, then the box: “It is known that… (we are sure)” or “It is said / believed that… (we are not sure)”. Watch: which word did you hear? Six lines from the trailer (inclined or included, believe or relieve, steal or feel, extraordinary or ordinary, accept or except, disclosure or closure), each with a ▶ time (no ▶ points between 0:41 and 1:02, to skip a harsh line of the trailer): tap it and the video jumps there. After: five questions about the trailer; the student taps the right answer. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Your Call: a dilemma in three rounds (the reporter on air, the lights over the farm, Earth’s message); the student chooses and says why. VI, It Is Believed: six things people say on the left (People say he stole the file…) and six careful sentences on the right (He is said to have stolen the file…); the student matches them. When all six are matched, the rule board with the two doors appears and stays in the corner. VII, The Redacted File: a typewritten page, “File 79-D”, with six lines under black bars. Each line is a sentence that may be right or wrong (He is said that he stole the file); the student taps Right or Wrong and, if it is wrong, says it the careful way. A right answer pulls the bar off and the line appears underneath. At the end, the whole page is open and a red stamp says Released. VIII, What People Say: a wheel with six questions about the student’s city (food, a haunted place, the richest person, the weather, what the city is known for, a legend); the student spins and answers with It is said that… IX: the interview.

THE COMMON ERRORS

It believes / It says that (Portuguese “diz-se que”), Is known that (no It), He is said that he stole (two doors), is said to stole, is believe, The signal thinks to, The reporter reported to have (no be), to are, the visitors is, to have knew.

YOUR CALL (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, a dilemma in three rounds from the world of the trailer: the reporter on air with a message her boss says not to read, the neighbour everybody laughs at for the lights over the farm, and who should write Earth’s one message to the visitors. The student chooses one card and says why the careful way (it is said that…, he is thought to be…); there is no right answer, and they can say their own idea. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (it says that, he is said that he, to have knew); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Secret File, V Your Call, VII The Redacted File, VIII What People Say and IX The Interview.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI It Is Believed.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
