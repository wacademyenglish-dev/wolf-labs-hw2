Seven Nights · The Sleep Curve

Tonight is about your student’s own sleep. There is no story: there are real voices from a sleep lab, and a sleep curve on the screen that changes with every rule they say about themselves. The grammar is the grammar of habits and facts: if + present, present (If I drink coffee late, I wake up at night.). They discover the pattern from the voices before they see the rule.

THEIR NUMBERS COME BACK

Scene I asks for three numbers from last night (hours of sleep, cups of coffee, minutes on a screen in bed). They shape the curve in scene IV and come back in the last scene.

THE CURVE

Scene IV is the heart. Six evening habits. For each one, the student says their own rule and you tap what it does to their sleep. If they say will, tap HEARD “WILL”: the curve doesn’t move, because a habit is not a prediction.

THE AUDIO · THE MISTAKES

Six recordings, checked against the voices on 06/10: they match. Listen for If I drink coffee, I will wake up (for a habit), if he drink, and when for something that doesn’t always happen.

THE WORST WAKE-UP (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VI is now a ranking: five ways to wake up, from the alarm at 5 AM to a work call at seven. Your student puts them in order (1 is the worst) and says what happens with if or when: when for what always happens, if for what may happen. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Show your order and agree on one top 3 together. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (the -s of he/she, no will); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Last Night in Numbers, IV Your Curve, V The Domino, VI The Worst Wake-Up, IX The Sleep Coach.
  • Listen: II, VII, VIII. Focus: III The Rule of the Body.
  • Write: X, their rules for a good night. It goes to their My notes.
