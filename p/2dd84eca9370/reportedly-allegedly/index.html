Reportedly, Allegedly · C1 Unit 8 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on source and distance: reportedly, allegedly (accused, not proven), according to + source, apparently, supposedly (with doubt), seemingly, sources say, rumour has it that.

The story: The bronze statue of the town’s founder vanished from Central Square overnight (invented town). City Radio reports it; the student checks three sources: a night bus driver, the museum director and a teenager with a shaky video.

About 54 min: I · Breaking News (10) · II · How Sure Is the Reporter? (9) · III · Protect Yourself (7) · IV · The Editor’s Call (8) · V · Check the Sources (14) · VI · What Have You Heard? (6).

Open with: “When you hear a surprising story, how do you decide if it’s true?”

These words protect the speaker: they say where information came from and how sure we are. Three traps: according to me (use in my opinion), allegedly for good news (use reportedly), and apparently × supposedly. The mission is a fact-check: you are three sources with different levels of proof.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

reportedly · allegedly / it is alleged that · according to + source · apparently · supposedly (with doubt) · sources say, rumour has it that

BREAKING NEWS

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

HOW SURE IS THE REPORTER?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

PROTECT YOURSELF

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

CHECK THE SOURCES

You are each source. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then you are the editor: the student says the news line with according to / allegedly / reportedly. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

WHAT HAVE YOU HEARD?

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

according to me, the men stole it (not proven), she allegedly won a prize, apparently (when you doubt it: supposedly), rumour has that, according the police. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE EDITOR’S CALL (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, your student is the editor at City Radio and makes three tough calls about the missing statue: the rumour that it was a copy, a listener’s blurry photo, and a tip from a friend at the museum. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Listen for reportedly, allegedly, according to and sources say. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Breaking News.
  • Focus: II · How Sure Is the Reporter? and III · Protect Yourself.
  • Out loud: IV · The Editor’s Call, V · Check the Sources and VI · What Have You Heard?.
