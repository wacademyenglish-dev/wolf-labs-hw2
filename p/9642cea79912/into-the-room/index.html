Into the Room · The Blueprint

Portuguese keeps the path in the verb (one verb to enter, one to leave, one to cross). English keeps it in a small road word: into, out of, onto, off, through, across, past. The screen is a blueprint: a ball moves exactly along the path the student describes. At the end, the student guides you, eyes closed, to a treasure.

THE T THAT MOVES

in is a place, into is a path. on is a place, onto is a path. Every road has a small diagram (the box and the arrow): point at it when they hesitate.

THE SLIPS

enter in the room (enter takes nothing) · go out the house (out OF) · got out the bus (buses, trains, planes: get on / get off; cars and taxis: get in / get out of) · crossed across.

EYES CLOSED

Scene VII: press “My eyes are closed” and close them for real. Move only when the instruction is clear. A wrong road word means a wall: say so, and let them fix it.

THE BEST ROUTE (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Your student’s real routes become a decision: five ways to get to work, from the best to the worst. Let them order all five, wait five seconds in silence, then they explain the top and the last one with the road words. Show your own order and disagree on purpose; use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly (walk across, not cross across); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
