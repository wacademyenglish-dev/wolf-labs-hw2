Speak About the Photo · DET Prep · Lesson 8 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the Speak About the Photo question: one photo, about 20 seconds to prepare, then 30 to 90 seconds of speaking. The student learns a four-stop route (big picture → places → details → a guess with a reason) so they never run out of things to say.

The story: No story: three photos (a market at dusk, a home kitchen, a small train station at night). The photos have no people, so the student practices places, objects and guesses.

About 43 min: I · The Route (9) · II · Make It Richer (7) · III · Three Photos (16) · IV · The Photographer’s Chair (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: Show photo 1 for five seconds, hide it, and ask: “What do you remember?” The route organizes exactly that.

Do not interrupt while the clock runs. After each photo, go through the checklist together and read the model: then let the student try the SAME photo again. The second try is where the learning happens.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

This photo shows… / It looks like… · in the foreground / in the background · on the left / next to · there is a… / there are some… · It might be… because… · fill 90 seconds

THE ROUTE

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

MAKE IT RICHER

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THREE PHOTOS

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Time the second try of one photo: most students gain 20 to 30 seconds with the route.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

there is many, on the background, it might is, it looks a…, stopping at 20 seconds. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE PHOTOGRAPHER’S CHAIR (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the three photos with the real clock, a hot seat. Now the student IS the photographer who took them, and you are Nina, the editor of a travel magazine choosing a cover. Ask the four questions in order (the market, why there are no people, the dark station, the cover), wait five seconds in silence after each, and don’t help with the route: it should come out naturally now. Your secret (only you see it): you love the station but think it’s too dark, so make them defend their choice. Then choose the cover on screen and use the PUSH line. Recast quietly while they talk (in the foreground, there are, it might be… because…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
