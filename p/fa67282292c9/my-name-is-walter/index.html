My Name Is Walter · Breaking Bad · The Field File

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A chemistry teacher, a sick man, a desert road.

The grammar: How to Ask: A question word, then the verb, then the person.

The class (about 55 min): I Warm-up and The Interview · III The Tape · IV OK or Not OK? · V Scene by Scene · VI How to Ask · VII The Field File · VIII You or He? · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Walter now. Where is he in the first scene? Why is he there? Guess.”

In the first scene of Breaking Bad (AMC, 2008, created by Vince Gilligan), Walter White, a chemistry teacher from Albuquerque played by Bryan Cranston, stands on a desert road and records a message: his name, his address, his family. That is exactly what an A1 student asks and answers. The grammar: question words (who, what, where, how old, how many, why), with be (What is your name? How old are you?) and with do for other verbs (Where do you live? What do you do?), and does for he / she.

A note for the teacher: the scene shows a gun at the end, and two men lying in the back of the RV. Watch it once before class; if it is not for your student, stop at 3:15. The video has no captions, so the five frames on the film strip are estimated, and each moment is told in our own words. A1: present simple and be only.

Warm-up and The Interview (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Interview: You are Walter White: the card with his facts shows only on your screen (dashed border). The student asks questions (the chips help: What is your name? How old are you? Where do you live?…) and taps each one they ask; answer in short A1 sentences, in character. Then the student guesses who you are and how your life is, and the photo shows the answer. A1: the question order is the point (Where do you live?, not Where you live?). Correct it gently by asking it back the right way.

The words (II): family, speaking, love, days and heart, before the video, in match the columns and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student sits in the hot seat as Walter on the road; the teacher is the police officer and asks five questions. Then swap: the student asks. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an old RV alone on a desert road at dawn, with a video camera on a tripod. I: Warm-up and The Interview. III: the scene in three steps. Before: five clues, one at a time (a desert, an old RV, a man and a video camera, fifty years old, a teacher); the student taps Next clue and guesses who he is. Watch: Which word did you hear? Five lines from the tape (name or game, Lane or Line, love or live…), each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: five questions (Where does he live? Who is he speaking to now?…); the student taps the right answer, with a ▶ to hear it again. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, How to Ask: six questions on the left (What is your name? How old are you?…) and six answers on the right; the student matches them. When all six are matched, the rule board appears and stays in the corner on the next pages. VI, The Field File: a police file in typewriter letters with six lines (name, age, city, job, wife, why here). On each line the answer is already on the file; the student asks Walter the question out loud, taps Check, and the right question appears. The answer is typed into the file. With all six lines, the stamp: IDENTIFIED. VII, You or He?: six questions about Walter, each with one wrong word (Where do Walter live?); the student says the right one and taps Check. VIII: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

What your name is?, How old you are?, How many years do you have? / I have 25 years, Where you live?, Where does you live?, What are you do?, Who are your wife?, How much children, Where he lives?

OK OR NOT OK? (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the first scene: your student judges three things Walter does on the road. He drives in his underwear, he drives very fast, he says his name and address to a camera. Two colored buttons, OK or Not OK, and an almost-ready sentence with a question word (Who cares? Where are his trousers? How fast is he?). There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Keep it light: nothing about crime. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Interview, IV OK or Not OK?, VII The Field File and VIII You or He?.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Tape and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI How to Ask.
  • Write: IX The Hot Seat.
