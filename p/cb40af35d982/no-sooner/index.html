No Sooner Had We Arrived · The Time Gap

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: 1921.

The grammar: Two events, one sentence, and the verb jumps: No sooner had we landed than the storm hit.

Short class (about 53 min): I Your Worst Trip · II The Journal · III At Once or Only Then? · IV The Council · V The Route · VI Brave or Selfish? · VII The Harbor · VIII The Last Page. On the practice pages the student speaks first and taps on their own screen. Full lesson: about 53 min.

Open with: “Tell me about a trip where everything went wrong, one thing after another.”

1921. A captain leads a small expedition across the Arctic ice, and nothing goes right. Her journal tells it with drama: every day, two events in a row become one inverted sentence. We landed. Then the storm hit. → No sooner had we landed than the storm hit. The grammar: No sooner had + subject + participle … than; Hardly / Scarcely had … when; Not until / Only when / Only after … did + subject + verb, where the inversion goes in the SECOND clause. And when NOT to invert: in a chat, as soon as is fine.

ON SCREEN

The time gap: two dots on a line. With NO SOONER and HARDLY the second dot slides into the first (AT ONCE). With NOT UNTIL and ONLY WHEN a green bar shows the waiting (ONLY THEN). In scene V the team moves along the ice map, camp by camp.

The Journal (II): one page of Elin Hale’s journal, on dark paper. The student reads it out loud, with drama, and taps the words that jump to the front (No sooner had we · than · Hardly had we · when · Not until · did we see · Only when · did I understand). Every word can be tapped; the last line (As soon as Tomas reads this) is a trap: no inversion, and that is right. Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line (had before the subject, than, when, did we see, at once, as soon as in a chat). The rule then stays in the corner of III and V.

At Once or Only Then? (III): six moments from the journal. The student taps a moment, then a box: AT ONCE (No sooner had … than · Hardly had … when) or ONLY THEN (Not until … did · Only when … did). The choice is about time, not form: Seconds later goes to AT ONCE, Then, at last goes to ONLY THEN. A green border is right, a red one goes back. Then the student says each moment as one sentence with the opening of its box. You listen and correct.

The Route (V): six camps. The clue gives two events and the first words (No sooner …, Not until …, Hardly …). The student says one dramatic sentence, then taps Check: the model sentence appears, the time gap shows AT ONCE or ONLY THEN, and the team moves to the next camp. At the end, the student tells the whole route in three sentences, without looking.

THE COMMON ERRORS

No sooner we had landed (no inversion), no sooner … when, hardly … than, Not until the fog lifted we saw (the second clause must invert), had we land, did we saw.

A NOTE

The live B2 inversion lesson teaches never, not only, rarely, little. This one is only the inversion that orders TIME in a story.

THE COUNCIL (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene IV is new: before the route home, the student sits on Captain Hale’s council and votes on three motions (leave the science behind, take the shortcut over thin ice, send two walkers to the ship): Yes, Propose a change, or No. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then vote against them and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. They justify each vote with an inverted sentence (Not until we… should we…). The last word is one minute as the captain. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Worst Trip, IV The Council, V The Route, VI Brave or Selfish?, VII The Harbor (you are the journalist).
  • Focus: II The Journal, III At Once or Only Then?
  • Write: VIII, the student’s disastrous trip as three dramatic sentences. They go to their My notes.
