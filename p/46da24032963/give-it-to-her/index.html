Give It to Her · The Birthday Table

It’s Grandma Rosa’s 80th birthday. The table is ready, but every name tag is wrong. The student fixes the table one present at a time: each right sentence turns the tag to the right name. Then the doorbell: eight guests, eight fast answers. Then the presents wheel and Grandma’s phone call the next day. At the end, the student writes thank-you notes. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

Object pronouns: me, you, him, her, it, us, them, after a verb or after to / for / with. A thing is it or them, never him. And English never drops the thing: Yes, I like it, not Yes, I like. The errors are in the wrong options: to she, give to her, for your, I love him for a hat.

The same target as Call Me, in another world: here the stress is on the thing (it) and on to / for / with.

THE BEST PRESENT (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the party, the student puts three presents for their best friend in order. Say your order first (the button shows it), then let them tap 1, 2, 3; wait five seconds in silence and let them explain number one with a pronoun: I give it to her. The PUSH line (only you see it) is a quick yes or no. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one light personal question. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and Presents, II The Wrong Tags, IV Ding Dong!, V Presents for Everyone, VI Thank You, Dear, VII The Best Present (a ranking: your order first).
  • Focus: III The Rule.
  • Write: VIII Thank-You Notes, with the red wavy line for to she, for we, I love., give to her and for your. It goes to their My notes.

YOU ARE THE VOICES

No recording in this lesson. At the door, the student taps the bell; you read the guest’s line in their voice and wait for a fast answer. Grandma calls the next day: read her question, wait for the answer, then tap “Answered · Grandma replies”.
