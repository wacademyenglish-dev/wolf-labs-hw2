Thirty-Three Point Nine · The Tracker

Something that started in the past and is still going takes have / has been + -ing: I’ve been meaning to call her for a week. Since + a point (since March), for + a piece of time (for five months). Students say I’m doing it since March and I have been work. The story is a 1994 study: students guessed 33.9 days for their own paper; it took 55.5. On screen, a habit tracker: sixty squares, one per day, with the estimate marked in ice blue and the reality in ember.

NOT A DIAGNOSIS

The hour measures a prediction error in a group of students, not your student. Keep the questions about the recordings. Scene VIII is a finding about other people, not advice.

THE STREAK

Scene II: each right sentence lights a square; a wrong tap resets the streak to zero. That’s part of the fun. After a wrong tap, the student can still tap the right one and say it.

THE RECORDINGS

Three recordings with pause and 0.75×; the questions and the transcript open after listening: the 1994 study (IV), a man describing the relief of putting something off (VI), and the one practical finding, when and where (VIII).

BOTH FORMS WORK

With live, work and study, I’ve lived here since 2019 and I’ve been living here since 2019 are both correct (scene IX, line 6).

THE LAST SCENE

The student’s five numbers from scene I come back next to 33.9 and 55.5.

THE DEADLINE (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene IX is now a negotiation. Your student is a web designer who promised thirty days and needs twenty more; you are the client. Your secret limit (fifteen days) shows only on your screen. Ask the four questions in the role and let them answer with have / has been + -ing and for / since; wait five seconds in silence before you accept anything, and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Then close the deal on the screen. Recast mistakes quietly (“I’m doing it since March” → “I’ve been doing it since March”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
