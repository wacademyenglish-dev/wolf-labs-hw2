The Noun Does the Work · The Verb Map

In Portuguese, one verb covers almost everything: photo, mistake, break. English splits the job across four verbs, and the noun chooses: make a decision, do the dishes, take a photo, have lunch. A few nouns live on the border: take / have a look, a break, a shower, a rest. Students say make a photo and do a mistake. The meaning of the verb helps a little; memory of the pair helps much more. On screen, a map with four territories (MAKE, DO, TAKE, HAVE) and a striped border strip shared by TAKE and HAVE; every noun drops a pin in its territory.

THE NOUN CHOOSES

Don’t ask “what does DO mean?”. Ask “what does this noun go with?”. In scene II the student says the whole phrase before tapping a territory.

THE RECORDINGS

Scene V: four recordings, one voice, each with pause and 0.75×; the verb is fast and quiet. Lines 1 and 2: the same sentence, and the noun changed the verb. Lines 3 and 4: the same request with TAKE and HAVE, both right.

A NOTE

make notes exists too (writing your own notes), but take notes is the usual one in a talk. take a decision is heard in British English; this lesson trains make a decision.

THE DAY

Scene VII: push for eight collocations in one story. The counter adds them up as you go; count them out loud together.

A GOOD DAY (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the Focus scenes, your student puts five pieces of a good day in order (take a real break, make a big decision, do some exercise…) and explains the top one and the last one, saying the verb with each noun. There is no right order: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Then show your own order and agree on one top three together. Recast verb slips quietly (make a break → take a break); at the end, ask them to fix one or two themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
