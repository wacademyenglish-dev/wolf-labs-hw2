In About Ten Minutes · The Office · The Time Card

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Nine in the morning to five in the afternoon… or is it?

The grammar: At, On, In: At five. On Friday. In March. In ten minutes.

The class (about 57 min): I Warm-up and Jim’s Phone · II The Words · III The Clip · IV Line by Line · V Green, Yellow, Red · VI At, On, In · VII The Time Card · VIII Your Week · IX The Interview. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the prank now. What time does Michael think it is when he wakes up?”

The Office (NBC, 2005–2013) is the American comedy filmed like a documentary about a small paper company, Dunder Mifflin, in Scranton, Pennsylvania. In this cold open (season 7), Michael, the boss, eats a whole chicken pot pie for lunch and falls asleep at his desk. Nobody wants him awake, so Jim changes the clock on his computer and Dwight changes his watch: Michael wakes up, thinks it is five o’clock, and sends everybody home. The grammar: at, on, in for time: at 9 a.m. / at five / at night, on Friday / on December 24th, in March / in 2010 / in the afternoon / in ten minutes (from now). Portuguese says all of them with “em / às / de”.

A note for the teacher: the lesson plays the cold open from Peacock’s official compilation (13:32 to 15:22); the show’s own channel does not play in embeds. The times follow its captions (24/09), and each moment is told in our own words. This is the second lesson with this show (lesson 269 is the memo wall); this one is the time card and the clock.

Warm-up and Jim’s Phone (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; A2: short answers are fine. 2 · Jim’s Phone: the screen becomes Jim’s phone on the afternoon of the clock prank. Messages arrive one at a time, full of times and days (at two, on Friday, in ten minutes); the student reads each one out loud and says what is happening. At the end they answer the last message.

The words (II): famous, recipe, coffee, slow and shelter, before the video, in hear it in the video and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Interview): the teacher is Erin, the receptionist; the student asks about times and days with at, on and in. Then swap: the student talks about their own week. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is the empty office at dusk. I: Warm-up and Jim’s Phone. III: the clip in three steps. Before: a slider (is it normal to sleep at your desk in the afternoon?), and the student says why. Watch: True or lie? and Which word did you hear?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: sort six times into at, on or in (they tap a card, then a box), then one tap (is the prank funny or mean?) and the student says why. IV: five frames on the film strip. V: Green, Yellow, Red, three office habits to judge. VI, the office board: five sentences with a wrong at / on / in. The student says the five fixes out loud, taps Check, and the rule appears. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Time Card: the office wall clock (no numbers, only marks) next to a Dunder Mifflin time card with six lines (the morning meeting, Dwight’s birthday, the paper sale, the Christmas party, the empty office, five o’clock). The student reads the clue, says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the sentence is written, the line is punched, the time is stamped in purple ink and the clock hands move, until five. VIII, Your Week: three people ask about time (a coworker, a friend, the boss). The student picks how to answer and the talk appears: read it out loud, you are the person. IX: the interview.

THE COMMON ERRORS

in 9 a.m. / on 9 a.m., in Friday / at Friday, on March / at March, on the afternoon / at the afternoon, in December 24th, in the night / on night (at night), after ten minutes meaning “from now” (in ten minutes), on 1990.

GREEN, YELLOW, RED (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student judges three office habits with a traffic light: the clock prank from the clip, a two-hour lunch, and the boss’s messages on Saturday night. Green is fine, yellow is careful, red is never. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own job or school. The starters use at / on / in: recast mistakes quietly (in Friday, on the afternoon), and at the end ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Jim’s Phone, V Green, Yellow, Red, VII The Time Card, VIII Your Week and IX The Interview.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Clip and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI At, On, In.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
