Backrooms · Level 0 · The Map

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A photo on the internet that became a film.

The grammar: Someone Has Been Here: I have been here every night since I found it.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and In the Dark · III The Trailer · V The Door · VI Someone Has Been Here · VII The Map · VIII Since and For · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Clark now. In the trailer, how long has he been there? Guess.”

Backrooms (2026), an A24 horror film by Kane Parsons, from his own YouTube series. Behind a small door in a furniture store there is a place that shouldn’t exist: empty rooms of old yellow wallpaper and damp carpet, forever. Clark found it; he has been there every night since, filming, and someone has been there before him. The grammar: the present perfect: have / has + past participle for things that started in the past and go on until now (someone has been here, I have been here every night since I found it), since (a point) × for (a period), never / just / ever between have and the participle, and How long have you…?

A note for the teacher: horror, but no blood (empty corridors, a voice on a loudspeaker, one short scream near the end). The times come from the trailer’s captions (07/10), told in our own words. The yellow on screen is kept dim on purpose.

Warm-up and In the Dark (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · In the Dark: the screen is covered and only the sound of the first 14 seconds of the trailer plays (a man: “I found something. In the store… The place.”). The student says who is talking, where he is and how he sounds; then take off the cover. Horror without scares: nothing is on the screen.

The words (II): store, believe, proof, slow and scared, before the video, in match the columns and mixed letters. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is Clark, back from the Backrooms; the teacher asks five questions (how long, what you have seen, have you ever met anyone, have you told your family, why you have gone back). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a dark corridor of yellow wallpaper with the lights off. I: Warm-up and In the Dark. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a 30-second clock: the student talks about empty buildings and scary places. Watch: One word is wrong (say the right one) and What does he say?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: four polaroids of the trailer to put in order, then a slider (would you go through that door?) and the student says why. IV: line by line. V, The Door: the arena (open the door to the world, or close it forever?), one minute each side, then swap. VI, Someone Has Been Here: the student sorts six times (Monday, three hours, 2019, a long time, I found it, two weeks) into two boxes, since and for. When all six are right, the rule appears and stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Map: the floor plan of Level 0 on a dark grid, almost empty. For each of six rooms the student reads the clue, says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the sentence is written and the room is drawn in dim yellow, with footprints and the dashed path from room to room. At the end, a door. VIII, Since and For: a die shows three things from the student’s life (your home, your job, English, your phone, an old friend, a place you have never been to); the student says a sentence for each, then a 45-second clock for a minute of talk. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

I have find, I have never saw, I am here since… (Portuguese “estou aqui desde”), since three hours, for 2019, someone have, was been, I never have seen, How long are you here?, How long you have been…?

THE DOOR (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student has seen Clark and the door in the store. The arena: open the door to the world, or close it forever? The student picks a side and defends it for one minute with the present perfect (nobody has found the end, millions have watched his videos, someone has been there before him); then tap Swap sides and they defend the other side. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (have find, I am here since, has went); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and In the Dark, V The Door, VII The Map, VIII Since and For and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Someone Has Been Here.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
