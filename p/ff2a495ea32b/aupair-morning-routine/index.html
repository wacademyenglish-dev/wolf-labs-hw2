Morning Routine · Au Pair · Lesson 17 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: daily routines with children and the language of instructions: kind and clear imperatives (Shoes on, please. Walking feet inside. Let’s… Can you…, please? First…, then…), time words and sequencers.

The story: Gabi, a real au pair from Czechia living in Virginia (Au Pair in America vlog), shows a working day with three girls. Then a school morning in order and a conversation about routines.

About 38 min: I · A Day With Gabi (10) · II · Kind and Clear (9) · III · The Morning in Order (7) · IV · Your Routine (8) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What was your morning like when you were seven?”

Children respond better to positive instructions (say what TO do), choices and routines. This is also cultural: many American families prefer “Walking feet, please” to “Stop running!”.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Let’s… · … on, please! · Can you…, please? · First…, then… · Walking feet inside · At 7:00, … Then… After that… Finally…

A DAY WITH GABI

Au Pair in America, “Life with APiA | Gabi’s 2nd Vlog” (7 min, captions checked on 15/10/2026). Some parts are only music: you can skip them; the speaking parts are at 0:33, 1:43, 2:44, 4:32 and 5:39.

KIND AND CLEAR

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THE MORNING IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

YOUR ROUTINE

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write the morning routine of the real family once she arrives, with the times.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Go to brush (Go brush / Go and brush), wake up the kids (wake the kids up), Stop! (without what to do), he don’t want. A2+: one correction per answer is enough.
