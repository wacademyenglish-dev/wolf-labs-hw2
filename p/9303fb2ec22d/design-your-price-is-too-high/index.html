Your Price Is Too High · English for Designers · Lesson 3 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: talking about price: asking about the problem and the result, explaining what’s included, changing the scope instead of the price (With a budget of…, I could…), refusing a discount politely, and never apologizing for your rate.

The story: A real role play from The Futur, then four fictional clients on the phone (recorded in American, British and Australian accents).

About 44 min: I · What Is It Worth? (8) · II · The Price Jury (7) · III · Confident or Sorry? (9) · IV · Four Clients on the Phone (16) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Has a client ever said your price was too high? What did you say?”

Many Brazilian freelancers apologize for their price or lower it immediately. Model a calm, short answer. The numbers in the lesson are examples in dollars; adapt them to the student’s market.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

What problem does this solve? · Here’s what’s included: · With a budget of…, I could… · That’s a fair question. · I keep my rates the same for all clients, but… · Would that work for you?

WHAT IS IT WORTH?

The Futur, a role play about a client with $1,000 (0:00–1:50): questions about the problem and the value.

CONFIDENT OR SORRY?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

FOUR CLIENTS ON THE PHONE

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write their real rate for one kind of project in English, with what’s included.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Sorry, I know it’s expensive, I can do cheaper (I can do it for less: avoid it), The value is…? (The price is… / My rate is…), Is included… (It includes… / What’s included is…). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE PRICE JURY (II) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the video, your student is the jury for three fictional designers who heard “It’s too expensive”: Bia drops her price at once, Leo offers a smaller scope and loses the client, Marina keeps full price for a friend and adds something extra. Read the evidence together, ask the question, and wait five seconds in silence before you help. There is no right verdict: use the PUSH line to make the choice harder, and ask what the designer should say, with the lesson’s language (Here’s what’s included, What problem does this solve?, If …, I could …). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
