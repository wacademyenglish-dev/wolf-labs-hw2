Sorry, Could You Repeat That? · Selling Services · Lesson 14

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 14 (phone phrases · spelling names and e-mails). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: A page from the Bluepeak sales training with the phrases that save a phone call. Then Greg Novak, finance manager of Westfield Plastics (fictional), calls Leo on a bad line, fast, and dictates his e-mail. The student fixes a colleague’s call script and writes his own phone card with his real e-mail in words.

About 56 min: I · Your Week (5) · II · The Phone Tips (7) · III · A Bad Line (9) · IV · Polite, Not “What?” (10) · V · Fix the Call Script (8) · VI · You Choose (5) · VII · Your Phone Card (9) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “How many calls did you have this week? How do you spell your first name?”

This is a survival lesson: the student’s real fear is the phone. Brazilian traps: answering “What?” (rude in English), a dry order (“Repeat, please.”), “more slow” instead of “more slowly”, and “arroba / point” in e-mails. Drill the alphabet with his real name and e-mail (A, E, I, G, J, R and Y are the hard letters for Brazilians). In the final card, YOU write what he spells: if your e-mail is wrong, he spells it again.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Sorry, could you repeat that? · Could you speak more slowly, please? · Could you spell that, please? · Let me check: … · at · dot · underscore · dash

YOUR WEEK

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE PHONE TIPS

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

A BAD LINE

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

POLITE, NOT “WHAT?”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

FIX THE CALL SCRIPT

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR PHONE CARD

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to keep the phone card next to the computer before every call in English.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Bad line · call back: Linha ruim (chiado, corte) · ligar de volta. “Can I call you back?” é a saída educada.
  • Spell (spelling): Soletrar. Nomes e e-mails sempre se confirmam soletrando: “Let me check: M-A-R-K?”.
  • At · dot · underscore · dash: Como se fala e-mail em inglês: @ = at, . = dot, _ = underscore, - = dash (ou hyphen). Nunca “arroba/ponto”.
  • Hold (put on hold): Deixar a ligação em espera. “Could you hold, please?” = pode aguardar na linha?
  • Mute / unmute: Microfone desligado / ligado em chamadas de vídeo. “Sorry, I was on mute.”

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

What? (Sorry, could you repeat that?), Can you speak more slow? (Could you speak more slowly, please?), Repeat, please. (Could you repeat that, please?), leo arroba bluepeak point com (leo at bluepeak dot com). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
