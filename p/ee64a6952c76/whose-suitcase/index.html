Whose Suitcase? · Gate 7 Lost and Found

An easy reader in five short chapters. It’s 11 PM at the airport; the student works at the lost and found of Gate 7. Five suitcases go around on the carousel, and five people wrote a claim form. Each suitcase opens with clues: the student reads the chapter out loud, answers one question and finds the owner. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

Whose …? The owner first with ’s; with s at the end, only ’ (the twins’); two names, one thing: one ’s (Mia and Sam’s). mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs follow the owner, never the thing; alone, the long word (It’s ours). Many things: Whose are these? They’re theirs.

THE TRAP

Many learners pick his / her from the thing or its color (the pink suitcase → hers), and say It’s our, It’s us, the twins’s, Mia’s and Sam’s, Whose is these?, They’re theirs sneakers, It’s him.

A REVIEW LESSON

Works as a follow-up to a lesson on whose and possessive pronouns: it checks the basics and spends the hour on plurals, two owners, ours and theirs.

WHOSE IS IT? (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the lost and found, three small things from real life: a pen on a desk, a brother’s T-shirt, a toothbrush. For each one, whose is it? Answer first, then let them choose; wait five seconds in silence and let them say the whole sentence with mine / yours / his / hers. The PUSH line (only you see it) is a quick yes or no. Recast quietly while they talk.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one light personal question. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and Your Bags, II The Carousel (reading out loud; before the rule), IV The Claim Desk (you play each person), V Mine or Ours?, VI The Night Manager Calls (a retell from memory; the big question closes it), VII OK or Not OK? (green or red, then the sentence).
  • Focus: III The Rule.
  • Write: VIII, four things in their bag and whose they are, with the red wavy line for It’s our / their, It’s him / us, -s’s, Mia’s and Sam’s, Whose is these, theirs sneakers, the suitcase of the twins. It goes to their My notes.

No audio or video in this lesson: your voice is every passenger’s.
