Fill in the Blanks · DET Prep · Lesson 3 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the Fill in the Blanks question: one sentence, one word with only its first letters, about 20 seconds. The student learns to read the grammar around the gap BEFORE typing: could + base verb, was + -ed, a lot of + noun, how? = adverb.

The story: No story: the student is the candidate. Notice (four pairs) → name the kind of word (six items) → eight timed sentences.

About 39 min: I · The Clue Around the Gap (10) · II · What Kind of Word? (6) · III · The Study Group Votes (7) · IV · Eight Sentences (12) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “The museum is clo___ on Mondays. Close or closed?” Let the student explain why.

Spelling counts: one wrong letter makes the gap wrong. Ask the student to read the whole sentence first, even with the clock running: it is faster than guessing twice.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

could / can / to + base verb · was / were / is + -ed · a / the / a lot of + noun · how? → adverb -ly · what kind? → adjective · look forward to + -ing

THE CLUE AROUND THE GAP

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

WHAT KIND OF WORD?

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

EIGHT SENTENCES

The gaps: the student types only the missing letters (the box moves to the next gap by itself). With a clock, the text stays blurred until you start it. Check paints right and wrong and shows the full word. Spelling counts.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. If a gap goes wrong, ask what kind of word it was. The answer is usually there.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

could you reminded, the museum is close, a lot of experienced, spoke very clear, looking forward to see. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE STUDY GROUP VOTES (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the eight sentences with the clock, a council vote. The student is in a study group with four weeks to the test, and votes on four rules: Yes, No, or Propose a change. Wait five seconds in silence, then vote the other way and argue a little. Use the PUSH line when the vote is too easy. At the end, the student proposes a rule of their own in 45 seconds and you vote. Recast quietly while they talk, especially the clues of the lesson (could + base verb, a lot of + noun, looking forward to + -ing); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · The Clue Around the Gap and II · What Kind of Word? and V · I Can.
  • Out loud: III · The Study Group Votes.
  • Write: IV · Eight Sentences.
