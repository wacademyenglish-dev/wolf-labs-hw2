Medium · The Size Ruler

Four boxes arrived this week, and all four shirts say MEDIUM, but they are four different sizes. The hour is a ruler: two things side by side, and the bar shows which is more. By the end, the student sees that medium is not a size at all: it is a comparison somebody else made. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

Short words take -er (bigger, cheaper, happier; two claps ending in -y also take -er). Long words take more (more expensive, more comfortable; modern has two claps and no -y: more). Then than, never then or that. Three break the rule: better, worse, farther. The common mistakes: more big, more cheaper (both at once), bigger then, more better.

THAN IS WEAK

In speech, than is /ðən/ and sounds like then. That is why people write the wrong one. Scene IV is about the spelling.

OK OR NOT OK? (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Three shopping choices from your student’s life, green OK or red Not OK: a cheaper store one hour away, a medium that is a little bigger, an online shirt that is cheaper but has no fitting room. There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) for a yes/no follow-up. The starters keep -er / more … / better + than in their mouth. Recast mistakes quietly (more cheap → cheaper); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Same Size Everywhere?, V The Rack, VII OK or Not OK? (three shopping choices), VIII At the Store, IX The Size Chart, X So What Is Medium?
  • Focus: II Short Word, Long Word (clap, then drawer), III The Ruler (form, then than, then the opposite sentence), IV Than, Not Then, VI Good, Bad, Far.
  • Write: XI Your Closet, with the red wavy line for more big, more cheaper, bigger then/that, expensiver, gooder, more better. It goes to their My notes.

AT THE STORE

Play the clerk. The student reads the problem and asks; then tap to see one way to say it. Any good request counts. Strong students: be a difficult clerk and say you don’t have it.

SCENE I

Don’t correct. Tap the forms you hear; mistakes have their own buttons. Scene XI brings them back.
