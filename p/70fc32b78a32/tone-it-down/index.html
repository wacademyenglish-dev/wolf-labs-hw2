Tone It Down · C1 Unit 9 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on decoding and using understatement (not exactly cheap, a bit of a problem, could be better), euphemism (let go, between jobs, passed away, economical with the truth) and irony (Oh, great. Brilliant. That went well.), plus “not bad” as a compliment.

The story: A very polite British critic (invented) reviews La Perla, a new expensive restaurant. On the surface the review is gentle; underneath, it is a disaster.

About 48 min: I · Honest or Polite? (6) · II · The Review (10) · III · What Does It Really Mean? (9) · IV · The Polite Complaint (8) · V · Tone It Down (7) · VI · The Hot Seat (8).

Open with: “If a friend’s restaurant is bad, would you tell them directly?”

Brazilian students often take English irony literally and read “not bad” as negative. The review is full of polite understatement: read it with a calm, dry voice. In the hot seat, answer with understatement and irony, and let the student decode the real meaning. Remind the student: tone and context decide.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

understatement: not exactly…, a bit of…, could be better · euphemism: let go, between jobs, passed away · economical with the truth · irony: Oh, great. / That went well. · not bad (at all) = a compliment · softening criticism: I’m not sure that’s wise

HONEST OR POLITE?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

THE REVIEW

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

WHAT DOES IT REALLY MEAN?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

TONE IT DOWN

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THE HOT SEAT

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

reading “Oh, great” literally, “not bad” = weak, your work was terrible (too direct), he died (at a formal event: passed away), a bit of problem (missing a), economic with the truth (economical). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE POLITE COMPLAINT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, your student was at La Perla last night and comes back to talk to you, the manager. They want something back for the dry steak and the warm wine, but they must stay polite: understatement and irony, never a raised voice. Your secret limit (only you see it): up to 50% off or a free dinner, never a full refund. Ask the four questions, wait five seconds in silence, use the PUSH line, then close the deal on the screen. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
