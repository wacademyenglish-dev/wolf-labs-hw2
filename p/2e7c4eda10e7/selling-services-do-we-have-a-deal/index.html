Do We Have a Deal? · Selling Services · Lesson 15

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 15 (review 6–14 · simple negotiation). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: The last meeting with Karen Wells, CFO of Lumen Logistics (fictional): she likes the proposal, but the price is a problem. Leo offers, says no politely, uses a past result, and closes. Then the student negotiates with you (you are the CFO), chooses in the arena, fixes a closing e-mail and writes his own.

About 54 min: I · Your Week (5) · II · The Last Meeting (9) · III · Your Negotiation (12) · IV · The Arena (7) · V · Fix the Closing E-mail (8) · VI · Your Closing E-mail (10) · VII · I Can (3).

Open with: “How was your week? Did you close a deal? Are you going to meet a client this week?”

Review lesson of units 6–14 inside one negotiation. Only three new chunks: What about…? / We can offer… / Do we have a deal? In III you are the CFO: read the answer on your screen, be a little difficult, and in task 6 really speak fast so he uses “Could you speak more slowly?”. In IV there is no wrong choice: the reason is the exercise. In V, every mistake comes from an earlier lesson: ask “which lesson?” after each fix.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

What about …? · We can offer … · Sorry, we can’t. But … · We’re going to start on … · Last year we saved … · Do we have a deal? · It’s a deal.

YOUR WEEK

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE LAST MEETING

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

YOUR NEGOTIATION

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

THE ARENA

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

FIX THE CLOSING E-MAIL

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

YOUR CLOSING E-MAIL

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. End of the first block: ask the student which lesson was most useful in his real work, and write it down for the next block.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Deal · close a deal: Negócio · fechar o negócio. “Do we have a deal?” = fechamos? “It’s a deal.” = fechado.
  • Discount: Desconto no preço. Muitas vezes o vendedor troca desconto por outra concessão (prazo, mês grátis, treinamento).
  • Free first month (concession): Primeiro mês grátis: uma concessão na negociação. Custa menos ao fornecedor que um desconto o ano inteiro.
  • Contract term (one-year / two-year contract): Prazo do contrato. Contrato mais longo costuma vir com preço mensal menor.
  • Supplier · vendor: Fornecedor. “Last year, a supplier was late” = o fornecedor anterior atrasou (objeção comum).
  • Sign the contract: Assinar o contrato: o passo depois do “It’s a deal”. No e-mail de fechamento, pede-se um prazo (“by Friday”).

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

We go to start on March 1 (We’re going to start), It’s more cheap (It’s cheaper), Last year we save 20% (we saved), Can you to sign…? (Can you sign…?). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
