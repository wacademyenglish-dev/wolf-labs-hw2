You’re Supposed to Bring a Gift · The Invitation

The student’s friend Emma is marrying Kenji in Kyoto. The student bought the perfect gift, a big coffee machine. Then the invitation arrives, black and gold, with a guest guide inside: guests are supposed to bring money in a special envelope, with new bills. The grammar: be supposed to (the rule, the plan, what people expect), be expected to (the same, more formal), not supposed to (the etiquette says no) and was / were supposed to (the plan, and it didn’t happen). On screen, the invitation with eight wax seals that crack open, and a seating chart of eight round tables that light up in gold.

THE INVITATION

Scene II, before the rule: the student opens a seal, reads the line and says what guests have to do. Tap exactly what you heard. Collect what they say: many will say We supposed to … or Is to bring …

THE TRAPS

Is to bring a gift? (no person, no be) · We supposed to (no be) · supposed bring (no to) · suppose to (no d) · supposedly (= people say, not the rule) · don’t have to for something the etiquette forbids · supposed to being.

THE SEATING CHART

Scene IV, after the rule, is the pressure: eight tables, eight guests with a question. You play each guest in character; the student answers fast, starting with the words under the line. Listen for were supposed to (Yuki, your plus-one) and not supposed to (Jess, Amy).

THE CUSTOMS

At a Japanese wedding reception, guests usually give money in a special envelope, sit where the seating chart says, keep speeches short and avoid sad words. Customs change from family to family: if the student knows Japan, let them add to it. This lesson has no recording: your voice is the audio.

THE ETIQUETTE JURY (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Leo’s call, your student is the jury for three guests who broke the guest guide: Mr. Sato’s twenty-minute speech, cousin Amy’s white dress, and their own coffee machine. They read the evidence, choose a verdict and say why; you defend the guest. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (“she not supposed to” → “she wasn’t supposed to”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
