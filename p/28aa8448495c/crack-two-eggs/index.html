Crack Two Eggs · The Silent Kitchen

A famous recipe video (Tasty, “Perfect Fluffy Pancake”) has no voice: only hands, a bowl and a pan. The student is the voice of the chef. First the eight recipe cards (every right order goes on the recipe card), then the rule, then the video in six chapters: after each one, the student gives the orders like a TV chef. Then the kitchen dangers (every right warning puts up a sign), the house rules wheel, and Sam, who can’t cook. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

An order is the verb alone: Crack two eggs. Mix. No you, no -ing, no to, no -s. NO is Don’t + verb: Don’t touch the pan! (not No touch, Not touch, Don’t to touch). Steps: first, then, next, finally. For a person, not a recipe: Please pass the salt.

YOUR KITCHEN, YOUR CALL (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

The pancakes from the video are ready: the student chooses what goes on top, then what to tell a friend whose pancakes are black. Answer first, then let them choose; wait five seconds in silence and let them say the whole order (the verb alone, Don’t …). The PUSH line (only you see it) is a quick yes or no or one word. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two orders themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one light personal question. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Kitchen, II The Recipe Cards, V Kitchen Dangers, VI House Rules, VII Sam Can’t Cook, VIII Your Kitchen, Your Call (the student chooses, then says the order).
  • Listen: IV The Silent Video. It has no voice: the student watches and gives the voice. The video opens chapter by chapter and stops by itself.
  • Focus: III The Rule.
  • Write: IX Your Recipe, with the red wavy line for you crack, mixing, to pour, flips, no touch and don’t to. It goes to their My notes.

THE VIDEO

Pick a chapter; it plays and stops by itself. Ingredient names appear in English on the video: use them. When the student has watched, tap “Watched · show the options”; they say the orders first, then tap what they said. The wrong options are the real mistakes (you, -ing, to, -s, no, don’t to).

SAM

You are Sam, a little panicked. Read his line, wait for the orders and a question back, then tap “Answered · Sam replies”.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

Everything the student taps moves on both screens: the recipe card, the signs, the wheel. G opens this guide.
