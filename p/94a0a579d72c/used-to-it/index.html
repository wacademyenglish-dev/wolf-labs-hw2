Used to It · The Moving Box

Same three words, two grammars. I used to wake up early is a habit that ended. I’m used to waking up early is normal for you today. The difference is BE, and that TO is a preposition, so -ING follows. Between them is the road: I’m getting used to it. On screen, a cardboard moving box where old habits drop in (the flaps close when it’s full), and a twelve-month strip that goes from the orange of home to the ice of Toronto, lighting up as strange becomes normal.

ONE QUESTION

Is it still happening? If not: used to. If it’s normal now: be used to. If it still costs something: getting used to.

DON’T CORRECT SCENE I

Write the three answers down. Listen for I’m used to wake up and I used to living. They come back in scene X.

ANA’S YEAR

Scene IV tells one year in six steps. Habits from back home drop into the box; the rest lights up the months. Step 5 is the trap: used to have. Ask what Ana still finds hard.

THE SWALLOWED BE

Scene VI has no recording: your voice is the audio. Tap the sentence you will read (only you see it), read it at natural speed, and the student says which one they heard. Then you both say both.

YOU MOVED

Scene VII: you play somebody who moved abroad three years ago. Invent freely; answer with all three forms. At the end, ask the student to tell your story back in three sentences.

HOT TAKES (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the interview, your student reads four hot takes about change and taps how much they agree. There is no right answer. Let them choose a number, wait five seconds in silence, then they defend it while you take the opposite number. Use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it personal. Recast quietly while they talk (I’m used to waking, not “wake”; I’m getting used to it); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
