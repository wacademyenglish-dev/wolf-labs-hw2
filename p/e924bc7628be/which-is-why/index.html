Which Is Why It Matters · Election Night

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: The US midterms: two years into a president’s term, all 435 House seats and about a third of the Senate are up for grabs, and only about four in ten people vote.

The grammar: The Rule: Sentence, comma, WHICH, comment.

Short class (about 57 min): I You and Voting · III The Explainer · V The Numbers · VI The Anchor’s Notes · VII The Rule · VIII Unpopular Opinions · XI Election Night · XII Your Country. Skip if you’re short on time: IV Line by Line, IX The Ballot Box and X Your Opinion (marked EXTRA on your screen). On the practice pages the student speaks first and taps Check on their own screen. Full lesson: about 71 min.

Open with: “Do you vote in every election in your country? Why, or why not?”

The US midterms: two years into a president’s term, all 435 House seats and about a third of the Senate are up for grabs, and only about four in ten people vote. Students can join two sentences, but they glue them with a comma (It was close, this means a recount) or say , what is why, , that means, which it means. This lesson is only about , which for a WHOLE idea: , which is why (a result), , which means (a consequence), , which surprised me (a reaction). On screen, an election night studio: an amber bracket that WHICH draws under the whole sentence, a hemicycle of seats, a cracked plate for the comma glue and a news ticker.

NEUTRAL

No party is good or bad here, and there are no party colors. The student’s own country is always the second example.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: a news explainer (about two minutes), sealed, in four chapters. Before: two guesses (no right answer yet; the chapters tell). Watch: every chapter has a short task next to the video: read it, play the chapter (it stops by itself), tap the answer (two per chapter: tap the answer, or true or false + say the truth). After: the whole video, two lines to notice (does which / this point back at one word or at the whole idea?) and four lines with one wrong word (the student says the right one, then Check). Every line has a ▶ time button: it plays the video from that moment.

The Numbers (V): four numbers (435, 33, 40%, 300). The clue gives the fact and the start of the comment (· which means …). The student says the whole sentence, then taps Check: the model sentence appears and the hemicycle (or the ballots, or the bars) fills in. Under it, one question for a short chat.

The Anchor’s Notes (VI): the anchor’s notes for the live show, on a dark screen. The student reads them out loud and taps every “, which …” comment (four; every word can be tapped, and who is in Ohio is a trap: it is about one person). Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line (the whole sentence or one word, a comma, which not what, which means, no extra it, which is why = the result). The rule then stays in the corner of VII, VIII and IX.

The Rule (VII): the amber bracket on top, then six lines from the newsroom, one wrong word in each (what, that, it means, this, mean, no comma). The student says all six the right way, then taps Check.

The Ballot Box (IX): six stories and six comments. The student taps a story, then its comment (a choice of meaning, not of form). Every right pair drops in the box and runs on the news ticker; the student says the whole sentence out loud, fast. At the end, read a story from today’s news and the student comments at once.

A NOTE

Another live B2 lesson teaches defining and non-defining clauses (my sister, who lives in Ohio). This one is the reinforcement for which after a whole sentence.

The words (II): elections, turnout, lawmakers, candidates and performance, before the video, in mystery meaning and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

UNPOPULAR OPINIONS (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the rule, four opinions about the news and election night (not about parties or politicians; X · Your Opinion is the one about voting). The student taps a number from 1 to 5 and defends it, commenting on their own reason with “…, which …”; you take the opposite number. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and Voting, V The Numbers, VIII Unpopular Opinions, IX The Ballot Box, X Your Opinion and XI Election Night (X and XI are no longer extra).
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Explainer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Anchor’s Notes and VII The Rule.
  • Write: XII Your Country.
