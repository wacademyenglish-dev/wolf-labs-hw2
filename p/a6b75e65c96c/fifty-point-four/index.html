Fifty Point Four · The Phone and the Drawer

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Present perfect for experience with no finished time: Have you ever deleted an app?

The grammar: The Phone and the Drawer: No finished time? It stays on the phone. A finished time? It goes to the drawer.

Short class (about 45 min): I Today, on Your Phone · II The Phone and the Drawer · III Fifty Point Four · IV How It Learns · V Have You Ever…? · VI Who Did the Filtering? · X Your Feed, Again. Skip if you’re short on time: VII, VIII Have You Ever Asked? and IX I Have Seen It Yesterday (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “How much of what they saw came from sources that already agreed with them?”

Present perfect for experience with no finished time: Have you ever deleted an app? Past simple as soon as a finished time appears: When did you delete it? I deleted it last year. Many students say I have seen it yesterday. The theme is the feed: a big 2023 study found that 50.4% of what people saw came from sources that already agreed with them. On screen: a phone and an archive drawer. No finished time keeps the post on the phone; a finished time drops it into the drawer.

THE STUDIES ARE CAREFUL

Three months changed nothing measurable. That is not “the feed is harmless”. Don’t let your student say more than the study did; the author’s voice in scene III says exactly this.

THE AUDIO

Two recordings: an engineer on how the ranking learns (IV) and the author of the study (III). The transcript opens after each one is played.

THE PAGES

  • II The Phone and the Drawer: six posts. The student taps a post, then the phone (no finished time) or the drawer (a finished time); the phone and the drawer fill up. Then they read each one out loud.
  • V Have You Ever…?: six pairs of question and answer. The student builds the follow-up out loud and asks you, then taps Check. The last one answers NO: no finished moment.
  • VIII Have You Ever Asked?: a wheel picks the question. The student asks you, you answer honestly, and they follow up in the past simple (they tap the counter each time). Then swap.
  • IX I Have Seen It Yesterday: six sentences, Right or Wrong. If it is wrong, the student says the whole sentence right.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I have seen it yesterday, When have you started…?, I saw never, Have you ever went…?

UNPOPULAR OPINIONS (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, your student gives a number from 1 to 5 to four opinions about their feed and defends it with one thing they’ve done and when they did it. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, pick the opposite number yourself, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (“I have deleted it last year” → “I deleted it last year”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Today, on Your Phone, VII Unpopular Opinions, VIII Have You Ever Asked?
  • Listen: III Fifty Point Four, IV How It Learns.
  • Focus: II The Phone and the Drawer, V Have You Ever…?, VI Who Did the Filtering?, IX I Have Seen It Yesterday.
  • Write: X, their own feed. It goes to their My notes.
