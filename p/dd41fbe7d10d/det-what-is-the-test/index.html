What Is the Test? · DET Prep · Lesson 1 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the first lesson of the prep course. The student learns how the test works (at home, about one hour, adaptive), what the score means (10–160 and four subscores) and tries two real tasks with the real clock.

The story: No story today: the student is the candidate. Treat it like the first meeting with a coach: what is the test, why the student is taking it, and a first taste of the pressure of the clock.

About 39 min: I · The Test in One Page (9) · II · True to the Test (6) · III · At Home or at a Center? (7) · IV · First Taste: Real or Fake? (5) · V · First Taste: Read, Then Speak (8) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “What score do you need, and for what?” Write the number down: it is the goal of the course.

The format was checked in October 2026 (in July 2025 the test lost Read Aloud and Listen, Then Speak, and gained Interactive Speaking). Formats change: before the student books, check the official website together. The test name is a trademark: we use it only to say what the course prepares for.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

at home, about one hour · adaptive questions · score 10–160 · Literacy, Comprehension, Conversation, Production · writing and speaking samples · the clock

THE TEST IN ONE PAGE

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

TRUE TO THE TEST

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

FIRST TASTE: REAL OR FAKE?

Read and Select: the words stay blurred until you tap Start the clock. The student taps only the REAL English words. At the end: green = found, dashed amber = missed, red = a fake word tapped (that costs points in the test).

FIRST TASTE: READ, THEN SPEAK

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Write down the score the student needs. Every lesson ends with the same question: are we closer?

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

stopping before 30 seconds, long silence while thinking, tapping every word that looks familiar, apologizing for the English. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

AT HOME OR AT A CENTER? (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the facts, a real argument. The student picks a side (one hour at home, or three hours at a test center) and defends it for one minute with the clock; then taps Swap sides and defends the other one. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, and don’t give your opinion first. Use the PUSH line (the internet drops in the middle of the test) when the answer gets too easy. Recast quietly while they talk (The main reason is…, For example…, That’s why…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
