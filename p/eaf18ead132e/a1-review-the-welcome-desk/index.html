The Welcome Desk · A1 Unit 1 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 1 (Hello, I’m…). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Your student works at the welcome desk of a small hotel tonight. You are the three guests.

About 45 min: I Six Doors (8) · II The Welcome Desk (15) · III The Night Shift (5) · IV Your Card (6) · V Small Talk (8) · VI I Can (4).

Open with: “Pick a door.” There is no order. One minute per door, no notes.

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again before the doors: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Each door is one topic of Unit 1. Tap Got it when the answer is good enough for A1, and Review when it isn’t. The amber doors come back at the end of scene I as a short list “to review”, and they are the first thing to practice in the homework.

THE SIX DOORS

Introductions and spelling (his / her) · giving the turn back (And you?) · be for age and feelings (I’m 30, not I have 30) · I’m from / I live in · he is / she is + a / an + job · days with on and How much is it?

THE WELCOME DESK

You are each guest. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the guest card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier: the questions get shorter. After three guests, the manager calls: the student tells you about one guest with he / she. Listen for the three slips on your screen (he has 34, she is nurse, he is of Canada) and tap the one you heard.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I have 30, she is nurse, he is of Canada, I live at Recife, his name for a woman, and a missing And you?. A1: one correction per answer is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE NIGHT SHIFT (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the three guests, a short break in the middle of the review with two light choices: work at the desk or in the café, and live in Toronto or in Osaka (I’m…, I live in…). Answer first yourself, as a model. Then let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, and ask the PUSH line (only you see it): a yes / no question. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves. About 5 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. There are three; use one when the review has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: II The Welcome Desk, III The Night Shift and V Small Talk.
  • Focus: I Six Doors and VI I Can.
  • Write: IV Your Card.
