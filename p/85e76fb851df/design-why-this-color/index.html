Why This Color? · English for Designers · Lesson 5 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: describing a design precisely: feels / looks + adjective, looks like + noun, too + adjective, adjective + enough, -ish colors, and the vocabulary of color (harmony, neutral, saturated, contrast, accent).

The story: A color theory video, then four pairs of fictional design options (graphic, UX and interiors) to choose between.

About 38 min: I · Where Do I Start? (8) · II · Too Much, Not Enough (12) · III · This One or That One? (14) · IV · I Can (4).

Open with: “What’s your favorite color to design with? And one color you never use?”

In the choice scene (escolha), show real references from the internet if you want, but the lesson works with the descriptions alone. Push for one feeling and one fact in each answer.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

It feels… · It looks a bit too… · It looks like… · … isn’t … enough. · a bluish / warmish… · I’d go with… because…

WHERE DO I START?

The Futur Academy, 5 color theory tips (0:46–3:28): harmonies, neutrals, less is more.

TOO MUCH, NOT ENOUGH

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THIS ONE OR THAT ONE?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to describe the colors of one of their own projects in three sentences.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

It’s very big, we must change (It’s too big), not enough big (not big enough), It looks a bank (It looks like a bank), It feels like warm (It feels warm). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Where Do I Start?.
  • Focus: II · Too Much, Not Enough and IV · I Can.
  • Out loud: III · This One or That One?.
