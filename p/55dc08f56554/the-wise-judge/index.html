The Wise Judge · Solomon’s Court

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Your student is the wise judge in King Solomon’s court.

The grammar: The Scale: After the verb: TO or -ING?

Short class (about 47 min): I The Two Pans · II The Words · III The Court Is Open · IV Solomon’s Paradox · VI The Scale · VII The Last Scroll · X The Royal Decree. Skip if you’re short on time: V, VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “Your friend hates their job. What should they do?” Then: “And you?” Last: “Do you follow your own advice?” Every “No, I don’t” puts a weight on the friend’s side of the scale.

Your student is the wise judge in King Solomon’s court. Five cases arrive on scrolls: a man who hates his job, a woman who keeps forgiving a liar, a man afraid of opening his bakery, a girl who keeps putting off the guitar, a man who can’t stop smoking. The judge gives each verdict in seconds. Then the video explains why that was so easy: Solomon’s Paradox, we reason more wisely about other people’s lives than our own. The last scroll has the student’s own name. They write their own case in the third person, judge it like a friend’s case, and sign a decree for themselves. The goal: the student leaves thinking about their own life.

The grammar: after a verb, -ING or TO. want, need, decide, try + to (He wants to quit); keep, stop, avoid, enjoy, finish + -ing (She keeps putting it off); after a preposition, always -ing (afraid of failing, instead of practicing); and the two meanings of stop: stop smoking (no more) ≠ stop to smoke (in order to smoke).

THE VIDEO

“I FIXED My Whole Life In 1 Night” (Henry Grey Earls, 2:26, we use 0:00–2:14). Sealed until you press play, in four chapters; each stops by itself: the friends’ problems vs. yours, Solomon’s Paradox (fear, history and ego get tangled up), the writing exercise in the third person, and the question “If this person were my friend, what would I tell them to do?”. After the chapters, the last step is ONE screen: the video stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again; the times play only inside the four chapters, and each one stops by itself), with six scrolls under it (CHANGE, DREAM, PARADOX, WISER, TANGLED, STOP); on the right, six True or Lie lines about what the video says. Every right answer seals a scroll (“Scrolls sealed 3 / 6”). On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, the court asks for one last piece of advice: the student taps an answer and says it out loud, with stop + -ing, try + to or afraid of + -ing. Note: the video says “if this person were my friend”. It is not the target today; let the student use it freely.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

In Portuguese, “to stop doing something” is built with a preposition and the infinitive, so word for word it becomes stop to smoke, which in English means stopping in order to smoke: the student says the opposite of what they mean. Portuguese also puts the infinitive straight after verbs like “want”, so I want quit (no to) sounds natural; after a preposition Portuguese uses the infinitive too, so afraid of fail (no -ing); and “continue + -ing” comes out as keeps to say.

THE COURT AND THE LAST SCROLL

Scene III comes before the rule: any good verdict counts; tap the version you heard, listening to stop + -ing and need + to. Scene VII: let the student write alone, in the third person (their own name, never “I”). If they write “I”, point to the five scrolls. Scene VIII: read the scroll aloud and judge it, one question at a time. The last question is the one from the video. Take your time there: it is the heart of the lesson.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I want quit, she needs stop, he stopped to smoke (meaning “no more”), she keeps to say, afraid of fail, avoid to talk, instead of practice.

THE COURT OF OPINIONS (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After your student writes and judges their own scroll, they rate four bold opinions from 1 to 5 and defend the number with TO or -ING. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then take the opposite number and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (stop doing, want to do, afraid of failing); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
