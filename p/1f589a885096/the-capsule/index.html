The Capsule · One Line, Twenty Years

In 2006, two friends, Clara and Nina, buried a time capsule. Twenty years later it is opened. Inside there is a note from Nina, and one line in it made Clara stop answering her friend for twenty years. The language is three verbs that change meaning with the form after them: remember, forget and stop. With -ing the arrow points back (the action happened first); with to it points forward (the action comes after).

THE ARROW

When your student is stuck, ask one question: Which happened first?

THE NOTE IS THE TURN

Scene VI unfolds Nina’s note one fold at a time. Don’t let your student jump to the end. The last line, I remembered to post it, is the whole story and the whole grammar. When the student sees it, stop talking.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I stopped to smoke (meaning they quit), Remember locking the door! as a reminder, and I forgot buying milk (meaning they didn’t buy it).

YOUR BET (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene IV is now a bet, before the note: Clara’s form never arrived and she stopped talking to Nina. Your student bets on what really happened and says why with remember / forget / stop + -ing or to. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Don’t tap Check yet: come back to IV after the receipt (VII) and open it together. The second round brings it to their life: a friend who forgot something important. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your 2016 Box, II The Ground, IV Your Bet, VIII Your Own Box, IX A Letter to 2036.
  • Focus: III Back or Forward?, V Two Films, VI The Folded Note, VII The Receipt.
  • Write: X, the note for their own box. It goes to their My notes.
