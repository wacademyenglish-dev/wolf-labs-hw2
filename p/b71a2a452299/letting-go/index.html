Learning the Ways of the Force · Star Wars: A New Hope · The Training Remote

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A farm boy, two droids, an old Jedi and a fast ship.

The grammar: -ing as a Noun: Learning the Force takes years. He is afraid of failing.

The class (about 58 min): I Warm-up and Pick a Side · II The Words · III The Clip · IV The Jedi Dilemmas · V Line by Line · VI -ing as a Noun · VII The Training Remote · VIII Your Training · IX A Master’s Advice. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Luke now. In the clip, what is he trying to learn? Guess.”

Star Wars (George Lucas, 1977), later called A New Hope: Luke Skywalker lives on a desert farm on Tatooine, a planet with two suns, and dreams about flying. An old man, Obi-Wan Kenobi, tells him his father was a Jedi Knight, gives him his father’s lightsaber and starts teaching him the Force. The grammar: -ing as a noun: as the subject (Learning the Force takes years, with a singular verb) and after a preposition (good at flying, afraid of failing, interested in becoming, before fighting, by using, without seeing), never to after a preposition.

A note for the teacher: the clip is the official one from the Star Wars channel, a calm conversation. The times follow its captions (23/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Pick a Side (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Pick a Side: Luke can stay on the farm or leave with Obi-Wan. The student chooses for him and defends it for thirty seconds. Then tap “The teacher pushes back” and let them answer. Twice. Push for -ing as a noun (leaving is…, staying means…, good at flying).

The words (II): pilot, warrior, weapon, betrayed and energy, before the video, in mystery meaning and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, A Master’s Advice): the student writes advice for someone who is learning English, with -ing as a subject and after a preposition. The corrector marks Learn English takes, afraid of make, good at to speak. It goes to My notes.

ON SCREEN

The cover is two suns setting over the dunes. I: Warm-up and Pick a Side. III: the clip in three steps. Before: clues, one by one, to guess what the old man gives Luke. Watch: One word is wrong (say the right one) and What does he say?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: match each person to who they are, then a slider (is learning easier with a teacher?) and the student says why. V: five frames on the film strip. VI, the message from Obi-Wan: the student reads it out loud, taps the -ing words, then finishes the rule. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Training Remote: the view through the helmet visor: the remote floats in the middle and fires six shots. The student reads the clue, says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the sentence is written, the saber blocks the shot and the visor gets darker, until the student trains with eyes closed, like Luke. VIII, Your Training: a 45-second clock for a minute of talk about learning; the student taps each -ing phrase (Learning is, good at, afraid of, Before, by, without) when they use it. IX: the advice.

THE COMMON ERRORS

Use the Force is hard, Learn the Force takes years, good at to fly / good at fly, afraid of fail, afraid to failing, interested in become, Trusting your feelings are hard, before to fight, by use, for using (by using), without see.

THE JEDI DILEMMAS (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the clip, three choices about the student’s own learning: the best way to learn something hard, who they would rather learn from, and what they trust in a big decision. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast -ing as a noun quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Pick a Side, IV The Jedi Dilemmas, VII The Training Remote and VIII Your Training.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Clip and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI -ing as a Noun.
  • Write: IX A Master’s Advice.
