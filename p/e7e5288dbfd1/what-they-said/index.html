What They Said · The Tin-Can Chain

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A sentence travels through four people.

The grammar: said / told me, one step back (will → would), questions without DID, IF and TO.

Short class (about 55 min): I It Stuck · II The Meeting Notes · III The Chain · IV Still True? · V The Question That Forgot · VI Yes or No · VIII The Leak · X Back to the Start. Skip if you’re short on time: VII The Promise That Moved and IX Your Own Words (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 68 min.

Open with: “What did somebody tell you this week that stuck in your head?”

A sentence travels through four people. It only arrives right if three things happen on the way: the verb takes a step back (will → would, am → was), the pronouns change, and SAY and TELL are in the right place. At B1 the student doesn’t get WOULD wrong; they avoid it, and nobody corrects a correct sentence. They also say she said me and she asked where did I live. On screen, four tin cans joined by string: a right report lights the string to the next can; a wrong one leaves it red and broken. The quotation marks fall off when the quote becomes a report.

THE PAGES

  • II The Meeting Notes: Priya’s notes after Monday’s meeting. The student reads them out loud, taps the words that report (said, told me, asked me if…), then finishes the rule (six lines). The rule stays in the corner on III to IX.
  • III The Chain: six messages. The student says how the next person reports it, out loud, then taps Check: the string lights up and the quotation marks fall.
  • IV Still True?: five lines. The student taps “still true” or “over”, then says the report shown on the screen.
  • V The Question That Forgot: six reports that still sound like questions. The student finds the question order and says it flat, then taps Check.
  • VI Yes or No: five yes/no questions. The student builds each report out loud (asked me if…), then taps Check.
  • VII The Promise That Moved: five reports of old promises. Right or wrong? If it’s wrong, the student says it right (WOULD).
  • VIII The Leak: four people tell what they heard about Maya’s secret. The student decides who told it first, who did the worst thing, and what they would do with a friend’s secret (see THE LEAK below).
  • IX Your Own Words: one minute with a timer: the student says what people really said to them and reports it, and counts the reports.

STILL TRUE?

Scene IV: reporting is a decision about the world, not a table. If it’s still true, the present can stay (She says she isn’t finished). Stepping back there isn’t wrong, just less natural.

THE QUESTION THAT FORGOT

Scene V: the student says the report flat, falling at the end. Rising intonation means it’s still a question.

WOULD, OUT LOUD

Scene VII: was going to is correct too, but make them say WOULD five times. If a promise is still in the future, She said she’ll be here is fine.

YOU ARE THE COLLEAGUE

This lesson has no recording: your voice is the audio.

THE LEAK (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VIII is now a mystery and a dilemma. Your student reads what Leo, Priya, Sam and Karen say, decides who told Maya’s secret first (tap Check the story after they choose), then who did the worst thing, and finally what they would do with a friend’s secret about another friend. Wait five seconds in silence after each question, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (she said me → she told me); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word is one minute; then you take the other side.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I It Stuck, III The Chain, IV Still True?, VI Yes or No, VIII The Leak, IX Your Own Words.
  • Focus: II The Meeting Notes, V The Question That Forgot, VII The Promise That Moved.
  • Write: X, their three reports. It goes to their My notes.
