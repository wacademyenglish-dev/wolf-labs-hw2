Behind the Wheel · Au Pair · Lesson 20 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: driving in the U.S. as an au pair: home country license, international driving permit, a state license (rules vary by state), practice with a licensed driver, weather, distracted driving, school buses. Grammar: giving advice with should / shouldn’t.

The story: Au Pair in America’s official FAQ on driving (video). Then six problems to give advice on, and twelve situations to classify.

About 34 min: I · Can Au Pairs Drive? (10) · II · Give Advice (8) · III · Should or Shouldn’t? (12) · IV · I Can (4).

Open with: “Do you drive in Brazil? How would you feel driving three kids in the snow?”

Driving rules depend on the state: the student must check with her coordinator. Encourage honesty about driving experience in interviews: it is a safety issue.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

international driving permit · state driver’s license · You should… · You shouldn’t… · distracted driving · I don’t feel safe driving today.

CAN AU PAIRS DRIVE?

Au Pair in America (US Office), “Can My Au Pair Drive While in the United States?” (4 min, official captions checked on 15/10/2026).

GIVE ADVICE

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

SHOULD OR SHOULDN’T?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. If the student doesn’t drive, plan with her: families in cities with public transportation, and saying it clearly in the profile.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

You should to practice, You don’t should, lying about driving experience. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON
