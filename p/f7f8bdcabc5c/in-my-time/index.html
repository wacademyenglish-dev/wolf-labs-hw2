In My Time · Avengers: Doomsday · The Assembly Table

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: The heroes who are left, and the man whose mask hides everything.

The grammar: Who, Which, That, Whose: The heroes who died. The shield that he carried. The man whose cloak can fly.

The class (about 51 min): I Warm-up and Pick a Side · III The Trailer · V Hot Takes · VI Who, Which, That, Whose · VII The Assembly Table · VIII Your Heroes · IX Questions. II The Words and IV Line by Line are short listening scenes. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the heroes now. In the trailer, who is the hero that you want to see most? Why?”

Avengers: Doomsday (Marvel, 2026) brings together the heroes who are still alive after the last great war. Something is coming that nobody can stop alone: Doctor Doom, a genius who wants to rule every world. Many warriors who were stronger than the Avengers have already died; the heroes who are left must forget their fights and stand together. The grammar: relative clauses at B1: who / which / that, where + a full sentence, whose + a noun, dropping the pronoun when it is the object (the decision we have to make), everything that (never what), and never doubling (the shield that he carried, not “carried it”). Lesson 281 (A2) practiced who / which / where; this one adds whose, that, the dropped pronoun and the doubling traps.

A note for the teacher: the trailer is dark and dramatic but has no blood. The subtitles don’t say who speaks each line, so the lesson never guesses. The times follow the captions (24/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Pick a Side (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Pick a Side: Two sides: the heroes who are left (Sam, the new Captain America, Thor, the Fantastic Four) and Doctor Doom, the genius whose face is behind a metal mask. The student taps one side and defends it for thirty seconds. Then tap “The teacher pushes back”: read the counter-argument on the screen, in your voice, and let them answer. Twice. Push for who / which / whose in the defense (“He’s a man whose country…”). After the trailer, ask if they changed sides.

The words (II): warriors, enemies, sacrifice, stronger and miracle, before the video, in mixed letters and match the columns. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, Questions): five questions about the people and places in the student’s life (the person you trust most, a place where you were happy, someone whose job you’d love, something you can’t live without, the best film you saw this year). Then the student asks the teacher two. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a dark war room with a round table and a hologram. I: Warm-up and Pick a Side. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: the student matches four objects to their heroes (shield, hammer, cloak, mask). Watch: Which word did you hear? and One word is wrong, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: True or lie, four sentences about the trailer; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Hot Takes: three unpopular opinions about heroes; the student taps 1 to 5 and defends the number. VI, Who, Which, That, Whose: six clues to sort by meaning into four boxes (a person, a thing, a place, an owner), then four sentences with one wrong word: the student finds it and says the fix (who his cloak → whose, that he carried it → delete it, which the warriors meet → where, what → that). VII, The Assembly Table: a round holographic table with six seats. The student reads the clue, says the sentence out loud and taps Check; the hero appears at the table with a line of light to the green core. The rule stays in the corner. VIII, Your Heroes: Ask Me: the student builds five questions with a relative clause (a teacher who…, a place where…, someone whose…), asks the teacher, and finds one thing in common. IX: questions.

THE COMMON ERRORS

the shield what, everything what, the hammer who, the warriors which, the man who his cloak (whose), the country which the warriors meet (where), that he carried it, who they died, where … there, everybody fears him.

HOT TAKES (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, three unpopular opinions about the trailer’s world: a hero who works alone is stronger than a team that argues; the villain is the best part of the film; a film with twenty heroes is too much. For each one the student taps a number from 1 (totally disagree) to 5 (totally agree) and defends it with who / which / that / whose; you take the opposite number and push back. This is a different format from Pick a Side in I: here there is no team to choose, only how strongly they agree. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own life. Recast quietly while they talk (the hero what, who his plan, that he loves him); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Pick a Side, V Hot Takes, VII The Assembly Table, VIII Your Heroes and IX Questions.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Who, Which, That, Whose.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
