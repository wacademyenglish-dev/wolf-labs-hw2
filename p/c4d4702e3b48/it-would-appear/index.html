It Would Appear That… · The Night Desk

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A psychologist on stage says we all believe we are “finished”, and we are wrong.

The grammar: The Rule: An editor never prints more than the evidence. Four careful tools.

Short class (about 57 min): I You and the News · III The Talk · V The Claim Meter · VI The Rule · VII The Press · IX The Reporter Calls · X The Editorial Board · XI To Print. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII Your Headlines (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 64 min.

Open with: “Where do you read the news? A site, an app, social media, TV?”

A psychologist on stage says we all believe we are “finished”, and we are wrong. By morning, a news site has turned his talk into loud headlines. The student is the night editor: before the page goes to print, every headline that says more than the evidence gets rewritten, carefully. People appear to underestimate change. It could be argued that teenagers choose for a stranger. The bees may have been killed by the heat. The study suggests; it does not prove. On screen, a claim meter: a red needle shows how much each headline claims, a green mark shows how much the evidence supports, and the careful version brings the needle down to the mark. Then the article is set in type, line by line, as the press roller moves.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: the talk (about seven minutes), sealed in five chapters. Near the end he hedges on purpose: “not entirely sure, but it probably has to do with …”. The student hears a scientist being careful. No speaker, stage or university name is on the screen.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

Portuguese has no empty subject, so the IT disappears (Would appear that …), and the Portuguese for “may have been” comes out as can have been. The passive loses been. Hunt all three all hour.

A NOTE

tend to, seem to, arguably, to some extent are taught in another live B2 lesson. They may come up as known words, but they are not the target here.

THE PAGES

  • III The Talk: before the video, two sliders (have you changed? will you?). In each of the five chapters the student reads two lines BEFORE pressing play and answers by tapping; tap the time to hear that moment again. After the talk: “proving or suggesting?”.
  • V The Claim Meter: six headlines. The student reads the headline and what the study found, says it again carefully out loud, then taps Check: the needle comes down and the careful version appears. Before the rule, any careful version is fine; collect “proves” and “causes”.
  • VI The Rule: the night editor’s note. The student reads it out loud, taps the careful tools, then finishes the five-line rule. It stays in the corner afterwards.
  • VII The Press: six lines go too far. For each, the student says it again out loud with the tool in the margin, then taps the tool: the line is set in lead. You can read the sentence with too much confidence first.
  • VIII Your Headlines: story cards about the student’s life and a dice with three tools. The student answers using at least one of them; you push back: “Is that proven?”
  • IX The Reporter Calls: four phone calls. You are the reporter. The student chooses how to answer, says it out loud, and sees what she says. Only the careful answer saves the page.

The words (II): regret, illusion, predict, underestimated and claim, before the video, in mystery meaning and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE EDITORIAL BOARD (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the reporter’s call, a midnight meeting: three proposals for the site (a rule against “proves”, going back to the click headline, a claim meter next to every headline). The student votes Yes, No, or proposes a change, and says why with the careful tools. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word is one minute on the talk: will their opinions change in ten years? Then you take the other side. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and the News, V The Claim Meter, VIII Your Headlines, IX The Reporter Calls and X The Editorial Board.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Talk and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Rule and VII The Press.
  • Write: XI To Print.
