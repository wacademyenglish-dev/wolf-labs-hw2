The Witness Room · B1 Unit 1 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 1 (Telling what happened). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: The winning cake of the town baking contest disappeared at eleven last night. Your student is the detective. You are the three witnesses: the night guard, the other baker and the judge. (The answer: the judge’s dog ate it.)

About 43 min: I · The Ladder (8) · II · The Witness Room (15) · III · The Deal (8) · IV · Your Stories (8) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Climb the ladder. Six steps, from easy to hard.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

past continuous · past continuous × past simple: when, while · past simple or present perfect · present perfect for experience · present perfect for news · how many times, so far, this week

THE LADDER

Six steps from easy to hard. A good answer: tap Go up. Not yet: Try again on the same step.

THE WITNESS ROOM

You are each witness. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then you are the chief: the student reports with was …ing, when / while and has … since then. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

THE STORY IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

YOUR STORIES

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I was check, I have seen him yesterday, when I was walking, I was hearing a crash, I have went, since two years, did you ever (for have you ever). B1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE DEAL (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In II your student asks and you answer; in III it is the other way round. They are the baker, you are the embarrassed judge: ask the four questions, then close a deal on the screen. Your secret limit is only on your screen: give in slowly, and only if they use facts from the night. Wait five seconds in silence before each answer and use the PUSH line to make it harder. Recast quietly (was putting, have baked, didn’t see); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · The Ladder and V · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · The Witness Room and III · The Deal and IV · Your Stories.
