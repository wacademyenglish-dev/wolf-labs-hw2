You’d Better Not Volunteer · The Hunger Games · The Sponsors

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Twelve districts, one Capitol, and a boy from the poorest place.

The grammar: Should or Had Better: You should find water. You’d better not run.

The class (about 51 min): I Warm-up and The Rules of Panem · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V Should or Had Better · VI The Sponsors · VII The Capitol Court · VIII Your Advice · IX Questions. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the Games now. In the trailer, what should the boy from District 12 do first? Guess.”

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping (2026), a prequel set 24 years before Katniss, at the 50th Games, the Second Quarter Quell, with twice the number of tributes. Haymitch Abernathy, from poor District 12, is one of them; mentors tell him what he should do and what he had better not do, and in the arena sponsors can send gifts by parachute. The grammar: advice and warnings: should / ought to + verb (advice), had better / ’d better (not) + verb (a warning with a danger: or else…), and Should I…? No to after should or had better; only ought takes to.

A note for the teacher: the film is about a deadly game. The lesson stays on advice and survival, never on how anybody dies. The lesson plays the whole trailer (2:24), countdown included. Lines checked against the official subtitles (25/09), told in our own words.

Warm-up and The Rules of Panem (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Rules of Panem: four rules of the country appear one at a time (the districts work for the Capitol, the Reaping, the Games, the special rule of the 50th Games). For each, the student taps Yes / No way / Only if… and gives advice (you should…, you’d better…). The lesson stays on advice and survival, never on how anybody dies.

The words (II): bet, glory, confess, power and broken, before the video, in hear it in the video and turn the cards. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, Questions): five questions about advice (a first day at a new job, your city at night, what to visit, your younger self, tourists in your country). Then the student gives the teacher two pieces of advice, one with should and one with had better. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a misty clearing at dawn with the golden horn in the middle. I: Warm-up and The Rules of Panem. III: the whole trailer in three steps. Before: a wheel (a tribute, a mentor, a sponsor, a Gamemaker, a friend at home, a reporter): the student says who they are in the Games. Watch: True or lie? Five sentences, each with a ▶ time before 1:30; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. After: five questions about the trailer; the student taps the right answer, with a ▶ to hear it again. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Should or Had Better: a letter from your mentor on a dark card. The student taps the advice and the warnings (should, ought to, had better), then finishes the rule board. The board stays in the corner on the next pages. VI, The Sponsors: the arena seen from above like a relief map (contour lines, the river, the Cornucopia, the force field). Six pieces of advice to build from words (“Water · you · find water · first”): the student builds it, says it, taps Check, and a silver parachute drops on one spot of the map with its gift (water, a warning, rope, bread, medicine, a pin). The next spot pulses in ember red. VIII, Your Advice: two conversations; the teacher picks A (a friend’s new job) or B (a tourist in your city) and the student gives advice. IX: questions.

THE COMMON ERRORS

should to find, had better to smile, should finding, should sleeps, you’d better don’t run, ought find, you’d better smiled (had better looks past but means now), had not better, Do I should…?, I should trust her?

THE CAPITOL COURT (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the Sponsors, your student is the jury in three light cases: a mentor who hid a note in the bread, a sponsor who stopped paying, a TV host who made a tribute cry on live TV. They read the evidence, choose a verdict and say why with should, ought to or had better. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Rules of Panem, VI The Sponsors, VII The Capitol Court, VIII Your Advice and IX Questions.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: V Should or Had Better.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
