A Small Black Coat · The Coat Rack

Day four of the race from London to Edinburgh. Bob is in Cambridge, it is 3°C, his train leaves in three hours, and he has no coat. The student dresses the hanger (the coat takes the color they say), tries three sizes on the mannequin, hangs the tags SIZE · COLOR · THING in order, and spends Bob’s £100 on the rack. Then they shop for three friends with you as the shop assistant, and write their own shopping list. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

size + color + thing: a small black coat. The thing always comes last. One thing takes a: I want a coat. The mistakes to listen for: a coat black, a black small coat, I want black coat, and want said like won’t. Never explain the rule before they have built two coats.

WHAT DOES BOB WANT? (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the video in Cambridge: it is 3 °C and the train leaves in three hours. Your student is Bob and puts three things in order (a warm coat, a hot coffee, a big hat). There is no right answer. Say your own order first, then let them tap 1, 2, 3, wait five seconds in silence, and use the PUSH line (only you see it). The starters keep I want a … in their mouth. Then show your order and agree on one top 3. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I What Are You Wearing?, IV What Does Bob Want? (three things in order), IX The Rack, X The Shop Assistant, XI Your Wardrobe.
  • Listen: II The Story So Far (listen first; the transcript opens after the audio ends), III Cold in Cambridge and VII In the Shop (the video: BEFORE, WATCH, AFTER), V The Colors.
  • Focus: VI The Sizes (the mannequin), VIII The Tags (only one order hangs).
  • Write: XII Your Shopping List, with the red wavy line for the order and the missing a. It goes to their My notes.

THE PLAYER

Every audio button plays and pauses. 0.75× makes every audio slower. In listening scenes the transcript only opens after the audio ends: first they listen, then they read.

THE SHOP ASSISTANT

You start: “Hello! Can I help you?” Be a little difficult (“We don’t have red. Blue?”). Tap each part you hear; the classic mistake has its own button, MISTAKE HEARD.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

Everything the student taps moves on both screens. You judge the speech. G opens this guide.
