Three Hundred Years · The Two Doors

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: In a blind test in Paris in 2014, most of 21 soloists preferred new violins to three-hundred-year-old ones, and the old ones still sell for millions.

The grammar: Two Doors: A sentence goes through the wide door. A thing goes through the narrow one.

Short class (about 46 min): I Nobody Was Watching · II The Blind Test · III Two Doors · IV A Thing or a Sentence? · V The Dealer · VI The Other Door · X Both Are Right. Skip if you’re short on time: VII, VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “What exactly did you do?”

In a blind test in Paris in 2014, most of 21 soloists preferred new violins to three-hundred-year-old ones, and the old ones still sell for millions. Both things are true. The hour is about holding them in one sentence. The door depends on what comes after it: a sentence takes although / even though; a thing takes despite / in spite of. Students say despite of, although … but and despite it cost. On screen, two doors: a wide one for a sentence and a narrow one for a thing; the piece of the sentence goes through the right one. Six violin cases from the blind test open at the end of scene II.

THE RECORDINGS

Twenty recordings, each with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens after listening. Two soloists (II), Enrico the dealer (V) and one reader with five pairs (VI). Enrico says “four hundred years” in one line; that’s his voice, leave it. Scene II: the student taps True or Lie on what each player said, and the six cases open at the end. Scene VI: the student matches each ALTHOUGH idea to its DESPITE version before hearing the pairs.

THE DEALER

Scene V: Enrico pushes back five times. After each of his lines the student taps how to answer (one reaction concedes and disagrees, and then he answers); don’t rescue the student: at B2, they hold the position. After each turn, push back once more in your own words.

THE DEBATE

Scene IX: every answer concedes first, then disagrees. Watch for students who concede and forget to disagree.

THE COUNCIL (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the blind test and Enrico, your student sits on the city music council and votes on three proposals: yes, propose a change, or no. There is no right answer: let them vote, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. Every reason concedes first (although, even though, despite, in spite of). Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word is one minute on old things, then you take the other side.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Nobody Was Watching, V The Dealer, VII The Council, IX Concede, Then Disagree.
  • Listen: II The Blind Test, VI The Other Door.
  • Focus: III Two Doors, IV A Thing or a Sentence?, VIII It Happened, or It Might.
  • Write: X, their story in one sentence with one door. It goes to their My notes.
