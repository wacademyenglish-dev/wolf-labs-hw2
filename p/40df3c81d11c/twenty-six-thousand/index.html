Twenty-Six Thousand · The Rewind

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A researcher collected regrets from over twenty-six thousand people; in a follow-up, 62.6% regretted something they did not do.

The grammar: The Rewind: A wish rewinds the verb. Now: one step back. Then: two steps back. Someone else: WOULD.

Short class (about 46 min): I Something Different · II Sixty-Two Point Six · III The Rewind · IV Two Correct Sentences · V Four Submissions · VI Four Kinds of Regret · IX Your Wish, Exactly. Skip if you’re short on time: VII, VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 59 min.

Open with: “What exactly would you change?”

A researcher collected regrets from over twenty-six thousand people; in a follow-up, 62.6% regretted something they did not do. Students talk about regret with I wanted to go: perfect English that says nothing about what happened. English steps back from the truth: I wish I spoke French (now, one step), I wish I had gone (then, two steps), I wish you would stop (someone else’s habit). If only works the same way, stronger. Students say I wish I have and I wish you will. III: the student reads a fact, says the wish out loud and taps Check; the tape rewinds and the verb slides back (six facts). IV: sort six sentences into two boxes (SAYS it didn’t happen / DOESN’T say). VI: tap the kind of regret, then say it with IF ONLY. VII: three calm questions on cards. VIII: five broken wishes; find the broken part and say the fix. On screen, a tape rewind: a tense ruler (PAST PERFECT · PAST · PRESENT) where the verb of the fact sits, and the verb of the wish slides one or two steps back while the reels turn.

CALM, NOT THERAPY

Keep it to decisions, things and habits. The student chooses what to share. Scene VII asks slowly; allow silence.

THE RECORDINGS

Scene V: four submissions, each with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens after listening. After listening, the student taps what the person wishes (the options mean different things), then says it with I wish or If only. None of the speakers says “I wish”: the student does. Voice 4 is the one thing somebody DID (I wish I hadn’t).

THE BIG ASK (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the four kinds of regret, your student lives a boldness regret before it happens: they ask their boss (you, Ms. Hale) for three months off to travel. Each side has a limit; yours is secret (six weeks plus one month remote). Ask the four questions, wait five seconds in silence after each, then close the deal on screen and use the PUSH line. Recast mistakes with I wish / If only quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Something Different, II Sixty-Two Point Six, VII Twenty-Six Thousand Days, VIII The Big Ask.
  • Listen: V Four Submissions.
  • Focus: III The Rewind, IV Two Correct Sentences, VI Four Kinds of Regret.
  • Write: IX, one wish for now and one for then. They go to their My notes.
