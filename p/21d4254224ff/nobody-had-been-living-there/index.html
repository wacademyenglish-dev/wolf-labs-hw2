Nobody Had Been Living There · The Crow Hill Archive

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A small-town legend.

The grammar: The Rule: The house had been empty for years.

Short class (about 43 min): I Town Legends · II Hawkins, 1983 · III The Rule · IV The Archive · V The Red String · IX The Front Page. Skip if you’re short on time: VI, VII, VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 63 min.

Open with: “Is there a legend or a “haunted” place in your town?”

A small-town legend. For nine years, Millbrook told a ghost story about the house on Crow Hill: a light in the attic, a green garden, somebody at the window. The student opens the local newspaper archive (1989–1998) and rebuilds, pin by pin, what had been happening before the night the police opened the door. The grammar is the past perfect continuous: had been + -ing for something that went on up to a moment in the past (When they opened the door, she had been living there for nine years), against had + past participle for finished or counted actions and for state verbs (had written three stories, had known). The twist: the house was never empty. Rosa Lane, the youngest daughter, had been living there quietly, watering the roses.

THE TRAILER

Scene II: Stranger Things, season 1 (Netflix). It starts at 0:12 on purpose: the first seconds have a swear word. After the trailer (step 3) there is ONE screen: the trailer stays on the left, with the six clippings of the archive under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines about the trailer. Every right answer pins a clipping (“Clippings found 3 / 6”). On a Lie the student says the true line, with had been + -ing when it fits. When all six are done, the reporter asks one last question (“How long had she been looking for him?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud. There are no time buttons here: this trailer has no timed captions in the lesson.

THE ARCHIVE · THE RED STRING · THE NEIGHBOR

Scene IV: six clippings; the student answers each question and you tap the version you heard. Scene V: two pins on the timeline, one sentence that joins them. Scene VI: you are Mr. Okafor, the neighbor, in 1998; the student asks the questions.

STINGY JACK

Scene VII: the old Irish story of Stingy Jack and why we carve pumpkins (turnips first). Read it out loud, then the four questions.

THE MISTAKES

The house was empty since 1989, They were waiting for two hours when…, have been in a past story, had been grow, had been knowing, since two hours.

THE JURY (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

The legend is over: Rosa Lane had been living at Crow Hill all along. Your student reads the evidence and gives a verdict on three parts of the story: Rosa, the Millbrook Herald and the neighbors. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast had been + -ing quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
