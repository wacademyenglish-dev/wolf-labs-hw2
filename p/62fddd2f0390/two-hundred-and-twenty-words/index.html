Two Hundred and Twenty Words · The X-Ray

A letter from the city arrives. It never once says what it is going to do, yet inside it a school bus route is being cut. The letter hides every event inside a noun: the discontinuation of certain low-utilization services. That is nominalization. At C1 the student needs both directions: unpack a nominalization (who did what?) and build one when a report must be short. English often uses the zero form (a cut, a rise, a change) where students reach for -tion or -ing. And every nominalization can delete the person responsible.

ON SCREEN

The letter is shown as an X-ray: in scene III, tap a marked noun and the lens shows the verb and the hidden person inside it. In scene V, every compression sends the person who disappeared to the wall of the missing (THE COMPANY, THE CUSTOMERS, THE TEAM…). Scene VI turns chains of OF into stacks, with the main noun last.

NOTES

Scene II is built to fail: if the student can’t say what is going to happen, that is the point. Scene VII is unfair on purpose: the compressed reply gets a meeting, the human one gets a form letter. Discuss it, and ask for a version that keeps both the number and the son.

THE ARENA (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VII is now an arena: the student picks the reply they would send to the city (the mother’s human one, or the neighbor’s compressed one) and defends it for one minute with nominalized phrases; then they swap and defend the other. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder, and finish by asking for one sentence that keeps both the number and the son. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
