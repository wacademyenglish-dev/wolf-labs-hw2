It’s Time We Stood Together · X-Men ’97 · The Team Vote

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A team without its teacher, and a will nobody expected.

The grammar: I’d Rather, It’s Time: I’d rather stay together. It’s time we stood up.

Short class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and Pick a Side · III The Trailer · IV Who Leads Now? · V Scene by Scene · VI I’d Rather, It’s Time · VII The Team Vote · IX A Message to the Team. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 51 min.

Open with: “You know the will now. In the trailer, would the team rather follow Magneto or split up? Guess.”

X-Men ’97 (2024), a Marvel animated series on Disney+ that continues the cartoon from the 1990s. Professor X is gone; he leaves a message saying he is proud of his X-Men. Then his will is read, and everything he built goes to Magneto. Cyclops wants the team to stay together; not everybody agrees. The grammar: would rather + base verb (I’d rather stay together than split up), would rather + person + past (I’d rather you listened), I’d rather not, Would you rather…?, and it’s (high) time + person + past (it’s time we fought back) or it’s time to + verb.

A note for the teacher: cartoon action, no blood. Lines checked against the official captions (24/09), told in our own words.

Warm-up and Pick a Side (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Pick a Side: Professor X left everything to Magneto. Stay and follow him, or leave the team? The student taps one side and defends it for thirty seconds. Then tap “The teacher pushes back” and let them answer. Twice. Push for I’d rather… than… and it’s time we….

The words (II): grateful, vigilant, dream, believe and team, before the video, in turn the cards and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, A Message to the Team): the student writes to a team of their own (work, school, family, friends): what they would rather do, what they would rather the others did, and what it is time they all did. The corrector marks would rather to, I’d rather you stay, it’s time we go. It goes to My notes.

ON SCREEN

The cover is the empty training room. I: Warm-up and Pick a Side. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: two sliders (stay together or go alone; alone or in a team), no right answer: the student says why. Watch: True or lie? and Which word did you hear?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: tap each of the five words as the student uses it to tell the Professor’s goodbye. V: five frames on the film strip. VI, Cyclops’s message: the student reads the letter out loud, taps the words after rather and after it’s time, then finishes the rule. The rule stays in the corner on the next page. VII, The Team Vote: the blue grid of the training room seen from above, six pods in a ring (Cyclops, Storm, Wolverine, Jean, Gambit, Beast). The student reads the clue and says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the right sentence is written and the pod lights up in yellow. At six, the team stands together. VIII, Would You Rather? six cards to open. IX: writing.

THE COMMON ERRORS

would rather to stay, would rather staying, I’d rather you listen, I’d rather you to listen, I’d rather he would be here, it’s time we fight / we will fight, it’s time making, it’s time for make, I’d rather don’t, I’d not rather, Do you rather…?

WHO LEADS NOW? (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer, your student ranks five possible leaders for the team, from the one they want most to the one they want least, then defends their number 1 and number 5 with I’d rather … led … and it’s time … took charge. Wait five seconds in silence before they speak, then show your own order and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. There is no right order. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Pick a Side, IV Who Leads Now?, VII The Team Vote (say it, then Check) and VIII Would You Rather?.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI I’d Rather, It’s Time.
  • Write: IX A Message to the Team.
