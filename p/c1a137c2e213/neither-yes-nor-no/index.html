Neither Yes nor No · C1 Unit 5 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on professional distance: the agentless passive (Your booking was moved; It has been decided that…; Your comments have been passed on), neither… nor, not only… but also, either… or, and the ethics of distance (Mistakes were made × We made a mistake).

The story: Casa Lume (invented restaurant) got a one-star review: a birthday booking was given away and nobody apologized. The owner, Lia, answers in public with careful distance, and ends with a personal apology.

About 42 min: I · The Owner’s Reply (10) · II · The Jury (7) · III · Where Did the People Go? (9) · IV · Cool and Professional (8) · V · Distance or Honesty? (8).

Open with: “Have you ever written a bad review? Did anybody answer?”

The interesting part is the judgment, not only the form: when does distance protect people, and when does it hide responsibility? The final scene has no single right answer: let the student argue. Watch for “neither… or” and double negatives with neither.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

agentless passive: was moved, has been decided · It has been decided / agreed that… · neither… nor (verb agrees with the nearest) · not only… but also · either… or · distance × honesty: Mistakes were made / We made a mistake

THE OWNER’S REPLY

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

WHERE DID THE PEOPLE GO?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

COOL AND PROFESSIONAL

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

DISTANCE OR HONESTY?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

neither… or (neither… nor), Neither X nor Y aren’t (Neither X nor Y is), It has decided that (It has been decided that), Mistakes were made in a personal apology, not only… also (not only… but also). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE JURY (II) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the owner’s reply, your student sits on a small jury: three everyday cases of blame (a cake on the wrong day, a meeting moved at night, cold food from a delivery app). They read the evidence, give a verdict, and say it twice: with distance (the agentless passive, neither… nor) and without it. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the verdict harder. Recast quietly while they talk, and at the end ask for one or two self-corrections.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
