The Extra Day · The Family Calendar

A number isn’t a date until it becomes another word: twelve → the twelfth. The screen is the family’s kitchen calendar. You read the birthdays; the student drags each sticker to the right day (or taps the sticker, then the day). Then their own dates, and the extra day of February. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

Ordinals: three new words (first, second, third), everything else -th, with bends (fifth, twelfth, ninth, twentieth). Big numbers: look at the last one (twenty-first, twenty-third). Two ways: the third of May / May the third. on a day, in a month or a year; next/last take nothing. The classic: the twelve of May, May two.

WOULD YOU RATHER (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After on and in, three light choices about your student’s own dates: a birthday in summer or winter, a party on Friday or Sunday, Christmas or New Year’s Eve. Answer first yourself, then let your student choose and say it with a date. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it): a quick yes or no. Recast the ordinals quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one date themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud — I Three Birthdays, VII Would You Rather, VIII The Extra Day.
  • Listen — IV The Family Calendar (only YOU see the dictation; read one line at a time, and answer “Sorry, which day?”), V Fourteenth or Fortieth? (one play each).
  • Focus — II The Number Changes (the number turns into the ordinal on screen), III The Last Number, VI On, In.
  • Write — IX Your Calendar, with the red wavy line for the twelve of, May two, twenty-oneth, in the 21st, on June and on next Monday. It goes to their My notes.

THE RECORDINGS

All ten recordings are the same British speaker; the screen spelling stays American.
