What Matters Is This · One Light

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Pitch Night at a small downtown theater: every speaker gets sixty seconds under one spotlight to ask the town for something.

The grammar: the student says Rosa’s lines with the frame first (V); then reads her final draft, taps the six frames and finishes the rule (VI).

Short class (about 50 min): I Your Voice · III The Talk · V The Teardown · VI Rosa’s Final Draft · VII The Town Votes · VIII Your Sixty Seconds · IX The Ghost Light. Skip if you’re short on time: IV Line by Line (EXTRA). Full lesson: about 57 min.

Open with: “Have you ever spoken in front of a crowd? What was it, and how did your body feel?”

Pitch Night at a small downtown theater: every speaker gets sixty seconds under one spotlight to ask the town for something. First the student takes apart a real persuasive talk, then fixes Rosa’s flat draft for her library on wheels, and at the end gives a sixty-second pitch of their own. The grammar is how good speakers move the light onto one idea: the what-cleft (What matters is the kids), the it-cleft (It isn’t the bus that changes lives. It’s the driver.), All I’m asking is…, The thing is…, The reason… is that…, the emphatic do / did, and the pause — and this is what matters —.

THE COMMON ERRORS

The important is… (an adjective can’t be the subject), Is the kids that… (no it), all what, the reason is because, the reason of, this is what that matters, did tried.

ON SCREEN

One light only. The flat line sits in the dark; when the cleft comes out right, a single tungsten cone sweeps across the sentence and stops on the words that matter. Read the lit words a beat later and a little louder. In scene VII, a row of the house lights up with every fixed line.

THE TALK

Scene III: a teacher and poet on the danger of silence (about four minutes), sealed until the first play, in four chapters. Each chapter shows two or three tap tasks next to the video: read them before you press play. After the talk: which word did you hear (▶ plays the moment), then two clefts said out loud with Check. The chapters skip 3:06–3:25 on purpose (a list of violent images). Chapter 3 mentions a boy beaten for being gay and a homeless man; if the student prefers, stay with the woman at the gala.

THE NEW PAGES

  • V The Teardown: Rosa’s flat line sits in the dark; the student says it with the frame first, then Check: the light lands on the right words.
  • VI Rosa’s Final Draft: tap the six frames, finish the rule. The rule then stays in the corner.
  • VII The Town Votes: five causes, only three get the money. The student ranks them, then compares with your order.

A NOTE

Inversion for emphasis is taught in other C1 lessons and is not practiced here.

The words (II): silence, consequences, honest, sacrifice and dignity, before the video, in mystery meaning and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE TOWN VOTES (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VII is now a ranking: five pitches tonight, and the town can fund only three. The student orders the causes and defends the top one and the last one with a cleft (What the town really needs is… / It isn’t … that matters; it’s…). There is no right order. Wait five seconds in silence, use the PUSH line (only you see it), then tap “Show my teacher’s order” and agree on one top 3 together. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Voice, V The Teardown, VII The Town Votes and VIII Your Sixty Seconds.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Talk and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Rosa’s Final Draft.
  • Write: IX The Ghost Light.
