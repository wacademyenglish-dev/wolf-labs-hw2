Two Mice, Three Children · The Meadow Notebook

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A naturalist builds a tiny wheat field for one harvest mouse. Day 1: one mouse. Day 80: seventeen mice.

The grammar: The Labels: one mouse, two mice. The plural of nouns, and the words that change.

Short class (about 52 min): I My Home in Numbers · II The Words · III Count Them · IV Day 1 to Day 80 · V The Labels · VI My Notebook · VII OK or Not OK? · VIII Your Pet Shop. Every scene is core; if you’re short on time, keep VII to one round. Full lesson: about 52 min.

Open with: “How many people live in your house?” The student says a number; you tap + or −. The word changes by itself: 1 person, 3 people.

A sister lesson. Another A1 lesson in the library teaches the plural too. Use this one as the review of the plural in your course, or as a second lesson for a student who needs more practice.

This is an A1 lesson for the nervous beginner. Every question is short and uses only be, have, can and do you…?. A number alone is a good answer. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier: it is help, not a mistake.

The grammar: the plural of nouns. Most words take + s or + es (cats, boxes); words ending in a consonant + y change to -ies (babies); some words are a new word (man → men, woman → women, child → children, person → people, mouse → mice); some change oo to ee (tooth → teeth, foot → feet, goose → geese); and some don’t change at all (fish, sheep).

THE VIDEO

“I Raised a Family of Tiny Mice (In 80 Days)” (Worcester Terrariums, 12:22). We use four short parts, sealed until you press play; each stops by itself: Day 1 (0:00–0:30, Bernard, a tiny mouse), Day 5 (1:06–1:48, Bianca and Gus arrive: three mice), Day 50 (8:39–9:30, the babies leave the nest) and Day 80 (10:41–11:00, seventeen mice). The narrator speaks fast and with a British accent: don’t ask the student to understand everything. Ask only the counting question on the screen. After the last part, the lesson ends with ONE screen: the video stays on the left (tap a time to see that part again, with no sound), with a specimen label under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines about the four parts. Every right answer adds one mouse to the label (“1 mouse, 2 mice, 3 mice…”). On a Lie the student says the true line (a short answer is fine). When all six are done, the naturalist asks one last question (“Is a mouse a good pet now?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud. Line by Line covers the first two minutes.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

In Portuguese, almost every plural ends in -s, so the student adds -s to everything: two childrens, five peoples, my foots, three mouses, five sheeps. And Portuguese “gente” is singular, so one people sounds natural: in English it is one person, two people.

COUNT THEM AND THE PET SHOP

Scene III comes before the rule: the student counts the animals in each photo and says the sentence. Any number they count is fine; listen to the word after the number, and tap the version you heard. In the last scene the student opens a pet shop: they choose how many mice, fish, puppies, rabbits and geese; the sign shows the right word. Then ask the three questions. The last one is a joke from the video: the mice have babies!

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

two childrens, five peoples, one people, my foots, three mouses, five sheeps, babys, and a number with a singular word: three child. A1: one correction per answer is enough.

OK OR NOT OK? (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the pet shop, three light choices about animals at home: cats on the kitchen table, three dogs in the bed, two mice in the office. Green is OK, red is not OK; there is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then let your student choose and say it with the plural. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it): one word. Recast the plurals quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
