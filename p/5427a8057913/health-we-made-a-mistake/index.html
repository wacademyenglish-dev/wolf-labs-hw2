We Made a Mistake · English for Healthcare · Lesson 11 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: disclosing an error: introduce the topic, say what happened in plain words (active voice), apologize sincerely, don’t blame colleagues, explain the possible effect honestly, say what was done and what will change, invite questions and explain the complaint route.

The story: A Geeky Medics station: a junior doctor tells a patient that a blood thinner overdose may have caused his knee bleed; the patient gets angry when he hears “one member of the team”. Then a bad apology to fix, six steps in order, and the student’s own disclosure (fictional cases).

About 41 min: I · A Medication Error (10) · II · A Bad Apology (8) · III · A Good Disclosure (7) · IV · Your Conversation (12) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Why is ‘Mistakes were made’ a bad sentence in an apology?”

Disclosure policies vary by country and institution: the student must follow their own. The point of the lesson is language and attitude: active voice (we made a mistake), no blame, honest effect, actions. In the role-play, you can be an angry patient: anger is legitimate and the student should not become defensive.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I need to talk to you about… · We made a mistake. · I’m really sorry. · This may have caused… · What we’ve done is… · If you’d like to make a complaint, …

A MEDICATION ERROR

Geeky Medics, “Explaining a Medication Error - OSCE Guide” (5 min, captions checked on 15/10/2026). British English, with an emotional patient.

A BAD APOLOGY

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

A GOOD DISCLOSURE

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

YOUR CONVERSATION

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. If the student shares a real story, listen without judging and focus on the language for next time.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Mistakes were made, blaming a colleague, It’s not a big deal, It will never happen again, I promise. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.
