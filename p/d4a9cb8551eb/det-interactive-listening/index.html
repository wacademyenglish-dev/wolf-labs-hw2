Interactive Listening · DET Prep · Lesson 7 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the Interactive Listening question: a conversation on campus, line by line. The student chooses the best response each time, completes notes from what they heard (Listen and Complete, new since July 2025) and writes a short summary of the conversation.

The story: Two short campus situations (invented): a student asks Professor Hayes for more time on an essay; a classmate, Sophie, invites the student to a study group. Then a voice message from Maya changes the plan.

About 41 min: I · Two Conversations (14) · II · Listen and Complete (8) · III · The Study Group Jury (7) · IV · Summarize It (8) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “A professor asks: ‘What happened?’ What do you say?” Collect two or three ideas before the audio.

Each line is recorded (neural voices, American and British). In the test, every line plays once: play each one ONCE first, then again only to discuss. The right answer always does two things: it answers, and it keeps the conversation going.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

answer + move on · a polite no: I’d love to, but… · asking a useful question back · closing: Perfect, see you then! · notes: day, time, place · summary: who, problem, solution

TWO CONVERSATIONS

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

LISTEN AND COMPLETE

The gaps: the student types only the missing letters (the box moves to the next gap by itself). With a clock, the text stays blurred until you start it. Check paints right and wrong and shows the full word. Spelling counts.

SUMMARIZE IT

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to say each chosen answer out loud, with natural intonation: it trains Interactive Speaking too.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

answers that repeat the question, answers that are true but not a reply, summary in the present tense, missing the deadline in the summary. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE STUDY GROUP JURY (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Maya’s voice message, the student becomes the jury for three cases from the same study group: who is to blame for a missed meeting, what to do with a classmate who always says “I’d love to, but…”, and whether a professor should give extra days for a late essay. The student reads the evidence, gives a verdict and explains it in the past tense; wait five seconds in silence, then defend the other side and use the PUSH line. This is good practice for the written summary that comes next (who, the problem, the solution). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Two Conversations and II · Listen and Complete.
  • Out loud: III · The Study Group Jury.
  • Write: IV · Summarize It.
  • Focus: V · I Can.
