The Pipeline · Selling Services · Lesson 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 24 (will: decisions now · the next step). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Friday pipeline meeting: Sara asks about every lead, and Leo decides the next step on the spot (I’ll call Mark on Monday; Will you talk to them?). The student sorts eight leads into MOVE FORWARD × NOT YET, reads chapter 6 of the Easy Reader (three slides… and a meeting at the airport), turns problems into decisions and writes the next step for four of his own leads.

About 59 min: I · Your Pipeline (5) · II · The Pipeline Meeting (8) · III · I’ll, Not “I Call” (8) · IV · Move Forward or Not Yet? (8) · V · You Choose (6) · VI · Easy Reader: Three Slides (6) · VII · Problem → Decision (7) · VIII · Next Steps for 4 Leads (8) · IX · I Can (3).

Open with: “How many leads do you have right now? Is there a client you need to call next week?”

Only one use of will today: a decision at the moment of speaking (OK, I’ll call him). Brazilians use the present (“OK, I call him tomorrow”), put “to” after will (“I’ll to send”) or say “I don’t will”. In IV the side matters less than the reason and the decision: after each tap ask “So, what’s the next step?” and wait for “I’ll…”. In VIII you are Sara: ask “And Costa Retail?” for each lead, like a real pipeline meeting.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

OK, I’ll call him on Monday. · I won’t … this month. · Will you talk to them? · Move forward / Not yet, because …

YOUR PIPELINE

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE PIPELINE MEETING

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

I’LL, NOT “I CALL”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

MOVE FORWARD OR NOT YET?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

EASY READER: THREE SLIDES

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

PROBLEM → DECISION

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

NEXT STEPS FOR 4 LEADS

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. If he uses a CRM, open it with him and write the real next steps in English.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Pipeline (sales pipeline): Funil de vendas: a lista de oportunidades em andamento, organizadas por etapa (contato, reunião, proposta, contrato).
  • Lead: Possível cliente (contato ou empresa) que ainda não comprou. “Hot lead” = muito interessado, perto de fechar.
  • Next step: Próximo passo combinado com o cliente (ligar, mandar proposta, reunião). Gerente de vendas sempre pergunta: “What’s the next step?”
  • Pipeline review / forecast: Reunião (geralmente semanal) em que o gerente revisa cada oportunidade e prevê quanto vai fechar no trimestre.
  • Quarter (Q1, Q2…): Trimestre. Metas de vendas costumam ser por trimestre: “We won’t count it this quarter.”
  • Demo: Demonstração do serviço ou sistema para o cliente.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

OK, I call him tomorrow (OK, I’ll call him tomorrow), I’ll to send it (I’ll send it), I’ll calling him (I’ll call him), I don’t will go (I won’t go). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
