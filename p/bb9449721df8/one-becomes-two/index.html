One Becomes Two · The Market Stall

Vera sells fruit and bread at the Saturday market, and she shouts what she has. The screen is her stall in dark wood: the /s/ crate, the /z/ crate, the -ES drawer, the -IES drawer and the odd basket for the stubborn ones. You are Vera’s voice. At the end, the student opens a stall and shouts. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

-s and -es (a hiss at the end, s · x · ch · sh, needs an extra beat). The two sounds of -S: books /s/, dogs /z/, and most plurals buzz. -y after a consonant: cherries (but days). The F family: knives, loaves. The stubborn ones: children, people, men, women, feet, teeth. The mistakes: the dropped S in fast speech (two tomato) and the plural of a plural (childrens, peoples).

OK OR NOT OK? (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

At Vera’s stall, your student judges three customers: two cherries before buying, ten bags for three apples, two children touching all the peaches. Green or red, no right answer. Answer first yourself, as a model. Then let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, and ask the PUSH line (only you see it): a yes / no question. Recast quietly while they talk (two cherry → two cherries, childs → children); at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves. About 5 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. There are three; use one when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Market, III Your Throat Knows (both of you, hand on the throat; don’t explain voicing), V The Knife Drawer, VI The Stubborn Ones, IX Your Own Stall.
  • Focus: II One Becomes Two (clap the beats), IV The Y Fork (look at the letter before the Y), VII Vera Shouts (spelling first; only a plain +S goes by sound), VIII OK or Not OK? (out loud).
  • Write: X Market Closed, a shopping list, with the red wavy line for two tomato, childs, peoples, knifes, cherrys, peachs. It goes to their My notes.

YOU ARE VERA

In scene VII shout each item loud and fast, like a real market: “Three peaches!” The student says the plural first, then sends it. Strong students: let them shout for you.

SCENE I

Don’t correct. Tap what you hear; the two classic mistakes have their own buttons. Scene X brings them back.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

The crates, the drawers, the signs and the stall move on both screens. G opens this guide.
