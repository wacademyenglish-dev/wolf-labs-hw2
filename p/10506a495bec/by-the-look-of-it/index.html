By the Look of It · Daredevil: Born Again · The Sound Map

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A blind lawyer who hears everything, and a criminal who became mayor. Matt is a lawyer by day, a vigilante by night, and a Catholic who goes to confession: a hero with two sides.

The grammar: Looks, Sounds, Seems: It looks expensive. It looks like a trap.

Short class (about 52 min): I In the Dark · II Who Are They? · III The Trailer · IV What Would You Do? · V Looks, Sounds, Seems · VI The Sound Map · VII The Lie Detector · IX The Hot Seat. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 59 min.

Open with: “Matt Murdock is blind. He knows the world by sound. Close your eyes: let’s see if you can do it too.”

Daredevil: Born Again (Marvel, 2025) brings back Charlie Cox as Matt Murdock and Vincent D’Onofrio as Wilson Fisk. Matt is a lawyer in Hell’s Kitchen, New York, and he is blind, but his other senses are extraordinary: he can hear a heartbeat across a room. Fisk used to be the biggest criminal in the city; now he is the mayor. They sit at one table for a polite conversation, and neither says one true thing. The grammar: looks / sounds / seems + adjective (it looks expensive, he sounds calm) × looks / sounds / seems like + a noun or a full sentence (it looks like a trap, it sounds like it’s raining), and how we say where the evidence comes from (by the look of it, I can hear…).

A note for the teacher: the lesson plays the whole official trailer (2:07). The first half is the conversation at the table; the second half has fights, a hatchet and masked men, in fast cuts. Watch it once before. The times follow the official captions, and each moment is told in our own words.

In the Dark (I): three steps. 1) The student closes their eyes. 2) The first 20 seconds of the trailer play with the screen covered: only the sound. The student says where they are, who is talking and if they are friends (the chips help; Show answer gives a model). Play it again if they want. 3) Take off the cover: the picture confirms or breaks their guess, and two questions about their life (a sound they love or hate, what their street sounds like). Don’t correct the grammar here: the rule comes in V. Let them use “it sounds like” the way they can.

II · Who Are They? (7 min): four cards, two questions each. Tap the tabs or “Next card”.

  • sec: 7 minutes. About 1.5 minutes per card; if you’re short, do one question per card.
  • do: the student reads the card out loud (or you read it), then answers the two questions. The chips are starters, tap the one they use. Show answer gives an example only after they try.
  • watch: the cards: 1 Matt (blind since a childhood accident; his other senses work like a radar; lawyer by day, vigilante by night). 2 Fisk (the Kingpin, now the mayor: polite, calm, outside the law). 3 The name: a daredevil is a person who does dangerous things without fear (dare + devil); kids called quiet young Matt “Daredevil” as a joke, and he took the name. In Brazil he is “Demolidor” (o que demole). 4 Two sides: a devil’s costume on a Catholic who goes to confession and talks to his priest; lawyer and vigilante; faith and fights.
  • stuck: give your own answer first, in one sentence, then ask again. Point to a starter: “It seems like…”.
  • strong: read the dashed PUSH line (only you see it) and argue the other side. On card 4, keep it about values, not religion: respect the student’s beliefs, no debate about faith itself.

III · The Trailer (6 min): BEFORE · WATCH · AFTER. The trailer stays whole (2:07).

  • sec: 1.5 min before, 2 min trailer, 2.5 min after.
  • do: BEFORE: Matt’s radar at the dinner table. The student guesses what Matt can hear, smell and feel, then taps the card to see Matt’s answer. WATCH: the whole trailer, with three things to watch for. AFTER: four conversation questions; Show answer gives an example.
  • watch: the second half of the trailer has fast fight cuts and masked men. Watch it once before class.
  • stuck: in BEFORE, model one: “He can probably hear Fisk’s heart. It sounds calm.”
  • strong: last question: “Is breaking the peace ever a good thing?” Ask for an example from history or their own life, not today’s politics.

IV · What Would You Do? (7 min): five dilemmas, one at a time, then the last word: Matt or Fisk?

  • sec: 7 minutes. Do three dilemmas plus the one-minute debate if time is short. The dots on top go straight to any card.
  • do: read the situation together. The student taps a side (or says their own idea) and explains why with a starter. Then you read the PUSH and argue against them. “Next dilemma” goes on.
  • watch: the five: 1 The client (your client tells you “I did it”). 2 The safe streets (a mayor cuts crime but breaks the rules: would you vote for him?). 3 The mask (you saw where the masked man went: do you tell the police?). 4 The envelope (money for your family’s restaurant if you sign and don’t ask). 5 The confession (Matt asks his priest: is it wrong to do the right thing the wrong way? The student is the priest). There is no right answer; never mark one as wrong.
  • stuck: “If I were…, I would…” is the bridge. Give two short options and let them choose.
  • strong: the last word: the student picks Matt or Fisk and talks for one minute (the timer is on screen). Then swap: you defend the other side and the student answers back.

The Lie Detector (VII): the student opens a topic card and tells three short stories about their week in one minute (the timer is on the screen), one is a lie. The teacher listens “to the heart” and judges out loud with the rule: You sound nervous. It seems like you’re lying. It sounds like a true story. Then swap: the teacher tells three, the student judges. Push for the reason: “Why does it sound like a lie?” Two or three rounds; this is the conversation of the lesson, not a quiz.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is Matt after dinner with the mayor; the teacher asks five questions (what did the restaurant sound like, how did Fisk’s voice sound, does he seem like a good mayor, what does the city look like to you, you look tired). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a wet street in Hell’s Kitchen at night. I: In the Dark (the trailer sound with the screen covered). II, Who Are They?: four cards (Matt, Fisk, the name Daredevil, two sides) over a dark photo, two questions each with starters and Show answer; the PUSH line is only on your screen. III: the whole trailer in three steps. Before: Matt’s radar, four sense cards at the dinner table (guess, then tap). Watch: the trailer and three things to watch for. After: four conversation questions. IV, What Would You Do?: five dilemma cards, one at a time (pick a side, say why, the PUSH), then Matt or Fisk? with a one-minute timer. V: sort six words into three boxes (adjective, like + a noun, like + a sentence); when all six are right, the rule appears. VI, The Sound Map: six photos of the restaurant, dark. Matt can’t see, so the student builds a question out loud (what does the table look like? does that noise sound like a glass?), taps Check, and asks you; you answer with looks, sounds or seems. Each question lights one photo in red sound, the way Matt “sees”: the table, the glass, the heartbeat, the door, the mayor’s chair, the window. VII, The Lie Detector: pick a topic card, one minute on the timer. VIII, Clues Around You: six situations; tap what fits, then say the whole sentence. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

looks like expensive, sounds like calm, sounds a glass / looks a trap, looks like trap, sounds calmly, the table look / the mayor seem / you looks, it sounds it’s raining, it seems like he lying.

  • Out loud: I In the Dark, II Who Are They?, IV What Would You Do?, VI The Sound Map (build the question, then ask), VII The Lie Detector, VIII Clues Around You and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: III The Trailer (the whole trailer).
  • Focus: V Looks, Sounds, Seems.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
