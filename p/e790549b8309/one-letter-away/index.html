Absolutely Exhausted · The Thermometer

Normal adjectives have degrees: a bit / quite / very tired. Extreme adjectives already mean “very, very”: absolutely exhausted, never very exhausted. And never absolutely good. Really works with both. Lena reads the review queue at night and deletes the reviews nobody can understand. The screen is a thermometer: very takes the mercury up to three; absolutely takes it to five.

DOES IT ALREADY MEAN “VERY, VERY”?

When your student is stuck, ask that one question. Yes: absolutely. No: very.

REALLY IS NEVER WRONG

If your student says really exhausted, accept it and praise it, then ask for the other word too.

SCENE I COMES BACK

Write down the two words your student uses for their best and worst movies. They come back in scene X, stronger.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

very exhausted, very amazing, very huge, absolutely good, absolutely tired, and the movie is bored.

THE REVIEW JURY (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VII is now a jury. Three reviews arrive in Lena’s queue with evidence (a one-star review because of cold popcorn, a café owner’s review of her own café, a hotel guest who says the room was absolutely filthy). Your student reads the evidence, gives a verdict and says why with very, absolutely or really. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, defend the other side, and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast mistakes quietly (“very exhausted” → “absolutely exhausted”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Best and Worst, VI Lena’s Queue, VII The Review Jury, VIII Your Review.
  • Listen: IV Six Reviewers (the transcript opens after they answer).
  • Focus: II Normal and Extreme, III The Thermometer, V Very Exhausted, IX The Deleted Review.
  • Write: X, reviews of their best and worst movies. It goes to their My notes.
