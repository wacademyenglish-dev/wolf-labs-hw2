How Will It Change Us? · Disclosure Day · The Crisis Room Radar

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: One old secret, and the day it ends.

The grammar: Will: Will and won’t: what you think about the future.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and Her Phone · III The Trailer · IV Line by Line · V The Jury · VI Will · VII The Crisis Room · IX The Interview. VIII Your Future is EXTRA: skip it if you are short on time.

Open with: “You know the secret now. In the trailer, how will people react? Guess.”

Disclosure Day (2026) is Steven Spielberg’s film for Universal. For seventy-nine years, governments kept a secret: we are not alone. Somebody steals the secret, a TV weather woman in Kansas City is in the middle of it, and soon the whole world will know. The big question of the trailer: how will the truth change us? The grammar: will / won’t + the verb for predictions, Will they…?, How will…?, I think … will / I don’t think … will, and there will be.

A note for the teacher: the trailer has tension and a short chase, but nothing violent is on the lesson screens; watch it once before class. The five frames follow the official captions (read on 24/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Her Phone (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; A2: short answers are fine. 2 · Her Phone: the screen becomes the phone of a woman who reads the weather on TV in Kansas City. Messages arrive one at a time; the student reads each one out loud and predicts what will happen (will / won’t). At the end they answer the last message, speaking.

The words (II): civilization, secrets, truth, frightened and empathy, before the video, in hear it in the video and turn the cards. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Interview): the teacher is the TV weather woman from Kansas City on Disclosure Day; the student interviews her with will and won’t. Then swap: the student talks about their own future. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a wheat field at night with a column of light far away. I: Warm-up and Her Phone. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a wheel (the internet, schools, the sky, money, airports, your family); the student spins it and says what will happen there on Disclosure Day. Watch: True or lie? Six sentences, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video plays that moment only (a short clean stretch that stops by itself); if it is a lie, the student says the truth. After: One word is wrong. Five lines with a ▶ (I have a left to know the truth…); the student says the right word. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Jury: the world knows we are not alone; three pieces of evidence, one vote each (better, not sure, worse), then a one-minute verdict. VI, Will: the weather woman’s messages on a dark screen. The student taps the will and won’t in the messages (will change, won’t, Will, will be, I don’t think it will), then finishes the rule board: five lines with two choices each. The board stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Crisis Room: the radar of the crisis room with six sectors (the internet, schools, the sky, money, airports, family). Six cards (“The internet · will”, “Schools · won’t”, “The sky · Will they …?”…): the student says a sentence for each, taps the card, and the sector lights up on the radar with its WILL or WON’T. With all six, the radar says ALL SECTORS REPORTED, and the student gives the president a report. VIII, Your Future (EXTRA): six questions (Will you live in the same house next year? Will robots do your job one day?…); the student taps yes or no and says why. IX: the interview.

THE COMMON ERRORS

will goes, will to go, won’t closed, will knowing, wills, don’t will / no will, Do they will…?, They will come?, people buy will, there will have (there will be), there will are.

THE JURY (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the video, the student is the jury: the world knows we are not alone. Three pieces of evidence (the sky, the internet, your family), one vote each: better, not sure, or worse. There is no right answer. Let them vote, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (will / won’t, I don’t think … will, there will be); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The verdict is one minute, then you argue the other side. VIII Your Future is now EXTRA: skip it if you are short on time.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Her Phone, V The Jury, VII The Crisis Room, VIII Your Future (EXTRA) and IX The Interview.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Will.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
