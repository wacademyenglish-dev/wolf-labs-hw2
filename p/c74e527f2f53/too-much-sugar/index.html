Too Much Sugar · Everybody Hates Chris · Rochelle’s Receipt

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Not much money, a lot of love, and one father who counts every cent.

The grammar: Count It or Don’t: Many candy bars, much sugar. Too much, too many, not enough.

The class (about 57 min): I Warm-up and The Interview · II The Words · III The Clip · IV Line by Line · V The Candy Budget · VI Count It or Don’t · VII Rochelle’s Receipt · VIII Your House · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Julius now. In the clip, how much does he think Rochelle spent on candy? Guess.”

Everybody Hates Chris (2005–2009) is the American sitcom about the teenage years of the comedian Chris Rock, told by Chris himself. Brooklyn, 1983: Chris lives with his mother Rochelle, his father Julius (who has two jobs and knows the price of everything), his brother Drew and his sister Tonya. In this clip it is Halloween: Rochelle buys a lot of candy for the neighborhood kids, Julius cuts the bars into pieces, and he can’t believe the bill. The grammar: much (you can’t count it) and many (you can count it), a lot of, too much / too many, too + adjective, not enough, and the questions How much…? How many…?

A note for the teacher: the clip is family comedy, 4:18; the lesson uses only parts of it (0:08 and 2:14 to 4:02; the school speech in the middle is skipped on purpose). It has no captions on YouTube now, so the five frames follow the times checked against its captions on 23/09, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and The Interview (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; A2: short answers are fine. 2 · The Interview: you are Julius, Chris’s father: the card with his facts shows only on your screen (dashed border). The student asks questions (How many jobs…? How much…?) and taps each one they ask; answer in character, always with a price. Then the student guesses who you are, and the photo shows the answer.

The words (II): candy, cheaper, prepare, expensive and neighborhood, before the video, in match the columns and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is Julius on Halloween night; the teacher asks five questions (how much money, how many bars, why the knife, how many kids, do your kids eat much candy?). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an old Brooklyn kitchen with a big bowl of candy. I: Warm-up and The Interview. III: the clip in three steps. Before: two taps (how much candy is too much? who spends too much?) and the student says why. Watch: What does he say? and Which word did you hear?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: True or lie? (and if it is a lie, the student says the truth), then a slider (is twenty-three dollars too much?) and the student says why. IV: five frames on the film strip. V: The Candy Budget, Rochelle and Julius make a deal. VI, Count It or Don’t: six words (sugar, candy bars, money, kids, time, dollars) to sort into much or many (the student taps a word, then a box). When all six are right, the rule appears. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, Rochelle’s Receipt: a 1983 receipt with six lines. For each line the student builds the question out loud (How many candy bars did Rochelle buy?), taps Check, and asks you; you answer. Every Check prints the line with its price, and the total grows until it reaches $22.99 (Julius: “$23 on candy”). VIII, Your House: three dice with words (candy, money, time, kids…); the student says a true sentence about their life with the three words (too much, too many, not enough) and taps Got it! for each one. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

too many sugar, sugars / moneys, too much candy bars, too many candy bar, How many money…?, How much kids…?, How much money they spent?, a lot candy, a lots of, too much expensive, chairs enough, there isn’t enough chairs.

THE CANDY BUDGET (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, a light negotiation: the student is Rochelle and wants good candy for the kids at the door; you are Julius. Only you see his limit ($15, and only if every kid gets one bar). Ask his three questions in character, wait five seconds in silence after each, then use the PUSH line (only you see it): “Ten dollars. Final offer.” Close by choosing one budget together on the screen. Recast mistakes quietly (too many sugar, How many money?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes. In the hot seat at the end (IX) the student plays Julius, so here they are on the other side.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Interview, V The Candy Budget, VII Rochelle’s Receipt, VIII Your House and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Clip and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Count It or Don’t.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
