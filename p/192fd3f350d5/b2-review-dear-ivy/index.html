Dear Ivy · B2 Unit 2 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 2 (Likely and imaginary). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Ivy writes the most famous advice column in town, and she is on holiday. This week your student answers the readers’ letters, on a board game. You read each letter in a dramatic voice.

About 48 min: I · Bingo (8) · II · The Letters (15) · III · Unpopular Opinions (7) · IV · This Week’s Answer (7) · V · Would You Rather? (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Nine squares. Pick one, answer, and make a line.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

first conditional · unless, provided that, as long as · second conditional: unlikely and imaginary · wish and if only · second × third conditional · third conditional

BINGO

A 3×3 card of quick tasks. The student picks a square and does it out loud; tap Done. Three in a line (across, down or diagonal) is a BINGO. Keep going for a second line if there is time.

THE LETTERS

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

THIS WEEK’S ANSWER

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

if you will tell her, unless you don’t go, if I would be you, I wish I have, if you would have asked, I wish I can. B2: one correction per answer is enough.

UNPOPULAR OPINIONS (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the letters, three statements from Ivy’s inbox (honest opinions, lending money, whether advice works). The student taps a number from 1 to 5 and defends it with unless, provided that and the conditionals; you take the opposite number and argue back. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the review has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · Bingo and VI · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · The Letters, III · Unpopular Opinions and V · Would You Rather?.
  • Write: IV · This Week’s Answer.
