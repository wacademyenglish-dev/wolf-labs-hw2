Never Before Had I · C1 Unit 3 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on inversion after negative and restrictive adverbials (Never before had I…, Not once did…, Rarely does…, Hardly had… when…, Little did I…, Only later did I…) and fronting for effect (Above us danced the lights; Cold it was).

The story: A travel writer (invented) has 100 words to describe the northern lights over Lake Inari, Finland, at 1:40 a.m. Then an intern’s flat draft about a desert night needs editing.

About 44 min: I · The Opening (10) · II · Make It Land (9) · III · The Editor’s Pen (8) · IV · The Best Opening (7) · V · Your Opening (10).

Open with: “What is the most beautiful thing you have ever seen in nature? Describe it in one sentence.”

Other lessons of this unit drill inversion; this one uses it for a purpose: a dramatic opening in travel writing. Remind the student that inversion is for writing and speeches. The writing card is the student’s real trip: that’s the best part of the lesson.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Never before had I… · Not once did… · Rarely does / do… · Hardly had… when… · Little did I know… · Only later did I… · fronting: Above us danced…

THE OPENING

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

MAKE IT LAND

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THE EDITOR’S PEN

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

YOUR OPENING

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Never before I had seen (had I seen), Not once anybody spoke (did anybody speak), Rarely the desert is (is the desert), Hardly had… then (when), Little I knew (Little did I know). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE BEST OPENING (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before writing their own opening, your student ranks five travel moments by how dramatic they would be as the first line of a story, then defends the top one and the last one with inversion and fronting (Never before had I…, Rarely does…, Not once did…, Above us danced…). Your order is hidden behind a button: show it after they explain, and agree on one top 3 together. Wait five seconds in silence, use the PUSH line (only you see it), recast quietly while they talk, and at the end ask for one or two self-corrections. Their number 1 can become the topic of “Your Opening”.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · The Opening.
  • Focus: II · Make It Land and III · The Editor’s Pen.
  • Out loud: IV · The Best Opening.
  • Write: V · Your Opening.
