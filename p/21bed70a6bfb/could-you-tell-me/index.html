Could You Tell Me Why…? · The Notepad

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: At the end of every job interview comes the real test: “Do you have any questions for us?” Your student watches a short mock interview at a bank, turns eight blunt questions into polite ones, and then interviews the interviewer.

The grammar: The Machine: A polite start, then a normal sentence.

Short class (about 43 min): I Asking Strangers · III The Interview · V The Blunt Notes · VI The Machine · VII Five Minutes Left · XI After the Interview. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX, X (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 63 min.

Open with: “Do you ask strangers for help, or do you use your phone?”

At the end of every job interview comes the real test: “Do you have any questions for us?” Your student watches a short mock interview at a bank, turns eight blunt questions into polite ones, and then interviews the interviewer. The grammar is the indirect question: a polite starter and then normal sentence order: Could you tell me what the salary is? The big question: is it rude to ask about money in an interview?

THE VIDEO

Scene III: sealed until your student answers two questions, then five chapters that stop by themselves. Chapter 3 has the key line: I also wanted to ask you what salary range you’d be most comfortable with.

After the five chapters, step 3 is ONE screen: the whole interview stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again), with six pages of the notepad under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines about what Mr. Wang and Mrs. Clark say. Every right answer writes a polite question in ink on a page. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, Mrs. Clark asks the student one last question (“Do you have any questions for me?”): the student taps an answer and says the polite question out loud.

THE BLUNT NOTES

Scene V: the pencil question gets crossed out and the polite one appears in ink. Tap the version you heard.

FIVE MINUTES LEFT

Scene V is the pressure: start the wall clock (it’s only theater), point at a topic, and your student asks at once, with a different starter each time.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Could you tell me where is the office?, Do you know what time does it start?, Can you tell me is it remote?, Would you mind to tell me…, and a question mark after I was wondering…

The words (II): accounting, strengths, pressure, weaknesses and honesty, before the video, in match the columns and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE OFFER (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the questions for Mrs. Clark, your student got the job and faces three choices: a lower salary, the interviewer in the elevator, and a second offer. For each one they choose, then say the polite question they would ask. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (“Could you tell me why is it lower?” → “why it is lower”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Asking Strangers, V The Blunt Notes, VII Five Minutes Left, VIII Is It Rude?, IX Your Questions and X The Offer.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Interview and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Machine.
