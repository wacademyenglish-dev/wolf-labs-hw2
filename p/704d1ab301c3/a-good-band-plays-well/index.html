A Good Band Plays Well · The Mixing Desk

A record shop on a Saturday, and a band plays in the corner. The screen is a mixing desk with two channels: THE THING (who or what something is: the adjective) and THE DOING (how something happens: the adverb). The student pushes the right fader up with a finger, and only the right channel puts the word into the sentence. At the end, they write a short review of a band they know. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

One question decides every sentence: who or what (good) or how (well)? The band is good. They play well. Most adjectives add -ly (slow → slowly, careful → carefully). fast and hard stay the same (hardly means “almost not”). good becomes another word: well. The common mistake is She sings good; the reverse is a well singer (scene VI hides one).

WOULD YOU RATHER? (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Feedback!, three choices of two: a band for your party (loudly or quietly), one new talent (sing well or dance well), and a taxi to the show (fast or carefully). There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, and use the PUSH line (only you see it). The starters keep well and the -ly words on the doing channel. Recast quietly while they talk (sings good → sings well); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I What Do You Do Well?, VII Would You Rather? (two choices, then well or -ly), VIII The Counter, IX The Talent Show.
  • Listen: II The Shop (three cards with audio, then the two words that do the job), IV Listen and Decide.
  • Focus: III Two Faders, V The -LY Machine, VI Feedback! (six sentences that squeal).
  • Write: X Your Review, with the red wavy line for sings good, played bad, a well singer, I’m well at, fastly and work hardly. It goes to their My notes.

THE FADERS

The student drags a fader up. Past the top quarter it locks: the right channel drops the word into the sentence; the wrong channel shakes and slides down, with the reason. “Push up” does the same for anyone who prefers to tap. Then play the sentence and have them say it.

THE TALENT SHOW

For each act, look at the stars and judge together: She’s a good singer, but she dances badly. Disagree sometimes. Tap each part you hear; the classic mistake has its own button, MISTAKE HEARD. At the end, the student picks the winner and says why.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

The faders move on both screens when the student lets go. You judge the speech (“Answered · show the follow-up”, “Said it · light the pad”). G opens this guide.
