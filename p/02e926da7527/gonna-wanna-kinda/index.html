Gonna, Wanna, Kinda · C1 Unit 9 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on spoken forms: gonna, wanna, gotta, kinda, sorta, dunno, lemme, ’cause, y’know, and spoken ellipsis (Coming? Sounds good. Want some?). The goal: understand all of it in films and series, use some of it with friends, and never in formal writing.

The story: Leo (invented) sends a voice message to his friend Ana to invite her to a concert on Saturday.

About 47 min: I · Series and Films (6) · II · The Voice Message (10) · III · Spoken or Written? (9) · IV · The Arena (7) · V · Memory (8) · VI · Where Would You Say It? (7).

Open with: “What series or films do you watch in English? With or without subtitles?”

Students love this lesson: it explains the films. Read the voice message fast and relaxed. Be clear about the limits: gonna in speech is normal, in an email to work it is a mistake. Three hard rules: no “gonna to”, no “wanna you to”, no “gotta to”.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

gonna = going to · wanna = want to (not with a person) · gotta = have got to · kinda / sorta / dunno / lemme · ’cause, y’know · spoken ellipsis: Coming? Sounds good.

SERIES AND FILMS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE VOICE MESSAGE

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

SPOKEN OR WRITTEN?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

MEMORY

A memory game. The student opens two cards; when they match, the student makes a sentence with the pair (example under Show answer).

WHERE WOULD YOU SAY IT?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

gonna in a work email, I’m gonna to, I wanna you to, I gotta to go, too many y’knows in a presentation, ain’t (avoid). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE ARENA (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the rule, your student picks a side: should a learner actually say gonna and wanna, or only understand them? Wait five seconds in silence while they choose, start the minute, and let them defend it; then tap Swap sides and they defend the other side. Use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. There is no right answer. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (watch for gonna to); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
