Before You · The Clothesline

Every word in Before to go out looks correct; the shape is what doesn’t exist. The screen is a clothesline: the student hangs six photos of one real day in order, then ties each pair with before, after or when. The day belongs to Meaty, a shelter dog (a real video, 2013). No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

before / after + a whole sentence (before he went out) or -ing (before going out), never to. No will after the knot: I’ll call you after I get home. when keeps both halves in the same tense: When I was a child, we had a dog.

THE VIDEO AND THE AUDIO

Scene II plays the whole video. If it doesn’t play, use the story audio under it (pause and 0.75×; checked against the audio). The six-sentence audio closes scene III.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Before You Sleep, III Hang the Day (the sentence before the tap), VI Two Ways, VII The Other End, VIII The Day That Divides, IX How You Met.
  • Listen: II One Day, Filmed.
  • Focus: IV Tie the Knots, V Before To, After Will.
  • Saori: the second-to-last scene is a tribute to a dog called Saori (2013–2026). It isn’t an exercise. Let the student read the line once, and tap the button together.
  • Write: X, a day that divides their life, with the red wavy line for before / after to, will after the knot, mixed tenses with when. It goes to their My notes.
