Before the Meeting Starts · Selling Services · Lesson 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 16 (small talk: like + -ing · How was your weekend?). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: The lesson opens in the Arena: you are Janet Moore, finance director of Pine Hill Foods (fictional), two minutes before a meeting. Then chapter 1 of the course story “Leo’s First Big Client”: Leo makes small talk in an elevator… with the CEO, and says he hates long meetings. The student hears six small talk questions, finds the Brazilian traps, asks YOU the questions in the hot seat, and writes five icebreakers.

About 58 min: I · The Small Talk Lift (9) · II · Easy Reader: The Elevator (8) · III · Hear It, Answer It (7) · IV · Like Playing, Not “Like Play” (10) · V · Your Turn to Ask (8) · VI · You Choose (5) · VII · Your 5 Icebreakers (8) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: No warm-up today: the lesson opens with the client. Say “Good morning! You’re early. How are you?” and wait.

Small talk is a real sales skill: the first two minutes decide the mood of the meeting. Grammar: like / love / don’t like + -ing, and short past answers (It was great · Yes, I did · No, I didn’t). Brazilian traps: “I like play” (no -ing), “Was great” (no subject), “Did you went?”, “What you did?”. In I, answer only what he asks; if he forgets “And you?”, wait in silence. In V he asks and you answer as a client: react to his reactions.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

How was your weekend? → It was great, thanks. And yours? · Do you like …ing? → Yes, I do / No, I don’t. · Did you…? → Yes, I did / No, I didn’t. · I love / like / don’t like + -ing

THE SMALL TALK LIFT

Arena do Cliente: YOU are the client. Read the client card on your screen (only you see the answers). The student asks; you answer ONLY what was asked, short and a little busy. Tap each fact when the student finds it. At the end, Show answer has model questions. (Soon the AI can play the client; the card stays the same.)

EASY READER: THE ELEVATOR

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

HEAR IT, ANSWER IT

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

LIKE PLAYING, NOT “LIKE PLAY”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

YOUR TURN TO ASK

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR 5 ICEBREAKERS

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask him to use one icebreaker from his card in his next real meeting and tell you how it went.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Small talk: Conversa leve antes do assunto principal (fim de semana, trânsito, esporte). Em vendas B2B é etapa real: cria confiança nos 2 primeiros minutos.
  • Icebreaker: “Quebra-gelo”: uma pergunta ou frase para começar a conversa sem constrangimento.
  • Rapport: Sintonia com o cliente. Vendedores falam em “build rapport” = criar conexão antes de vender.
  • CEO: Chief Executive Officer: o presidente/diretor-geral da empresa, a pessoa mais alta na hierarquia.
  • Shall we start?: Fórmula educada para encerrar o small talk e abrir a reunião (“vamos começar?”).

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I like play tennis (I like playing tennis), Was great. (It was great.), Did you went…? (Did you go…?), What you did? (What did you do?). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
