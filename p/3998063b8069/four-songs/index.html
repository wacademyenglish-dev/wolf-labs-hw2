Four Songs · The Record Player

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: English has three small shapes for what music does: it reminds me of + a noun, it sounds like + a noun (or sounds + an adjective), and I feel like + -ing.

The grammar: It reminds me of school. It sounds like a party. It sounds sad. I feel like dancing.

Short class (about 58 min): I The First Song · II Four Voices · III The Note · IV Six Tracks · V Sounds, or Sounds Like? · VI Remember or Remind · VIII Your Call, DJ · IX Your Soundtrack · X Side B. Skip if you’re short on time: VII (marked EXTRA on your screen). On the practice pages the student speaks first and taps on their own screen. Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “Name a song that reminds you of someone. Who?”

English has three small shapes for what music does: it reminds me of + a noun, it sounds like + a noun (or sounds + an adjective), and I feel like + -ing. The screen is a record player: in IV, every two right sentences bring the needle closer, and on the sixth the record spins. At the end the student builds the soundtrack of their life in four tracks. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

reminds me of (of never leaves); remember is what you do, remind is what the song does; sounds like + noun, sounds + adjective; feel like + -ing or a noun. The mistakes: it reminds me my father, it remembers me, it sounds like good, I feel like dance.

THE AUDIO

Seven real-sounding stories (four voices, two more people and a lab result), with pause and 0.75×; the words appear after listening. The voices sometimes say it makes me; that’s fine, this hour doesn’t ask for it.

THE PAGES

Four Voices (II): tap a voice and play it. After listening, two questions about the meaning (when, what, who, why), with three different answers to tap. The second answer shows the model sentence: the student says it out loud (It reminds her of the kitchen.). The voices first, the rule after.

The Note (III): a note a daughter left inside her father’s old record. The student reads it out loud and taps the words about songs and feelings (reminds me of, remember, sounds like, sounds, feel like calling, feel like). Every word can be tapped; wrong taps go red. Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line. The rule then stays in the corner of IV and VI.

Six Tracks (IV): six clues, each with the verb to use (Slow piano, a soft voice · sound). The student says the sentence, then taps Check: the model sentence appears and the needle comes down. Under it, one question to ask you.

Sounds, or Sounds Like? (V): six lines. The student reads each one out loud and taps Right or Wrong; if it’s wrong, they say it right (the screen shows the fix).

Remember or Remind (VI): five questions to build from the words in brackets (what song · remind you · your childhood). The student says the question, taps Check, then asks you. Answer for real, and ask back.

YOUR CALL, DJ (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before their own soundtrack, your student makes three real music choices: the first song at a friend’s wedding, who chooses the music on a long road trip, a concert or a birthday party. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Push for reminds me of, sounds (like) and feel like + -ing. Recast quietly; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: II Four Voices, VII The Lab (ask about their parents’ songs).
  • Out loud: I The First Song (don’t correct; write their lines down), VIII Your Call, DJ, IX Your Soundtrack (no longer extra).
  • Focus: III The Note, IV Six Tracks, V Sounds, or Sounds Like?, VI Remember or Remind.
  • Write: X, their four tracks, with the red wavy line for a missing of, remembers me, sounds like + adjective and feel like + base verb. It goes to their My notes.
