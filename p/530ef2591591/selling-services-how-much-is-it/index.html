How Much Is It? · Selling Services · Lesson 8

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 8 (big numbers · percent · how much / how many). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: The proposal summary for Serra Steel (fictional): cost today, monthly fee, savings. Daniel Price asks “How much is it?” and asks for a discount. The student chooses the right words in six proposal lines and presents his own three numbers.

About 56 min: I · Your Week (6) · II · The Proposal Summary (8) · III · “So, How Much Is It?” (8) · IV · Thousand, Not “Thousands” (10) · V · Numbers in the Proposal (7) · VI · You Choose (5) · VII · Your Three Numbers (9) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “How many e-mails do you get in a day? How much is a coffee near your office?”

Four Brazilian traps with numbers: the plural s (“two thousands”, “twenty percents”), How much × How many (“how much employees”), and the decimal point (Brazil writes 1,2 milhão and 4.000; English writes 1.2 million and 4,000). Have the student SAY every number out loud: 1,200 = one thousand two hundred (or twelve hundred), 1.2 million = one point two million. “Percent” is one word in American English. Reais is fine in English: “seventy-five thousand reais”.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

four thousand · one point two million · How much is it? How many people …? · You save … That’s … percent. · The monthly fee is …

YOUR WEEK

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE PROPOSAL SUMMARY

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

“SO, HOW MUCH IS IT?”

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

THOUSAND, NOT “THOUSANDS”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

NUMBERS IN THE PROPOSAL

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR THREE NUMBERS

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. If the student has a real proposal, bring the three numbers (approximate) and practice saying them.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Savings: Economia (o valor que o cliente deixa de gastar). Em vendas de BPO é o número mais forte da proposta.
  • Project fee (one time) × monthly fee: Valor de implantação, pago uma vez (setup) × mensalidade recorrente do serviço.
  • Paycheck: Contracheque/holerite. “3 percent of paychecks” = 3% dos holerites com erro.
  • Business case: A conta que justifica a compra: custo hoje × custo com o fornecedor × economia.
  • Decimal point × thousands comma: Em inglês: 1.2 = um vírgula dois (ponto decimal) e 4,000 = quatro mil (vírgula de milhar). O contrário do português; erro frequente em proposta.
  • Discount: Desconto. “Can you do a hundred thousand?” = pedido de desconto típico no fechamento.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

two thousands employees (two thousand employees), How much employees? (How many employees?), twenty percents (twenty percent), R$ 1,2 million (R$ 1.2 million). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
