Review: Ready for the Client · Selling Services · Lesson 29

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 29 (review 16–28). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: A week after the meeting, Leo calls Karen Wells, CFO of Lumen Logistics (fictional): small talk, the reason for the call, a price objection, what is included, who signs, the next step. Then the escape room “Close the Deal”: twelve squares, one challenge from each lesson 16–28. The student fixes a follow-up e-mail and writes his own.

About 59 min: I · Your Two Weeks (5) · II · The Follow-Up Call (8) · III · Thirteen Lessons, Four Traps (7) · IV · Close the Deal (15) · V · Fix the Follow-Up (7) · VI · Your Follow-Up E-mail (8) · VII · You Choose (6) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “How was your weekend? What do you love doing after work? Have you ever had a really difficult client?”

Review lesson of units 16–28. The four traps that came back most: love + -ing (“I love sail”), a decision with I’ll (“OK, I send it”), “Have you ever…?” (“You already worked…?”) and it doesn’t include (“it don’t include”). In IV the die follows a fixed sequence; the student does about seven squares in one trip. Any good answer counts, but ask for ONE more detail on each square. In V ask “which lesson is this from?” after each fix.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

How was your weekend? I love …ing. · This is … I’m calling about … · I understand. But … so … · It includes … I’ll send … Would you like …?

YOUR TWO WEEKS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE FOLLOW-UP CALL

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

THIRTEEN LESSONS, FOUR TRAPS

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

CLOSE THE DEAL

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

FIX THE FOLLOW-UP

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

YOUR FOLLOW-UP E-MAIL

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Tell him the next lesson is the final project: ask him to bring one real client (company type, size, system) to use in it.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Follow-up: Retorno/acompanhamento depois de uma reunião ou proposta (ligação ou e-mail) para manter o negócio andando.
  • Calendar invite: Convite de reunião pelo Outlook/Google Calendar: “I’ll send an invite.”
  • Voicemail: Caixa postal: recado gravado quando o cliente não atende.
  • Gatekeeper: Quem “guarda a porta”: assistente ou recepção que filtra as ligações para o diretor.
  • As we discussed: Expressão padrão de e-mail de follow-up: “conforme conversamos”.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I love sail (I love sailing), OK, I send it today (OK, I’ll send it today), You already worked with SAP? (Have you ever worked with SAP?), It don’t include travel (It doesn’t include travel). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
