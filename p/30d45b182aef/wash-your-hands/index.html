Wash Your Hands, Kevin · The Office · The Memo Wall

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A paper company, a terrible boss, and a lot of pranks.

The grammar: Do This, Don’t Do That: An order starts with the verb. Don’t + verb. Let’s + verb.

The class (about 55 min): I The Office · III The Clip · IV Your Order · V Scene by Scene · VI Do This, Don’t Do That · VII The Memo Wall · VIII The Rules of Your House · IX One Minute. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Jim and Dwight now. In the clip, Dwight’s desk is not in its place. What does Jim say to him? Guess.”

The Office (2005–2013) is the American comedy that looks like a documentary, set in a small office of a paper company, Dunder Mifflin, in Scranton. Jim loves pranks, and his favorite person to prank is Dwight. In this clip, Dwight’s desk is in the men’s bathroom. The grammar: imperatives: the verb first (Give me my desk! Calm down.), don’t + verb, let’s + verb, please, and be with adjectives (Be quiet. Don’t be late.).

A note for the teacher: the five frames follow the clip’s lines (read on 23/09), and each moment is told in our own words. A1: no portraits of the actors.

Warm-up and Dwight’s Phone (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). A1: short answers are fine. 2 · Dwight’s Phone: the screen becomes Dwight’s phone on the morning his desk disappears. Messages arrive one at a time, full of orders (Don’t touch…, Come here…, Let’s…); the student reads each one out loud and says what is happening. At the end they answer the last message with an order.

The words (II): desk, weird, funny, warmer and discount, before the video, in match the columns and mixed letters. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, One Minute): the student picks a topic (the rules of their house, how to make their favorite food, advice for a new person at work or school) and talks for one minute with do, don’t, let’s and please. Then the teacher. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an office desk inside a small bathroom at night. I: the office (the show, the company, the prank). III: the clip in three steps. Before: four cards that turn over (Dwight says… Give me my desk!); the student reads each order out loud. Watch: two short tasks, each line with a ▶ time (tap it and the video jumps there): Which word did you hear? (desk / disk, calm / come…) and One word is wrong (Where is your desk?). After: Who said it? and True or lie? with ▶ times. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Do This, Don’t Do That: six cards (Wash your hands. Don’t touch my desk. Let’s have a meeting!…); the student taps a card and then a box: Do it, Don’t do it, or You and me. When all six are in the right box, the rule board appears and stays in the corner. VI, The Memo Wall: the office wall with six places for memos. A card gives who speaks and the idea (“Dwight to Jim · give me my desk”); the student says the order out loud and taps Check. The memo prints in typewriter letters and is taped to the wall. VII, The Rules of Your House: six places (kitchen, bedroom door, car, classroom, cinema, this class); the student taps a rule and then says two more. VIII: one minute.

THE COMMON ERRORS

You give me my desk!, Gives me…, To calm down, Please to wash, Please washing, Not touch, No move, Don’t touching, Don’t moves, Let’s to / Let’s we, Are quiet.

YOUR ORDER (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the clip: Dwight’s desk is in the bathroom. And if it is your desk? Three short choices, one of three each: what you say about your desk, what you say to a nervous friend at work, one rule for your office. The options and the almost-ready sentences keep the imperative, Don’t + verb and Let’s + verb. There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Office, IV Your Order, VII The Memo Wall and VIII The Rules of Your House.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Clip and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Do This, Don’t Do That.
  • Write: IX One Minute.
