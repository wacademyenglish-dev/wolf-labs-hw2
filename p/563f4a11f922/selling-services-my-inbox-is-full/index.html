My Inbox Is Full · Selling Services · Lesson 23

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 23 (need to · have to · don’t have to). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Monday, 8 a.m.: Leo has forty e-mails, and Sara helps him choose what is urgent. The student puts six e-mails in order, debates phone × e-mail, reads chapter 5 of the Easy Reader (Nina writes: “Leo, we have to talk”), fixes Leo’s reply and writes two real replies.

About 57 min: I · Your Inbox (5) · II · “Forty E-mails, Sara!” (7) · III · Need To, Not “Need Send” (8) · IV · Six E-mails in Order (7) · V · The Debate: Phone or E-mail? (6) · VI · Easy Reader: We Have to Talk (7) · VII · Fix the Reply (6) · VIII · Your Two Replies (8) · IX · I Can (3).

Open with: “How many e-mails do you get every day? What do you have to do first on Monday?”

Brazilians translate “tenho que” as “I have that”, forget the “to” in “need to” (“I need send”) and say “she have to”. The big meaning trap: “don’t have to” = it isn’t necessary (não precisa). In IV the order is clear from the times (before 10, at 11, by 6 p.m., this week, Friday, optional): make him say the whole plan out loud. In V you take the OTHER side every time, so he has to defend his choice with “because”.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I have to … / I need to … · She has to … / He needs to … · You don’t have to … It can wait. · Do I have to …?

YOUR INBOX

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

“FORTY E-MAILS, SARA!”

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

NEED TO, NOT “NEED SEND”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

SIX E-MAILS IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

THE DEBATE: PHONE OR E-MAIL?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

EASY READER: WE HAVE TO TALK

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

FIX THE REPLY

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

YOUR TWO REPLIES

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask for two real e-mails from his inbox this week and write the replies with him.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Inbox · subject line: Caixa de entrada · assunto do e-mail. “Subject: we have to talk” = assunto do e-mail.
  • Case study: Estudo de caso: documento curto que conta o resultado de um cliente real (problema → solução → número). Vendedor manda para convencer.
  • Approve a discount: Aprovar um desconto: na maioria das empresas, o vendedor não pode dar desconto sozinho; o gerente ou a diretoria aprova.
  • Webinar: Seminário online (web + seminar), geralmente opcional, com palestra e perguntas.
  • Monthly report: Relatório mensal de vendas/resultado que o vendedor manda para a área financeira ou para o gerente.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I need send it (I need to send it), I have that call him (I have to call him), She have to approve (She has to approve), You have not to come (You don’t have to come). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
