What This Test Will Show · English for Healthcare · Lesson 6 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: explaining tests, results and procedures: agenda, the result in context (normal range), simple words, steps in order (first, then, after that, finally), the future with will / won’t, and checking understanding.

The story: A University of Nottingham video: a doctor explains a blood test result (anemia) and builds a diet plan with the patient. Then a dental filling explained in order, and the student’s own explanation.

About 45 min: I · Explaining a Result (12) · II · Steps in Order (8) · III · Unpopular Opinions (7) · IV · Your Explanation (14) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What test or procedure do you explain most often at work?” That will be the student’s final task today.

The video shows only 0:47–5:30. Point out the techniques: agenda, normal range, reassurance (common and treatable), simple words, and involving the patient (asking about diet and preferences).

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I’m going to explain… · This test will show… · First, … Then… After that… Finally… · You’ll feel… / you won’t feel… · The results will be ready… · Does that make sense so far?

EXPLAINING A RESULT

University of Nottingham, “Clinical communication skills - explanation skills”, only 0:47–5:30 (captions checked on 15/10/2026). British English.

STEPS IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

YOUR EXPLANATION

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write the explanation of their most common procedure: it becomes a script they can reuse.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

The exam will show (test), You will feel nothing pain (won’t feel any pain), Did you understand? (Does that make sense?). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

UNPOPULAR OPINIONS (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After putting the filling explanation in order, the student takes a position on HOW to explain. Four opinions: patients only need to trust you; results should reach the phone before the explanation; a drawing beats five minutes of talking; no questions means a good explanation. For each one the student taps a number from 1 to 5 and defends it; you take the opposite number and argue back. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (will / won’t, first … then …, Does that make sense so far?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
