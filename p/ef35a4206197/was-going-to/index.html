The Life I Was Going to Have · Dark Matter · The Other Lives

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: One walk home, and a box full of other lives.

The grammar: Was Going To: He was going to go home. He was about to open the door.

The class (about 56 min): I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie · II The Words · III The Trailer · IV Unpopular Opinions · V Line by Line · VI Was Going To · VII The Other Lives · VIII Your Other Lives · IX Questions. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Jason now. In the trailer, what was he about to do when everything changed?”

Dark Matter (Apple TV, 2024), from Blake Crouch’s novel, with Joel Edgerton as Jason Dessen, a science teacher in Chicago with a wife, Daniela, and a son, Charlie. One night he walks home and never gets there: he wakes up in a world where he never married Daniela and is a famous scientist. He built a box, and behind every door is a life he could have lived. The grammar: the future seen from the past: was going to (the plan), was about to (the moment just before), was supposed to (the arrangement), would (what was going to happen, told), and the plan that didn’t happen: was going to…, but…

A note for the teacher: suspense, no blood. The times come from the trailer’s captions (07/10), told in our own words. The WOLFFY lesson Dead Memories used doors; this one uses windows.

Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Two Truths and a Lie: three rounds about Jason (his life, the night at the bar, the box). The student reads the three sentences, says which one is the lie and why, then taps. In the last round, they say two truths and one lie about plans they had, and you guess.

The words (II): believe, consciousness, crazy, version and scared, before the video, in hear it in the video and mystery meaning. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, Questions): five questions (what you were going to study, where you were supposed to be last Saturday, what you were about to do when your phone rang, whether you thought you would live here, what you were going to do this weekend). Then the student asks the teacher two. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an endless corridor of doors. I: Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a wheel (spin: your other life) and the student says “I was going to be …, but …”. Watch: Which word did you hear? and One word is wrong, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: three taps (what was Jason going to do? who took his life? what does he want?), then the student says it with was going to. V: five frames on the film strip. VI, Was Going To: six sentences to sort into three boxes: the plan (was going to), just before (was about to), seen from then (would). The student taps a sentence, then a box, and reads each box out loud. VII, The Other Lives: a corridor of six dark windows. The student matches each plan (“He was going to become a famous scientist”) to what really happened (“In one world, he did”); every right pair lights a window in warm amber with a small scene of that life (the teacher at home, the scientist, the painter, the door, the empty house, home again). The rule stays in the corner. Then the student says each pair: “He was going to …, but …”. VIII, Your Other Lives: three people ask (a friend, a coworker, you ten years ago). The student picks how to answer and the talk appears: read it out loud, you are the person. IX: questions.

THE COMMON ERRORS

was going go, going to went, he were going, Daniela going to be (no be), was about open, about to opened, they supposed to, they was supposed, he knew he will, would found, What he was going to do?

UNPOPULAR OPINIONS (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer, four statements about the lives Jason was going to have, and then about your student’s own plans. They tap a number from 1 to 5 and defend it; you take the opposite number. Wait five seconds in silence before they speak, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. There is no right answer. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences with was going to / was about to themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie, IV Unpopular Opinions, VII The Other Lives, VIII Your Other Lives and IX Questions.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Was Going To.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
