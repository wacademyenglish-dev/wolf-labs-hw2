Burnt Toast · Three Tubes

Tuesday, 11:40 PM: the fire alarm wakes the whole building, and the manager wants everybody’s night, hour by hour. The screen is a machine with three glass tubes, /t/, /d/ and /ɪd/, and a vault. A regular verb drops into a tube by its sound; an irregular goes into the vault. The student builds an alibi, reports the neighbors at the building meeting, and at the end finds out: nobody pressed anything. Somebody burned the toast. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

The past of regular verbs: worked /t/, played /d/, wanted /ɪd/. Only -ted / -ded add a beat. The common irregulars: went, took, bought, saw, ate, left, made, fell. A night in order: First, Then, After that, Finally. Negatives and questions in the past are another hour; here the student only answers.

BEST NEIGHBOUR (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

The morning after the alarm, before the toast is found: your student ranks the three neighbours (Mr. Costa watched TV, Ms. Reed made toast, the Parks played cards). They tap 1, 2, 3 and say why in the past (They played cards and went to bed). There is no right order. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). At the end, open Show my teacher’s order and agree on one order together. Recast mistakes quietly (maked → made); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Week, IV Twelve, Cold, V Build Your Alibi, VII The Building Meeting, VIII Best Neighbour (rank the three, then show your order).
  • Listen: III Three Tubes. The student hears the word first, claps the beats, and only then sees it.
  • Focus: II Tubes or Vault?, VI First, Then, After That, IX So Who Did It?
  • Write: X Your Real Night, with the red wavy line for goed, taked, eated, a present verb with last night, stoped, and didn’t went. It goes to their My notes.

THE EAR, NOT THE EYE

The letters -ED look the same in all three tubes, so the word stays hidden until the audio ends. Clap together: worked is one clap, like work; wanted is two. Every audio button plays and pauses, and 0.75× makes it slower.

THE MEETING

You are the manager. Knock on each door; the student reports the neighbor in order (First, Mr. Costa watched TV. Then he fell asleep…). At Ms. Reed’s door, ask only “Toast? At what time?”. Let the student guess the ending before scene IX opens the photo.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

Everything the student taps moves on both screens. You judge the speech. G opens this guide.
