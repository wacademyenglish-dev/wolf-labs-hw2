I’m Afraid the Results… · English for Healthcare · Lesson 9 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: breaking difficult news with SPIKES (Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotions, Strategy & Summary): a warning shot, the key fact in plain words, naming emotions, silence, a plan, open questions.

The story: A Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh video: a doctor tells a son that his mother had a complication after thyroid surgery. Then SPIKES in order, clear vs vague language, and the student’s own conversation (fictional cases by profession).

About 43 min: I · Breaking Bad News (9) · II · The SPIKES Route (8) · III · Clear and Kind (8) · IV · Your Conversation (14) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What is the hardest news you have had to give at work?” Let the student share only if they want.

This lesson can touch personal experiences. Go slowly; the student chooses the case. In the role-play, react like a real patient (silence, a question, some emotion) so the student practices responding, not just speaking.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Shall we go somewhere quieter? · Can you tell me what you already know? · I’m afraid… / Unfortunately… · I can see this is hard to hear. · Here’s what we’ll do next. · What questions do you have?

BREAKING BAD NEWS

Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh, “Breaking Bad News” (3 min, captions checked on 15/10/2026). British English.

THE SPIKES ROUTE

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

CLEAR AND KIND

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

YOUR CONVERSATION

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Remind the student: in real life, follow the protocols of their institution for difficult news.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

giving the news without a warning, hiding behind jargon, Don’t worry, it will be fine., Any questions? (closed). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.
