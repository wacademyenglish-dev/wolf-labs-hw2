Leave the Room · The Two Doors

One night, and a woman called Nadia writing to a machine at three in the morning after a fight with her sister. The grammar is the mechanic: the door your student takes happens (warm light), and the door they didn’t take can only be said with WOULD (cold glass). If + a past form, would + the plain verb. The past form doesn’t mean finished; it means not real. Students say If I would have… and If I was… I will….

THE HOUR IS ABOUT THE MACHINE

Every case belongs to Nadia, an invented person. Scene I and scene XII are the only places your student speaks as themselves, and both are light.

THE DOORS ARE THE GRAMMAR

Scene VI: before every tap, your student predicts (“If I sent that one, she would…”). After the tap, the blue door shows the other world; make them read it out loud. There is no right door: one opens the conversation, one closes it.

THE THREE RECORDINGS

The programmer (1966), a therapist, a psychologist. Each plays with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens only after listening.

IF YOU ARE BEHIND

Cut scene III and two items from V. Don’t cut II, VI or XI.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

If she would ask…, If I was you, I will…, I would to go, I would asked, If I had time, I would have read.

AT 3 A.M. (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VII is now a ranking. Like Nadia, your student can’t sleep after a fight with a friend: they put four options in order (a brother or sister, another friend, an AI app, nobody) and explain the top and the last with IF + past and WOULD. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, use the PUSH line (only you see it), then show your own order and agree on one top 3 together. Recast mistakes quietly (“If I would call her” → “If I called her”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
