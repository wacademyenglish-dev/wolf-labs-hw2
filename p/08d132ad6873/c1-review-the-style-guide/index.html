The Style Guide · C1 Unit 9 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 9 (Register and style). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Your student starts as an editor at Northwind Books (invented). First they read the house style guide (six rules), then they switch sentences between registers and decide which voice fits each situation.

About 44 min: I · Bingo (8) · II · The Style Guide (10) · III · The Editors’ Meeting (7) · IV · Switch the Voice (8) · V · Which Voice? (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Nine squares. Pick one, switch the voice, and make a line.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

formal × friendly · spoken × written: gonna / going to · phrasal × formal verbs · contract language in plain English · understatement, euphemism, irony · opinion with temperature

BINGO

A 3×3 card of quick tasks. The student picks a square and does it out loud; tap Done. Three in a line (across, down or diagonal) is a BINGO. Keep going for a second line if there is time.

THE STYLE GUIDE

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

SWITCH THE VOICE

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

WHICH VOICE?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Dear… Cheers (mixed), gonna in a client email, I will ascertain (to a friend), shall = will (in a contract), the food was terrible (in a public review), I couldn’t care more. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE EDITORS’ MEETING (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after reading the style guide, your student joins the editors’ meeting and votes on three changes to it (yes, no, or propose a change): emojis to young authors, plain-English contracts, and blunt words in reviews. You chair the meeting. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. Listen for the Unit 9 register (I’m all for, I couldn’t care less, subject to, not exactly), recast quietly while they talk, and at the end ask for one or two self-corrections.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
