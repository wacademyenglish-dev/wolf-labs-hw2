Why We Are Here · Silo · Down the Stairs

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Ten thousand people underground, and one question nobody can ask.

The grammar: The Question Inside: We don’t know why we are here. I wonder if it is safe.

The class (about 51 min): I Warm-up and The Silo Rules · III The Trailer · V The Hot Seat · VI The Question Inside · VII Down the Stairs · VIII What You Wonder · IX Questions. II The Words and IV Line by Line are short listening scenes. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the silo now. In the trailer, what does Juliette want to know? Guess.”

Silo (Apple TV, 2023), from Hugh Howey’s books, with Rebecca Ferguson as Juliette, an engineer. Ten thousand people live in a giant underground tower, 144 floors deep; a screen at the top shows a grey, dead world outside. Nobody knows who built the silo or why they are there, and there is one rule: never say you want to go outside, or you will be sent out. The grammar: indirect questions: Do you know / Can you tell me / I wonder / I don’t know + question word + subject + verb (why we are here, who built it), if / whether for yes / no, no do / does / did inside, and I wonder… ends with a full stop.

A note for the teacher: the trailer is tense but not violent. The times follow its captions (23/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and The Silo Rules (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Silo Rules: four rules of the silo appear one at a time (144 floors and no elevator, the screen is the only window, old objects are illegal, never say you want to go outside). For each, the student taps Yes / No way / Only if… and says why, with an indirect question when they can (I wonder if…, I’d want to know why…). At the end, they invent a fifth rule.

The words (II): underground, sanctuary, theory, truth and mysteries, before the video, in predict the story and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, Questions): five questions with a question inside (when your grandparents were born, what you did last Sunday, why the sky is blue, if there is life on other planets, what you will do next year). Then the student asks the teacher two, the polite way. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover looks down the silo’s giant spiral staircase. I: Warm-up and The Silo Rules. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: three clues, one by one, and the student guesses what it is (tap “I know it!”). Watch: One word is wrong, three lines from the trailer, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment; the student says the right word. After: True or lie, four sentences about the trailer, each with a ▶ time; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Hot Seat: the student is Juliette, the teacher is the sheriff (with a secret limit); four questions, answered with a question inside. VI, The Question Inside: six direct questions (“Who built the silo?”) to match with the polite version (“Do you know who built the silo?”). The student sees the pattern before it is explained, then says both versions out loud. VII, Down the Stairs: a cut through the concrete silo with the staircase zigzagging down through six landings, from Floor 1 (the screen) to Floor 144 (Mechanical). The student builds each question out loud and taps Check: the right question appears, and the lantern goes one landing down and lights the steps in amber. The rule stays in the corner. At the bottom, a door nobody opened. VIII, What You Wonder: three people in the silo talk to you (a stranger, the sheriff, a friend). The student picks how to answer and the talk appears: read it out loud. IX: questions.

THE COMMON ERRORS

why are we here (inside: why we are), who did build, where do the stairs go / where go the stairs, what did Juliette find / what found Juliette, I wonder is it safe (if it is), if is it, does the door open (if the door opens), I wonder what is down there?

THE HOT SEAT (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student is Juliette, the engineer who wants to know why they are all underground. You are the sheriff. Your secret limit is on your screen only: you want the truth too, and if she asks you one good question, you tell her one secret (invent it). Ask the four questions on the screen; the student answers in the role with a question inside (I don’t know why…, I wonder if…, Can you tell me who…?) and must never say the forbidden sentence: “I want to go outside.” There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence before they reply, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to raise the pressure. Recast quietly while they talk (I don’t know why are we here, I wonder is it safe, do you know where did he go); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Silo Rules, V The Hot Seat, VII Down the Stairs, VIII What You Wonder and IX Questions.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Question Inside.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
