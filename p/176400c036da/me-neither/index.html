Me Neither · Two Mirrors

Portuguese has one word for agreeing, and adds a no for negatives. English splits it: a YES echoes with too / So do I / Me too; a NO echoes with either / Neither do I / Me neither. So the student says Me too to a negative, or I don’t like it too. Marina moves on Saturday and can’t choose between two apartments. The screen has two mirrors: tap the wrong one and it cracks.

THE NOT DECIDES

Not who is speaking, not the verb: the NOT in her sentence. And the little verb comes from her sentence too: I’m tired → So am I, I can’t sleep → Neither can I. Neither already has the NOT: never Neither don’t I.

THE AUDIO

Six pairs (Marina and a friend), checked against the recordings. The student listens first; the transcript opens after.

FIVE IN COMMON

Scene VII: say each card true for you, and say no to at least two things, so the student gets to say Neither do I. Tap SAME or DIFFERENT; five SAMEs win.

THE HOT SEAT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene IV is now a short hot seat: you are Marina looking for a flatmate, and your student answers five of your sentences with an echo (So am I, Neither can I, Neither did I) or a short disagreement (I’m not, I can). Say each line yourself; the example answers open with Show answer. Your secret (only you see it): you hate noise after 10 PM, so use it in the PUSH. Then you two choose a flat together. Wait five seconds in silence before they answer; recast the little verb quietly; at the end, ask them to fix one or two echoes themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON
