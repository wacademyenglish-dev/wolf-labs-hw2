It Was Written · Dune: Part Three · The Visions

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A boy who was to save the desert, and an emperor who would not stop.

The grammar: It Was Written: He was to lead them. He was supposed to stay.

The class (about 58 min): I The Story · II The Words · III The Trailer · IV Promises and Prophecies · V Line by Line · VI It Was Written · VII The Visions · VIII Your Plans · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Paul now. In the trailer, what was he supposed to do, and what did he do instead?”

Dune: Part Three (2026), Denis Villeneuve, with Timothée Chalamet and Zendaya. Paul came to the desert as a young duke; the Fremen believed he was the one their prophecy talked about, and he loved Chani. Years later he is emperor, his war has destroyed thousands of worlds, and Chani reminds him he promised he would never take power in his own name. The grammar: the future seen from the past: was to / was never to (destiny), was supposed to (the plan that failed), would (the promise, told), was going to, was about to, and the passive of the prophecy, it was written that…

A note for the teacher: the lesson plays the whole trailer (2:50); it has a knife fight and battles, so watch it once before. The lesson never tells how this film ends. Lines checked against the official subtitles (25/09), told in our own words.

The world (I): The map of arrakis. Five stops, in order: The Book, Arrakis, The Prophecy, Chani and The Emperor. The student reads each page out loud and answers one question about their own life; Show answer gives a model. You don’t need to know the story: the map tells it, and the questions before the video come from it.

The words (II): trusted, promised, power, convinced and home, before the video, in match the columns and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is Paul, emperor. The teacher asks five questions (what you were supposed to be, what you promised Chani, was it written, what you were about to do, were you ever to go home). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is dunes at night under a big moon. I: the story, stop by stop on the map. III: the trailer (the whole trailer, no cuts) in three steps. Before: three cards to open and talk about (destiny, a promise, the plan). Watch: True or lie? and One word is wrong, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: two taps (was his story written? did he keep his promise?) and a slider (are some things written before we are born?); the student says why each time. V: five frames on the film strip. VI, Chani’s letter: the student reads it out loud, taps the verbs of destiny, plans and promises (was written, were to lead, were supposed to, would, was about to, were going to), then finishes the rule. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Visions: the dune crest under two moons, with six signs hidden in the sand. The student matches each plan (what was to be) with what happened (what was); every right pair opens a sign and writes the pair in the book below. Then they say each pair out loud: He was to lead them, and he led them. VIII, Your Plans: three friends talk about plans and promises (a trip that didn’t happen, a call that didn’t happen, destiny). The student picks how to answer and the talk appears: read it out loud, you are the friend. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

was to led, was lead (no to), promised he will, would took, Chani supposed to (no be), was suppose to, were go to, they was going, was about begin, about to began, It wrote that… (passive: it was written), never was to, What he was supposed to do?

PROMISES AND PROPHECIES (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer, three hard choices: what Chani should do about Paul’s broken promise, who is responsible for what Paul became, and then the same choice in your student’s life (a friend who broke a promise). Let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. There is no right answer. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences with was to / was supposed to / would themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Story, IV Promises and Prophecies, VII The Visions, VIII Your Plans and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI It Was Written.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
