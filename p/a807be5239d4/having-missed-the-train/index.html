Having Missed the Train · The 11:52 Night Train

A thriller in short, cinematic sentences. Nora Vane misses the 9:40 and takes the night train. In compartment 12 there is a brown briefcase, left alone. At 2:10 the lights go out for forty seconds, and when they come back the briefcase is gone. The grammar: a participle clause has no subject of its own; it borrows the subject after the comma. Having + past participle happened first; -ing is at the same time or the reason; not goes first; a past participle alone means something was done to the subject. On screen, a departure board with flipping letters, a ticket that gets punched at every shot, and the conductor’s report with every subject under a black bar.

SIX SHOTS

Scene II, before the rule. The student reads each shot like a movie script. Many will say After she missed: that’s fine out loud. The three options show the short version and the two classic mistakes.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

In Portuguese, a gerund clause can have its own subject: word for word, Arriving at the station, the rain started is fine there. In English that says the rain arrived at the station. And the Portuguese version of Having missed sounds formal, so students avoid Having missed. In English stories and reports it is normal.

THE CONDUCTOR’S REPORT

Scene IV is the pressure. Read each line as the inspector, a little suspicious. The student puts the real subject after the comma; the black bar lifts and a red stamp lands on that passenger. Ms. Ortiz ends with four. After the last line, ask: “So who took it? Prove it with two lines.”

NO JUMP SCARES

Suspense, not horror: rain, a dark carriage, a missing case. Nothing jumps, nobody gets hurt.

UNPOPULAR OPINIONS (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the inspector at dawn, your student rates four opinions about Nora’s night from 1 to 5 and defends the number with a participle clause; you take the opposite number. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The rule is practised again in the homework, so nothing is lost by skipping the old quick check.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
