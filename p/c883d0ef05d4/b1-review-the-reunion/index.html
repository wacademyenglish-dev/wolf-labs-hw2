The Reunion · B1 Unit 2 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 2 (How long and how it was). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Twenty years after school, the whole class meets again. Your student makes the “Where are they now?” slideshow. You are three old friends: Marina (pilot in Lisbon), Tom (baker who never left town) and Dani (yoga teacher on a boat).

About 41 min: I · The Invitation (8) · II · Where Are They Now? (15) · III · Dear Younger Me (7) · IV · Would You Rather? (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “The invitation has just arrived. Are you going?”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

present perfect continuous · for and since · used to × present simple · should have, could have · be used to, get used to

MEMORY

A memory game. The student opens two cards; when they match, the student makes a sentence with the pair (example under Show answer).

WHERE ARE THEY NOW?

You are each old friend. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student presents the friend to the class with has been …ing, used to and should have. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

DEAR YOUNGER ME

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I live here since 2010, I’m used to wake up, I should learned, I use to (for the past), I’m waiting for an hour (for I’ve been waiting), since two years. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE INVITATION (I) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

The lesson now opens with a decision: the reunion invitation has just arrived. Three situations (the message, the old fight, the school photo); your student chooses or says their own idea, then says why. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly (haven’t seen them since, used to be, should have said); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I · The Invitation, II · Where Are They Now? and IV · Would You Rather?.
  • Write: III · Dear Younger Me.
  • Focus: V · I Can.
