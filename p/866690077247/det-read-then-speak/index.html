Read, Then Speak · DET Prep · Lesson 9 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the Read, Then Speak question: a written topic with guide questions, about 20 seconds to prepare, then 30 to 90 seconds of speaking. The student learns a shape for any topic: answer → reason → example → wrap-up.

The story: No story: a model answer in pieces, six quick topics with the die, then three real topics with the clock.

About 42 min: I · The Story in Order (7) · II · The Die (8) · III · Computer or Person? (7) · IV · Three Topics (16) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What is your favorite season?” Let the student answer freely; then show how the four pieces make the same answer stronger.

The most common problem is a slow start (“Well… so… this topic is about…”). Train a first sentence that answers the question directly. The guide questions on the card are the map: one sentence or two for each.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

answer in the first sentence · The main reason is… · For example,… · That’s why… · On the other hand,… · would + verb for wishes

THE STORY IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

THE DIE

Tap Roll the die: the number chooses one of the six topics (no repeats until all six are done). The student does the challenge from memory.

THREE TOPICS

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Record the student (with permission) in Topic 1 and Topic 3: the difference in shape is easy to hear.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

starting with “this topic is about”, no example, stopping at 30 seconds, I would like learn, the reason is because. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

COMPUTER OR PERSON? (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the three topics with the real clock, a warm-up argument in the shape of the lesson. The student picks a side (a computer or a person should score speaking) and defends it for one minute: answer, reason, example, wrap-up. Then they tap Swap sides and defend the other one. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, and don’t give your opinion first. Use the PUSH line (150 from the computer, 120 from a person). Recast quietly while they talk (The main reason is…, For example…, That’s why…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I · The Story in Order and III · Computer or Person? and IV · Three Topics.
  • Focus: II · The Die and V · I Can.
