Safety First · Au Pair · Lesson 18 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: child safety rules and the modals to say them: must / have to (necessary), mustn’t (not allowed), don’t have to (not necessary). Water, car seats, medicines, safe sleep, stairs, strangers.

The story: An AuPairCare director explains how the agency supports au pairs (video). Then twelve safety rules to classify.

About 33 min: I · You Are Supported (6) · II · Must, Mustn’t, Don’t Have To (9) · III · Must or Mustn’t? (14) · IV · I Can (4).

Open with: “What is the most dangerous place in a house for a three-year-old?” (Bathroom, kitchen, stairs, pool.)

The program requires child safety instruction before placement (at least 8 hours, 4 about infants) and child development instruction. This lesson is NOT a first aid course: recommend a CPR / first aid class and remind the student to follow each family’s rules. The baby sleep rule taught here (on the back, in the crib) is the general safe-sleep advice; families may give more details.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

must / have to · mustn’t · don’t have to · out of reach · car seat · Do I have to…?

YOU ARE SUPPORTED

AuPairCare, “Is Being an Au Pair in the USA Safe in 2026? Here’s What You Need to Know” (1 min, captions checked on 15/10/2026). Short: play twice.

MUST, MUSTN’T, DON’T HAVE TO

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

MUST OR MUSTN’T?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Homework: write five questions about safety rules to ask the host family in the first week.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

must to, she musts, mustn’t = not necessary (wrong), Do I must…?. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

DAY ONE (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After “Must or mustn’t?”, the student ranks five questions to ask the family in the first hour: the address and emergency numbers, car seats, medicines and cleaning products, pool and bath rules, screens and sweets. There is no single right order; the student explains the top one and the last one with must / don’t have to. Then tap “Show my teacher’s order” (address, pool and bath, car seats, medicines, screens) and agree on one top 3 together. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
