The Eight Doors · The Octagon

Tonight your student takes a real self-assessment: a testing room with eight doors, one for each kind of intelligence. No number comes from a click. Your student gives gut numbers first; then each door is scored by what they prove out loud. The screen draws their shape on an octagon (solid: proved; dashed: guessed) and fills a one-page report. The language is how to talk about your abilities without boasting and without putting yourself down: good at, I’d say I’m quite…, although, haven’t … yet.

THE GUT NUMBERS COME BACK

Scene I: three questions, then a quick guess for each door (tap the step you heard), and one number for everything. Don’t comment. Scene VII draws the guesses as a dashed line; scene XI puts the two numbers side by side.

THREE WAYS OF SCORING

Doors 1–3: proof. Fill one bubble for each thing you hear (an example, a detail, a careful sentence); move the number only if your student says why; lock it. Doors 4–6: evidence, the true line sets the number. Doors 7–8: a scale, with a reason before every tap.

THE TWO RECORDINGS

Scene II: the idea (intelligence is not one thing, and it can change). Scene VIII: the other side (talents, not intelligences; the word may be wrong). Each plays with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens after listening. Nobody says the abilities are fake: the argument is about one word.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

good in music, intelligent in math, I didn’t try yet, I’m the best in everything, I’m not good in nothing. In scene IV there is a dashed button for “good in”.

THE ARENA (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the critic’s recording, your student picks a side: can practice make anybody good at anything, or does talent decide? There is no right answer. Let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then start the one-minute clock. Use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it personal, then tap Swap sides and they defend the other side. Recast quietly while they talk (good at, haven’t … yet); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Before the Test, IV–VI the doors, VII Your Shape, IX The Arena, X The Job Interview.
  • Listen: II The Idea, VIII The Other Side.
  • Focus: III How to Say It.
  • Write: XI, their report. It goes to their My notes. PRINT opens the printer.
