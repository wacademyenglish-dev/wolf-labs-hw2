Lights, Camera, Me! · Au Pair · Lesson 3 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the application video (about two minutes): what to show and say. Grammar: the present perfect for life experience (I have babysat, I have worked, Have you ever…?) versus the past simple with a time.

The story: A real applicant’s video (she applied to Australia with AIFS; the shape works for the USA too). Then the student climbs a ladder of experience questions and records a first take, live.

About 49 min: I · A Real Video (10) · II · I Have… or I …ed? (9) · III · The Ladder (8) · IV · Your First Take (12) · V · What Families Watch (6) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “What would you show a family in two minutes of video?” List three scenes.

The video is a real person’s application: be respectful when commenting on it. Encourage the student to record the real video at home later, in a quiet place with good light, after this first take in class.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I have + participle · Have you ever…? — Yes, I have. · past simple with a time · been · done · taught · taken · I have never… · smile and look at the camera

A REAL VIDEO

“My AIFS Aupair Application Video” (3 min, a real applicant; captions checked on 15/10/2026). She is German and applying to Australia: point out that the structure is the same for the USA.

I HAVE… OR I …ED?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THE LADDER

Six steps from easy to hard. A good answer: tap Go up. Not yet: Try again on the same step.

YOUR FIRST TAKE

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to record the real video this week and send it to you: next class, give feedback with the checklist.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I have work, I have traveled in 2022, I have teached, Did you ever…?. A2+: one correction per answer is enough.

WHAT FAMILIES WATCH (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the first take, the student ranks five things in an application video (a big smile in the first second, me with children, my home and family, my hobbies, perfect English) and explains the top one and the last one. Then open your order and agree on one top 3 together. Your order is only one opinion: children first, the smile second, perfect English last. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (families have seen…, I have never…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
