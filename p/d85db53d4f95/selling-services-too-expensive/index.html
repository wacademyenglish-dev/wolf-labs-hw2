It’s Too Expensive · Selling Services · Lesson 19

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 19 (answering objections: but · because · so). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Chapter 3 of the course story “Leo’s First Big Client”: Rita Gomes, CFO of Orla Beverages (fictional), says the price is too expensive; Leo answers with but, because, so… and then she shows a proposal 30% cheaper. In the dialogue, Daniel Price, of Serra Steel (fictional), gives three objections. The student opens six objection cards (you are the client), debates “Is a discount a good idea?” with you, and writes three answers for his real clients.

About 59 min: I · Your Clients (5) · II · Easy Reader: The Price (8) · III · Three Objections (8) · IV · But, Because, So (9) · V · Six Objection Cards (12) · VI · Mini-Debate: Is a Discount a Good Idea? (6) · VII · Your 3 Answers (8) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “What do your clients say about your price? Do you like negotiating?”

Objection handling with three connectors: but (a new side), because (the reason), so (the result), plus “too expensive”. Brazilian traps: “too much expensive”, “but is good” (no subject), “then” for “então” (result = so), “why” for “porque” in answers. In V you read each objection like a real client, a little tired; give a green door only with a connector used right. In VI (mini-debate) always take the OTHER side with one reason; he answers with “I don’t agree because…”.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I understand, but … · … because … · …, so … · It’s too expensive. (no “much”)

YOUR CLIENTS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

EASY READER: THE PRICE

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

THREE OBJECTIONS

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

BUT, BECAUSE, SO

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

SIX OBJECTION CARDS

Six doors, one per topic of the unit. The student picks any door and does the task from memory. Tap Got it or Review; amber doors come back as a short list “to review”.

MINI-DEBATE: IS A DISCOUNT A GOOD IDEA?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR 3 ANSWERS

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask him to bring next class one real objection he heard this week and the answer he gave.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Objection: Objeção: a resistência do cliente (“está caro”, “não temos tempo”). “Objection handling” = técnica de responder objeções.
  • Value · value proposition: Valor percebido / proposta de valor: o que o cliente ganha além do preço (tempo, segurança, menos erros).
  • Margin: Margem de lucro. Dar desconto reduz a margem; por isso vendedor prefere “adicionar valor”.
  • Competitor: Concorrente. “A competitor is 30% cheaper” é a objeção clássica.
  • Budget: Orçamento aprovado para gastar. “No budget this year” = não há verba este ano.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

too much expensive (too expensive), but is a good price (but it’s a good price), it’s fast, then you save (so you save), it’s cheap why it’s automatic (because). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
