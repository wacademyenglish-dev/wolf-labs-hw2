The Story Arc · C1 Unit 10 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on story signposts for talks: It all started when…, At first…, Things got worse when…, That’s when…, It turned out that…, Fast forward to…, In the end…, Looking back…, The lesson I learned was…, with narrative tenses (past simple, past continuous, past perfect continuous).

The story: A baker (invented) tells, in minute two of her talk, how her grandmother’s recipe, a supermarket, and an old man with a food blog changed her life.

About 48 min: I · A Story You Remember (6) · II · The Baker’s Story (10) · III · The Signposts (9) · IV · The Jury (8) · V · The Story in Order (7) · VI · Memory (8).

Open with: “What’s a story from your life that you always tell at parties?”

Second lesson of the talk unit. Every good talk has one story. Read the baker’s story slowly, pausing at each signpost. In the order game, the story is about a first job; at the end, ask the student to tell one of THEIR stories with the same arc: it will go into their final talk.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

It all started when… / At first,… · Things got worse when… · That’s when… · It turned out that… · Fast forward to today: … · Looking back, … / The lesson I learned was…

A STORY YOU REMEMBER

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE BAKER’S STORY

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

THE SIGNPOSTS

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THE STORY IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

MEMORY

A memory game. The student opens two cards; when they match, the student makes a sentence with the pair (example under Show answer).

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

it turned out what, fast forward, … (no destination), at the end, we survived (in the end), telling the past in the present, that was when (fine) vs that’s when (more dramatic), looking back, I learn (learned). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE JURY (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the signposts, your student is the jury of the baker’s story: who really saved the bakery, is the supermarket the villain, and is her talk still honest if she left something out? Each case has three pieces of evidence. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the verdict harder. Ask them to tell their reason like a story, with It turned out that…, That’s when…, Looking back…. Recast quietly while they talk (watch for turned out what); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I · A Story You Remember, IV · The Jury and V · The Story in Order.
  • Listen: II · The Baker’s Story and VI · Memory.
  • Focus: III · The Signposts.
