Sixty Seconds · The Elevator

Tell me about yourself. An A2 student can say I work in marketing, and then stops, or runs back to 1994. This hour builds a real answer in three blocks (now, before, next) glued with and, but, so, because, after that. The screen is an elevator: it goes up one floor for every connector you hear.

NOT A GRAMMAR PROBLEM

The student knows the words. What’s missing is order, glue and a place to stop. so and because: same two facts, opposite order (cause, so, result / result, because, cause). Drill the order.

THE ELEVATOR · 4-3-2

Scene VII: three rides, 90, 60 and 45 seconds. Press START; tap + CONNECTOR every time you hear one. Loose sentences don’t move it. Same answer, less time: the last ride is always the best.

THREE DOORS (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, before the elevator, your student makes three real interview choices: a broken elevator, the “biggest weakness” question, two job offers. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Push for so and because in every answer. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
