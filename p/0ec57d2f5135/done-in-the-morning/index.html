It Was Done in the Morning · Severance · Macrodata Refinement

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A boring job that became a show, and a company where nothing is explained.

The grammar: It Was Done: It was done in the morning. Nobody says by whom.

The class (about 56 min): I Warm-up and The Lumon Rules · II The Words · III The Trailer · IV Line by Line · V The Deal with Ms. Cobel · VI The Memo · VII Macrodata Refinement · VIII Your File · IX One Minute. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Lumon now. In the trailer, what are the workers told? What aren’t they told?”

Severance (Apple TV, 2022), created by Dan Erickson, with Adam Scott as Mark S.: Mark works at Lumon Industries, and a small chip cuts his memory in two. At work he doesn’t remember his life; at home he doesn’t remember his work. Every day, numbers are sorted on old computers; nobody knows why, and nobody says by whom. The grammar: the passive voice: be + past participle in the present, past and future (are sorted, was promoted, will be forgotten), by only when the agent matters, the negative (wasn’t told) and the question (Was it done?, never did).

A note for the teacher: the trailer is strange and quiet, not violent. The times follow its captions, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and The Lumon Rules (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Lumon Rules: four rules of the company appear one at a time (a chip is put in your brain, the founder is loved, nothing is explained, the work is checked). For each, the student taps Yes / No way / Only if… and says why. The rules are already in the passive; at the end they write a fifth rule in the passive.

The words (II): personal, complete, warned, friendships and handshake, before the video, in match the columns and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, One Minute): the student picks a topic (how your favorite food is made, your city: what was built and what it is known for, a day at Lumon) and talks for one minute in the passive. Then the teacher does one. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a long empty office corridor in dim teal light. I: Warm-up and The Lumon Rules. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: two taps about forgetting your work, and the student says why. Watch: One word is wrong (the student says the right one) and Pick the word you hear, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: sort six passive sentences into now (is / are), before (was / were) and later (will be): tap a sentence, then a box. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Deal with Ms. Cobel: the student is Mark and negotiates with Ms. Cobel (the teacher, with a secret limit), then closes one deal on the screen. VI, the memo: a Lumon memo on a terminal screen. The student reads it, taps the passive verbs, and finishes the rule (are sorted, were taken, was set, was told, will be forgotten). The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, Macrodata Refinement: the old Lumon screen, a grid of numbers where one group of “scary” numbers shakes. Six sentences: Right or Wrong? A right answer refines the group (the numbers fade) and fills one of the six bins at the bottom. If the sentence is wrong, the student taps Wrong and says it right out loud; the right sentence appears under it. At the end: “Quota met.” VIII, Your File: five passive questions to build (where was your phone made?, when was your house built?…): tap Check to see the question, ask the teacher, then find one thing you have in common. IX: one minute.

THE COMMON ERRORS

the numbers sorted every day (are sorted), are sort, was promote, were took, is cutted, the files was, didn’t told, Did the job was done?, Was done the job?, cleaned from the night team (by), will forgotten.

THE DEAL WITH MS. COBEL (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student has seen Mark promoted without being told why. Now they are Mark, the new department chief, in Ms. Cobel’s office: he wants to be told what the numbers are for. You are Ms. Cobel. Your secret limit is on your screen only: you can give one small truth, but only if the quota is raised by 10%. Ask her three questions; the student answers in the passive (I wasn’t told why, the numbers are sorted, the quota will be met). Then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the offer harder, and close one deal on the screen. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence before they reply. Recast quietly while they talk (was promote, I didn’t told, the numbers sorted); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Lumon Rules, V The Deal with Ms. Cobel, VII Macrodata Refinement, VIII Your File and IX One Minute.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI It Was Done.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
