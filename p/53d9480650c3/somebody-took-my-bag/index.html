Somebody Took My Bag · The Halloween Party

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: The Halloween party in Mia’s building is over, and her bag (with her keys) is gone.

The grammar: The Rule: Somebody, anybody, nobody, everybody.

Short class (about 43 min): I Party Talk · II Do Not Disturb · III The Rule · IV The Search · V Six Guests · VIII The Group Chat. Skip if you’re short on time: VI, VII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 56 min.

Open with: “Do you like big parties or small ones?”

The Halloween party in Mia’s building is over, and her bag (with her keys) is gone. Everybody was wearing a costume and nobody saw anything. The student searches the apartment and questions the guests. The grammar is some- / any- / no- + -body, -thing, -where: some- in positive sentences, any- in questions and with not, no- is already negative (one negative is enough), and everybody / somebody take a singular verb. The twist: Nobody took it. The bag was under the candy in the cauldron all the time.

THE TRAILER

Scene II: Hotel Transylvania (2012). Before, during and after. During the trailer, the student listens for Dracula’s line: It’s just a normal hotel, nothing going on here. After it, the last step is ONE screen: the trailer stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again), with six lesson photos under it (the hotel rooms); on the right, six True or Lie lines (three have a time button: 0:42, 1:13 and 1:28, the only lines checked in the trailer). Every right answer opens a room. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, Dracula asks one last question (“Did you see anybody?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud, with somebody, anybody, nobody or something.

THE SEARCH · THE GUESTS

Scene IV: six rooms on the floor plan. The student taps a room, reads what’s there and says it (There isn’t anything in the kitchen); you tap the version you heard. The hall is the last clue. Scene V: the student asks each guest a question (Did you see anybody near the bag?) and you answer as the guest.

TRICK OR TREAT

Scene VI: a short story about where trick-or-treating comes from (the Scottish and Irish “guising”). Read it out loud, then the four questions.

THE MISTAKES

There isn’t nothing, Nobody didn’t see, There’s anybody in the bathroom, Everybody are, Somebodies, Every people.

THE MORNING AFTER (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

A short hot seat after the twist: your student is Mia, and you are Mrs. Park from 2B, a little annoyed because everybody looked for an hour. Ask the four questions (Next question), let them answer in the role with somebody / anything / nobody, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). They close by choosing how Mia says sorry. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Party Talk, IV The Search, V Six Guests.
  • Listen: II Do Not Disturb, VI Trick or Treat?
  • Focus: III The Rule, VII The Morning After.
  • Write: VIII, a message to the building’s group chat. It goes to their My notes.
