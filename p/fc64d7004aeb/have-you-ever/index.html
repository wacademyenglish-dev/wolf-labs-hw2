Have You Ever? · Far From Home

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A street in Toronto.

The grammar: The Rule: The experience has no date. The detail goes to the past.

Short class (about 43 min): I Your Travels · II Experience Bingo · IV The Street Interview · VI The Rule · VII The Passport · X Far From Home · XI Home. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 63 min.

Open with: “Where were you born? Do you still live there?”

A street in Toronto. People from everywhere answer one question: have you ever lived in another country? The grammar is the present perfect for life experiences (Have you ever + participle?) and the switch: the experience has no date, the detail goes to the past (Yes, I have. I went in 2018.). The screen is a passport: every right question inks a stamp.

THE BINGO

Scene II. The student asks, you answer about your real life. Tap the question you heard (the wrong versions are the classic ones: Did you ever…, eated). Then stamp YES or NO. Four in a line is BINGO; the target is eight stamps. Be honest: the fun is in your stories.

THE VIDEO

Scene III. A real street interview, sealed in five chapters (each one stops by itself). Talk before you play the next one.

THE PASSPORT

Scene V. You are the traveler. The student asks Have you ever…?; you say yes, then they ask the detail. Tap the detail question you heard: When did you go?, never When have you gone?

The words (III): abroad, culture, weather, homesick and community, before the video, in match the columns and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

FAR FROM HOME (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene X is now a dilemma in three rounds, from the street interview: a free trip (been there, never been, surprise), one year abroad (Toronto, Lisbon or Tokyo) and the boss who says “stay forever”. Your student chooses and says why with an experience (I’ve never…, I’ve been to … twice) and the detail in the past (I went in 2019). Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Travels, II Experience Bingo, VII The Passport, VIII Never Have I Ever, IX On the Street and X Far From Home.
  • Listen: III The Words, IV The Street Interview and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Rule.
  • Write: XI Home.
