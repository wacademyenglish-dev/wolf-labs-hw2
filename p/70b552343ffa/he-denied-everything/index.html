He Denied Everything · Digger · The Press Conference

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A crack in the ice, and a man who wants to control the story.

The grammar: What They Really Said: He denied everything. Then he admitted knowing.

The class (about 57 min): I Warm-up and The Morning Paper · III The Trailer · IV The Deal · V Scene by Scene · VI The Reporter’s Notes · VII The Press Conference · VIII Report It · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Digger now. In the trailer, what do you think he denies? What does he admit?”

Digger (2026), a dark comedy by Alejandro G. Iñárritu, with Tom Cruise as Digger Rockwell, the richest oil man in the world. A crack opens in the ice near one of his projects; the worst case would cost the world eighteen trillion dollars. Digger knew long before everybody else, and now he wants to be the hero who saves the world from the disaster he caused, and to control the story. The grammar: reporting verbs and the shape after each one: deny / admit + -ing, promise / refuse + to, blame someone for, accuse someone of + -ing, warn someone (not) to, apologize for + -ing.

A note for the teacher: a comedy about a disaster, no violence. Lines checked against the official subtitles (25/09), told in our own words.

Warm-up and The Morning Paper (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Morning Paper: the front page on the day the crack in the ice became news, with three stories about Digger Rockwell. The student picks one, says what they think happened, reads the start and reacts. The stories already use the reporting verbs (he denied knowing, he refused to stop, they accuse him of hiding).

The words (II): salary, disaster, scenario, waste and truth, before the video, in mystery meaning and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is Digger after the press conference; the teacher is a journalist with five questions (did you deny everything, what did you promise, who do you blame, did anyone accuse you, will you apologize). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an oil rig on the ice at night. I: Warm-up and The Morning Paper. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a slider (“A very rich man says: I’m sorry, I will fix it. Do you believe him?”, from Not at all to Completely); the student places it and says why. Watch: True or lie? Six sentences, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. After: four lines from the trailer, each with a ▶; the student taps what they DO with their words (they blame him, they warn the President…). V: five frames on the film strip. VI, The Reporter’s Notes: a dark screen with the reporter’s notes. The student taps the reporting verbs and what comes after them (denied causing, promised to fix, blamed … for, accused … of lying, warned … not to go…), then finishes the rule board: six lines with two choices each. The board stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Press Conference: six microphones in front of the podium. A card gives who spoke, the verb and the words (“Digger · deny · I didn’t cause the crack”); the student says the report out loud and taps Check. The sentence appears, one mic turns live (the red on-air light) and the report runs on the news ticker. At the end, every mic is live. VIII, Report It: six things people said to the student; each card has the clues in brackets (“refuse · give us a raise”); the student builds the report, says it and taps Check. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

denied to cause, admitted to know, admitted me that, promised fixing, refused signing, promised to fixing, blamed him of / because, accused him to lie, accused him of lie, warned them don’t go, warned to them, apologized to cause.

THE DEAL (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer, a negotiation: your student leads the scientists and you play Digger. Your secret limit is on your screen only (you pay up to half, you never admit causing the crack). Ask the three questions in the role, wait five seconds in silence after each, and let them push with deny / admit + -ing, promise / refuse + to. Then close the deal on the screen together and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. There is no right deal. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to report the deal (“He agreed to …, he refused to …”) and fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Morning Paper, IV The Deal, VII The Press Conference, VIII Report It and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI What They Really Said.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
