Red Flags · Au Pair · Lesson 14 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: warning signs before the match (secrets, contact outside the agency, requests for money or documents, no private room, promises too good to be true) and in the house (taking the passport, extra hours, tasks that are not childcare, isolation, inappropriate comments or touching, no days off).

The story: AuPairWorld’s five red flags (video), twelve situations to classify, and a scam message (fictional) to dissect.

About 42 min: I · Five Red Flags (9) · II · OK or Red Flag? (14) · III · Spot the Scam (8) · IV · Traffic Light (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Have you ever received a message that was too good to be true?”

Keep the tone calm: most families are wonderful. The message is not fear, it is a plan: a red flag means talking to the coordinator, and in danger, calling 911. If the student shares a personal situation, listen, don’t judge, and point to the right help. The video is from a European platform: correct the “extra pay for extra hours” idea; in the U.S. program, 45 hours per week is the limit.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

red flag / warning sign · scam · never send money · I’d like to keep my passport with me. · isolation · talk to my coordinator

FIVE RED FLAGS

AuPairWorld, “5 red flags when looking for a host family” (4 min, captions checked on 15/10/2026). Point out the U.S. difference at the end.

OK OR RED FLAG?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

SPOT THE SCAM

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Tell the student: if something ever feels wrong, she can write to you too, and you will help her find the right contact.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

thinking a red flag is her fault, keeping secrets from the coordinator, sending documents to strangers. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

TRAFFIC LIGHT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the scam, five grey moments in the house: the dog, extra hours, the phone at night, sharing the bedroom, and friends on Saturday. For each one the student picks green (it’s OK), yellow (talk to them first) or red (call my coordinator), then says what they would do. Most of these are opinion, so let them choose; only two are program rules (more than 45 hours a week, and a private room). Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (I’d like to …, it’s a red flag because …); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word: their own red line, then you play the family and ask for it.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
