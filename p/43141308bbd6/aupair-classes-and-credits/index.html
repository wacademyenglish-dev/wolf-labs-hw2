Classes and Credits · Au Pair · Lesson 23 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the education part of the program: at least six semester hours (credits) of academic credit, or the equivalent, at an accredited U.S. post-secondary institution; the host family pays up to $500 (EduCare: 12 credits and up to $1,000). Reading a catalog, doing the math, planning the schedule, enrolling.

The story: A fictional college (Lakeview Community College) with five courses for au pairs. Five cases to calculate. Mariana’s enrollment form (fictional).

About 47 min: I · English Tips (5) · II · The Course Catalog (9) · III · Do the Math (12) · IV · Your Six Credits (7) · V · Enroll (10) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “If you could study anything in the USA for free, what would it be?”

Rules checked on 15/10/2026 in the federal regulation and on j1visa.state.gov. Real course options and prices vary a lot: the agency and the coordinator help the student choose. The college and courses in this lesson are fictional.

ENGLISH TIPS

Cultural Care Au Pair, “Matching tips 3: Tips for improving your English as an au pair” (40 seconds, captions checked on 15/10/2026).

THE COURSE CATALOG

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

DO THE MATH

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

ENROLL

A practice form in English, filled in with a FICTIONAL person’s data (never the student’s real documents in class). The student reads the profile card and fills in the form; Check the form shows each field right or wrong, with the correct answer.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student which course she would choose and why; next lesson is the final project.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

thinking any course counts, enrolling before planning the schedule with the family, forgetting the receipt. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

YOUR SIX CREDITS (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the math, the student’s own choice with the same catalog, in three rounds: fast or slow (one weekend course or courses every week), useful or fun (Child Development or Photography), online or in the classroom. There is no right answer, so let them choose; only the program rules are fixed (six credits, the family pays up to $500). Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (I’d choose …, I’d pay the rest, Could we plan my schedule?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
