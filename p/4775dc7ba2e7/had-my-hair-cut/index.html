I Had My Hair Cut · The Claim Tags

Lena gets married on Saturday, and she does nothing herself. She has her hair cut, her dress shortened, her car washed. The grammar is the causative: have + the thing + participle (She had her hair cut), with get as the everyday version. Every job is a claim tag on a shop rail: the photo stays grey until your student says the right sentence, then the tag is stamped READY. The big question: is it better to do things yourself, or to pay someone?

WHO HELD THE SCISSORS?

Scene II: She cut her hair is not wrong English. It is a different story: she did it herself. The tag gets a DIY? stamp and the screen says so. Laugh about it, then ask for the salon version.

THE WEDDING WEEK

Scene IV: you are the planner. Ask each day’s question fast; your student answers with the right tense of HAVE. After each answer, ask “Which HAVE was that?”

DIY OR PAY?

Scene V: your student picks a side on each card; the score shows how DIY they are. Take the other side on purpose.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I cut my hair at the salon, had her car wash, had washed her car (= she washed it), got fixed my phone, cutted, maked.

THE RUSH JOB (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Two days before the wedding, your student is Lena and negotiates a rush job with the dressmaker: you. Your secret limit is only on your screen. Ask the three questions, let them argue, then close a deal on the screen. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (“I cut my dress” → “I had my dress shortened”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON
