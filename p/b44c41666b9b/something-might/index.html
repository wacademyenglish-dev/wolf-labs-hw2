Something Might Be in the House · The Hallway at Night

Halloween night. The student is home alone in an old house, the power is out, and there is only a flashlight. Behind every door there is a noise. What is it? Nobody knows, so we guess: It might be the cat. It may be the rain. It might not be a ghost. The grammar is might / may + verb for a possibility now. Nothing jumps and nothing screams: every noise has a silly answer.

THE HALLWAY · THE SHADOWS

Scene II, before the rule: six doors, six noises; the student guesses in their own words, you tap what you heard, then open the door. Scene IV, after the rule: eight shadows on the wall; two guesses each, fast, with might and might not.

THE MISTAKES

It can be the cat (can is ability), It might is, It mights, It might to be, It might don’t, Maybe it might.

NO JUMP SCARES

The lesson is spooky, not scary. If a student doesn’t like fear, they can play the brave one: It might just be the wind.

THE NIGHT JURY (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VII is now a jury with clues: three small mysteries in the house (the open fridge, music at 3 AM, wet footprints on the stairs). Your student reads the clues, chooses a verdict and says why with might, may, might not or maybe + a full sentence. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). If you hear It can be the cat, just ask: Can, or might? Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Noises at Night, II Six Doors, IV The Flashlight, V Tonight, Maybe, VI Sam Is Scared, VII The Night Jury.
  • Focus: III The Rule.
  • Write: VIII, three guesses about their own house. It goes to their My notes.
