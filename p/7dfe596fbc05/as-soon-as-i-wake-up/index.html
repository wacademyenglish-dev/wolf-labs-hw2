As Soon As I Wake Up · The 5:30 Alarm

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A listening lab about mornings.

The grammar: The Rule: Talking about tomorrow? The time half stays in the present.

Short class (about 43 min): I You and Your Mornings · III The Listening Lab · V Lena’s Morning · VI The Rule · VII Before the Sun Comes Up · IX Three Hard Mornings · X Your Tomorrow. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 57 min.

Open with: “What time does your alarm ring? Do you get up right away?”

A listening lab about mornings. First a real video about daily routines, sealed in four parts. Then an easy story in six chapters on a digital alarm clock: Lena, a nurse in Chicago, and her normal morning. Tomorrow is her first day at a new hospital, and tonight her alarm clock is broken. The student plans her morning, and every right plan lifts the sun over the city. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

The time half stays in the present: when I arrive, as soon as I wake up, until the bus comes, by the time you get up. The other half has the future: I’ll call you. as soon as = right after; until = all the time up to; by the time = at that moment, or earlier. Review: when / before / after + a full sentence.

THE TRAP

Many languages have a special future form after when, so learners grab will or going to: when I will arrive. Also: until you don’t come (an extra not), until for a deadline (by the time), before to leave, and a promise in the present (When I arrive, I call you).

A REVIEW LESSON

Works as a follow-up to a lesson on when / before / after; here the center is as soon as, until, by the time and the present for the future.

The words (II): breakfast, jogging, shower, podcast and exercise, before the video, in memory and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THREE HARD MORNINGS (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Lena’s call from the bus, your student plans three hard mornings of their own: no alarm, too early on day one, and late for work. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Push for as soon as / until / when / by the time + present with will in the other half. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
