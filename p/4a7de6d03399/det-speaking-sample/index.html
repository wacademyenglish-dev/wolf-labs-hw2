Speaking Sample · DET Prep · Lesson 11 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the Speaking Sample: at the end of the test, one topic, about 30 seconds to prepare and 1 to 3 minutes of speaking. It has no score, but the video goes to the universities with the results. The student learns a five-step story: situation, challenge, action, result, lesson.

The story: A model answer from an invented candidate (a first job, meetings in English), then the student’s own story, then two real topics with the clock.

About 44 min: I · A Model Answer (9) · II · The Council Vote (7) · III · Your Plan (8) · IV · The Real Thing (16) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “If a university could watch you for three minutes, what would you want them to see?”

This is the most personal task of the test. Help the student choose a TRUE story they can tell well; it can be small. Practice the look: eyes on the camera, a smile at the start, a clear last sentence.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

situation: Two years ago, … · challenge: The challenge was… · action: So I decided to… · result: As a result, … · lesson: What I learned is that… · eyes on the camera

A MODEL ANSWER

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

YOUR PLAN

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

THE REAL THING

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. If possible, record Topic 1 (with the student’s permission) and watch it together: posture and eye contact improve fast.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

a story with no lesson, present tense for the past story, reading a memorized text, stopping at one minute. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE COUNCIL VOTE (II) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the model answer, the student sits on the admissions council of a fictional university and votes on four proposals about the Speaking Sample: a script on the screen, one topic for everyone, the video before the score, a second chance. For each one: yes, no, or propose a change, then say why. You vote too, often on the other side. Wait five seconds in silence before they start. Use the PUSH line when the answer is too safe. At the end, the student makes one new rule in 45 seconds and you vote. Recast quietly while they talk, and push the five-step language (The challenge was…, So I decided to…, As a result…, What I learned is that…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · A Model Answer.
  • Write: III · Your Plan.
  • Out loud: II · The Council Vote and IV · The Real Thing.
  • Focus: V · I Can.
