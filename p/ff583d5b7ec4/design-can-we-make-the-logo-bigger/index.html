Can We Make the Logo Bigger? · English for Designers · Lesson 8 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: receiving client feedback: asking about the real worry, thanking, disagreeing politely (I see where you’re coming from, but…; My only concern is…), proposing alternatives (What if we…? That way,…) and naming scope (That’s outside the scope we agreed).

The story: Five recorded client comments (American, British, Indian and Australian accents), then two fictional clients: a bakery website and a small apartment.

About 47 min: I · Five Client Comments (10) · II · From Blunt to Polite (12) · III · Say No, Kindly (14) · IV · The Client Deal (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What is the most common change your clients ask for? Bigger logo? More colors?”

There is no video in this lesson; the recorded clients are the listening. After the multiple choice, play the client yourself and repeat one of the comments, more insistently, so the student practices staying calm.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Can I ask what worries you about…? · I see where you’re coming from, but… · My only concern is… · What if we…? That way,… · Let me show you … side by side. · That’s outside the scope we agreed, so…

FIVE CLIENT COMMENTS

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

FROM BLUNT TO POLITE

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

SAY NO, KINDLY

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to remember a real client change request and how they would answer it now.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

You are wrong (I see it differently), I see where are you coming from (where you’re coming from), What if we make…? (correct; also What if we made…?), It’s out of the scope (outside the scope). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE CLIENT DEAL (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the two timed answers, a real back-and-forth. Your student is the designer; you are Carla from a fictional café. You ask for a much bigger logo, red everywhere and a team photo at the top, but your secret limit (only you see it) is softer: a bigger logo only on the home page, red is not important, and the photo stays but can go lower. Ask the four questions in order, insist a little, wait five seconds in silence after each, and don’t give your limit first. Then close the three points on screen and use the PUSH line (another designer said yes to everything). Recast quietly while they talk (I see where you’re coming from, what if we …, outside the scope); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
