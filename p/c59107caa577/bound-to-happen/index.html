Bound to Happen · C1 Unit 2 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on the future with nuance: be bound to, be set to, be due to, be likely / unlikely to, be about to, be on the verge of + -ing.

The story: City Radio’s 7 AM bulletin (invented city): a new bridge, the mayor’s speech, a bus strike and a storm. Then a prediction board about the student’s own life.

About 52 min: I · Your Next Week (6) · II · The Morning News (9) · III · On Air (7) · IV · How Sure? (9) · V · Speed Round (6) · VI · The Prediction Board (15).

Open with: “What’s coming up for you in the next few days?”

The student already says will and going to. Today is about precision: how sure, and why. The radio bulletin shows the phrases in their natural home (news); the comparison makes the nuance clear; the board makes the student choose the right one again and again.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

be bound to (almost certain) · be set to (planned, news) · be due to (timetable) · be likely / unlikely to · be about to · be on the verge of + -ing

YOUR NEXT WEEK

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE MORNING NEWS

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

HOW SURE?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

SPEED ROUND

A 60-second speed round. Tap Start the clock, then Right or Pass for each item; the answer is on your screen. At the end of class, play again and beat the score.

THE PREDICTION BOARD

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

prices must rise (sounds like an obligation), on the verge to quit, it is probable to rain, the train is due leaving, I’m about of leaving, it is bound that it rains. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

ON AIR (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the bulletin: your student is the chief engineer of the new bridge, live on City Radio, with a secret on screen (the lights failed a test last night). You are the reporter who heard a rumour. Ask the four questions in character, wait five seconds in silence after each, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to corner them: tell the truth, or protect the opening? There is no right answer. Listen for how sure they sound (set to, bound to, unlikely to); recast quietly, and at the end ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I · Your Next Week, III · On Air and VI · The Prediction Board.
  • Listen: II · The Morning News.
  • Focus: IV · How Sure? and V · Speed Round.
