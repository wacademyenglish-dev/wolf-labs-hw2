Can You Hear Me? · Selling Services · Lesson 26

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 26 (present continuous on video calls). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Leo presents a proposal to Mark (Costa Retail, fictional) on a video call full of problems: breaking up, the wrong screen, a second phone. The student listens to six call moments, debates camera on × off, reads chapter 7 of the Easy Reader (the airport video call with Mr. Duarte), saves a bad call with you as the client and writes his own video call kit.

About 58 min: I · Your Video Calls (5) · II · “You’re Breaking Up!” (8) · III · What’s Happening? (7) · IV · You’re Breaking Up, Not “You Breaking Up” (8) · V · The Debate: Camera On or Off? (6) · VI · Easy Reader: The Airport Call (6) · VII · Save the Call (7) · VIII · Your Video Call Kit (8) · IX · I Can (3).

Open with: “How many video calls do you have this week? Where are you right now? What’s happening around you?”

Present continuous for what is happening NOW on the call. Brazilians drop be (“You breaking up”), forget -ing (“I’m share my screen”), say “Are you hearing me?” (hear/see stay simple: Can you hear me? Can you see it?) and “My camera don’t working”. III has audio: let him play each moment, answer, and then SAY the answer like on a real call. In V take the other side every time. In VII act the problems for real: talk without sound, freeze your face, so he reacts.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Can you hear me? Can you see my screen? · Sorry, you’re breaking up. You’re on mute. · I’m sharing my screen. I’m moving to another room. · My camera isn’t working. Is it better now?

YOUR VIDEO CALLS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

“YOU’RE BREAKING UP!”

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

WHAT’S HAPPENING?

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

YOU’RE BREAKING UP, NOT “YOU BREAKING UP”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THE DEBATE: CAMERA ON OR OFF?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

EASY READER: THE AIRPORT CALL

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

SAVE THE CALL

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

YOUR VIDEO CALL KIT

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask him to use three phrases from his kit on his next real call and tell you which ones.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Breaking up (on a call): A voz/vídeo “picota”, corta: “You’re breaking up” = sua conexão está falhando. (Não confundir com terminar namoro.)
  • On mute · unmute: Com o microfone desligado · ligar o microfone. “You’re on mute” = você está no mudo.
  • Frozen (video): Vídeo congelado: a imagem parou, mas às vezes o áudio continua.
  • Share (my) screen: Compartilhar a tela no Teams/Zoom/Meet para mostrar a proposta ou o slide.
  • Echo: Eco: quando a pessoa ouve a própria voz de volta (geralmente por usar caixa de som em vez de fone).

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

You breaking up (You’re breaking up), I’m share my screen (I’m sharing my screen), Are you hearing me? (Can you hear me?), My camera don’t working (My camera isn’t working). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
