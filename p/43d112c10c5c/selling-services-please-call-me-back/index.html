Please Call Me Back · Selling Services · Lesson 18

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 18 (voicemail: numbers, times, e-mails · please + verb). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Ana Ruiz, from Tallow Foods (fictional), leaves Leo a voicemail: the student listens and types it, sentence by sentence. Then a second voicemail, from Victor Hale, controller at Granite Labs (fictional), goes into the CRM: name, reason, phone in digits, best time with a.m./p.m., e-mail. The student finds the Brazilian traps, calls Victor back (you are Victor), and records his own voicemail.

About 58 min: I · Your Phone (5) · II · A Message from Ana (10) · III · The Message in the CRM (10) · IV · “Call Me Back”, Not “Return Me” (9) · V · Call Him Back (8) · VI · You Choose (5) · VII · Your Voicemail (8) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “Do you listen to your voicemails? What time do you start work? And finish?”

A listening and numbers lesson. The skill: catch and give a phone number, a time and an e-mail with no mistakes. Phone numbers are said digit by digit, in groups (0 = “oh” or “zero”); times are 1–12 with a.m./p.m. (never “15h”); e-mails use at / dot. Brazilian traps: “Please return me” (me retorne), “call me in the number”, “at 15h”, “Please to call”. In II, ask him to type numbers as digits. In VII, YOU write the number and e-mail he says: if yours is wrong, he says it again, slowly.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Please call me back at 555-0143. · Five five five, oh one four three. · I’m free on Tuesday after 3 p.m. · My e-mail is ana dot ruiz at tallow dot com.

YOUR PHONE

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

A MESSAGE FROM ANA

Listen and Type: the audio plays up to 3 times, like the test. The student types, then Check compares word by word: green is right, red is missing or misspelled. Read the tip out loud.

THE MESSAGE IN THE CRM

A practice form in English, filled in with a FICTIONAL person’s data (never the student’s real documents in class). The student reads the profile card and fills in the form; Check the form shows each field right or wrong, with the correct answer.

“CALL ME BACK”, NOT “RETURN ME”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

CALL HIM BACK

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR VOICEMAIL

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask him to change his real voicemail greeting to English, or to leave one real voicemail in English this week.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Voicemail: Caixa postal / recado de voz. “Leave a voicemail” = deixar recado na caixa postal.
  • Call back · return a call: Retornar a ligação. “Please call me back” (peça) · “I’m returning your call” (quem retorna).
  • CRM: Customer Relationship Management: sistema onde o vendedor registra clientes, ligações e próximos passos (ex. de marcas: Salesforce, HubSpot).
  • Controller: Controller: responsável pela contabilidade/controladoria, abaixo do CFO. Costuma ser quem analisa a proposta de BPO contábil.
  • a.m. / p.m.: Antes / depois do meio-dia. 12 p.m. = meio-dia; 12 a.m. = meia-noite. Em inglês não se usa “15h”.
  • Oh (0): Em números de telefone, o zero costuma ser falado “oh”: 555-0143 = “five five five, oh one four three”.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Please return me (Please call me back), Call me in the number… (Call me at…), at 15h (at 3 p.m.), Please to send… (Please send…). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
