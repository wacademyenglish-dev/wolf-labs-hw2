My Case Study · English for Designers · Lesson 10 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: presenting a case study: context and role (I / we), problem, goal, process, solution, result; past simple and sequencers (First, Then, In the end, As a result); a reflection (If I did it again, I would…).

The story: A design lead’s advice on portfolio reviews, a fictional library app case study to put in order, then the student’s own project (or a fictional bakery rebrand).

About 40 min: I · The Case Study Section (9) · II · Put the Story in Order (9) · III · Present Your Project (18) · IV · I Can (4).

Open with: “Which project in your portfolio are you most proud of? Why that one?”

Strongly prefer the student’s own project in the final task. Time it (two minutes), then ask one interviewer question: “What was the hardest part?” or “What would you do differently?”.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

This project was for… · My role was… · The problem was that… · Our goal was to… · First, I… Then, we… · As a result,…

THE CASE STUDY SECTION

femke.design, presenting portfolio projects in a design interview: the case study section (3:56–6:50).

PUT THE STORY IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

PRESENT YOUR PROJECT

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to prepare their final pitch: one project, from the brief to delivery, for the last lesson.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

We maked (We made), I was responsible to (responsible for), The result was very good (give a number), If I did again, I will (If I did it again, I would). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · The Case Study Section.
  • Focus: II · Put the Story in Order and IV · I Can.
  • Out loud: III · Present Your Project.
