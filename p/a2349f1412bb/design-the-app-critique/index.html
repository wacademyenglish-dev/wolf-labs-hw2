The App Critique · English for Designers · Lesson 11 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the app critique: overview (This app helps… to…), users and one flow (Let me walk through one flow), strong comments (user + element + effect: …, so…), hedging (a bit, might, I wonder if…), opportunities and trade-offs.

The story: A product designer’s critique structure, comments to sort into strong and weak, then two fictional apps (a food delivery app and a habit tracker) to critique live.

About 47 min: I · A Structure for Any App (9) · II · Strong or Weak? (9) · III · Fix It First (7) · IV · Critique It Live (18) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Which app on your phone do you love, and which one makes you angry? Why?”

For graphic and interior designers, this is still useful: the same structure works to critique a poster or a hotel lobby. After the cards, ask the student to critique a real app on their phone, live.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

This app helps… to… · The main users are probably… · Let me walk through one flow: · One problem is that…, so… · One opportunity could be… · The trade-off is…

A STRUCTURE FOR ANY APP

chunbuns, how to ace the app critique in design interviews: the structure (3:24–6:23).

STRONG OR WEAK?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

CRITIQUE IT LIVE

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to practice one live critique of any app before the final lesson.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

The app is very intuitive (say why), It helps the users to find (fine; also helps users find), The trade-off is that is (that it is), I would change all (be specific). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

FIX IT FIRST (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the live critiques, a ranking. A fictional cinema app has five problems, and the team can fix one a month. Your student taps them in order (1 = fix first) and defends the first and the last. Then tap “Show my teacher’s order” (it starts with the hidden tickets and the six payment screens), disagree on one item and agree on one top 3 together. Wait five seconds in silence before you help, and use the PUSH line (the manager wants payment first). There is no right order. Recast quietly while they talk (user + element + effect, One problem is that …, so …, The trade-off is …); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
