Match! · Au Pair · Lesson 12 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: managing family connections on the agency platform: answer within 24 hours, say no kindly, say yes with thanks, explain a choice, and plan the arrival with going to and will.

The story: Cultural Care’s matching tips (log in daily, answer in 24 hours, be open-minded). Then four pairs of fictional families to choose from, and the student’s match message to the Parkers.

About 37 min: I · Matching Tips (6) · II · Yes and No, Politely (9) · III · Family A or Family B? (8) · IV · Your Match Message (10) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Is it rude not to answer a message?” In matching, silence is worse than a kind no.

The choice of a family is personal. In the Family A or B scene, value reasons connected to the student’s experience and goals, and the questions she would ask (e.g., why previous au pairs left).

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Thank you so much…, but… · I don’t think we are the right match · I would love to be your au pair! · Could I have until…? · I’m going to arrive on… · I will…

MATCHING TIPS

Cultural Care Au Pair, “Matching tips 1: How to manage your connections as an au pair” (48 seconds, captions checked on 15/10/2026). Play twice.

YES AND NO, POLITELY

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

FAMILY A OR FAMILY B?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR MATCH MESSAGE

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Remind the student: before accepting, check the agreement (Lesson 7) and the questions from Lesson 9.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

No. (alone), silence, I go to arrive, I will to bring. A2+: one correction per answer is enough.

TRAFFIC LIGHT (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the match message, four new messages arrive: a farm far from the city, snow driving in Minnesota, a family that wants an answer in two hours, and first-time parents with a baby. For each one the student picks green (I would love to), yellow (I’d ask first) or red (a kind no), then says the answer to the family. There is no right color: it depends on their experience and goals. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (Thank you so much …, but …; Could I have until …?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
