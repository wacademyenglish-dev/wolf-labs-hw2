Slowly, Carefully, Badly · The Kitchen Grand Prix

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: An old fridge thinks its owner wants a new one, so it stands up and leaves the kitchen.

The grammar: The Rule: How? Adjective + -ly. Four words break the rule.

Short class (about 43 min): I You and How You Do Things · III The Film · V The Grand Prix · VI The Rule · VII Live on Air · X The Fridge Shop · XI After the Race. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 64 min.

Open with: “Do you eat fast or slowly? What about your family?”

An old fridge thinks its owner wants a new one, so it stands up and leaves the kitchen. The student watches the short film (no words, only music) in four sealed chapters and tells how each character does things. Then the kitchen goes crazy: eight appliances run the Kitchen Grand Prix, and the student is the commentator.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

Portuguese often uses the adjective as an adverb, so the student says runs quick, plays good. It also puts the adverb before the object: speaks very well English. work hardly means almost not working; lately means recently; fastly doesn’t exist. After look / feel / be: the adjective (looks sad).

THE FILM · THE GRAND PRIX · LIVE ON AIR

Scene III: the same film as the old lesson, sealed in four chapters; there are no words, so every answer is the student’s own narration. Scene V, before the rule: each racer gives a clue word; tap exactly what you heard. Scene VII, after the rule: you read the replay; the student says it at once; the applause meter fills.

The words (II): fridge, excuse, jump, knew and replace, before the video, in match the columns and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

THE FRIDGE SHOP (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Back to the film: your student owns an old fridge that works badly but they love it, and you are the salesperson. Ask the three questions, then try to sell (only you see your secret limit: 30% off, or a repair for $150). There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence after each question, then use the PUSH line to make the choice harder, and close with the deal box. Push for adjective + -ly, well / fast / hard and looks + adjective. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
