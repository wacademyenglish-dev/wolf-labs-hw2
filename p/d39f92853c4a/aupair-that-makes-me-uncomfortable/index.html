That Makes Me Uncomfortable · Au Pair · Lesson 21 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: saying no kindly and firmly (acknowledge, say no clearly, offer a next step), telling a normal family conversation from a red flag, and dealing with culture shock.

The story: Au Pair in America on culture shock (video). Then four pairs of answers (too soft, rude, just right) and five recorded requests from a fictional host dad: a task on her day off, the passport, extra hours, a normal family request, and an inappropriate comment.

About 35 min: I · Culture Shock (7) · II · Kind and Firm (10) · III · The Host Dad Asks (14) · IV · I Can (4).

Open with: “Is it hard for you to say no? Why?” Many Brazilians find it hard: that is why we practice.

Request 5 is an inappropriate comment. Handle it calmly: the student practices a firm sentence, and you make clear that it is never her fault and that the next step is her coordinator (and the help line or 911 if she feels in danger). If the student becomes uncomfortable, skip it. Request 4 is a NORMAL family wish: the point is to tell it apart from red flags.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I understand, but… · I’m not comfortable with that. · That’s not part of my duties. · I need to check with my coordinator first. · Could we write the schedule down? · I’d like to keep my passport with me.

CULTURE SHOCK

Au Pair in America, “What is culture shock and how can you overcome it?” (1 min, captions checked on 15/10/2026).

KIND AND FIRM

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THE HOST DAD ASKS

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Tell the student she can always practice a difficult conversation with you before having it with the family.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

OK… (too soft), That’s not my job! (rude), apologizing for saying no, laughing off an inappropriate comment. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE HOT SEAT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the host dad’s requests, the student changes places: they are an au pair in their second year, on a video call with Júlia, who flies next month. You are Júlia: ask about the strangest thing in the first month, culture shock, saying no to the family, and what they would do differently. They can invent their story. Your secret (only you see it): Júlia is afraid to say no, so push harder on question 3. Then they choose one tip for her. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line. Recast quietly while they talk (I understand, but …; Could we …?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
