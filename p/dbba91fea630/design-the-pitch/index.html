The Pitch · English for Designers · Lesson 12 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the final pitch: a whole project as a story (client and role, problem and goal, brief questions, decisions with reasons, polite disagreement, revisions and delivery, result, reflection) and answering the client’s questions.

The story: A fictional model pitch (a home office for a night nurse), then the student pitches step by step to the teacher as client, then the full three-minute final pitch.

About 53 min: I · A Model Pitch (10) · II · Step by Step With the Client (14) · III · The Pitch Jury (7) · IV · Your Final Pitch (18) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Which project did you choose for today? Why that one?”

This is the last lesson: let the student talk. In the step-by-step scene (cadeira), react as a real client, including one honest opinion they must answer politely. After the final pitch, ask two questions the student didn’t prepare. Feedback on three things only: one strength, one phrase to change, one thing to practice.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I’d like to show you… · The client was… My role was… · The problem was that… · We chose… because… · My only concern was…, so… · As a result,…

A MODEL PITCH

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

STEP BY STEP WITH THE CLIENT

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

YOUR FINAL PITCH

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Celebrate the end of the course. Ask which phrase the student will use with their next client, and which situation still feels hard.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I will show you now my project (I’d like to show you…), The client wanted that I make (wanted me to make), We delivered on the time (on time), If I did again (If I did it again). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE PITCH JURY (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Just before the final pitch, your student is the jury for three fictional designers who pitched to a new bakery: Nina (25 slides of research before the idea), Rafa (“I don’t know, I’ll send it by Friday”) and Joana (beautiful images, “They’re trendy”). Read the evidence together, ask for a verdict (Hire, Not yet, It depends) and what the designer should do differently. Wait five seconds in silence, don’t judge first, and use the PUSH line to make the verdict harder. There is no right verdict. Recast quietly while they talk (The problem was that …, We chose … because …, My only concern is …); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves, and remind them to avoid Nina’s and Joana’s mistakes in their own pitch.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
