Nine Times Longer · The Gears

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: computers and people share one problem: time spent choosing is time not doing. A spacecraft on Mars learned it in 1997.

The grammar: The more / -er X, the more / less / -er Y (two THEs, one comma) · the fewer + things you can count · the short form (the sooner, the better) · twice as long as, three times longer than.

The lesson (about 55 min, ~45 without the extras): I Your Gears · II Mars, 1997 · III The Two THEs · IV Six Voices · V The Words · VI The Machines · VII Line by Line (extra) · VIII The Debate · IX Your Numbers · X Fix the Findings (extra) · XI Your Laws.

Open with: “Pick something you do every week. What changes when you do more of it?”

A CONVERSATION LESSON

This is B2: the student talks most of the time. Only the six voices have options to choose; everything else is talk. Your job is to ask the follow-up, take the other side, and listen for the second THE.

WHAT EACH SCENE DOES

  • I · Your Gears (talk, 6 min). The student picks gear A (an activity) and gear B (what changes), then says if they turn together or against each other. Four questions follow. Write down their first sentence exactly (“When I sleep more…” or “More I sleep, more I…”): you use it in III.
  • II · Mars, 1997 (predict and defend, 6 min). The student picks a theory and defends it; push back. Then open the file: “The more small tasks it did, the less important work it finished.” Two questions about their life.
  • III · The Two THEs (rule, 8 min). The grammar stays on screen. The student links the columns: a first half, then the half that finishes it; a line joins them and the gears turn. For each sentence ask: “Is it true? When is it not true?” Then go back to their sentence from I and fix it together.
  • IV · Six Voices (listen and retell, 8 min). After each voice, the student chooses the sentence that says what they mean (the wrong ones change the meaning) and reads it out loud. Then one question about them. Voice 6 disagrees: use it for a mini-debate.
  • V–VII · The video (words, video, Line by Line). After the video, the last step is ONE screen: the video stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again), with the two gears from scene I under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines about what the video says (an inbox that doubles, the Linux fix, interruptions). Every right answer fills a block, and the gears start to turn. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, the teacher asks one last question (“Your inbox doubles tomorrow. What is your plan?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud, with two THEs.
  • VIII · The Debate (10 min, the heart of the lesson). Read each idea and take the other side (your card says how). The student answers with “Up to a point…”, “Not necessarily…”, “That depends on…”. The last one: they make a claim and you answer.
  • IX · Your Numbers (6 min). Real numbers from their life. While they talk, tap the multiplier (½, ×2, ×3…) and the blocks build. Watch for “two times longer as” and “nine times more long”.
  • X · Fix the Findings (extra, 5 min). Six lab notes; five are wrong, number 4 is right.
  • XI · Your Laws (6 min). Three laws of their life with two THEs. Try to break one of them. It goes to their My notes.

THE COMMON ERRORS

More you practice, more you learn (no THE) · the more…, less… (the second THE falls) · the most / the least · the more long / the more good · nine times more long · the less messages (fewer).

THE COUNCIL (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Your student sits on the office council and votes on three rules from the video: email twice a day, a no-meeting Wednesday, three buckets instead of a ranked list. They vote yes, no, or propose a change, and say why with two THEs or a multiplier; you vote the other way. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Gears · II Mars · VIII The Debate · IX Your Numbers · X The Council.
  • Listen: IV Six Voices · V–VII the video.
  • Focus: III The Two THEs.
  • Write: XI Your Laws (and the homework).
