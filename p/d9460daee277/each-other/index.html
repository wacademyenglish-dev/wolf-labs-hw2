Each Other · Two Brothers, Two Stories

Two brothers opened a bakery nine years ago. A filmmaker, Dana, interviewed them separately, and their stories don’t match: one says we taught ourselves, the other says we taught each other. The screen draws the action as arrows: an arrow that comes back to the same person is -self; two arrows that cross are each other. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

myself / yourself / himself / herself / ourselves / themselves when the action comes back; each other when it crosses. Some verbs take nothing: feel, get up, remember, relax, focus, complain. by myself = nobody helped; alone = nobody was there. The mistakes: themselves for each other, I feel myself tired, got himself up, each others, hisself.

THE HOT SEAT (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the two microphones, your student becomes Rafa and you are Dana with the camera on. Four short questions: who is right, what he taught Tomás, the first year, one word for the film. You secretly like Tomás’s version, so make Rafa defend his. Wait five seconds in silence after each question; use the PUSH line (only you see it) at the end. Recast quietly while they talk (each other, not themselves; by himself); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The Editor’s Cut (VIII) is still the decision at the end.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Two of Your Stories, III The Hot Seat, IV The Arrow, VIII The Editor’s Cut (you are Dana: push back three times, then give in; there is no right answer).
  • Focus: II Two Microphones, V Which Way?, VI What English Throws Away, VII Count the Room, IX The Credits.
  • Write: X, their two stories, with the red wavy line for -self after feel / get up / relax / remember, each others, was by myself for an empty room, hisself. It goes to their My notes.

Nobody is angry: the brothers disagree about a word, not about each other. Keep it light. No audio or video: your voice is Dana’s.
