Only a Suggestion · The Task Board

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: At four o’clock somebody says “nobody told me to”, and the board proves them right.

The grammar: Ada’s minutes first (scene II): the student taps each verb with its person, then finishes the rule. Every form found pins a badge on the task board.

Short class (about 49 min): I Ten to Eleven · II Ada’s Minutes · III Who Has a Chair? · IV Keep the Person · V The -s Comes Off · VI Four O’Clock · VII Eleven O’Clock · X On the Record. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII and IX Your Advice (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 61 min.

Open with: “What exactly would you suggest?”

At four o’clock somebody says “nobody told me to”, and the board proves them right. Some verbs have a chair for a person right after them: tell, ask, remind, advise (I asked Marina to send it). Others refuse: suggest, recommend, insist, explain, mention. With those, the person goes into a that clause with a plain verb (I suggest that Marina send it), the idea takes -ING (I suggest sending it), or the person goes to the end (explain the delay to Ada). Students say I suggest you to, recommend me to, explained me. On screen, a task board: the right line pins the owner’s badge on the card; a line with no room for the person leaves the card with NO OWNER.

RECOMMEND IN THE UK

recommend + person + to (I recommend Marina to …) is also used in British English. This lesson trains the international form, recommend that + person + plain verb. If the student says the British form, accept it and show the other. suggest + person + to is always wrong.

THE PLAIN VERB

After suggest / recommend / insist that, the verb is plain: she take, he be. That she is there is heard in speech; be is the formal target.

THE NEW PAGES

  • II Ada’s Minutes: read the minutes, tap the six verb + person forms, finish the rule (five lines). The rule then stays in the corner.
  • III: six verbs, CHAIR or NO CHAIR.
  • IV Keep the Person: build each line from the pieces out loud, then Check. Then the -ING version.
  • V The -s Comes Off: one word is wrong in each line; the student says the plain verb, then Check.
  • VII Eleven O’Clock: you read the boss line; the student says the decision first, then Check; ask the question under it.

THE MEETING

Scene VII: you chair. Every decision the student makes must name a person; after each line ask “Who exactly?”. Then swap: they chair, you make vague suggestions.

THE HOT SEAT (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the meeting, your student is a work advisor and you are Mr. Costa, the owner of a small company. Ask the four questions, wait five seconds in silence, and only accept advice with a name and a day on it (I suggest that Ada send …, I’d advise you to …). Your limit is on your screen only: vague answers get the PUSH line. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (watch for “suggest you to”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Ten to Eleven, VII Eleven O’Clock, VIII The Hot Seat, IX Your Advice.
  • Listen: no recordings in this lesson; your voice chairs the meeting.
  • Focus: II Ada’s Minutes, III Who Has a Chair?, IV Keep the Person, V The -s Comes Off, VI Four O’Clock.
  • Write: X, their suggestion with a real name. It goes to their My notes.
