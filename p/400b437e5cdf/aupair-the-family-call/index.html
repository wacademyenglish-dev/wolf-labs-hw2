The Family Call · Au Pair · Lesson 9 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the video interview with a host family: answering the host mom’s first questions (recorded voice) and asking good questions back. Grammar: question forms (do / does, How old are…?, What does… look like?, will I…?).

The story: The Parkers (fictional): Emma, 6, and Leo, 3. Mrs. Parker’s questions are recorded; in the last scene, you play Mrs. Parker and the student asks.

About 49 min: I · What to Ask (7) · II · Make a Good Question (8) · III · The Host Mom Asks (14) · IV · Now You Ask (9) · V · Your Verdict (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “If you could ask a family only ONE question, which one?”

The program requires a phone/video interview with the family before departure. Practice the attitude: warm, honest, specific. Remind the student that she is also choosing the family.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

What do the kids like? · How old are they? · What does a typical day look like? · How many hours will I work? · I would… · I have taken care of…

WHAT TO ASK

Cultural Care Au Pair, “Matching tips 2: What interview questions to ask a host family” (1 min, captions checked on 15/10/2026).

MAKE A GOOD QUESTION

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THE HOST MOM ASKS

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

NOW YOU ASK

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write her five best questions for real family calls.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

What the kids like?, How many years has she?, When I will start?, one-word answers. A2+: one correction per answer is enough.

YOUR VERDICT (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After both sides of the call, the student decides like a jury. Round 1: evidence from two calls: the Parkers (two kids, near Chicago, never more than 45 hours, the last au pair still visits) and the Kims (fictional: one girl, California, a car only for her, but no answer about the hours). Round 2: one last question before saying yes. There is no right answer, but praise any answer that notices the missing information. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (What does… look like?, How many hours will I…?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
