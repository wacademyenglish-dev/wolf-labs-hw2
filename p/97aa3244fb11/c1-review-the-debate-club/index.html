The Debate Club · C1 Unit 7 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 7 (Argue and persuade). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: The final of a university debate club (invented). The board is twelve motions; on each square the student argues for one minute, conceding, contrasting and persuading. Then pick-a-side questions and feedback.

About 48 min: I · The Die (8) · II · The Motions (16) · III · Pick a Side (7) · IV · The Coach (6) · V · After the Debate (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Roll the die. The number chooses the challenge.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

synthesis: in short, the bottom line is · granted, be that as it may · persuasion: imagine, what if, mixed conditionals · feedback: what I liked was, it might be worth · on the contrary × on the other hand · assuming, given that, providing

THE DIE

Tap Roll the die: the number chooses one of the six topics (no repeats until all six are done). The student does the challenge from memory.

THE MOTIONS

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

PICK A SIDE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

AFTER THE DEBATE

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

on the contrary (for on the other hand), be that as it is, what if we would, it might worth, given that (for a hypothesis), in short of. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE COACH (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Pick a Side, your student becomes the team coach and you play Sam, a nervous first-year debater ten minutes before the final. Ask Sam’s four questions; the student answers in role with the Unit 7 tools (what I liked was, it might be worth, granted / be that as it may, assuming). Sam’s secret: Sam opens if the coach praises one real strength first and allows one note card. Wait five seconds in silence after each question, use the PUSH line (only you see it), recast quietly while they talk, then close the plan on screen and ask for one or two self-corrections.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
