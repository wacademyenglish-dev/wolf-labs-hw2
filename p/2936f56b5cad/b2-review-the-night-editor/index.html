The Night Editor · B2 Unit 6 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 6 (The passive that reports). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Your student is the night editor at The Evening Lamp (an invented newspaper). It prints at midnight. You are three reporters with three unconfirmed stories: a ferry stopped in the bay, a lost painting found in a garage, and the town’s oldest bakery closing.

About 42 min: I · Memory (8) · II · Twenty Minutes to Print (15) · III · Page One (7) · IV · On the Rooftop (8) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Open two cards. Find the pair, then make the sentence.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

is said to, is thought to · it is believed that, is expected to · to have been done (the past) · being done · modal passives: must be approved, could have been avoided · have / get something done

MEMORY

A memory game. The student opens two cards; when they match, the student makes a sentence with the pair (example under Show answer).

TWENTY MINUTES TO PRINT

You are each reporter. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student dictates the headline and first line with the reporting passive. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

ON THE ROOFTOP

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

it is said to stop yesterday, it believes that, it could be avoided (past), it is expected to sell (for to be sold), the photos must approve, I need to have fixed my laptop. B2: one correction per answer is enough.

PAGE ONE (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After checking the stories, the editor decides the front page: four headlines, only one on top. The student taps them in order and explains the top one and the last one with is said to, is thought to and is expected to. Then tap Show my teacher’s order, compare, and agree on one top 3 together. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the review has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Memory.
  • Out loud: II · Twenty Minutes to Print, III · Page One and IV · On the Rooftop.
  • Focus: V · I Can.
