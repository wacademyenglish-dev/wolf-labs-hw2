We Would Always · The Home Movie

would carries what you did again and again in the past, and nothing else: not how things were, not the thing that happened once. The screen is a home movie from every summer at a grandmother’s house by the water: a repeated action becomes a filmed frame; a state leaves the frame empty, because you can’t film being or having. Then comes the summer she sold the house, and the past simple closes the story.

THE FILM TEST

When your student is stuck, ask: Could you film it? If yes, WOULD can carry it. If not, only USED TO.

THE PERIOD FIRST

When I was ten, we would… Without the period, WOULD sounds like a condition.

ONE TIME KILLS WOULD

Scene IV: one more time, one last time, that summer take the past simple. Watch the two refusals: He wouldn’t come and nobody would talk sound like somebody refused.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I would have long hair, we would be happy, WOULD with no period, she would sell the house that spring, I use to.

BRING IT BACK? (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Three old summer habits (no internet, the same cake, the long drive). Your student chooses green, yellow or red and says why with WOULD or USED TO. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
