Strengths and Weaknesses · WOLF HIRED · Lesson 3 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: strengths and weaknesses: I tend to…, I used to… / Now I…, I’m working on… by…, I’ve started…; strengths with proof (I’m very… For example,…; One of my strengths is…); avoiding fake weaknesses and clichés.

The story: A recruiter’s story formula, answers to sort into good and red flag, then the student’s own weaknesses and strengths.

About 43 min: I · Always Be Telling Stories (7) · II · Good Answer or Red Flag? (9) · III · Weakness + Action + Result (16) · IV · The Team Council (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What would your last boss say is your best quality? And the thing you should improve?”

Help the student choose a real weakness that is not a deal-breaker for their target job. Many Brazilians answer with “perfeccionista”; acknowledge it, then dig for a real one.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I tend to…, so I’ve started… · I used to… Now I… · I’m working on… by… · I’m very… For example,… · One of my strengths is… · I pick things up quickly.

ALWAYS BE TELLING STORIES

Self Made Millennial, “What is your greatest weakness?” answer sample (0:28–2:30).

GOOD ANSWER OR RED FLAG?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

WEAKNESS + ACTION + RESULT

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write their weakness answer and one strength with proof for their target job.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I am perfectionist (I’m a perfectionist, and avoid it), I tend to stressed (I tend to get stressed), I am working in my English (on my English), I am very organizated (organized). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE TEAM COUNCIL (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the transformations, the student sits on the council of a fictional company and votes three proposals: a “weakness Monday” every January, five minutes of feedback every Friday, and no more weakness question in interviews. Yes, propose a change, or no: there is no right answer, but the vote must come with a personal example. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. Recast quietly while they talk (I tend to…, I’m working on…, I used to…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word: the student proposes their own rule, and you vote no.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
