Not Yet · The Goal Board

Tomorrow I travel is correct English: it just sounds like a habit or a timetable, not a plan. This hour builds am / is / are going to for plans and for things you can see coming. The screen is a goal board with three lanes (this week, this month, this year) and dark sticky notes. The student fills it by speaking; then you become the coach and ask about the goals. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

am / is / are + going to + the plain verb, with any subject. A plan (I’m going to call her) and evidence (It’s going to rain) use the same form. Negative and question: only the first word moves (isn’t going to, Are you going to…?). Say gonna, write going to. The mistakes: Tomorrow I travel (the habit trap) and I going to (the missing partner).

YOUR SATURDAY (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

A free Saturday and three plans: sleep late, go to the beach, clean the house. Your student taps them in order and says it with going to; then they open your order and you agree on one together. There is no right order. Say your order first, then wait five seconds in silence while they choose, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) for one more question. The starters keep I’m going to and I’m not going to in their mouth. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I This Weekend, IV Look Again (six numbers on the photo of an empty room), VI Gonna, VII Your Saturday (a light ranking), VIII The Goal Board, IX The Coach, X Certain, or Maybe?
  • Focus: II The Missing Piece, III Every Week, or One Day? (two calendars: every Saturday marked, or one day marked), V Tomorrow I Travel
  • Write: XI Not Yet, the goal board in writing, with the red wavy line for I going to, Tomorrow I travel, going to traveling, I’m go to, amn’t and gonna in writing. It goes to their My notes.

YOU ARE THE COACH

Scene IX takes the first three notes from the student’s board (or three spares if the board is short). For each goal, ask two of the four questions. Push for reasons and dates. Strong students: What is going to happen if you don’t?

SCENE I

Don’t correct. Tap what you hear; the two classic mistakes have their own buttons. Scene XI brings them back.
