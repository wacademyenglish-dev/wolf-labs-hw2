The Same Name · Lost and Found

The student takes over the school’s lost and found counter. On screen, a dark wooden cabinet with seven drawers, seven names, and two of them are Marta. Every object has a clue; the student finds the owner, says it with the owner first (Pedro’s jacket) and asks Whose…? Then people come to the counter, and the answers get short: It’s mine. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

The owner first, with ’s: Pedro’s jacket, never the jacket of Pedro (of is for things: the door of the car). Two-word names take the s at the end: Marta Reis’s cap. Whose…? asks for the owner (not who’s). When the thing is clear, drop it: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.

SCENE IV · YOU READ

The sentence appears only on your side (the student doesn’t see it). Read it once, at normal speed; the student taps the word that got the s.

WHAT IS THE WORST? (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Behind the lost and found counter, your student ranks three things: my phone, my keys, my friend’s book. What is the worst to lose? Answer first yourself, as a model; then let them tap their order, wait five seconds in silence, and ask the PUSH line (only you see it): a yes / no question. “Show my teacher’s order” shows yours. Recast quietly while they talk (the book of my friend → my friend’s book); at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves. About 5 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. There are three; use one when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Desk, VI At the Counter (you play each visitor), IX Your Things.
  • Listen: IV The Sound.
  • Focus: II The Owner Comes First, III Seven Tags, V The Drawers, VII Mine, Yours, His, VIII What Is the Worst? (ranking, out loud).
  • Write: X, tags for their own things, with the red wavy line for the … of my sister, who’s bag, it’s my., mine car and your’s. It goes to their My notes.
