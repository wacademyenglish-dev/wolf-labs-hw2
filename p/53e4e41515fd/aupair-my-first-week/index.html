My First Week · Au Pair · Lesson 24 of 24 · Final Project

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the final project: the coordinator’s first-day message, the arrival conversation with the host mom (recorded), and the student’s complete Safety Card (contacts, help lines, rights, key phrases, someone in Brazil).

The story: Ana arrives at the Parkers’ house in Illinois (fictional). Karen, the local coordinator (fictional, phone 555-0142), writes. Mrs. Parker welcomes her.

About 48 min: I · A Message From Your Coordinator (8) · II · Arrival Day (14) · III · The Jury (7) · IV · My Safety Card (14) · V · I Can (5).

Open with: “Close your eyes: you are at the door of your host family’s house. What do you feel?”

Program rules used here (checked on 15/10/2026): a parent or responsible adult stays at home for the first three days; the local coordinator contacts the family and the au pair within 48 hours and meets them in person within two weeks. Check the help line numbers again before each new group (j1visa.state.gov and humantraffickinghotline.org). The Safety Card must have the student’s REAL contacts: help her complete it after the class if she doesn’t have them yet (agency, coordinator, consulate).

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Thank you so much! I’m so happy to be here. · Could I have…? · Could we write my schedule down? · I don’t have any allergies. · Honestly, I’m… · the Safety Card

A MESSAGE FROM YOUR COORDINATOR

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

ARRIVAL DAY

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

MY SAFETY CARD

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. This is the end of the course: congratulate the student, stamp the course, and ask her to send you a photo of her Safety Card on paper.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

forgetting the house address, only digital contacts, no fixed time to talk with Brazil. B1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE JURY (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the Safety Card, the student is the jury for Ana’s first week (fictional): alone with the kids on day 2, no schedule on paper on day 5, and the host dad asking for her passport on day 6. Each case has three pieces of evidence; the student chooses talk to the family, call Karen, or wait and see, and says why with the evidence. Two program rules are fixed (an adult at home for the first three days; the passport stays with the au pair); the rest is opinion, so accept good reasons. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (Ana should …, I need to check with my coordinator first, I’m not comfortable with that); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The phrases they use go straight to the Safety Card.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
