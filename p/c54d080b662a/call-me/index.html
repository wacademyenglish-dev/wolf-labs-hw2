Call Me · The Contact Book

Lia’s birthday is on Saturday, and the party is a surprise. The student organizes it by phone. The contact book has eight people; each wire only connects to the party board with the right object pronoun. On Friday night the phone keeps ringing (you play the callers), and the party list fills up. At the end, the student writes five messages to the party group. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

me, you, him, her, it, us, them after a verb (call him) and after with, for, to, about (with her). In spoken Portuguese the subject form after a verb sounds normal, so I saw he sounds fine to the student: scene IV is for that. call takes no to. And Portuguese puts the pronoun before the verb; English never does.

THIS OR THAT (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the party week, three light choices: buy or make Lia’s gift, tell her or keep the secret, dance with your friends or cook for them. Answer first yourself, as a model. Then let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, and ask the PUSH line (only you see it): a yes / no question. Recast quietly while they talk (tell she → tell her); at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves. About 5 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. There are three; use one when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Good News, V Say It Fast, VII The Phone Keeps Ringing, VIII This or That (would you rather), IX Your Own Contacts.
  • Focus: II Two Teams, III The Contact Book, IV I Saw He, VI Two in One Line.
  • Write: X The Group Chat, with the red wavy line for tell she, with they, call to him, to I and explain me. It goes to their My notes.

YOU ARE THE AUDIO

There is no recording in this lesson. In scene V you say the written line and the spoken one (Call him · CALL-im) twice, then hand it over; the fourth repetition counts. In scene VII you read each caller’s question in their voice and wait for a full answer: an answer that stops at the verb (Bring…) gets “bring what?”.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

Everything the student taps moves on both screens: the wires, the answered calls, the party list. G opens this guide.
