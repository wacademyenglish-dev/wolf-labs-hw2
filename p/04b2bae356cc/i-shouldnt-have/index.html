I Shouldn’t Have Wished · Obsession · The Rewind

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: One wish, and no way to cancel it.

The grammar: Should Have: I shouldn’t have made the wish.

The class (about 56 min): I Warm-up and Put It in Order · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V The Worst Mistake · VI Should Have · VII The Rewind · VIII Your Rewind · IX A Letter to Last Year. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Bear’s wish now. In the trailer, what should he have done instead? Guess.”

Obsession (2026), a horror film about love and a wish. Bear is too shy to tell his friend Nikki he loves her; in a strange little shop he buys a branch that gives you one wish, and wishes Nikki loved him more than anyone in the world. It works, too well, and it can’t be cancelled: “as long as you live”. The grammar: past modals of regret and criticism: should / shouldn’t have + participle, could have (there was another way), couldn’t have (it was impossible), needn’t have (I did it, but it wasn’t necessary), and What should I have done?

A note for the teacher: the trailer has jump scares and screams. Warn the student first. The times come from the trailer’s captions (07/10), told in our own words. Our older lesson One Wish uses the same film from the side of the wish.

Warm-up and Put It in Order (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Put It in Order: four moments of Bear’s story, shuffled. The student taps them in order (“First… then…”); then the cards show the real order, and the student retells it, judging every step (he should have…, he could have…).

The words (II): flirted, goodnight, wish, sudden and stuck, before the video, in turn the cards and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, A Letter to Last Year): the student writes to last year: what they should and shouldn’t have done, what they could have done, what they needn’t have worried about. The corrector marks should of, shouldn’t have went, could asked, needn’t have to. It goes to My notes.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a willow tree at night with a faint pink glow. I: Warm-up and Put It in Order. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: four clues, one at a time (a shy man, a little shop, a willow branch, one wish); the student guesses what happens and taps “I know it!”. Watch: two short tasks, each line with a ▶ time (tap it and the video jumps there): Which word did you hear? (wish / which, thought / taught…) and One word is wrong (hated / loved…). After: six cards (asked Nikki out, read the rules, known the wish had a price…); the student taps a card and then a box, “He should have …” or “He couldn’t have …”, and says the sentence. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Worst Mistake: the student ranks Bear’s five mistakes, then compares with the teacher’s order. VI, Should Have: six texts from Bear, each with one wrong word (I shouldn’t have make the wish…). The student says the right word, taps Check, and the rule board appears and stays in the corner. VII, The Rewind: a VHS tape with its two reels and the VCR display, and six cards (“2:00 AM · I · not make the wish”). The student says the sentence and taps the card: the left reel grows, the right one shrinks, the display shows the time and the sentence goes on the list. With all six, the tape is at the start: before the wish. VIII, Your Rewind: six questions about the student’s life (Did you buy something you didn’t need?…); the student taps yes or no and says the sentence. IX: writing.

THE COMMON ERRORS

shouldn’t have make, shouldn’t had made, could asked, should of told, needn’t have to buy, shouldn’t have drank / went / knew, shouldn’t drink so much last night, What I should have said?, and must have for a regret (it is a deduction).

THE WORST MISTAKE (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, a ranking: Bear’s five mistakes (he never asked Nikki out, the branch from a shop at night, the wish without asking about the rules, he didn’t tell his friends, he waited too long to call). The student taps them from the worst to the least bad and explains the top one and the last one with should have / could have / couldn’t have / needn’t have. Then tap Show my teacher’s order, compare, and agree on one top 3 together. There is no right order. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (should have make, should of, could asked); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Put It in Order, V The Worst Mistake, VII The Rewind and VIII Your Rewind.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Should Have.
  • Write: VIII A Letter to Last Year.
