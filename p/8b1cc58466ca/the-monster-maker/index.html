The Monster Maker · Studio 13 · Night Shoot

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A night shoot at a horror film studio. The student is the creature designer.

The grammar: The Rule: He’s got long black hair.

Short class (about 48 min): I Before the Shoot · III The Trailer · V The Rule · VI The Creature Sheet · VII Casting · X The Scariest · XI Your Creature. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 62 min.

Open with: “Do you watch horror films?”

A night shoot at Studio 13, on a horror film. The student is the creature designer: they fill in the creature sheet part by part (the portrait changes on the screen), do the casting with the files of eight classic film monsters, and at the wrap party draw the guest in costume that you describe. The grammar is has / has got to describe: He’s got scars = He has scars; hasn’t got; Has it got…? Yes, it has. And the order size + color + thing: two small yellow eyes, with no -s on colors. The color of the body uses is: He’s grey.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: the official trailer of Frankenstein (2025, Guillermo del Toro). Part 1 (0:00–1:00): the creature remembers, “I remember… pieces.” Part 2 (1:00–2:23): “Victor Frankenstein. My creator.” Each part stops by itself. After the two parts, the lesson ends the trailer with ONE screen: the trailer stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again; the time buttons play only the part where the creature speaks, up to 1:40, and each one stops by itself), with the creature’s portrait under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines about what he says. Every right answer adds one part to the portrait. On a Lie the student says the true line (a short answer is fine). When all six are done, the director asks the last question (monster or victim?): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud, with has got / hasn’t got.

THE CREATURE SHEET · CASTING · THE WRAP PARTY

Scene VI: six parts; the student taps a choice, the portrait changes, and the student says the sentence. Scene VII: eight casting files (Dracula, the Mummy, the Werewolf, the Witch, the Zombie, the Creature, the Ghost, the Invisible Man), each with its film and its features; the secret one has a SECRET tag on your screen only. The student asks Has it got fangs?, you answer Yes, it has / No, it hasn’t, and anybody can tap a file to put it out. Scene IX: you read a guest’s description twice; the student draws on paper or on the board, then you show the guest. At the end, the student describes a costume and you draw it.

WHY PUMPKINS?

Scene VIII: a short A1 story about the Irish turnip lanterns that became American jack-o’-lanterns.

THE MISTAKES

He have got, He has got grey, hair black, two reds eyes, It don’t have, Have he got…?

The words (II): maker, remember, pieces, creator and run, before the video, in match the columns and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

THE SCARIEST (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the creature file, your student ranks three monsters for the film: the werewolf, the zombie, the ghost. There is no right order. They tap 1, 2, 3 and say their number one with It’s got / It hasn’t got; then show your own order and agree on one top 3. Wait five seconds in silence before helping; the PUSH line (only you see it) is a quick yes or no. Recast quietly; at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Before the Shoot, VI The Creature Sheet, VII Casting and X The Scariest.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer, IV Line by Line, VIII Why Pumpkins? and IX The Wrap Party.
  • Focus: V The Rule.
  • Write: XI Your Creature.
