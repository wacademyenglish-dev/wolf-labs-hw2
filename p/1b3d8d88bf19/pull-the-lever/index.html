Do I Have to Pull the Lever? · The Good Place · The Point Board

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A woman in the wrong place, and a teacher who has to save her.

The grammar: Have To: Have to, has to, don’t have to. And the question: Do I have to…?

The class (about 49 min): I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie · III Place Your Bets · IV The Clip · V Line by Line · VI Have To · VII The Point Board · VIII Ethics Class · IX The Debate. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Chidi now. In the clip, what does he have to decide? Guess.”

The Good Place (2016–2020) is the American comedy by Michael Schur, with Kristen Bell as Eleanor and Ted Danson as Michael. Eleanor dies and arrives, by mistake, in a perfect little town for very good people; Chidi, a professor of ethics, has to teach her to be good, and every action in life has points. In this scene Michael turns Chidi’s class into the trolley problem: five workers on one track, one on the other, and a lever. The grammar: have to / has to (necessary), don’t / doesn’t have to (not necessary), questions Do I have to…? Does she have to…?, the past had to, and Michael’s line there have to be…

A note for the teacher: the scene has a trolley and a comic, cartoonish accident; nothing violent is on the lesson screens, but watch it once before class. The clip has no captions on YouTube now, so the five frames on the film strip are estimated, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; A2: short answers are fine. 2 · Two Truths and a Lie: three rounds about the show (Eleanor, the points, Chidi). The student reads the three sentences, says which one is the lie and why, then taps. In the last round, they say two truths and one lie about their own life, and you guess.

The words (II): choice, decision, simulation, real and switch, before the video, in hot seat and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Debate): “You have to pull the lever.” The student picks a side (pull it / don’t pull it); the teacher takes the other one. Three reasons on each side and three helping phrases. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an old railway track that splits in two at dusk, with an iron lever at the fork. I: Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie. III: Place Your Bets, three bets on the clip (tap Check after the video). IV: the clip in three steps. Before: a 30-second clock (“No time, dude!”): the student says what they have to do this week. Watch: Which word did you hear? Five lines, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: four questions about the clip; the student taps the right answer, with a ▶ to hear it again. V: five frames on the film strip. VI, Have To: Chidi’s notes, six lines with one wrong word each. The student says the right word, then taps Check to see the fixes; only then does the rule board appear, and it stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Point Board: the board of afterlife points and the little pink trolley on six ties. Six deeds to sort into two boxes: necessary (have to / has to) or not necessary (don’t / doesn’t have to). Every deed in the right box adds its points and moves the trolley one tie. With all six, the student chooses: pull the lever or don’t, and the board answers with a question. VIII, Ethics Class: three real-life dilemmas (the wallet, the test, the 11 p.m. message). The student taps how they answer, and the conversation appears with have to and don’t have to. IX: the debate.

THE COMMON ERRORS

I have that choose (have to), Eleanor have to, they has to, don’t must, haven’t to, doesn’t has to, Have I to…?, Do I must…?, Do Tahani have to…?, have tell, had to planned, there has to be stakes.

PLACE YOUR BETS (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Just before the clip, the student bets on the story: what Chidi does, if the workers are real, and who is on the other track. Let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to ask a little more. Don’t tap Check yet: watch the clip, then come back (or open the bets on the screen after the clip) and tap Check on each one. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (have to / has to / don’t have to); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie, III Place Your Bets, VII The Point Board, VIII Ethics Class and IX The Debate.
  • Listen: II The Words, IV The Clip and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Have To.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
