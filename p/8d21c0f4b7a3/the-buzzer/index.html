The Buzzer · A Game Show About the Future

The school rule says WILL is the far future and GOING TO is the near one. It’s wrong. Both are the future; what matters is when the decision happened. If the plan or the proof was already there, it’s going to. If it’s born while you speak (an offer, a promise, a refusal, an opinion), it’s will. The screen is a game show: a stage, a scoreboard and a red buzzer.

THE SCOREBOARD · THE BUZZER

Every right answer on the first try adds 100 points; read the score out loud like a host. Scenes V and VIII: the student presses, then answers in two seconds; speed matters more than polish. Tap “Good answer” to add the points.

NO WILL TO

I will to help is the error of the hour. WILL takes the plain verb. And will for a plan that’s already booked.

THEY HOST

Scene IX: the student asks you. Answer for real. Will you…? asks you to do something; Are you going to…? asks about a plan.

THE PRODUCER (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

A light negotiation. Your student just won tonight’s show; you are the producer and you want them back on TV next Saturday. Three short questions, then the deal on the screen. You can add a second prize (the PUSH line, only you see it), but no money. Wait five seconds in silence after each question. Listen for the door: a plan that already exists is going to; an offer, a promise or a refusal born right now is I’ll / I won’t. Recast quietly while they talk (never I will to); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-Up, IV Your Week, V The Buzzer Round, VII The Producer, VIII Promise, Offer, Refuse, IX Now You Host.
  • Focus: II What Happens Next, III Two Doors, VI The Forecast.
  • Write: X, a plan, a decision now and a promise. It goes to their My notes.
