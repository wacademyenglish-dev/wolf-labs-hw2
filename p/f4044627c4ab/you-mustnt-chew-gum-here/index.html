You Mustn’t Chew Gum Here · The Sign Wall

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A review lesson: it works as a follow-up to a lesson on have to / don’t have to.

The grammar: Three Signs: MUST is a rule. MUSTN’T is a wall. DON’T HAVE TO is an open door.

Short class (about 43 min): I You and Rules · III An Insider’s Guide · V Lia’s Diary · VI Three Signs · VII Passport Control · X The Bet · XI The Flight Home. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 64 min.

Open with: “When you were a kid, what were the rules in your house?”

A review lesson: it works as a follow-up to a lesson on have to / don’t have to. Lia travels through Japan with a notebook: every city gives her a rule, and it becomes a sign-sticker in her rule book. Your student watches a short guide to good manners in Japan, reads Lia’s diary, goes through passport control, and ends with the rules of their own home. The center: mustn’t is a wall (NOT ALLOWED); don’t have to is a free door (NOT NECESSARY).

THE VIDEO

Scene III: sealed until your student answers the two questions, then four chapters that stop by themselves. At 1:25 the guide says Talking on trains: just don’t do it; at 1:32, if you do have to have a chat, just keep it down: a ban and a softer rule, side by side. After the video (page III, step 3): ONE screen. The video stays on the left, with Lia’s rule book under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines from the video (tap a time to hear that moment again). Every right answer sticks a new sign in the book. The two train lines (1:26 “just don’t do it” and 1:31 “just keep it down”) are still there: a ban and a softer rule, side by side. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, Yuki asks for one rule for her first day: the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud, with must, mustn’t or don’t have to. (The video stops by itself at 2:14, before the last tip.)

THE TRAP

Students often mix not allowed and not necessary. They say You mustn’t pay, it’s free (they mean: not necessary) and You don’t have to smoke here (they mean: not allowed). Ask: Is it a wall, or a free door?

THE PASSPORT

Scene V is the pressure: eight signs, one stamp each. Read the sign; your student says the rule at once.

THE FACTS

In Singapore you can’t buy chewing gum in shops, and you can’t bring it into the country. In Japan tips are not expected, and swimsuits are not allowed in a traditional hot spring.

The words (II): respect, customs, trash, polite and frustrated, before the video, in match the columns and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE BET (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Lia’s rule book is full, so your student bets on the rules of three new places: a Sunday in Germany, a sandwich in Venice, a tip in New York. Let them bet and say why, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Only then tap Check the real rule. A wrong bet is fine: ask what they think of the real rule. Recast must / mustn’t / don’t have to quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and Rules, V Lia’s Diary, VII Passport Control, VIII Your House Rules, IX The Houseguest and X The Bet (they bet first, then check the real rule).
  • Listen: II The Words, III An Insider’s Guide and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Three Signs.
  • Write: XI The Flight Home.
