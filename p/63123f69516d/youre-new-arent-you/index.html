You’re New, Aren’t You? · B1 Unit 9 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new B1 lesson on question tags: positive sentence → negative tag, negative → positive, the right auxiliary (do / does / did, can, have, will), the special ones (I’m…, aren’t I? Let’s…, shall we?) and falling intonation for small talk.

The story: The student’s first night at a cooking class (invented school). Maya, at the same station, has done the class three times. The teacher looks serious but tells jokes all night.

About 45 min: I · Breaking the Ice (9) · II · The English “né?” (10) · III · Add the Tag (8) · IV · The Arena (8) · V · Your Turn to Break the Ice (10).

Open with: “How do you start a conversation with a stranger at a party or a class?”

Brazilians use “né?” for everything, and in English it often becomes “no?” or “right?”. Tags sound friendly and natural in small talk. Model the falling intonation (you’re almost sure) and the rising one (you really ask). In the hot seat, be a friendly stranger at the class.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

+ sentence, − tag · − sentence, + tag · do / does / did when there is no auxiliary · can / have / will in the tag · I’m…, aren’t I? Let’s…, shall we? · falling × rising intonation

BREAKING THE ICE

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

THE ENGLISH “NÉ?”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

ADD THE TAG

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

YOUR TURN TO BREAK THE ICE

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

It’s hot, no? (isn’t it?), You’re new, are you? (aren’t you?), He likes it, isn’t he? (doesn’t he?), You went, don’t you? (didn’t you?), I’m late, amn’t I? (aren’t I?). B1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE ARENA (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the student breaks the ice with you, they defend a side: a cooking class full of strangers, or videos at home alone. One minute on their side, with question tags on the starters; then Swap sides and one minute for the other side. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence before they start, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly (“doesn’t he?” for “isn’t he?”) and listen for falling intonation; at the end, ask them to fix one or two tags themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Breaking the Ice.
  • Focus: II · The English “né?” and III · Add the Tag.
  • Out loud: IV · The Arena and V · Your Turn to Break the Ice.
