If He Hadn’t Gone In · Solo Leveling · The Replay

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A web novel, a webtoon, an anime, and one door.

The grammar: If He Hadn’t: If he hadn’t gone in, none of this would have happened.

The class (about 58 min): I Warm-up and The System · II The Trailer · III Scene by Scene · IV The Jury · V If He Hadn’t · VI The Replay · VII Your What-Ifs · VIII The Debate. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the story now. In the trailer, what would have happened if Jinwoo hadn’t gone in?”

Solo Leveling (2024), a Japanese anime from a Korean web novel. Sung Jinwoo, the weakest hunter in the world, goes into a hidden double dungeon; inside, giant statues come alive and almost everyone dies. He survives, and the System chooses him. If he hadn’t gone in, none of his story would have happened. The grammar: the third conditional: if + had + participle in the if part, would / might / could + have + participle in the result, for a past that did not happen. No would in the if part.

A note for the teacher: the same trailer as lesson 296 (Japanese voices, English subtitles on the picture), with other moments. Fights and a little blood, fast. The times come from the subtitles on the picture (24/09), told in our own words. Lesson 296 was the blue System window; this one is the ink page.

Warm-up and The System (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the anime under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The System: the screen becomes the blue window that only Jinwoo can see. Messages arrive one at a time; the student reads each one out loud and imagines the other past (if he hadn’t…, he would have…). At the end they answer the System, speaking.

The ending (VIII, The Debate): “One choice can decide a whole life.” The student chooses a side, the teacher takes the other one, with if + had…, would have… Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an ink drawing of the dungeon door. I: Warm-up and The System. II: the trailer in three steps. Before: two taps about doors, luck and choices; the student says why. Watch: four taps about what the student sees, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment (the times come from the English subtitles on the image; the video has Japanese voices, so there are no spoken lines to quote). After: a counter (Got it! for each true sentence with If he hadn’t …, with a model to show) and a slider (was going in alone right or wrong?). III: five frames on the film strip. Tap one: the clip plays and stops by itself, then two or three tap tasks (True or Lie, or choose by meaning; no ▶, because this trailer has no spoken lines to time). On a Lie the student says the true line. When all are answered, the moment and its grammar open. IV, The Jury: three cases from the trailer (Jinwoo, the party, the System), three pieces of evidence each; the student gives a verdict and defends it. V: six pieces to sort into three boxes (the if part, the result, a question); when all six are right, the rule appears. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VI, The Replay: a manhwa page with six crooked ink panels; each one says what really happened, in red. For each panel the student builds the other road from the clue out loud, taps Check, and blue ink wipes over the panel and writes the sentence. At six, the page is rewritten. VII, Your What-Ifs: three friends talk about the past (a new job, a missed bus, an old team). The student picks how to answer and the talk appears: read it out loud, you are the friend. VIII: the debate.

THE COMMON ERRORS

if he didn’t go in, … would have, if he would have been, none of this would happen, wouldn’t laughed, might escaped, could saved, would have took, hadn’t chose, will have died, What you would have done?, would you had done.

THE JURY (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Scene by Scene, the student is the jury for three cases from the trailer: Jinwoo going into the hidden dungeon (brave or foolish?), the party that leaves him alone with the boss (guilty?), and the System (a gift or a trap?). Each case has three pieces of evidence from the trailer; the student gives a verdict and defends it with the third conditional (if he had quit, his family would have…). There is no right verdict, and the final debate is not repeated here. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own life. Recast quietly while they talk (if he didn’t go, if he would have been, would happen); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About seven minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The System, IV The Jury, VI The Replay, VII Your What-Ifs and VIII The Debate.
  • Listen: II The Trailer and III Scene by Scene.
  • Focus: V If He Hadn’t.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
