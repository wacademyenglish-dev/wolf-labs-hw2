The Big Party · A2 Unit 4 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 4 (How much, how many, and who). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Your student is in charge of a friend’s birthday party tonight. The plan happens on a board game around the table.

About 44 min: I · Speed Round (6) · II · The Party Board (15) · III · What Makes a Party? (6) · IV · The Invitation (6) · V · Would You Rather? (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Sixty seconds. Ready? Start the clock.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

a few / many (count) × a little / much (don’t count) · a lot of · too much / too many, enough · some / any / every / none · somebody / anybody / nobody, something / anything / nothing · a / an / the

SPEED ROUND

A 60-second speed round. Tap Start the clock, then Right or Pass for each item; the answer is on your screen. At the end of class, play again and beat the score.

THE PARTY BOARD

A board game around the table. Tap Roll the die: the token moves and the square shows a task that mixes the grammar of the unit. The student does it out loud; tap Done and roll again until the end of the board.

THE INVITATION

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

how much cups, a few ice, many juice, there isn’t nothing, everybody are, a apple. A2: one correction per answer is enough.

WHAT MAKES A PARTY? (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the board, your student ranks five things a great party needs and defends their first and last with a lot of / a few / enough / nobody. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Then open your own order and agree on one top 3 together. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · Speed Round and VI · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · The Party Board, III · What Makes a Party? and V · Would You Rather?.
  • Write: IV · The Invitation.
