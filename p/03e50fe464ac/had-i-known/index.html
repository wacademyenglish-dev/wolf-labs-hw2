Had I Known · The Invert Switch

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: In January 2009, a plane landed on the Hudson River in New York.

The grammar: Drop the IF. Move the verb to the front.

Short class (about 57 min): I Your Turning Points · III Seat 1D · V The Other Path · VI The Letter · VII The Black Box · VIII The Deal · IX Your Turning Point · X Your Speech. Skip if you’re short on time: IV Line by Line (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 57 min.

Open with: “What was a real turning point in your life?”

In January 2009, a plane landed on the Hudson River in New York. Everybody survived. The passenger in seat 1D gave a five-minute talk about three things he learned. The student rewrites his turning points with the most formal conditional English has: the inversion. Had I known (= if I had known), Were it not for (= if it weren’t for), Should you ever (= if you ever). Common errors: If had I known, Hadn’t he turned, Had not he, Was it not for, Should you needed. On screen, a cockpit panel: the INVERT switch drops the IF piece and moves the auxiliary to the front; and a route where the other path lights up when the student matches it.

THE PAGES

  • III Seat 1D: two guesses first (the screen says who was right), then six short chapters. Each chapter has a tap task (the words they hear, true or false). Tap the ▶ time to hear that moment again. After the video: why he says “I collect bad wines”, and fix four sentences that have one wrong word.
  • V The Other Path: five turning points. The student matches each one to its other path (an inversion); the route lights up. Then they read the other paths out loud, slowly, like a speech.
  • VI The Letter: the passenger’s letter to his daughter. The student reads it out loud, taps every inversion, then finishes the rule (five lines). The rule stays in the corner on VII and IX.
  • VII The Black Box: six IF-sentences from the recorder. The student says each one inverted, out loud, then taps Check: the switch flips.
  • VIII The Deal: a negotiation after the landing. The student is the passenger in seat 1D; you are the airline’s manager, with a secret limit.
  • IX Your Turning Point: you are the old friend and ask four questions. The student answers with an inversion, then taps Check.

FEAR OF FLYING

If a student is afraid of flying, say it first: the plane landed, and nobody died. There is nothing scary on screen: just a man on a stage.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: the talk (about five minutes), sealed until the first play, in six chapters. It ends with a question for you: how would you change?

A NOTE

Live lessons for the third conditional (B1) and mixed conditionals (B2) already exist. This one is only the inversion. “If he had…” is correct English: accept it, then push for HAD.

The words (II): explosion, impact, postpone, regret and eliminate, before the video, in mystery meaning and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE DEAL (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VIII is now a negotiation: a week after the landing, the airline calls the passenger in seat 1D. You are the manager; your secret limit is on your screen only ($5,000 and a free flight; start at $1,000). Ask the four questions, give ground slowly, and let the student close the deal on the screen. Wait five seconds in silence after each question, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) when they get stuck on money. They should argue with inversions (Should you raise the offer… / Were I to accept…). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Turning Points, V The Other Path, VIII The Deal and IX Your Turning Point.
  • Listen: II The Words, III Seat 1D and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Letter and VII The Black Box.
  • Write: X Your Speech.
