Have You Got a Minute? · The Coat Pockets

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Have got is have, said the way people say it, and it brings its own helper, so the question needs no DO.

The grammar: One Helper: HAVE GOT is HAVE, said the way people say it.

Short class (about 51 min): I Your Bag · II What People Say · III One Helper · IV The Short Way · V Two Words or Three · VI The Coat · VII Your Pick · XI My Bag. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX, X (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 71 min.

Open with: “What’s in your bag right now? Three things.”

Have got is have, said the way people say it, and it brings its own helper, so the question needs no DO. The screen is a coat with six closed pockets: a pocket opens only with the right question, and answers the short way. Then the word left over, the travel check (you check the student’s bag, then the student checks yours) and “not only things”. II: the student matches six situations to what people really say (Have you got a car? I haven’t got time. Yes, I have…) and says each line out loud. III: the three cards, then four lines with a broken part (Do you have got, I don’t have got, Yes, I got, I have got not): the student says the fix and taps Check. IV: the student sorts six people into ’VE got or ’S got (she, they, my brother, I, my parents, the dog), then says them fast. At the end, the student writes about their bag and their life. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

I’ve got / she’s got. Have you got…? (never Do you have got…?). I haven’t got (never I don’t have got, I have got not). Short answers repeat the helper: Yes, I have. / No, she hasn’t. (never Yes, I got). Have got is also for family, illness, plans, time and trouble.

YOUR PICK (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the coat, three light choices with have got: a magic pocket, one new thing for the house, and the question of the title, “Have you got a minute?”. Answer first yourself, then let your student choose. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it): a quick yes or no, answered the short way. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Bag, IV The Short Way, VI The Coat, VII Your Pick, VIII The Travel Check, X About You.
  • Listen: V Two Words or Three. HAVE or HAVE GOT: the clue is the T of got. The line appears after the right answer.
  • Focus: II What People Say, III One Helper, IX Not Only Things.
  • Write: XI My Bag, with the red wavy line for two helpers, got not, Yes, I got and the wrong ’s / ’ve. It goes to their My notes.

THE COAT

The student picks a pocket and asks out loud: Have you got keys in this pocket? You tap the thing they asked about. The pocket answers: No, I haven’t. (the guess is crossed out) or opens: Yes, I have. What is in each pocket never changes.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

The pockets, the bag and the shelves move on both screens. Every audio plays and pauses; 0.75× makes it slower. G opens this guide.
