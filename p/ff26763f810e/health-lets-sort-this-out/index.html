Let’s Sort This Out · English for Healthcare · Lesson 12 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: de-escalating an angry patient: listen without interrupting, name the feeling, apologize for the experience (I’m sorry that…), explain, offer what you can do with a time, and set a calm limit if the patient becomes abusive (and protect your safety).

The story: No video: pairs of answers, twelve reactions to classify, and four recorded angry patients (a long wait, “you never listen”, antibiotics on demand, “I want your manager”).

About 38 min: I · Words That Help (10) · II · Helps or Makes It Worse? (10) · III · Four Angry Patients (14) · IV · I Can (4).

Open with: “What do you usually say to an angry patient in Portuguese? Does it work?”

Safety first: if a situation becomes physically unsafe, the professional leaves and calls for help according to the workplace policy. In the role-play, the recorded patients are fast and loud on purpose: the student practices answering more slowly and lower.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I can see you’re really frustrated. · I’m sorry that you’ve had to… · Tell me what happened. · Here’s what I can do… · I’ll come back to you in … minutes. · I want to help, but I can’t continue if…

WORDS THAT HELP

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

HELPS OR MAKES IT WORSE?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

FOUR ANGRY PATIENTS

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write their three go-to sentences for angry patients.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Calm down., It’s not my fault., There’s nothing I can do., That’s the policy. (and nothing else). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: III · Four Angry Patients.
