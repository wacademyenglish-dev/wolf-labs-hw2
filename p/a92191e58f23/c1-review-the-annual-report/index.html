The Annual Report · C1 Unit 6 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 6 (The dense written sentence). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: The Green River Trust (an invented charity) must send its annual report to the board tomorrow. The student reads one page, fixes a draft page, and rewrites an informal message as a formal paragraph.

About 46 min: I · Memory (8) · II · The Report (10) · III · Find the Mistakes (7) · IV · The Council Meeting (7) · V · Rewrite It (10) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Open two cards. Find the pair: the informal version and the report version.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

nominalisation · complex noun phrases · participle clauses: having reviewed · verbless clauses: though tired · formal connectors: in light of, notwithstanding, insofar as · the subjunctive: that he be informed

MEMORY

A memory game. The student opens two cards; when they match, the student makes a sentence with the pair (example under Show answer).

THE REPORT

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

FIND THE MISTAKES

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

REWRITE IT

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

having review, though was tired, in light to, it is vital that he is informed (formal: be), notwithstanding of, we reduced the costs and this was (for the reduction in costs). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE COUNCIL MEETING (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the draft is corrected, your student is the director of Green River Trust in the hot seat; you are a tough council member. Your secret (only you see it): you vote for three years only if they mention the volunteers; otherwise one year. Ask the five questions, wait five seconds in silence after each, and use the PUSH line to make them switch from dense to plain English. Listen for Unit 6 (Having reviewed…, Though underfunded…, In light of…, Notwithstanding…, It is vital that… be…); recast quietly while they talk, tell them your vote at the end, and ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: III · Find the Mistakes and VI · I Can.
  • Write: V · Rewrite It.
