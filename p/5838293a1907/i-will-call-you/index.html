I Will Call You · B1 Unit 3 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new B1 lesson on will for promises (I’ll call you, I won’t forget), offers (I’ll carry it, Shall I…?) and decisions made at the moment, contrasted with going to for plans made before.

The story: It is 11 p.m., the night before Bia’s wedding (invented). The cake is late, the flowers are the wrong color and the DJ is sick. Her friends offer help in the group chat.

About 42 min: I · The Wedding Chat (9) · II · Now or Before? (8) · III · Save the Wedding (10) · IV · The Promise Jury (8) · V · Would You Rather? (7).

Open with: “Have you ever helped to organize a party or a wedding? What went wrong?”

The key question for every sentence: when did you decide? Now → will. Before → going to. In the hot seat you are Bia, a bit desperate: give the student real problems so they offer help naturally.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

will for promises · will for offers · will for decisions at the moment · Shall I…? · won’t · going to for plans

THE WEDDING CHAT

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

NOW OR BEFORE?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

SAVE THE WEDDING

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I will to call (I’ll call), I call you tomorrow, I promise (I’ll call), Will I help you? (Shall I help you?), I’m going to answer it! for a ringing phone (I’ll answer it), I will not to dance (I won’t dance). B1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE PROMISE JURY (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

The wedding is over. Rafa, Leo and Camila each made a promise the night before; the student reads three pieces of evidence for each and gives a verdict (kept it, broke it, broke it but forgiven), quoting the promise with will / won’t and the plan with going to. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the verdict harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 8 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · The Wedding Chat.
  • Focus: II · Now or Before?.
  • Out loud: III · Save the Wedding, IV · The Promise Jury and V · Would You Rather?.
