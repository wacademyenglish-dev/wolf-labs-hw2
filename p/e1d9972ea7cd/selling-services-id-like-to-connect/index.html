I’d Like to Connect · Selling Services · Lesson 21

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 21 (I’d like to… · Would you like to…?). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Emma Lee, HR director at Brightline Retail (fictional), saw Leo’s LinkedIn message and calls him: she is busy, so Leo offers a case study and a call in two weeks. The student finds the Brazilian traps, fixes a bad LinkedIn message (“I want to sell you our service”), chats with you on “LinkedIn” (you are Emma), and writes three real messages: connect, follow up, invite.

About 58 min: I · You and LinkedIn (5) · II · She Calls Back (9) · III · I’d Like To, Not “I Like To” (9) · IV · Fix the Message (8) · V · The Chat (9) · VI · You Choose (6) · VII · Your 3 Messages (9) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “Do you use LinkedIn for work? Do clients answer your messages? What would you like to learn this year?”

Polite wishes and invitations: I’d like to… / Would you like to…? / Yes, I’d love to. / I’d like to, but… Brazilian traps: “I’d like connect” (no to), “I like to meet you” for a wish now (gosto ≠ gostaria), “Do you would like…?”, “Yes, I like.” as an answer. Also the cultural point: “I want to sell you…” sounds pushy; one short message with one question works. In V you are Emma on a chat: say “I’d like to, but…” at least once so he offers another time.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

I’d like to connect with you. · Would you like to meet / have a call? · Yes, I’d love to. · Yes, please. · I’d like to, but …

YOU AND LINKEDIN

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

SHE CALLS BACK

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

I’D LIKE TO, NOT “I LIKE TO”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

FIX THE MESSAGE

A short note with mistakes. The student finds a mistake and SAYS the right form first; then tap it to show the correction and the reason. At the end, the student reads the whole note correctly.

THE CHAT

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR 3 MESSAGES

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask him to send one of his three messages to a real prospect this week and to show you the answer.

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Connection request: Pedido de conexão no LinkedIn. A nota curta que acompanha o pedido é a primeira impressão.
  • Follow-up: Mensagem de acompanhamento depois de um contato (“Thanks for connecting…”).
  • Prospect: Cliente em potencial: ainda não comprou, mas tem perfil.
  • Case study: Estudo de caso: 1 página com o resultado de um cliente real (problema → solução → números).
  • Pushy: Insistente, invasivo. “I want to sell you…” soa pushy em inglês.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I’d like connect (I’d like to connect), I like to meet you on Monday (I’d like to meet you), Do you would like to…? (Would you like to…?), Yes, I like. (Yes, I’d love to.). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
