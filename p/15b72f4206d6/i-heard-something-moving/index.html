I Heard Something Moving · Night Calls

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Halloween week, 3 AM, a radio show called Night Calls.

The grammar: The Rule: I heard someone walking. I saw him close the door.

Short class (about 44 min): I Night Noises · II Only the Sound · III The Rule · IV Night Calls · V The History Minute · IX The Last Call. Skip if you’re short on time: VI, VII, VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 63 min.

Open with: “What noises can you hear from your bed at night?”

Halloween week, 3 AM, a radio show called Night Calls. Listeners call to tell what they heard and saw at night; the student is the host, and then calls the show too. The grammar is the verbs of perception: hear / see / watch / feel + person + -ING (a part of the action, it was still going on: I heard someone walking) and + the base verb (all of it, from start to finish: I saw him close the door). Every scary story has a silly answer: the cat, the radio, the wind.

THE VIDEO · SOUND FIRST

Scene II: the official trailer of A Quiet Place (2018), a movie about not making a sound. The first time, the picture stays covered: the student only listens and says what they hear. Then they watch with the picture. Before the video the student picks a reaction and slides a ruler. In the sound-only round, two short lines come back with ▶ (which word did you hear?); with the picture, True or Lie. After the video: did you hear it or see it? These sentences already use the grammar, before the rule.

THE CALLS · THE WITNESSES

Scene IV: you read each call out loud (the student sees the text only after you tap). Then the student taps True or Lie on three lines about the call and says it again with the right form. Scene VII: the student reads one sentence and says if the witness saw all of it or only a part. All of it: saw the door open. A part: saw the door opening.

THE HISTORY MINUTE

Scene V is a station break: you read where Halloween comes from (the Celtic festival of Samhain, All Hallows’ Eve, the Irish turnips that became American pumpkins). The answers use the grammar: they could hear the dead walking, they watched the bonfires burning. The student taps True or Lie on five sentences.

THE MISTAKES

I heard someone to walk, I saw he closing, I saw her opens, We heard the dog barked, I listened someone crying, I could to hear. Scene III: find the broken part of six lines (the rule stays in the corner). Scene VIII: Right or Wrong on six sentences.

NO JUMP SCARES

The trailer is tense but not gory. If a student doesn’t like fear, they can call the show as the brave one: It was just the wind.

THE ARENA (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the calls and the History Minute, your student decides a rule for Night Calls: tell the silly explanation, or keep the mystery? They pick a side and defend it for one minute, then swap and defend the other side. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (heard someone walking, saw him close); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: II Only the Sound, IV Night Calls, V The History Minute.
  • Out loud: I Night Noises, VI You’re On Air, VIII The Arena.
  • Focus: III The Rule, VII Open or Opening?.
  • Write: IX, their own call to the show. It goes to their My notes.
