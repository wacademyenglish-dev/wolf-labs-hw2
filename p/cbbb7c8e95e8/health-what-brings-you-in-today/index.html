What Brings You In Today? · English for Healthcare · Lesson 2 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: taking the history of a complaint with SOCRATES (Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, Associated symptoms, Time course, Exacerbating/relieving factors, Severity), and the present perfect for duration (How long have you had…? for / since).

The story: A Geeky Medics clip: a doctor takes a chest pain history. Then YOU play a patient with knee pain (works for doctors and physios; dentists can switch to toothache) and the student asks the questions.

About 43 min: I · SOCRATES (8) · II · How Long Have You Had…? (10) · III · The First Minute (7) · IV · Take a History (14) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What is the first question you ask a patient in Portuguese?” Then find the English version: What brings you in today?

In the hot seat, answer as the patient using the answers on screen, or adapt them to the student’s profession (toothache for a dentist, a cough for a pharmacist). The clip stops at 1:47: the end is an advert.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

What brings you in today? · Where exactly is the pain? · When did it start? · What does it feel like? · How long have you had…? · On a scale of 0 to 10…

SOCRATES

Geeky Medics, “SOCRATES Example | History Taking”, only 0:00–1:47 (captions checked on 15/10/2026). British English.

HOW LONG HAVE YOU HAD…?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

TAKE A HISTORY

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to adapt SOCRATES to the most common complaint in their job.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

How long do you have…?, since two weeks, When has it started?, What is your problem? (too cold). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE FIRST MINUTE (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the history-taking, the student ranks five habits for the first minute of a consultation (use the name, let them talk, look at them and not at the screen, ask how long early, smile and sit down) and explains the top one and the last one. There is no right order. Wait five seconds in silence, use the PUSH line (only you see it: seven minutes per patient), then tap Show my teacher’s order and agree on one top 3 together. Recast quietly while they talk (I’ve noticed that …, patients have always …); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
