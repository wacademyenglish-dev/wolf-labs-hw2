So What You’re Saying Is… · English for Designers · Lesson 2 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: clarifying vague client words (When you say “modern”, do you mean…?), asking for examples, confirming (So what you’re saying is…, Just to clarify…) and writing a short brief from a client email.

The story: A UK brand designer’s creative direction questions, then a fictional yoga studio (Northwind Yoga) that emails what it wants.

About 43 min: I · Creative Direction (8) · II · What Do You Mean by…? (10) · III · Dangerous Words (7) · IV · Write the Brief (14) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What’s the most confusing thing a client has ever asked you? ‘Make it pop’?”

After the brief form (formulario), ask the student to read it back to you as the client, starting with “So what you’re saying is…”. React as the client and correct one detail, so the student has to adjust.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

When you say…, do you mean…? · Could you show me an example of…? · Which part…? · Just to clarify,… · So what you’re saying is…, right? · What date do you have in mind?

CREATIVE DIRECTION

Abi Connick, the questions designers ask their clients: the creative direction part (3:58–5:53).

WHAT DO YOU MEAN BY…?

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

WRITE THE BRIEF

A practice form in English, filled in with a FICTIONAL person’s data (never the student’s real documents in class). The student reads the profile card and fills in the form; Check the form shows each field right or wrong, with the correct answer.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write a short brief for their own client (real or imagined) before the next lesson.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

What means modern? (What do you mean by modern?), You can show me an example? (Could you show me…?), So you are saying that… (natural, but add “right?”), I didn’t understand nothing (I didn’t quite get…). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

DANGEROUS WORDS (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before writing the brief, a ranking of five vague client words (modern, clean, fun, premium, make it pop): which one causes the most revisions? The student orders them and explains the top one and the last one, with the clarifying question they would ask. Then tap “Show my teacher’s order” and agree on one top 3 together. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it): one email with all five words. Recast quietly while they talk (when you say…, do you mean…?, so what you’re saying is…, just to clarify…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
