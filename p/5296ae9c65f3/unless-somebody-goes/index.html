Unless Somebody Goes · The Lord of the Rings · The Council

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Elves, Dwarves, Men, a wizard and four hobbits at one table.

The grammar: If, Unless, When: If you don’t find a way, no one will.

The class (about 50 min): I The Story · III The Prologue · IV Scene by Scene · V The Road to the Fire · VI If, Unless, When · VII The Council · VIII Your Crossroads · IX The Debate. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the Council now. In the film, who will take the Ring? Guess.”

The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (2001), Peter Jackson, from Tolkien. Long ago the Dark Lord Sauron made one Ring to control all the others; now a hobbit, Frodo, has it. At the Council of Elrond, Elves, Dwarves, Men, a wizard and hobbits sit at one round table: the Ring can’t be used or hidden, and unless somebody takes it to the fire where it was made, it will never be destroyed. The grammar: the first conditional: if + present, will + verb (if the Ring stays here, Sauron will find it), unless = if … not (unless somebody goes), and when / as soon as + present. No will after if, unless or when.

A note for the teacher: the video is the film’s official opening from Warner Bros.; we stop at 3:56. The times were checked for the old lesson (24/09). There is an old battle in the middle (swords and armies, no blood); each moment is told in our own words.

The world (I): The road to rivendell. Five stops, in order: The Shire, Gandalf, Aragorn, Old Enemies and The Council. The student reads each page out loud and answers one question about their own life; Show answer gives a model. You don’t need to know the story: the map tells it, and the questions before the video come from it.

The words (II): rings, deceived, secret, alliance and corrupted, before the video, in match the columns and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Debate): “The smallest person can do the biggest job.” The student chooses a side, the teacher takes the other one, with if / unless + present and will. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an elven valley at dusk. I: the story (the film, the Ring, the Council). III: the prologue in three steps. Before: True or lie? Four sentences about the Ring; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. Watch: five sentences with a missing word, each with a ▶ time that jumps to that line of the prologue; the student taps the word that fits. After: Choose Your Response. The student is Isildur next to the fire, with one chance to destroy the Ring: three ways to answer (throw it in the fire, keep it, ask for help), and the screen shows the conversation. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Road to the Fire: two hard choices for the trip (the road, the friend); the student picks one and defends it. VI, If, Unless, When: four sentences cut in two halves; the student matches them (If the Ring stays here → Sauron will find it). When all four are matched, the rule board appears, and it stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Council: the round stone table seen from above: six seats (Elrond, Boromir, Gandalf, Aragorn, Sam, Frodo) and the Ring in the middle. For each clue the student taps who is speaking, the seat lights up in gold and a thread runs to the Ring, and the student says the sentence and answers the question under it. At the end the Ring leaves the center and goes to Frodo, the smallest of them all. VIII, Your Crossroads: two posters, A (The Plan) and B (The Choices), with three questions each. The teacher picks one; the student answers and taps each word (If … will, Unless, As soon as, When, I won’t) when they use it. IX: the debate.

THE COMMON ERRORS

if the Ring will stay, when we will leave, unless somebody doesn’t take it, if we used the Ring, we will win (a real future: if we use), Sauron finds it (will find), it doesn’t will break, will protecting, will safer.

THE ROAD TO THE FIRE (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student knows the Ring has to be destroyed. Now they plan the trip that comes after the Council: two hard choices with no right answer. The road (over the snowy mountain, through the old mines, or the long road around) and the friend (Sam, Aragorn or Gandalf: only one more person on the team). The student picks one, or says their own idea, and defends it with if / unless / when + present and will. This is not the debate of the ending: here they plan, there they argue. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (if we will take, unless he doesn’t come, Sam comes with me → will come); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Story, V The Road to the Fire, VII The Council, VIII Your Crossroads and IX The Debate.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Prologue and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI If, Unless, When.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
