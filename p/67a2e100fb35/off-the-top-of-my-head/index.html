Off the Top of My Head · The Gallery

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Friday night at a tiny story club in Chicago.

The grammar: An idiom is a fixed picture. Keep the words; move only the verb (she spilled the beans).

Short class (about 56 min): I You and Stories · III The Hunt · V The Arena · VI The Wall · VII The Host’s Notes · VIII The Open Mic · IX Off the Top of Your Head · X Last Call. Skip if you’re short on time: IV Line by Line (marked EXTRA on your screen). On the practice pages the student speaks first and taps Check on their own screen. Full lesson: about 60 min.

Open with: “Who tells the best stories among your family and friends? Why?”

Friday night at a tiny story club in Chicago. Before the open mic, the host plays a talk by a veteran teacher, a born storyteller, and the student hunts the expressions in it. Then the club’s famous wall: six paintings of idioms taken literally (beans spilled on a table, an elephant next to a sofa). After the rule, the open mic: six stories, each with a broken idiom to fix. The grammar of an idiom: the words are fixed (every the, every -s), only the verb and the possessive move (she spilled the beans, off the top of her head), and you never translate one word for word: you find the English picture.

THE COMMON ERRORS

Students translate their own idioms word by word and paint a picture nobody in English knows: the eyes of the face, the last drop, stepped on the ball, the chip fell, two rabbits. The most dangerous one: kick the bucket means to die, never “to give up in anger”. Say it with a smile, then never let it pass. The second trap is changing what is fixed: spilled the bean, an elephant in the room, from the top of my head.

ON SCREEN

The gallery: each painting in a dark frame with a brass plate that is engraved when the student taps Check. The OPEN MIC sign: the bulbs light up when the six stories are checked.

The Wall (VI): six paintings. The student describes the painting and guesses the idiom; the card below tells what happened (Maya’s little brother told Sam everything). The student says it with the idiom, then taps Check: the model sentence appears and the brass plate is engraved with the idiom and its meaning. Under it, one question to talk about.

The Host’s Notes (VII): Dee, the host, writes down the best moments of the night, on dark paper. The student reads the notes out loud and taps every idiom (six; every word can be tapped, wrong taps go red). Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line (the beans, broke, her head, the elephant, an arm and a leg, throw in the towel; kick the bucket = to die). The rule then stays in the corner of VIII.

The Open Mic (VIII): six short stories, each ending with a translated, broken idiom (the eyes of the face, kicked the bucket, the last drop…). The student tells each story out loud with the right idiom, then taps Check: the fixes appear and the bulbs light. Then the student tells one story like these from their own life.

THE TALK

Scene III: the talk (about eight minutes), sealed, in four chapters. Before: two guesses (no right answer yet; the chapters tell). Watch: every chapter has a short task next to the video: read it, play the chapter (it stops by itself), tap the answer. In chapters 2 and 3 the hunt shows the MEANING (end of discussion) and the student taps the words the speaker used (case closed, a bunch of hooey, sucks all the life out of you). Chapter 4 is true or false. After: why she talks like this (one tap) and four lines with one wrong word: the student says the right one, then Check. Every line has a ▶ time button that plays the talk from that moment: the exact words matter, because idioms are fixed. Note: the captions write “on the road” where she says “on a roll”, so that moment is not a task.

The words (II): educators, poverty, relationships, apologize and difficult, before the video, in predict the story and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE ARENA (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the talk: the colleague’s line, “They don’t pay me to like the kids.” Your student picks a side and defends it for one minute (start the timer) with two idioms from tonight, then taps Swap sides and defends the other one. There is no right side. Wait five seconds in silence before the first minute, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (fixed words: case closed, a bunch of hooey); at the end ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and Stories, V The Arena, VI The Wall, VIII The Open Mic and IX Off the Top of Your Head (no longer extra).
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Hunt and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VII The Host’s Notes.
  • Write: X Last Call.
