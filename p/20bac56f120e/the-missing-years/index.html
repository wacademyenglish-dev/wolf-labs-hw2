The Missing Years · The Odyssey · The Sea Route

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A poem older than almost everything, and a trip that took ten years.

The grammar: Was, Were, Didn’t: The war was over. The men were tired. They didn’t know.

The class (about 57 min): I The Story · II The Words · III The Trailer · IV Line by Line · V The Long Way Home · VI Was, Were, Didn’t · VII The Sea Route · VIII Your Journey · IX Questions. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the story now. In the trailer, what did Odysseus lose at sea? Guess.”

The Odyssey is Homer’s Greek poem, almost three thousand years old; in 2026 Christopher Nolan made it into a film (Universal). Odysseus was the king of Ithaca; he went to the war in Troy, and the war lasted ten years. The way home took ten more: storms, monsters, magic islands, while his wife and his son waited for him. The grammar: past simple for a voyage: was / were, irregular verbs (took, ate, forgot, came), didn’t + base verb, and Did he…? / Yes, he did. (Lesson 280 also practices the past simple: that is reinforcement on purpose; here the focus is was / were and the irregulars.)

A note for the teacher: this is the second lesson with this film (lesson 267 is the house plan in Ithaca; this one is the sea), with the same official trailer. It has battles and monsters, but nothing violent is on the lesson screens, and one line with a swear word is not used. Each moment is told in our own words.

The world (I): The map of the poem. Five stops, in order: Homer, Troy, The Cyclops, Poseidon and The Film. The student reads each page out loud and answers one question about their own life; Show answer gives a model. You don’t need to know the story: the map tells it, and the questions before the video come from it.

The words (II): king, kingdom, wife, remember and gods, before the video, in memory and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, Questions): five questions about the student’s past (where were you ten years ago, what did you want to be, when were you very tired, did you ever get lost, the best year of your life). Then the student asks the teacher two. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an ancient ship on a dark sea at night. I: the story (the poem, the war, the missing years). III: the trailer in three steps. Before: three cards to open and talk about (twenty years away, the wife, the son). Watch: True or lie? and Which word did you hear?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: sort six things into was or were, then a slider (was it a good idea to go home after twenty years?). IV: Line by Line. V: The Long Way Home, three hard choices from the trailer. VI, Penelope’s letter: the student reads it out loud, taps the verbs in the past (and the verb after Did), then finishes the rule. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Sea Route: a sea chart with six stops from Troy (east) to Ithaca (west): the storm, the lotus island, the cave, the sirens. For each island the student builds the question out loud (Did the Cyclops see Odysseus?), taps Check, and asks you; you answer in the past (Yes, he did. No, he didn’t.). Every Check sails the ship to the next island and draws the route in bronze. VIII, Your Journey: spin for a question about a trip; the student answers in the past and taps each verb (went, was / were, took, saw, didn’t, came home) when they use it. IX: questions.

THE COMMON ERRORS

the war were, the men was, taked, did took, eated, forgeted, didn’t saw, no saw, Did he stayed…?, He stayed for a year? (Did he stay…?), didn’t heard, comed.

THE LONG WAY HOME (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, three hard choices from the trailer: was Odysseus brave or foolish to defy the gods, what Telemachus had to do, and what he needed first when he came home. There is no right answer: let the student choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. The starters are in the past (was / were, didn’t): recast mistakes quietly while they talk, and at the end ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Story, V The Long Way Home, VII The Sea Route, VIII Your Journey and IX Questions.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Was, Were, Didn’t.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
