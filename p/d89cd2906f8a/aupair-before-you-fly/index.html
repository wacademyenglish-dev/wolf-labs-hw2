Before You Fly · Au Pair · Lesson 8 of 24

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: preparing the trip: what goes in the carry-on (passport, visa, DS-2019, contacts on paper, daily medicine, charger) and what goes in the big suitcase; copies separate from the originals; the student’s own checklist.

The story: Libby, a real au pair (Au Pair in America vlog), flies from Jersey to London to New York, repacks an overweight bag, faces a delay and hands in her documents at orientation.

About 42 min: I · Libby Takes Off (9) · II · Carry-On or Suitcase? (10) · III · My Before-You-Fly List (12) · IV · Trouble at the Airport (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “If your big suitcase gets lost, what do you need in your hand?”

The DS-2019 is the document from the sponsor agency for the J-1 visa: it travels with the passport. Remind the student that each airline has its own weight rules: check the ticket.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

carry-on / hand luggage · the big suitcase / checked bag · passport, visa, DS-2019 · copies in another place · overweight / delayed · contacts on paper

LIBBY TAKES OFF

Au Pair in America, “Life with APiA | Libby’s 1st vlog”, only 0:00–2:16 (the video plays only this part). British accent, fast and natural: play it twice.

CARRY-ON OR SUITCASE?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

MY BEFORE-YOU-FLY LIST

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to photograph her list and keep it in the phone AND on paper.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

documents in the checked bag, no copies, only digital contacts, no prescription for the medicine. A2+: one correction per answer is enough.

TROUBLE AT THE AIRPORT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

The student’s list meets Libby’s problems: the suitcase is 3 kg over the limit, the flight is four hours late with the family waiting, and the phone dies when the charger is in the big bag. For each one the student chooses what to do and says why. Most of it is opinion; the useful lessons are “tell the family early” and “numbers on paper”. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (carry-on, keep… with me, I’d…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
