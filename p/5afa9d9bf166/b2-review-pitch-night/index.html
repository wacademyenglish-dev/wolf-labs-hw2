Pitch Night · B2 Unit 5 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 5 (Describing with precision). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Pitch night at an inventors’ workshop. Your student is the investor with money for one idea. You are three inventors (invented products): Luna (a sleep lamp for night workers), Bruno (a plant sensor) and Dona Cida (a box that records grandparents’ recipes).

About 49 min: I · Six Doors (8) · II · The Pitches (15) · III · The Investor’s Dilemma (7) · IV · Your Pitch (7) · V · The Hot Seat (8) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Six doors. Choose one, open it, and do the challenge.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

defining and non-defining relative clauses · sentence relatives: which is why, which means · reduced relative clauses · preposition + -ing · the to on its own · collocations with make

SIX DOORS

Six doors, one per topic of the unit. The student picks any door and does the task from memory. Tap Got it or Review; amber doors come back as a short list “to review”.

THE PITCHES

You are each inventor. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student announces the investment with which means / which is why. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

YOUR PITCH

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

THE HOT SEAT

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

a lamp what changes color, my aunt, that is a nurse, instead of change, I did a decision, a woman which car (for whose car), the book which written by. B2: one correction per answer is enough.

THE INVESTOR’S DILEMMA (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the pitches, three hard calls with the same inventors: Luna wants to hire her brother, a big company wants to buy Bruno’s sensor, a TV channel wants Dona Cida’s recordings. The student chooses (or says their own idea) and explains with which, whose, which is why and instead of. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the review has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · Six Doors and VI · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · The Pitches, III · The Investor’s Dilemma and V · The Hot Seat.
  • Write: IV · Your Pitch.
