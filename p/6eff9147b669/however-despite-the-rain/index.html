However, Despite the Rain · The Torn Ticket

Everything at Lanternfield went wrong and everything was still good: rain, mud, a band two hours late, a power cut. Each problem is a torn ticket: the bad half, the good half, and the word that stitches them. The question is always the same: after the word, a clause (although, even though) or a noun / -ing (despite, in spite of)? And However starts a new sentence. Students say despite of, despite it was, although… but, and , however we.

LET THEM SAY BUT FIRST

Scene II: the eight tickets come before the rule. Most students join them with but, and that is right. The stitches arrive in Scene III as more options, not as corrections.

THE DEADLINE HAS A WORD

Scene IV: each line has its word on the wristband. Read the notes, your student says the full line with that word, you tap what you heard. The review fills up as the clock moves to midnight.

YOU ARE ROSA

Scene V: you play the festival organizer the morning after. Be a little hurt about the mud. Push once: “So it was bad?” Your student has to defend the night with despite or however.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

despite of the rain, despite it was raining, although it rained, but…, it rained, however we…, in spite the mud, although the rain.

THE HOT SEAT (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Rosa’s call, your student changes sides: now they are the singer of The Northern Hum, two hours late, and you are a journalist with a secret (only on your screen). Ask the three questions, then they choose what to give the fans. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (“despite of the rain” → “despite the rain”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
