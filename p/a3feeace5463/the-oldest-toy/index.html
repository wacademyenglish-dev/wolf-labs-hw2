The Oldest Toy in the Box · Toy Story 5 · The Thrift Shelf

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Thirty years of toys that come alive.

The grammar: The Oldest, the Best: The oldest, the funniest, the most useful, the best.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and The Toy Box · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V The Best Toy Friend · VI The Oldest, the Best · VII The Thrift Shelf · VIII The Best and the Worst · IX Your Toy Box Awards. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the toys now. In the trailer, who is the newest toy in the house? Guess.”

Toy Story (Pixar, 1995) was the first film made all with computers: the toys are alive, but only when people don’t look. Woody and Buzz now live with Bonnie, with Jessie, Rex the dinosaur and Forky, a spork. In Toy Story 5 (2026) a tablet arrives, Lilypad, who talks, listens and does everything, and the old toys feel forgotten. The grammar: superlatives: the + -est (the oldest, the biggest), y → -iest (the funniest, the shiniest), the most + long adjectives (the most useful, the most modern), irregular the best / the worst, and in + a place (in the box, in the world) / of + a time (of my life).

A note for the teacher: the trailer is family animation, nothing to worry about. The times follow its captions (24/09), and each moment is told in our own words. The toys on the shelf are objects (a hat, a helmet, a dinosaur…), drawn simply, not the film’s characters.

Warm-up and The Toy Box (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Toy Box: five things come out of the box, one at a time: Andy’s room, the toys, the box for Bonnie, Forky, and the new tablet. With each, the student answers a short question with a superlative (the oldest, the funniest, the most modern). At the end, they say which toy is the most important and why.

The words (II): package, device, abandoned, century and mission, before the video, in mixed letters and match the columns. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, Your Toy Box Awards): the student writes three awards for the things and people in their life: one with -est, one with the most, one with best or worst. The corrector marks the most old, the goodest, the funniest of the house. It goes to My notes.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a child’s bedroom at night with an open toy box and a glowing tablet. I: Warm-up and The Toy Box. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a ruler from Disagree to Agree (“Old toys are the best toys. Tablets are boring.”): the student moves the pin and says why. Watch: One word is wrong. Five lines, each with a ▶ time: tap it, hear the line, say the right word (the video jumps to that moment). After: five lines from the trailer to match with what they mean. IV: five frames on the film strip. V: The Best Toy Friend, a ranking of five toys. VI, The Oldest, the Best: six words to sort into three boxes (the … -est, the most …, special). When all six are in the right box, the rule appears under the boxes. The rule board stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Thrift Shelf: two shelves with six forgotten toys, grey under the lamp. For each toy the student reads the clue, taps the tag that fits (the oldest, the shiniest, the most nervous, the most modern, the most useful, the best), and says the whole sentence; the toy comes back to color and a handwritten tag hangs on it. VIII, The Best and the Worst: a wheel with six questions (the best restaurant, the worst trip, the oldest thing, the funniest person, the most useful app, the happiest day). The student spins, answers with a superlative and spins again. IX: the awards.

THE COMMON ERRORS

the most old, the older toy (comparative), the shinyest / the funnyest, the nervousest / the usefulest, the most modernest, is most nervous (no the), the more modern, the goodest / the most good, the baddest / the most bad, the funniest toy of the house (in).

THE BEST TOY FRIEND (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the trailer’s big fight (old toys or the tablet?) becomes a ranking: the student puts Woody, Buzz, Jessie, Forky and Lilypad in order, from the best friend for a child to the worst, and explains the top one and the last one with the -est / the most / the best / the worst. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, use the PUSH line (only you see it), then tap “Show my teacher’s order” and agree on one top 3 together. Recast mistakes quietly (the most old, the goodest); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Toy Box, V The Best Toy Friend, VII The Thrift Shelf and VIII The Best and the Worst.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Oldest, the Best.
  • Write: IX Your Toy Box Awards.
