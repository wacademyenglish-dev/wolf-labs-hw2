Whoever Takes It · The Lord of the Rings · The Bearers

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: One Ring, and everyone who ever held it.

The grammar: Whoever Takes It: Whoever takes it will be changed.

The class (about 57 min): I Warm-up and Pick a Side · II The Words · III The Prologue · IV Line by Line · V The Next Hand · VI Whoever Takes It · VII The Bearers · VIII Whatever You Say · IX The Interview. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the Ring now. In the film, what happens to whoever holds it? Guess.”

The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (2001), Peter Jackson, from Tolkien. The Dark Lord made one Ring to rule all the others; whoever holds it wants to keep it, and it changes them. It passed from Sauron to Isildur, to a river, to Gollum, to Bilbo, and then to Frodo, who must take it back to the fire. The grammar: whoever / whatever / wherever / whenever / however: one word; whoever takes a verb with -s; however + adjective / adverb + subject + verb (however hard he tries); and the present for the future after -ever (wherever you go, I will go).

A note for the teacher: the video is the film’s official opening (Warner Bros.); we stop at 3:56. An old battle, no blood. Times checked for the old lesson (24/09), told in our own words. Lesson 299 (same film) was the Council table; this one is the journey on the map.

Warm-up and Pick a Side (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Pick a Side: destroy the Ring, or use it to fight the Dark Lord? The student picks a side and defends it for thirty seconds. Then tap “The teacher pushes back” and let them answer. Twice. Push for whoever / whatever / however.

The words (II): given, power, cruelty, freedom and chance, before the video, in memory and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Interview): the teacher is Bilbo, who kept the Ring for sixty years. The student asks five questions with whenever, however, wherever, whoever, whatever. Then swap: the student is Frodo. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an old ink map on a dark table with a gold ring. I: Warm-up and Pick a Side. III: the prologue in three steps. Before: a wheel that picks a question (a ring you find, what you never give away…). Watch: Which word did you hear? and What does he say?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: two taps (what would you do with the Ring? who is stronger?) and the student says why. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Next Hand: a dilemma in three rounds (Isildur at the fire, a ring in a river, who carries it to the fire); the student chooses and says why. VI, Whoever Takes It: the student matches five words (whoever, whatever, wherever, whenever, however hard) to their meaning (any person who, any thing that…). When all five are right, the rule appears. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Bearers: an old map with six hands (Sauron, Isildur, Gollum, Bilbo, Frodo, the fire). For each hand there is a sentence with -ever: the student says if it is true or a lie, and if it is a lie they say the true sentence out loud. Every right answer carries the gold Ring one stop and draws the road. VIII, Whatever You Say: six cards with a question (Whatever you cook…, Wherever you travel…). The student opens one and answers for 30 seconds on the clock, without stopping. IX: the interview, the teacher is Bilbo.

THE COMMON ERRORS

who ever / what ever / where ever (one word), whoever take it, whoever who, however he tries hard, however you are strong, wherever you will go, whatever it will cost, whatever it cost (now: costs).

THE NEXT HAND (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, three hands from the prologue, three choices: Isildur at the fire (what do you do, standing next to him?), a gold ring in a river (nobody saw you), and who should carry the Ring back to the fire. The student chooses one card and says why with whoever / whatever / wherever / however; there is no right answer, and they can say their own idea. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk (who ever, whoever take, however he tries hard); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: II The Words, III The Prologue and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Whoever Takes It.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
