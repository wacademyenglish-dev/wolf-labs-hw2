How Much Is Too Much · The Gauge

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Too much goes with things you don’t count (time, coffee, sleep, noise); too many with things you count (hours, cups, people).

The grammar: Count It First: Before you pick much or many, ask one question: can I count it?

Short class (about 48 min): I Your Week, Guessed · II Guess the Number · III The Gauge · V Count It First · VI Eighty Against a Third · VIII The Council Vote · X Your Gauge Again. Skip if you’re short on time: IV, VII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 68 min.

Open with: “Hours a day on a screen, not counting work.”

Too much goes with things you don’t count (time, coffee, sleep, noise); too many with things you count (hours, cups, people). Enough goes before a noun and after an adjective: enough time, big enough. Not enough is the complaint. Students say too much people and enough big. The theme is screens and the week; every amount becomes a dashboard gauge: not enough, enough, too much.

NOBODY CHECKS THE GUESSES

Scene I: five numbers about your student’s own week (scene II is the same game with a recording that answers after each guess). They are not corrected. Scene X gives them back.

THE GAUGE IS PERSONAL

Scene III: the green zone is a suggestion, not a rule. If your student disagrees, that disagreement is the conversation.

YOU ARE FOUR FRIENDS

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

too much people, too many coffee, enough big, I have not enough, too much tired. Scene V: the student finds the broken part of five sentences and says the fix (the rule stays in the corner). Scene IX: sort six words by where ENOUGH goes.

THE COUNCIL VOTE (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Your student is on the town council. Three proposals for teenagers and phones (the school, the parents, the teenagers): for each one they vote yes, no, or propose a change, and say why with too much, too many and enough. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence after each proposal, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. Recast quietly while they talk (too much people → too many people); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last step is one minute for their own rule; then you play a 15-year-old who thinks it is unfair.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Week, Guessed, III The Gauge, IV Same Number, Two People, VI Eighty Against a Third, VII The Screen Goes Dark, VIII The Council Vote.
  • Listen: II Guess the Number (five recordings).
  • Focus: V Count It First, IX What Isn’t Enough.
  • Write: X, their own gauge. It goes to their My notes.
