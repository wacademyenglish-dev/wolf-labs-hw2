Not Your Shoe Size · The Balance

A claim about intelligence at 100% is almost always wrong. Fluent speakers bring it down with a small set of tools: tend to (a pattern), seem to (what the evidence suggests), arguably (strong, but open to debate) and to some extent (partly true). Students state things as facts (Intelligence is genetic) or break the form (tend to being, seems be). On screen, a balance: the claim on one pan, the evidence as weights on the other. A sentence that is too sure tips the scale; a broken form makes it shake; the right sentence leaves it level.

THE NUMBER

Scene I: the student rates their own intelligence from 0 to 10, with no scale. Write the first defense exactly; the number comes back at the end.

THE RECORDINGS

Two recordings, each with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens after listening: the claim (scene II) and the critic (scene VI). The invented lines are the ones people quote; the critic is more careful than students expect.

THE BALANCE

Scene III: read the evidence first. The right hedge is the one that matches it, not the weakest one. Watch for students who pick maximum doubt every time.

THE DEBATE

Scene VII: state each claim at 100%. Push the student to answer below 100%, then take the opposite side once.

THE PANEL (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the critic, your student makes three real decisions with incomplete evidence: a job, a scholarship, a label for children. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Every reason must be hedged (tend to, seem to, arguably, to some extent). Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word is one minute on IQ or habits, then you take the other side.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Out of Ten, III The Balance, VII Three Claims, VIII The Panel, IX About You.
  • Listen: II The Claim, VI The Critic.
  • Focus: IV The Form, V Claim or Evidence?.
  • Write: X, their number defended below 100%. It goes to their My notes.
