As If He Knew Me · Severance · The Wellness Room

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Two people in one body, and a door between them.

The grammar: As If, As Though: He looks at me as if he knew me.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and The Interview · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V The Board Vote · VI As If, As Though · VII The Wellness Session · VIII As If · IX The Interview. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the procedure now. In the trailer, how does Mark look at his own life? Guess.”

Severance (Apple TV, 2022), directed by Ben Stiller, with Adam Scott as Mark. At Lumon, some workers have a chip that separates their memories: at work they remember nothing of home, at home nothing of work. When an “innie” is upset, he goes to the wellness room, where a calm voice reads him nice facts about his “outie”. The grammar: as if / as though: what is NOT true goes one step back (as if he knew him, as though she were his friend, as if she had never been here); what is probably true, because you can see it, stays in the present (it looks as if the elevator is stuck). No would after as if.

A note for the teacher: strange, not violent. Lines checked against the trailer’s subtitles (23/09), told in our own words. Lesson 294 (macrodata, the numbers) is also Severance; this one is the other room on the floor.

Warm-up and The Interview (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Interview: you are Mark at work, his “innie”: the card with his facts shows only on your screen (dashed border). You don’t remember anything about your life outside, so answer as if you had never left the building. The student asks questions and taps each one; then guesses who you are, and the photo shows the answer.

The words (II): procedure, consent, memories, retain and forbids, before the video, in predict the story and mystery meaning. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Interview): the teacher is Mark S., the innie. The student asks five questions (how he feels when work starts, how Ms. Cobel looks at him, Petey, the outside world, acting happy). Then they swap: the student is an innie on the first day. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a long dim Lumon corridor. I: Warm-up and The Interview. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: the wellness counselor says something kind; the student taps how they answer (say thank you, ask a question, say nothing) and the conversation appears. Watch: Which word did you hear? Five lines, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: One word is wrong. Five lines with a ▶; the student says the right word. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The Board Vote: three proposals (the chip, the wellness room, phones in a locker); the student votes Yes, Propose a change or No and defends it. VI, As If, As Though: five true facts on the left (Mark does not know Petey…) and five sentences with as if on the right; the student matches them. When all five are matched, the rule board appears and stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Wellness Session: a dark room with an armchair, a painting of hills and a floor lamp. For each of six facts the student reads the clue, says the sentence softly, then taps Check: the right sentence appears, the lamp warms from cold green to amber, the painting gets its color back and the fact appears on the session card (six dots for six facts). VIII, As If: six cards (your boss, a neighbor, a child, the sky, a tourist, you); the student taps a card and says how they look, with as if or as though. IX: the interview.

THE COMMON ERRORS

as if he knows him (when it is false), as if he would know, as though she will be, as if I knew her for years (had known), like as if / like if, as if you be happy, had readed, as if the elevator stuck (is stuck). The corrector does not mark the present for false things (sometimes it is true); the lesson teaches the step back.

THE BOARD VOTE (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student sits on the board of a company like Lumon and votes on three proposals from the trailer: the chip for every worker (optional, free), a wellness room on every floor, and phones in a locker during work hours. For each one they tap Yes, Propose a change or No, and defend the vote with as if / as though (they talk about it as if it were a gift…). There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. Recast quietly while they talk (as if he knows, like if, as if it would be); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Interview, V The Board Vote, VII The Wellness Session, VIII As If and IX The Interview.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI As If, As Though.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
