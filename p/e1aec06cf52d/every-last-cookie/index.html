Every Last Cookie · A2 Unit 4 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new A2 lesson on all / every / some / any / none / no, and everybody / nobody / somebody with a singular verb.

The story: The cookie jar in the office kitchen (invented company) was full on Monday and empty on Tuesday. Notes on the fridge, three colleagues to question, and the CEO with crumbs on his desk.

About 45 min: I · Notes on the Fridge (9) · II · Every or All? (9) · III · The Investigation (15) · IV · Place Your Bet (6) · V · Would You Rather? (6).

Open with: “Is there a kitchen where you work or study? What is in it?”

A light office mystery. The key contrasts: every + singular × all + plural; some (+) × any (− ?); nobody = already negative. In the mission, play the CEO a bit guilty, so the student can accuse him at the end.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

all + plural · every + singular · some × any · none / no · everybody / nobody / somebody + singular verb · only one negative

NOTES ON THE FRIDGE

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

EVERY OR ALL?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THE INVESTIGATION

You are each colleague. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student tells the boss what each colleague said. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

every cookies (every cookie), all the cookie (all the cookies), I didn’t eat some (any), Nobody didn’t (Nobody ate), Everybody are (Everybody is). A2: one correction per answer is enough.

PLACE YOUR BET (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the investigation (the CEO did it!), your student bets on the rest of the week: what Mr. Grant does on Wednesday, what the team does, and how many cookies are in the jar on Friday. Let them choose and say why, wait five seconds in silence, use the PUSH line (only you see it), and only then tap Check what happened. Any bet is fine: the point is the reason (every, some, none, nobody). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 6 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Notes on the Fridge.
  • Focus: II · Every or All?.
  • Out loud: III · The Investigation, IV · Place Your Bet and V · Would You Rather?.
