Formal or Friendly? · C1 Unit 9 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on switching register: openings and closings, contractions, indirect requests (Would it be possible to…?), Latin-origin verbs vs phrasal verbs (postpone / put off), and set phrases (I am writing to let you know / Just a heads-up; I apologise for any inconvenience / So sorry!).

The story: Paula’s flight from Lisbon is four hours late (invented). She writes to the director, Ms. Reed, and to her friend Jo.

About 45 min: I · Two Messages (10) · II · Which Voice? (9) · III · Switch the Register (8) · IV · The Hot Seat (8) · V · Your Two Messages (10).

Open with: “Do you write to your boss and to your friends in the same way? What changes?”

The student sees the SAME news written twice. The goal is not “formal is better”: it is choosing the right voice and keeping it from start to end. Common traps: mixed register (Dear Sir… Cheers), “I’m writing you to inform that”, and being too formal with a friend (it sounds angry). The writing card asks for both versions of one problem.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Dear… / Kind regards × Hi… / Talk soon · contractions and !! · Would it be possible to…? × Can we…? · postpone × put off, attend × go to · I am writing to let you know × Just a heads-up · I apologise for any inconvenience × So sorry!

TWO MESSAGES

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

WHICH VOICE?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

SWITCH THE REGISTER

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

YOUR TWO MESSAGES

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Dear Sir… Cheers! (mixed), I’m writing you to inform that, gonna in a formal email, Dear Jo, I regret to inform you (too formal with a friend), I apologize for the inconvenient, Hope you’re fine (formal: I hope you are well). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE HOT SEAT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, your student is Paula, stuck at Lisbon airport, and you “call” her four times: twice as Ms. Reed, the director, and twice as Jo, her best friend. They switch register with every call (Would it be possible to…? / Can we…?; I apologise… / So sorry!). Wait five seconds in silence after each question, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to test them: a director who sounds friendly still gets a formal answer. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Two Messages.
  • Focus: II · Which Voice? and III · Switch the Register.
  • Out loud: IV · The Hot Seat.
  • Write: V · Your Two Messages.
