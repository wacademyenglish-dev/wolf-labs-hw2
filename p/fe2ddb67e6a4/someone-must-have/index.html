Someone Must Have · B2 Unit 4 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new B2 lesson on past modals for deduction (must have, might / could / may have, can’t have) and for criticism (should / shouldn’t have), with the form modal + have + past participle.

The story: The town’s cake competition at Miller’s Bakery (invented). At 6 a.m. the winning cake has a small bite missing. The back door is open, there are tiny round prints in the flour, and Biscotti, the baker’s cat, is not in his basket.

About 42 min: I · The Clues (10) · II · How Sure Are You? (9) · III · Three Suspects (15) · IV · The Deal (8).

Open with: “You find your chocolate gone from the fridge. How do you decide who ate it?”

A light mystery with a funny ending. Two traps: the form (“must saw”) and the meaning of impossible (English uses can’t have, not mustn’t have). In the mission, play each suspect seriously: the student should feel like a detective.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

must have (almost sure) · might / could / may have (possible) · can’t / couldn’t have (impossible) · should / shouldn’t have (criticism) · modal + have + past participle · irregular participles: seen, gone, been

THE CLUES

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

HOW SURE ARE YOU?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THREE SUSPECTS

You are each suspect. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the owner asks about each suspect and the student gives a deduction with must have / might have / can’t have. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

must saw (must have seen), mustn’t have for impossible (can’t have), might have went (gone), must have for criticism (should have), could had (could have). B2: one correction per answer is enough.

THE DEAL (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Case closed: the cat did it. Now your student is Mr. Miller and you are Mrs. Hale, the judge. Ask the four questions in role; the student answers with must have, should have and can’t have. Your secret limit is only on your screen: he keeps the title only if he gives half the prize to the second baker. Don’t give in fast. Wait five seconds in silence after each question, and use the PUSH line to make it harder. Then close the deal together on the screen. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · The Clues.
  • Focus: II · How Sure Are You?.
  • Out loud: III · Three Suspects and IV · The Deal.
