After the Journey · Frieren

Most fantasy stories end when the hero defeats the Demon King. Frieren starts there. The party comes home after a ten-year journey; Frieren, an elf who lives for more than a thousand years, thinks ten years was nothing. Fifty years later Himmel dies, and she cries without knowing why: she never tried to know him. So she walks the road again. A story about time, grief and regret, told quietly.

The grammar carries the story: time used as an adjective. The journey lasted ten years → a ten-year journey (in front of a noun: a hyphen, and no S). Portuguese builds it the other way (uma aventura de dez anos), so the student says a ten-years adventure or an adventure of ten years. The student is the chronicler of the party: she walks the map, fixes the apprentice’s copy, and writes her own chronicle.

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: an elf who thinks ten years is nothing, and learns too late to know her friends.

The grammar: it lasted ten years → a ten-year journey (in front of a noun: hyphen, no S).

The lesson (about 50 min, 56 with VIII): I Your Longest Journey · II The Journey Map · III The Trailer · IV The Label (a letter) · V The Chronicle (listening) · VI The Apprentice’s Copy · VII Before the Meteors Fall (six meteors) · VIII Your Chronicle (extra) · IX Shall We Go?

Open with: “What is the longest trip you have ever taken?”

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud — I Your Longest Journey (write down every “two-hours trip” you hear and say nothing yet), II The Journey Map (three stops), VII Before the Meteors Fall, IX Shall We Go?
  • Listen — III The Trailer, in three steps (Japanese voices, English subtitles burned into the picture). Before: three taps (the third is your opinion). Watch: five True-or-Lie lines; every line has a ▶ time, tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: two meaning taps and a slider. V The Chronicle: a 55-second recording, Fern (American) and Stark (British) read the diary of the road. First the story (four True-or-Lie lines; the transcript opens after they listen), then fact by fact: the student hears a fact, says the label out loud (a three-day walk), then taps Check. At the end she tells the chronicle again and taps each label she uses. Pause and 0.75× on every fact.
  • Focus — IV The Label comes right after the trailer: Heiter’s letter. The student taps the time in front of a noun (a ten-year journey, a three-day walk…), then finishes the rule in four lines; the rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VI The Apprentice’s Copy uses it: one wrong word in each subtitle line; she says the fix and taps Check.
  • Write — VIII Your Chronicle (extra). The red wavy line marks ten-years, a ten years trip, a trip of ten years, a thousand years old elf and a eight-year. It goes to her My notes.

CAREFUL

Stop 3 on the map (the funeral) can touch a real loss. Offer the easy way out (“or a teacher, a friend you lost touch with”) and never push.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

The student walks the map, flips the cards, plays the diary, names the facts, finds the wrong word and writes. In VII every Check lights a meteor (six in all). Only you open the follow-up in I. Arrows move the scenes; G opens this guide.
