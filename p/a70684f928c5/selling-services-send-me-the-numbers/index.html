Can You Send Me the Numbers? · Selling Services · Lesson 7

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 7 (can · can’t · Can you…? · Could you…, please?). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Daniel Price (Serra Steel, fictional) answers Leo: some numbers yes, the salaries no. Then Leo calls Karen Wells (Lumen Logistics) to ask for data for the proposal. The student orders a model e-mail and writes his own data request.

About 57 min: I · Your Week (6) · II · The Client’s Answer (8) · III · A Call for Numbers (8) · IV · Can, Not “Can To” (10) · V · Put the E-mail in Order (8) · VI · You Choose (5) · VII · Your Data Request (9) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “Can you work from home this week? What can you do in Excel?”

Three Brazilian traps: “can to send” (translating “poder enviar” with “to”), questions without inversion (“You can send me…?”), and requests that sound like orders (“Send me the numbers.”). With clients, train “Could you …, please?” as the default. Short answers: Yes, I can. / No, I can’t. Note the American pronunciation: can’t has a long, open “a” and a soft t; let the student hear the difference can × can’t in the dialogue.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

We can send … · I can’t meet … · Can you send me …? · Could you send me …, please? · Can I call you on …?

YOUR WEEK

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE CLIENT’S ANSWER

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

A CALL FOR NUMBERS

A short audio dialogue (American voices). BEFORE: one question. LISTEN: play it once without stopping (0.75× is fine for A1), then the questions; Show answer after the student tries. The transcript opens only after the audio ends: read it together and point to the highlighted words.

CAN, NOT “CAN TO”

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

PUT THE E-MAIL IN ORDER

The events are mixed. The student taps them in order; wrong order shows in red. When it’s right, the student tells the whole story with first, then, after that, finally.

YOU CHOOSE

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR DATA REQUEST

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. If the student needs data from a real client this week, send the e-mail (no confidential numbers in class).

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Headcount: Número de funcionários (quadro de pessoal). É o primeiro número que o BPO pede para precificar a folha.
  • NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement): Acordo de confidencialidade. O cliente costuma exigir antes de mandar dados internos.
  • Payroll report: Relatório da folha (com salários, descontos, encargos). Confidencial; o vendedor normalmente não precisa dos salários, só de volumes.
  • Export (from the ERP): Extrair/exportar dados do sistema (ex.: planilha gerada pelo Oracle ou TOTVS).
  • Data request: Pedido de dados ao cliente para montar a proposta (volumes, número de notas, funcionários, sistemas).

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

We can to send the report (We can send the report), You can send me the headcount? (Can you send me the headcount?), I can’t to meet on Monday (I can’t meet on Monday), Send me the numbers. (Could you send me the numbers, please?). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
