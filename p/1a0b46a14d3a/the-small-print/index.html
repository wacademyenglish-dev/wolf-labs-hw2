The Small Print · C1 Unit 9 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on reading contract language and explaining it in plain English: shall / shall not (must / must not), prior to (before), upon (when), subject to (it depends on), provided that (only if), in accordance with, liable for, terminate, notwithstanding, hereby, herein.

The story: A residential lease (clauses 4–7, invented), and three clients who signed without reading: a gym member who wants to cancel, a tenant worried about her deposit and a customer with a broken phone screen.

About 49 min: I · The Contract (11) · II · Contract or Plain English? (9) · III · Speed Round (6) · IV · The Residents’ Vote (8) · V · Three Worried Clients (15).

Open with: “Do you read every line of a contract before you sign? Be honest!”

The goal is not to write contracts: it is to understand them and explain them simply. The big trap: shall read as the future (“vai”); in contracts it means must. In the mission you are three worried clients: the student reads your clause and explains it in plain English, with advice. All contracts are invented.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

shall / shall not = must / must not · prior to + -ing / noun · upon + -ing · subject to = it depends on · provided that = only if · liable for, terminate, notwithstanding, hereby

THE CONTRACT

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

CONTRACT OR PLAIN ENGLISH?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

SPEED ROUND

A 60-second speed round. Tap Start the clock, then Right or Pass for each item; the answer is on your screen. At the end of class, play again and beat the score.

THREE WORRIED CLIENTS

You are each client, a bit worried. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student explains your clause in plain English and gives advice. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

shall = will (the future), prior to sign, subject to = no conditions, liable to the damage, terminate = finish the payment, hereby = here. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE RESIDENTS’ VOTE (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the clients, your student sits on the residents’ committee: the landlord wants three new clauses (no pets, notice before a party, a fee for leaving early). For each one they explain it in plain English and vote Yes, Propose a change or No. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. In Your clause, start the 45 seconds; then you play the landlord and argue back. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (watch for prior to sign); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON
