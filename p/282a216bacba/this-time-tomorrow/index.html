This Time Tomorrow · Disclosure Day · The World Clocks

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A director who keeps looking up, and a plan for the whole world at once.

The grammar: Will Be Doing: This time tomorrow, the whole world will be watching.

Short class (about 55 min): I · III The Trailer · IV The Arena · VI The 2 a.m. Message · VII The World Clocks · VIII Ask Me · IX. Skip if you’re short on time: V Line by Line. On every practice page the student speaks first and taps Check on their own screen.

Open with: “You know the plan now. In the trailer, what will the man be doing this time tomorrow? Guess.”

Disclosure Day (2026), Steven Spielberg, with Emily Blunt and Josh O’Connor. A man has stolen secret data, proof that we are not alone and that the truth has been hidden for decades, and he plans “full disclosure, to the whole world” at the same moment. A TV reporter in Kansas City starts speaking in sounds nobody understands. The grammar: future continuous: will be + verb-ing for a scene in progress at a moment in the future (this time tomorrow, at nine), won’t be + -ing, the polite question Will you be using…?, and where it stops: state verbs (everybody will know, never will be knowing).

A note for the teacher: tense, not graphic (a chase, a jump from a train). Lines checked against the official captions (24/09); the train moment (1:52 to 2:25) is ESTIMATED, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Tomorrow’s Paper (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Tomorrow’s Paper: the front page of tomorrow, the day the secret comes out, with three stories. The student picks one, says what they think will happen, reads the start and reacts with will be + -ing (this time tomorrow, people will be…).

The words (II): steal, secrets, understand, truth and disclosure, before the video, in hear it in the video and mystery meaning. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The Trailer (III): after “Watch the trailer now”, two short tasks sit next to the video. Which word did you hear? four lines with a pair of words that sound alike (steal or still, can or can’t, part or path, will or would); the student taps one. One word is wrong: the student says the right word, then taps Check. Every line has a ▶ time button: it plays the trailer from that moment.

The 2 a.m. Message (VI): the message the man sends to the whole world at 2:04 a.m. (our text, in the world of the film), on a dark screen. The student reads it out loud and taps the verbs about tomorrow (every word can be tapped; wrong taps go red: are looking is now, not tomorrow). Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line (be + verb-ing, in progress, won’t be hiding, Will you be, will know). When the rule is complete, a small copy of it stays in the corner on VII and VIII.

The World Clocks (VII): the truth comes out at 9 a.m. in Kansas City; six clocks show the same moment in six cities. Each card gives the city, its local time and one person’s day (Tokyo · 11 p.m. · Yuki: dinner at 7, TV at 9, bed at 10:30). The time decides the answer: the student finds where that hour falls in the person’s day and says it (Yuki will be sleeping. She won’t be watching the news.), then taps Check: the right sentence appears and the clock lights up. Under it, one question about the student’s tomorrow. At the end, the student works out the time in their own city. You listen and talk; there is nothing for you to tap.

Ask Me (VIII): the student asks about your tomorrow. They build each question out loud from the prompt (where · have lunch · tomorrow), tap Check, then ask you. Answer for real, and they compare (You’ll be …, but I’ll be …).

The ending (IX, This Time Tomorrow): you pick A or B. A: the student is the TV reporter, live tomorrow at nine, and you are the man with the files. B: the student’s own tomorrow, hour by hour; you ask one polite question at each stop. Three minutes. Then one minute of writing (this time tomorrow, one polite question, one hard thing); the corrector marks will working, will be work, will be knowing, You will be…?, and it goes to My notes.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an empty highway at night under a strange light in the clouds. I: Warm-up and Tomorrow’s Paper. III: the trailer in three steps, with the two tasks next to the video. V: five frames on the film strip. VI: the message on a dark screen, and the rule plaque. VII: The World Clocks. Six analog clocks, six cities, each at its local time tomorrow; every sentence the student checks spins the hands a full day and lights the face with the scene (reading the news, at the museum, sleeping). At the end all six glow together: one moment, everywhere. VIII: Ask Me. IX: two posters, A and B, and the note for My notes.

THE COMMON ERRORS

I will watching (no be), she will be read (no -ing), the scientists are talking this time tomorrow, You will be using the car?, Will you using…?, everybody will be knowing.

THE ARENA (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer, your student takes a side: should the whole world find out tomorrow at nine, all at once? There is no right answer. Let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then start the one-minute clock. When they finish, tap Swap sides: now they defend the other side. Use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast will be + -ing quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Tomorrow’s Paper, IV The Arena, VII The World Clocks and VIII Ask Me.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The 2 a.m. Message.
  • Write: IX This Time Tomorrow (talk, then one minute of writing).
