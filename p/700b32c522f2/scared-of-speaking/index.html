Scared of Speaking · Open Mic

Lena moved countries for work. Her English was tested: four B2s. Twelve days later her phone rang and she didn’t answer it. For thirty days she recorded a voice memo every night, and this hour is the tape. The grammar is one shape: adjective + small word + -ING. Portuguese puts the plain verb after every preposition, so the student will say scared of speak and good in. The screen is an open mic: the nerves meter drops with every whole sentence; at zero, ON AIR.

THE SMALL WORD

The -ING is learned in ten minutes; the small word takes years. Demand the whole sentence every round in scene V. Scene I: keep their three sentences word for word; they come back at the end.

SEVEN RINGS · LET THEM FAIL

Scene IV: say nothing while it rings. The discomfort is the content. Scene VI: the fast voicemail is real speed; accept “almost nothing” before the slow one.

THE AUDIO

Six recordings, checked on 06/10. The transcripts follow the voice (it’s, I’m, that’s, we’ll); memo 30 starts straight with “I answered the phone today.”

THE CALL BACK (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Lena’s fears, your student becomes Lena and calls Dan back to get the package. You are Dan: ask the questions on the screen, and keep your limit (only you see it: Saturday costs £5 extra; on Thursday you can come until 8 PM). Wait five seconds in silence before you help. Use the PUSH line, then close the deal on the screen together. Recast quietly (worried about missing, not worried to miss); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON
