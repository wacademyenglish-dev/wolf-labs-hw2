At the Conference · English for Healthcare · Lesson 15 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: presenting a poster or a short talk (aim, method, findings, meaning, limitation) with careful scientific language (suggests, may, appeared to, in our sample) and answering audience questions, including the ones you can’t fully answer.

The story: A fictional poster: text reminders reduced missed physiotherapy appointments from 22% to 9% in one clinic. Then four recorded audience questions (American, British and Indian accents).

About 45 min: I · A One-Minute Pitch (9) · II · Proves or Suggests? (9) · III · The Team Vote (7) · IV · Questions From the Audience (16) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Have you ever presented at a congress? What scared you most: the talk or the questions?”

Brazilian presenters often lose points by over-claiming (“this proves”) or by panicking at questions they can’t answer. Model the honest answer: “We didn’t look at that in this study, but…”. If the student has a real study, use it in the Q&A instead of the fictional one.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Our aim was to… · We studied / We found that… · This suggests that… may… · However, one limitation was… · That’s a great question. · We didn’t look at that, but…

A ONE-MINUTE PITCH

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

PROVES OR SUGGESTS?

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

QUESTIONS FROM THE AUDIENCE

The task with the real clock of the test. Read the task together, tap Start: the prep time runs, then the answer time starts by itself. Do not help while the clock runs. When it stops, go through the checklist together (tap what was there), then Show answer for the model. Try again is a good idea: the second try is always better.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to prepare a one-minute pitch of their own work (or a study they admire) for the final station.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

This proves that…, All patients…, I don’t know. (and nothing else), reading the poster aloud word by word. B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE TEAM VOTE (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the questions from the audience, a council vote at a team meeting: three proposals (one ten-minute talk a year for everyone, the clinic pays for a conference only if you present a poster, one meeting a month only in English). For each one the student votes yes, no, or proposes a change, then says why with careful language; you vote the other way. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk (it may…, it might…, this suggests…, I’d like to propose a change); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
