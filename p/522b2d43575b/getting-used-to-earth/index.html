Getting Used to Earth · Supergirl · The Earth Manual

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A girl who used to have nothing, getting used to everything.

The grammar: Used To, Used To It: She used to live under a red sun. She is getting used to this one.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and Kara’s Bag · III The Trailer · V Where Should She Live? · VI Used To, Used To It · VII The Earth Manual · VIII New for You · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Kara now. In the trailer, what is she still not used to on Earth? Guess.”

Supergirl (2026), DC Studios, with Milly Alcock as Kara Zor-El, Superman’s cousin. Kara grew up on a piece of Krypton under a red sun, with almost nobody but her dog Krypto. Now she lives on Earth, under a yellow sun; her cousin Clark keeps calling, worried she won’t find her people, and she says she has none. The grammar: used to + verb (a past habit that is over: she used to live under a red sun) × be used to + -ing / a noun (normal now: Krypto is used to flying) × get used to + -ing (becoming normal: she is getting used to eating Earth food), plus didn’t use to and Are you used to…?

A note for the teacher: superhero action, no blood. Lines checked against the official captions (23/09), told in our own words; the song under the trailer is never written.

Warm-up and Kara’s Bag (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Kara’s Bag: five things come out of Kara’s bag, one at a time: Argo City, the red sun, Krypto, the window on Earth, the city where Clark lives. With each, the student contrasts what she used to do with what she is (not) used to or getting used to. At the end, they tell her story in their words.

The words (II): worried, lucky, innocent, pain and home, before the video, in turn the cards and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is Kara, one month on Earth. The teacher asks five questions (what you used to do on Krypton, the yellow sun, what you are getting used to, people, what you miss). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a farmhouse under a sky with one red star. I: Warm-up and Kara’s Bag. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a role card (you moved to a new country, alone, with your dog); tap to reveal the twist (your cousin keeps calling, worried you will never find your people). Watch: Which word did you hear? and What happens?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: four cards (what Kara is not used to yet, what she used to have, what Krypto is used to, what was strange for you once). IV: line by line. V, Where Should She Live?: the arena (with Clark in the city, or alone with Krypto?), one minute each side, then swap. VI, Used To, Used To It: the student matches five forms (she used to live, Krypto is used to flying, she is getting used to eating, she didn’t use to have friends, Are you used to living alone?) to their meaning. When all five are right, the rule appears and stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Earth Manual: Kara’s notebook, open: on the left the index “How to live on Earth”, on the right the page just written, with a small drawing and a stamp. For each of six pages the student reads the clue and picks the stamp that fits the meaning (used to, used to it, getting used to it, not used to it yet), then says the line for the manual out loud. Every right stamp writes one page. VIII, New for You: the teacher picks A (a new place) or B (your childhood) for the student; the student talks about it and taps each form (used to, be used to, get used to, didn’t use to, Are you used to) when they use it. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

she was used to live (a past habit: used to live), she use to, isn’t use to, doesn’t used to, getting used to eat, is used to fly, Krypto used to flying (no be), get used with, Do you used to…?, didn’t used to.

WHERE SHOULD SHE LIVE? (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the arena: Kara is new on Earth. Should she live with Clark, in the city, or alone with Krypto? The student picks a side and defends it for one minute with used to / be used to / get used to (she used to be alone, she isn’t used to noise yet); then tap Swap sides and they defend the other side. There is no right answer, and the hot seat at the end is not repeated here. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own life. Recast quietly while they talk (she is used to live, get used with, didn’t used to); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Kara’s Bag, V Where Should She Live?, VII The Earth Manual, VIII New for You and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Used To, Used To It.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
