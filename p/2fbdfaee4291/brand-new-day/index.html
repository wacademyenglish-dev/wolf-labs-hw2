Brand New Day · Spider-Man · The Split Screen

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A hero who used to have everyone, and has been alone since.

The grammar: Used To × Have Been: He used to have friends. He has lived alone since then.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V Used To × Have Been · VI The Split Screen · VII What Would You Give Up? · VIII The Debate. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Peter now. In the trailer, how long has he been alone? Guess.”

Spider-Man: Brand New Day (Sony and Marvel, 2026) is the fourth Spider-Man film with Tom Holland. Peter Parker used to have everything: Aunt May, MJ, his best friend Ned, and everybody knew his name. To save the world, a spell made everyone forget him; since then he has lived alone, and nobody has remembered him. Now his body is changing, and he needs help. The grammar: used to for a past habit that is over (he used to live with May; did you use to…?; didn’t use to) and the present perfect from then until now, with for (a period), since (a point) and ever (experience), and the past simple for finished time (I saw her yesterday).

A note for the teacher: the trailer has some action and one or two mild swear words (adult class); nothing violent is on the lesson screens. The times follow its captions (23/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up (I): a conversation, one question at a time, about the student’s life: then and now, old friends, being alone in a new place, being forgotten. Under each photo, one short line connects the question to Peter’s story, so the world comes in little by little. Let them talk; ask more with the line under the question. The chips help them start (used to…, I have… since…). Don’t correct the grammar yet: the rule comes in V.

The words (II): pretend, forget, dangerous, vulnerable and neighbor, before the video, in predict the story and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (VIII, The Debate): “It is better to be forgotten, if it keeps the people you love safe.” The student picks a side; the teacher takes the other one. Three reasons on each side and three helping phrases. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is Peter’s small apartment at night. I: Warm-up. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a slider (“Would you let everybody forget you to keep the people you love safe?”, from No way to Yes, I would); the student places it and says why. Watch: True or lie? Six sentences, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. After: six cards about Peter’s life (He lived with Aunt May, He lives alone…); the student taps a card and then its box, Before the spell or Since the spell. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, Used To × Have Been: six messages, each with one wrong word (We use to know each other…). The student says the right word, taps Check, and the rule board appears and stays in the corner. VI, The Split Screen: one photo of the apartment cut in half: the left half in sepia is BEFORE (used to), the right half in color is NOW (present perfect). Six captions to build from words (“before · Peter · live with Aunt May”): the student says it, taps Check, and the caption sticks on its side; the control under the photo slides from “used to” to “has / have + done”. VII, What Would You Give Up?: a ranking of five things the student would give up to keep someone safe, then the teacher’s order and one top 3 together. VIII: the debate.

THE COMMON ERRORS

we use to know, used to lived, were used to walk, used walk, did you used to, not used to, he lives alone since, I am here since Monday, I live here for two weeks, since two years, for Monday, he haven’t, Did you ever met…?, Have you ever meet…?, I have seen her yesterday.

WHAT WOULD YOU GIVE UP? (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After Peter’s split screen, the student ranks five things they would give up to keep someone they love safe, from first to last. There is no right order. Let them tap, wait five seconds in silence, and ask them to explain their first and their last; then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Tap “Show my teacher’s order”, compare, and agree on one top 3 together. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (used to, the present perfect with for / since); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up, VI The Split Screen, VII What Would You Give Up? and VIII The Debate.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: V Used To × Have Been.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
