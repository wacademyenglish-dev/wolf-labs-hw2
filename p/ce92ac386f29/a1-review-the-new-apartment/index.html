The New Apartment · A1 Unit 4 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 4 (Where things are). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Your student helps at a small rental agency. You are the owner of three apartments, and then the client.

About 43 min: I · Speed Round (6) · II · The New Apartment (15) · III · OK or Not OK? (5) · IV · Your Home (6) · V · Would You Rather? (7) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Pick a door.” There is no order. One minute per door, no notes.

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

at / in / on with places · next to, in front of, between · there is / there are · Is there…? / Are there…? · some / any · how much / how many

SPEED ROUND

A 60-second speed round. Tap Start the clock, then Right or Pass for each item; the answer is on your screen. At the end of class, play again and beat the score.

THE NEW APARTMENT

You are the owner. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the apartment card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then you are the client: the student describes one apartment with there is / there are and where it is. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

YOUR HOME

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says one sentence with there is / there are. You answer first.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

have two bedrooms (for there are), in the home, between of, next of, there isn’t some, how much chairs. A1: one correction per answer is enough.

OK OR NOT OK? (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the three apartments, the student is the client: three quick cases (no kitchen, a school in front, no elevator), OK or not OK, with there is / there isn’t / there are. Answer first yourself, as a model. Then let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, and ask the PUSH line (only you see it): a yes / no question. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves. About 5 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. There are three; use one when the review has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · Speed Round and VI · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · The New Apartment, III · OK or Not OK? and V · Would You Rather?.
  • Write: IV · Your Home.
