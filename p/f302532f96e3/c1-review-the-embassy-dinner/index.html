The Embassy Dinner · C1 Unit 5 Review

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 5 (Distance and diplomacy). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: A dinner at an embassy (invented). Your student is the host. You are three delicate guests: the ambassador’s wife (bad seat), a journalist (no comments) and a young chef (her name is missing from the menu).

About 49 min: I · The Ladder (8) · II · Three Delicate Guests (15) · III · The Morning After (7) · IV · Say It the New Way (7) · V · Delicate Questions (8) · VI · I Can (4).

Open with: “Climb the ladder. Six steps, from easy to hard.”

A review is not a new lesson. Do not explain the grammar again: let the student try first. Mistakes here are useful: they show you what to review. Every review of the course uses a different mix of games, so the student never does the same thing twice.

THE TOPICS OF THIS UNIT

academic hedging · diplomatic requests · crisis language: agentless passive · difficult news with empathy · diplomatic disagreement · passive without agent + neither… nor

THE LADDER

Six steps from easy to hard. A good answer: tap Go up. Not yet: Try again on the same step.

THREE DELICATE GUESTS

You are each guest, a little offended. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then the student answers you diplomatically. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

SAY IT THE NEW WAY

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

DELICATE QUESTIONS

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

it is proven that (overclaiming), I wonder if you can (stiff), the waiter made a mistake (blaming), that’s not my problem, you are wrong, neither checked nor not approved. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

THE MORNING AFTER (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Dinner saved; the next morning the ambassador (you) wants to know who was responsible for three incidents (the seating, the journalist’s post, the missing name on the menu). Each case has three pieces of evidence. Your student chooses a verdict and says it with Unit 5 distance (The evidence suggests…, It would appear that…, Mistakes were made…, I take your point, but…). There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Focus: I · The Ladder and IV · Say It the New Way and VI · I Can.
  • Out loud: II · Three Delicate Guests and III · The Morning After and V · Delicate Questions.
