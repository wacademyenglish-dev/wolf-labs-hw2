Nobody Does That Anymore · The Museum

The whole hour is two questions: Did it happen many times? Is it over? If both answers are yes, the sentence can say USED TO; if either is no, it can’t. Portuguese has one past shape for all of this, so the difference isn’t in the student’s head yet. The screen is a small museum: eight cases of ordinary things everybody used to use. The student engraves each plaque.

THE TWO DOORS ARE THE HOUR

Scenes III and IV. Make the student say each door out loud before tapping. The door people walk through: I used to like pizza, and they still do. Don’t explain; ask and now? and wait.

THE D NOBODY HEARS

used to and use to sound the same. On paper, didn’t used to is the mistake. And I use to for the present is usually.

THE AUDIO

Nine plaques (eight cases and the Sunday call), checked against the recordings. Play first; the text opens after.

BET ON THE PAST (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the Two Doors, your student bets how people used to do three things before phones and the internet: meet friends, send a document fast, hear a song again. Let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, and ask why, with used to. Then press Check what happened and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their life. Recast quietly (didn’t use to, not didn’t used to); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
