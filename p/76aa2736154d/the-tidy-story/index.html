The Tidy Story · The Evidence Ladder

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: “It takes ten thousand hours.” Everybody says it; almost nobody can say who counted.

The grammar: The Rule You Hear: Think of a rule for success that you hear all the time.

Short class (about 50 min): I The Rule You Hear · II Who Counted? · III Move the Name · IV The Time Goes Inside · VII One Minute, Count the Owners · XI Unpopular Opinions · XII File It. Skip if you’re short on time: V, IX, X (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 70 min.

Open with: “Where did you first hear it?”

“It takes ten thousand hours.” Everybody says it; almost nobody can say who counted. This hour teaches students to hand a claim back to its owner, or to notice there is none. Portuguese has one door and gives It is said that …. English has a second: Federer is said to have tried ten sports, still a sentence about Federer. The reporting verb stays present; the past goes inside the infinitive (to have tried). Students say He is said that he … and is said to tried. On screen, an evidence ladder with five rungs: said, thought, believed, reported, known. Each claim climbs only as high as its evidence pays for.

NOT A DEBUNKING

The hour doesn’t say the rule is false. It asks who counted, and how to say it without signing it. III: the student says each rumor with the name first, then taps Check. IV: sort six sentences into NOW and BEFORE. V: match each situation to the sentence that keeps the topic first. X: three friend cards. XI: three unpopular opinions on a 1–5 scale (see below).

THE RECORDINGS

Each with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens after listening. Scene II: two colleagues at the coffee machine; the chain of sources lights up back to a question mark; the student taps True or Lie on four lines. Scene IX: a researcher uses four reporting verbs, the student taps the verb she used, and each claim climbs to its rung.

THE VIDEO

Scene VII: about one minute (0:07 to 1:15), sealed until the student slides the ruler. While it plays, the student taps True or Lie (the ▶ replays the line); afterwards, matches each claim to its owner.

A NOTE

The sister lesson “It Is Thought to Have Been …” goes deeper into the perfect infinitive; here the center is choosing the door (name first or IT) and the weight of the verb.

The words (VI): practice, curious, athletes, specializing and careers, before the video, in mystery meaning and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (VIII, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

UNPOPULAR OPINIONS (XI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the video, the ladder and the friend, your student finally signs (or doesn’t sign) three tidy stories: a better-looking CV, talent against practice, and kids who choose early. For each statement they tap a number from 1 to 5 and defend it; take the opposite number and argue back. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence before they speak, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the position harder. Listen for the ladder verbs (is said to, is known to have) and recast quietly (is said that he → he is said to); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The Rule You Hear, X Keep Your Distance and XI Unpopular Opinions.
  • Listen: II Who Counted?, VI The Words, VII One Minute, Count the Owners, VIII Line by Line and IX The Evidence Ladder.
  • Focus: III Move the Name, IV The Time Goes Inside and V Whose Sentence Is It?
  • Write: XII File It.
