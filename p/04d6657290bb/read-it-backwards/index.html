Read It Backwards · The Reading Lamp

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A woman gave her father’s workshop to a small museum, and every object needs a label: a worn late-Victorian rosewood smoothing plane.

The grammar: The Rule: English builds to the LEFT of the noun, and joins with nothing.

Short class (about 50 min): I An Object of Yours · II Find the Head · III The Drawers · IV The Rule · V The Hyphen · VI The Hot Takes · VII Too Far · IX Your Label. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 57 min.

Open with: “What is it made of, and what color is it?”

A woman gave her father’s workshop to a small museum, and every object needs a label: a worn late-Victorian rosewood smoothing plane. Six words before you find out it’s a plane. Many languages build to the right of the noun and join with a preposition; English builds to the left and joins with nothing. So: find the head (the last word), then read backwards. Modifiers follow an order no native can recite: opinion, size, age, shape, color, origin, material, purpose. Inside the stack, numbers go singular: a two-year-old car. And at C1, the skill is knowing when to stop.

ON SCREEN

The reading lamp: the words sit on a wooden rail; when the student taps the head, the lamp lights it and walks left, and the phrase unpacks in steps below (driver → delivery driver → part-time delivery driver …). The drawers (scene III): nine labeled drawers, OPINION to PURPOSE and the HEAD; every right tap drops a modifier into its drawer. Scene IV: the curator’s letter (the student taps the head of each phrase, then finishes the rule; the rule stays in the corner on the next pages). Scene V: she rewrites five sentences with the number inside (a two-year-old car), says them and taps Check.

DON’T TEACH THE LIST

Scene III: don’t make them memorize the eight slots. After each label, let them say the wrong order on purpose and hear it. In scene VI every stack is grammatical; the question is whether a reader can follow it. There is no single right place to cut: accept a defended answer.

THE SALE

Scene VIII: the customer speaks on the screen; each of the three labels has one mistake and the student says the right one (Check shows it). Then a one-minute timer: she sells her own object. You are the customer: ask “What exactly is it?” and make her unpack it backwards.

THE HOT TAKES (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VI is new: four unpopular opinions about old things, second-hand objects and online shops. The student taps a number from 1 to 5 and defends it, using at least one long noun phrase with the head last (a solid forty-year-old oak table). There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then take the opposite number and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I An Object of Yours, VI The Hot Takes, VIII Sell It.
  • Focus: II Find the Head, III The Drawers, IV The Rule, V The Hyphen, VII Too Far.
  • Write: IX, the object from scene I as a museum label, unpacked. It goes to their My notes.
