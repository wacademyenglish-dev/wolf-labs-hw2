Nobody Makes Him · The Training Board

One of the best-paid football players alive fries his own eggs at seven in the morning. Nobody makes him. Portuguese says one thing for have to and another for don’t need to, and the student builds I must to, I don’t must, I haven’t to. English treats have to as a normal verb: don’t have to, do you have to, had to. The screen is a training board: every task gets a stamp.

THE VIDEO STAYS

The same video as the old lesson, in sealed clips: A (his morning, scene II), then B and C (sixteen, alone, scene VIII, right after the bet in VII). Each clip stops by itself. Answer before, talk after.

A NORMAL VERB · MUST HAS NO PAST

HAVE TO takes DO / DOES / DID in questions and negatives, and the S sits on HAS. The past of both must and have to is had to. And don’t have to (free) is the opposite of mustn’t (forbidden): scene VI.

PLACE YOUR BET (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right before the video of his sixteen-year-old self, your student bets on what happened: how long his father stayed, the hardest thing he had to do, what he says about it now. Let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own life. Bet too, so there is something to win. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (had to, never musted); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. Then go to VIII and skip its first guess: play the clips and press Check to see who won. Short on time? Press Check without the video.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
