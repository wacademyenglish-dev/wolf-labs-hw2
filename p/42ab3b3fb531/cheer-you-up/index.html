Cheer You Up · Pluribus · The Happy Map

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: One morning, the whole world is happy. Except one woman.

The grammar: The Little Word: Cheer her up, figure it out. The little word goes in the middle.

The class (about 50 min): I Warm-up and Carol’s Phone · III Place Your Bets · IV The Trailer · VI The Little Word · VII The Happy Map · VIII Your Bad Days · IX A Note for a Bad Day. II The Words and V Line by Line are short listening scenes. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Carol now. In the trailer, how do people try to cheer her up? Guess.”

Pluribus (Apple TV, 2025) is the series by Vince Gilligan, the creator of Breaking Bad, set in Albuquerque, New Mexico. One morning everybody on Earth wakes up happy, calm and kind, and they all speak with one friendly voice. Everybody except Carol, a writer, who is still herself and still unhappy. The whole planet wants to cheer her up; Carol wants to figure it out. The grammar: separable phrasal verbs. With a pronoun, the pronoun goes in the middle (cheer her up, figure it out, turn it off, pick me up, give it back, look it up); with a noun, both orders are fine (turn off the TV / turn the TV off).

A note for the teacher: the trailer is strange and calm, with one moment where Carol tests the voice by asking for weapons; nothing violent is on the lesson screens. The five frames follow the official captions (read on 23/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Carol’s Phone (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Carol’s Phone: the screen becomes Carol’s phone on the morning everybody on Earth woke up happy. Messages arrive one at a time; the student reads each one out loud and says what is happening. At the end they answer the last message, speaking. The phrasal verbs (cheer you up, figure it out, turn it off) are already in the messages: let the student notice them, the rule comes later.

The words (II): morning, happy, question, different and free, before the video, in mixed letters and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, A Note for a Bad Day): the student writes a short note to a friend who is sad, with three of the verbs and the little word in the right place. The corrector marks cheer up you, figure it, pick me at. It goes to My notes.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an empty, too-perfect suburban street at dusk. I: Warm-up and Carol’s Phone. III: Place Your Bets, three bets before the trailer (Check after the video). IV: the trailer in three steps. Before: a ruler (everybody is happy all the time: good or scary?). Watch: Which word did you hear? and One word is wrong, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: three taps (what do the voices want? what does Carol ask? what will they find out?), then the student says the answer with the phrasal verb. V: five frames on the film strip. VI, The Little Word: a note under Carol’s door. The student reads it out loud and taps the six phrasal verbs (cheer you up, turn it off, pick you up, look it up, give it back, figure it out); then the rule on the right is finished with the student (pronoun in the middle, a noun can go either side, the little word is part of the verb). VII, The Happy Map: the city seen from above: two columns of three neighborhoods, a street in the middle with Carol’s grey house. The student reads the clue, says the sentence and taps Check; the neighborhood turns pink and gold with a smile. Carol’s house stays grey to the end. The rule stays in the corner. VIII, Your Bad Days: three people talk to you (a sad friend, a classmate, a neighbor). The student picks how to answer and the talk appears: read it out loud. IX: the note.

THE COMMON ERRORS

cheer up her, figure out it, turn off it, pick up me, give back it, put on it, look up it, turn down it; the lost little word: figure it, cheer her, pick me at nine, put it, turn it, please; turn it of.

PLACE YOUR BETS (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right before the trailer, the student bets on Carol’s story: what the friendly voice wants, what Carol asks, and what the voices promise her. There is no wrong bet: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own life. The starters use the phrasal verbs with the pronoun in the middle (cheer her up, turn it off, figure it out). After the video, come back for one minute and tap Check on each bet: who was right? Recast mistakes quietly (cheer up her); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About five minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Carol’s Phone, III Place Your Bets, VII The Happy Map and VIII Your Bad Days.
  • Listen: II The Words, IV The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Little Word.
  • Write: IX A Note for a Bad Day.
