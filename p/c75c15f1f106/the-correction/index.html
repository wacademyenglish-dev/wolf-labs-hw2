The Correction · The Missing “A”

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A newspaper got one letter wrong.

The grammar: The Report: the survey report, before the paper changed one letter.

Short class (about 45 min): I Few or a Few? · II The Report · III Which Side of Zero? · IV The Help Desk · V Most of the What? · VI Can You Count It? · X Back to You. Skip if you’re short on time: VII The Word That Decides, VIII The Banned Words and IX Write the Correction (marked EXTRA on your screen). On the practice pages the student speaks first and taps Check on their own screen. Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “Friends in your city”

A newspaper got one letter wrong. The report said A few teachers still ask for paper copies (some, and it’s fine). The paper dropped one letter and printed FEW TEACHERS STILL ASK FOR PAPER COPIES (almost none, so the city wants to stop printing them). The phone hasn’t stopped since seven. a few / a little = some; few / little = hardly any. few with things you count, little with things you don’t. Plus most / most of the / most of them. Both versions are correct English: that’s why the mistake survives for years. The headline is set in metal type; the loose copper “A” goes in and out.

NO RECORDINGS IN THIS ONE

Your voice is the recording. Where the screen asks a question, ask it out loud with real feeling (the callers in scene IV are angry).

THE PAGES

I Few or a Few?: four situations. The student taps how it is for them (enough / almost none), the sentence appears, and they say it out loud and why. Their answers come back in scene X.

II The Report: a survey report on a paper-style screen. The student reads it and taps every few / a few / little / a little (every word can be tapped; only the four count). Then they finish the rule on the right by choosing inside each line. When it is ready, the headline above gets its missing A back and the rule stays in the corner of the next pages.

III Which Side of Zero?: six sentences and six meanings. Tap a sentence, then what it means (almost empty room / some people were there). No article-picking: the meaning picks the pair.

IV The Help Desk: six blinking lines. The student reads the caller, explains in their own words, then taps Check: the model answer appears and the light goes off. Under it, one question for a short chat.

V Most of the What?: six lines with the wrong shape (the most of, most them, most of teachers, most the). The student says each right, then Check. The three shapes stay in the corner.

VI Can You Count It?: six words (money, friends, time…). Tap a word, then its box: I can count it / I can’t count it. If a word goes in the wrong box, tap it to take it back.

VII The Word That Decides: five teachers, five lines. True or lie? If it is a lie, the student says the truth (the article decides: the model appears under the line).

VIII The Banned Words (extra): six numbers out of 140. The student says each one without most, many or a lot, then taps Check.

IX Write the Correction: three drafts of the correction, one wrong word in each. The student says the right word, then Check; the headline appears with its A. Then they tell the correction with the facts and tap each word when they use it (most of the, a few, none of them, barely any).

THE NOUN NEVER DECIDES

In few / a few, the article decides. If your student hesitates, cover the noun. The corrector can’t tell a “few” that should be “a few”: ask “Is it a complaint?”

SCENE I COMES BACK

Your student’s four answers about their own life return in the last scene.

THE JURY (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the five teachers, your student is the jury for the missing “A”: the editor who cut it, the paper (where the correction goes) and the readers who shared the headline. They read the evidence, choose a verdict and defend it with a few, few, a little or most of; you argue the other side. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (watch for “the most of the” and “a few money”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Few or a Few?, IV The Help Desk, VII The Word That Decides, VIII The Jury, IX Write the Correction (VII and IX are no longer extra).
  • Focus: II The Report, III Which Side of Zero?, V Most of the What?, VI Can You Count It?
  • Write: X, their own life. It goes to their My notes.
