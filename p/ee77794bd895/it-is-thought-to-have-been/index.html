It Is Thought to Have Been… · The Dig at Kestrel Hill

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Summer on Kestrel Hill, a grassy mound above the Mera River (invented).

The grammar: The Rule: The belief is now. The event is before now.

Short class (about 51 min): I You and the Past · II The Words · III A Famous Hill · V Report, Section 1: The Core · VI The Rule · VII Report, Section 2: The Radio · X The Arena · XI End of the Season. Skip if you’re short on time: IV Line by Line, VIII Your Own Layers and IX The Interview (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 65 min.

Open with: “What is the oldest building you have ever been inside? How old was it?”

Summer on Kestrel Hill, a grassy mound above the Mera River (invented). A report from 1931 tells the local legend as a fact: the Burning King set the town on fire in one night. This season a new team is digging, and the student is the new trench supervisor writing the season report. The layers tell a different story, and a good report never says more than the evidence: The town is thought to have been destroyed by fire. · The jars are thought to have held oil. · Trench C is being excavated. · We had the charcoal dated. On screen, a soil core with six colored layers (each gets a museum tag when it becomes a report line) and a field radio whose tag decides the form.

REVIEW LESSON

Works as a follow-up to a lesson on is said to / is thought to / it is believed that. Here the present form is only a quick review; the center is the perfect infinitive, being done and have something done.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: an animated lesson (about five minutes) on the search for an ancient city from an old story, sealed in four chapters. No line from the video is on the screen; the student answers in report language.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

Portuguese reports the past with a present verb and a normal past after it, so students keep the simple infinitive (is believed to be built) or drop the passive (is believed to have built). The continuous passive comes out as the site is excavating. And Portuguese says “we dated the charcoal” even when a lab did it: English needs we had the charcoal dated.

THE PAGES

  • III A Famous Hill: before the video, a slider (did the city exist?) and a short choice. In each of the four chapters the student reads two lines BEFORE pressing play, taps Right or Wrong, and for the wrong ones says the true line; tap the time to hear that moment again. After the video: three report lines, said out loud, then Check.
  • V The Core: six layers. The student reads the field note, says the report line out loud (without signing it), then taps Check. Before the rule, collect what comes out: most will say is thought to be destroyed. Layer 5 is the other way round: the jars DID the holding, so no been.
  • VI The Rule: five report lines with one wrong part each. The student finds it and says it right, then taps Check. The five kinds sit in the corner.
  • VII The Radio: read each radio note like a crackly walkie-talkie. The student taps the tag (REPORT IT · RIGHT NOW · THE LAB); the screen shows the line to say and writes it on the report page.
  • VIII Your Own Layers: six cards about the student’s real life, each with the beginning of a sentence.
  • IX The Interview: three radio questions in cards and a 60-second clock. The student answers on air in report language, then asks you back (your answers are in the teacher box).

The words (II): magnificent, skeptical, scholars, treasure and evidence, before the video, in hear it in the video and mystery meaning. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE ARENA (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

End of the season: should the bronze helmet and the twelve jars stay in a small museum in the village by the hill, or go to the national museum in the capital? The student picks a side and defends it for one minute in report language (is thought to have been, is being built, have copies made); then they swap and defend the other side. Wait five seconds in silence before they start, and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and the Past, V Report, Section 1: The Core, VIII Your Own Layers, IX Report, Section 3: The Interview and X The Arena.
  • Listen: II The Words, III A Famous Hill and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI The Rule and VII Report, Section 2: The Radio.
  • Write: XI End of the Season.
