He Wouldn’t Be Here · The Boys · The Vought Ranking

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Heroes as a product, and a normal guy who lost everything.

The grammar: Then and Now: If it hadn’t happened, he wouldn’t be here.

The class (about 57 min): I Warm-up and Pick a Side · III The Trailer · IV The Hero Jury · V Scene by Scene · VI Then and Now · VII The Vought Ranking · VIII Your Other Life · IX One Minute. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Hughie now. In the trailer, what would be different if Robin were still alive? Guess.”

The Boys (Prime Video, 2019): superheroes owned by a company, Vought, rich, famous and often terrible. Hughie is a normal guy until a hero, A-Train, runs through his girlfriend Robin in the street; Vought calls it an accident. Vought’s PR team decides what the public thinks of each hero. The grammar: mixed conditionals: past → now (if + had + participle, would + verb: if A-Train hadn’t run so fast, Robin would be alive now) and now → past (if + past, would have + participle: if Homelander were honest, he would have told the truth last year).

A note for the teacher: the trailer has strong violence and strong language. Watch it first and decide whether to show it; the lesson works without it. Only clean lines are used, checked against the subtitles (23/09) and told in our own words. The heroes on the board are names only, no faces.

Warm-up and Pick a Side (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the show under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Pick a Side: Vought and its heroes, or the Boys. The student taps one side and defends it for thirty seconds. Then tap “The teacher pushes back”: read the counter-argument in your voice and let them answer. Twice. Push for mixed conditionals (if they hadn’t…, they would…).

The words (II): superheroes, upset, damage, enemies and surprises, before the video, in mystery meaning and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, One Minute): the student picks a topic (the road you didn’t take, if you were a different person, Hughie’s life without The Boys) and talks for one minute with mixed conditionals. Then the teacher. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is the Vought tower at night. I: Warm-up and Pick a Side. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a 30-second clock; the student says as many mixed conditionals as they can (“If I hadn’t started English, I would be … now”), then the teacher. Watch: five questions with two answers each (What do people love? What kind of day is it?…), each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment; only clean lines were chosen, but the teacher still decides whether to show the trailer. After: Which word did you hear? Five lines (cozy or crazy, bad or bed, friends or trends…), each with a ▶. V: five frames on the film strip. VI, Then and Now: six sentences and two boxes: Past → now (the result is now) and Now → past (the result was in the past). The student taps a sentence and then its box; when all six are in the right box, the rule board appears and stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Vought Ranking: a backstage approval board with six heroes and their scores. For each clue (“if · A-Train · not run so fast · Robin · be alive now”) the student says the sentence, taps Check, and the right sentence appears; the hero moves up or down with an arrow and the new score, and the PR note explains the fact. At the end the whole board has moved. VIII, Your Other Life: two conversations; the teacher picks A (the road you didn’t take) or B (if you were a different person). IX: one minute.

THE COMMON ERRORS

would have been alive now (now: would be), if he didn’t run…, would be alive now, if he were honest, he would tell the truth last year (would have told), if he would be honest, wouldn’t been, wouldn’t lied, won’t be, hadn’t spoke, Where you would be…?, never had met.

THE HERO JURY (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer, your student judges three choices the trailer shows: Starlight in her dream team, Hughie with Butcher, and Vought’s PR team with the truth. Read the evidence together, let them choose a verdict, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. There is no right answer. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two mixed conditionals themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Pick a Side, IV The Hero Jury, VII The Vought Ranking, VIII Your Other Life and IX One Minute.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Then and Now.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
