The Tricky Question · WOLF HIRED · Lesson 6 of 12

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: handling tricky questions: buying time (That’s a good question. Let me think about that for a second.), checking (Just to make sure I understand,…?), explaining your thinking (I don’t know exactly, but I’d start by…), positive answers about past jobs, I haven’t… but I have…, and what to say when your mind goes blank.

The story: An Indeed career coach on trick questions, five recorded tricky questions (American, British, Australian and Indian accents), then turning panic answers into calm ones.

About 45 min: I · Explain Your Thinking (8) · II · Five Tricky Questions (12) · III · Buy Time, Then Answer (14) · IV · The Hardest Question (7) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “What’s the hardest interview question you’ve ever been asked?”

At the end, fire three tricky questions at the student without warning (Why should we hire you? What would you bring to a desert island? Why is there a gap in your CV?). The goal is the first sentence: always a time-buying phrase, calm voice.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

That’s a good question. · Let me think about that for a second. · Just to make sure I understand,…? · I don’t know exactly, but I’d start by… · I haven’t…, but I have… · Could we come back to that?

EXPLAIN YOUR THINKING

Indeed, top 10 tricky interview questions: the final “trick question” (12:49–14:45).

FIVE TRICKY QUESTIONS

Listen or read, then choose one option. The colors show right and wrong, and one line says why. Ask the student WHY before you move on.

BUY TIME, THEN ANSWER

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write three “buy time” phrases on a sticky note for their next interview.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Can you repeat? (Could you say that again, please?), I don’t have idea (I have no idea / I don’t know exactly), I never did that (I’ve never done that / I haven’t… yet), My boss was horrible (stay positive). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE HARDEST QUESTION (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the buy-time practice, the student ranks five interview questions from the hardest to the easiest for them. There is no right order. When the list is done, the student explains the top one and the last one; then tap Show my teacher’s order and agree on one top 3 together. Wait five seconds in silence before they explain. The PUSH is the real test: you ask their number 1 as the interviewer, and they buy time first (That’s a good question. Let me think…). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
