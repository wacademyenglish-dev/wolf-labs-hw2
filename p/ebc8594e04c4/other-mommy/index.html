Other Mommy · Two Mothers in the Dark

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A girl, a closet, two mothers.

The grammar: She Wears, She Has: He and she: the verb + -s. She doesn’t. Does she?

The class (about 55 min): I The House · II The Words · III What Does She Do? · IV The Trailer · V Line by Line · VI Three Clues · VII She Wears, She Has · VIII Two Mothers · IX Your People · X Questions. V (Line by Line) is the one scene marked EXTRA: skip it if you are short on time.

Open with: “You know the closet now. What does Other Mommy wear? Guess.”

Other Mommy (Universal, 2026) is a horror film by Rob Savage. A little girl, Bela, says there is somebody in her closet, and she calls it “Other Mommy”: it looks like her mother, but not exactly. The grammar: present simple with he / she: the verb + -s (she wears, she sings), has, doesn’t + verb, and Does she…?

A note for the teacher: this is a horror film at A1. The lesson stays calm: no jump scares and no flashes. Watch the trailer once before class; if it is too much for your student, use only the first minute. The trailer was checked by hand (YouTube’s automatic captions, a local transcript and one frame every second): the times in the lesson are real. The small pictures on the film strip are lesson art, not frames from the trailer. No portraits: the two mothers are dark shapes with words.

Warm-up and In the Dark (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; A1: short answers are fine. 2 · In the Dark: the screen is covered and only the sound of the first 14 seconds of the trailer plays (a girl and a woman play with hats; then “Mommy?”). The student says who is talking and how they sound, with the chips; then take off the cover. Light horror: nothing scary is on the screen.

The words (II): hat, bigger, scared, ready and nice, before the video, in match the columns and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

After the trailer (IV) and the three clues (VI): first the student makes a guess about the woman in the yellow dress (the girl’s mommy, Other Mommy, or not sure) and says it in one sentence. Then three short clips from the trailer: the bath (0:00–0:34), at night (0:37–0:51) and the car (1:36–1:41). For each sentence the student chooses I see it or I’m not sure, and can change it. Ask “Where do you see it?” or “Why are you not sure?”, and only then tap SHOW ME to read what the clip shows. Nothing is marked right or wrong on screen: the choice is the student’s, the proof is the clip. On the board, the student keeps or changes the first guess, says three sentences (she has… she wears… she doesn’t…) and asks one Does she…? question. Note: the trailer never says who the woman in the bath or in the car is; “I’m not sure” is a good answer there.

The ending (X, Questions): four questions that use the board from VI: describe the woman in the yellow dress in three sentences, what you don’t know about her, two Does she…? questions for the teacher, and one optional sentence about someone in the student’s family. After each answer, ask “How do you know?”: the student answers with a clue. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a dark bedroom at night with the closet door open. I: the film (the girl, the problem). III: the trailer in three steps. Before: three cards (horror films, the closet, your mother) and the student answers out loud. Watch: Which word did you hear? and True or lie?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: the student’s guess, as before. V: three clues from the trailer and the board (I see it / I’m not sure). VI, She Wears, She Has: Bela’s notebook: the student reads it out loud, taps the verbs after she, then finishes the rule. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, Two Mothers: Mommy and Other Mommy side by side in the dark, with six clues (glasses, a fancy hat, short hair, long black nails, no singing at night, sings at night). For each clue the student says the sentence out loud first, then taps Check: the sentence is written and the flashlight leaves the difference lit. VIII, Your People: a wheel picks a person in the student’s life (mother, father, best friend, grandmother, neighbor, teacher); the student talks about them and taps each form (she wears, she has, she doesn’t, he / she …s, Does she…?) when they use it. IX: the questions.

THE COMMON ERRORS

Mommy wear, she is wear, she have / haves, she don’t, she doesn’t sings, Do she…?, Does she smiles?, she wearing (for every day).

WHAT DOES SHE DO? (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Just before the trailer: Bela hears a voice at night. What does she do? Then two people from your student’s life: a friend watching a horror film, a brother who hears a sound in the kitchen. One of three each, with an almost-ready sentence in she / he + -s and doesn’t. Keep it light: no scares. There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I The House, III What Does She Do?, VIII Two Mothers and IX Your People.
  • Listen: II The Words, IV The Trailer, V Line by Line and VI Three Clues.
  • Focus: VII She Wears, She Has.
  • Write: X Questions.
