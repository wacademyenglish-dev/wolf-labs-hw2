Four Weeks · The Two Commas

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: In 2006, surgical residents learned to stitch with the same materials and the same total hours: one group in a single day, the other spread over four weeks.

The grammar: Some, or All: Same words. Two commas decide if the chart shows two groups or everybody.

Short class (about 42 min): I How You Study · II Two Groups · III Some, or All · IV Four Doors · VIII The Source · XII Your Method, Again. Skip if you’re short on time: V, VI, X, XI (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 68 min.

Open with: “Describe the last thing you learned this way.”

In 2006, surgical residents learned to stitch with the same materials and the same total hours: one group in a single day, the other spread over four weeks. A month later, the four-week group did better on a real surgery. Reporting that needs a defining relative clause: The residents who spread their training over four weeks did better. Add two commas and the sentence says all the residents spread it out, and the study disappears. Students add commas where they breathe, use THAT after a comma, say my mother that is a doctor and start sentences with That I need is …. On screen, the sentence has two comma slots and a chart: no commas, two bars (some of them, compared); two commas, one bar (all of them).

SCENE I

Write the third answer exactly; it often comes with the person that learns faster, he …. Don’t correct yet. The method comes back in scene X.

THE RECORDINGS

Two readings of the same words (scene V), each with pause and 0.75×. Reading 1 has no pauses; reading 2 has two. After playing, a wave shows the silences. The student reads the other four out loud and you tap SOME or ALL from their pauses.

THE VIDEO

Scene VIII: about four minutes (0:20 to 4:33) on the study, the brain and three techniques, sealed until the student guesses one technique. After the video, the last step is ONE screen: the video stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again), with the two-group chart from scene II under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines about what the video says. Every right answer lifts the bars and fills a mark (“Evidence found 3 / 6”). On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, a friend with an exam asks one last question (“What should I do?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud, with a defining relative clause (who, that).

OUTSIDE THE FIELD

Scene X: you are the friend with no science background. After every answer, ask once: “Which ones? All of them?”

The words (VII): techniques, memorize, flashcards, mistakes and sleep, before the video, in hear it in the video and mystery meaning. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IX, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

YOUR CALL (XI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the video and the friend, your student uses what the study showed in three real decisions: a course, a nephew before a test, a study group. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Every reason says exactly which one (the course that …, my friend, who …, what I need is …). Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last word is one minute on big exams, then you take the other side.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: V The Pause You Can Hear, VII The Words, VIII The Source and IX Line by Line.
