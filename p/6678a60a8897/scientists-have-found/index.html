Scientists Have Found… · The Flap Board

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Tonight your student is the night editor at a science newsroom.

The grammar: The Thing First: New news: have / has + participle. The thing first: has been + participle.

Short class (about 43 min): I You and the News · III The Mars Story · V The Sample Rack · VI The Thing First · VII The Flap Board · XI After the Bulletin. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX, X (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 63 min.

Open with: “How do you get your news: TV, a website, social media, a friend?”

Tonight your student is the night editor at a science newsroom. At 9 PM the news goes on the air, and the big story is on Mars: a rover has found a rock with strange “leopard spots” that could be a sign of ancient life. The grammar of news: have / has + past participle for new news (Scientists have found a rock) and the passive, has / have been + past participle, when the thing matters more than who did it (A rock has been found on Mars). Details with a time go to the past simple (It was collected in July 2024). The twist: a rival website writes LIFE FOUND ON MARS; only a negative passive fixes it: Life hasn’t been found. The screen is the newsroom’s split-flap board: headlines flip in letter by letter.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: a short explainer (3:47), sealed until you press play, in four chapters; each stops by itself. During the chapters the questions are open: your student tells you what they saw and heard. After chapter four, the last step is ONE screen: the whole video stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again), with the six sample tubes of the rack (scene V) under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines from what the video says. Every right answer fills a tube. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, Dana asks one last question (“What is the headline tonight?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud, with has / have + participle or has / have been + participle. If a chapter stops mid-sentence, adjust it.

THE PORTUGUESE TRAP

In Portuguese the passive often puts the subject after the verb, and it sounds perfect. Word for word, that becomes Was found a rock on Mars or, with an invented subject, It has been found a rock. In English the thing found is the subject, and it goes first. The second trap: the Portuguese past covers both found and has found; with just, so far, yet English needs the present perfect.

THE SAMPLE RACK AND THE BOARD

Scene III comes before the rule: “Scientists found…” is fine English, let it go; tap has / have and find / found exactly. Scene V is the pressure: eight headlines, 8:52 to 9:00. The last two are the rival site’s mistakes.

A NOTE ON THE FACTS

The Mars facts come from the public announcement: the rock is called Cheyava Falls, the sample was sealed in a tube in 2024, and it is still on Mars. It is a possible sign of life, not proof. The other stories (the mushroom, the comet, the footprints, the ship, the batteries) are invented for the newsroom.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Scientists has found, has find, has being found, has been discover, It has been found a rock, has been just found, has been collected in 2024.

The words (II): evidence, ancient, scientists, samples and alone, before the video, in hear it in the video and turn the cards. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE DEAL (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the bulletin, your student is the reporter who wants tomorrow’s big interview; you are the lab’s press officer. Your secret limit (only you see it): ten minutes are OK if they promise not to say “life”, and no photo before Friday. Ask the three questions, let them argue with have / has been + participle, then close the deal on screen. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence before each reply, then use the PUSH line to make the deal harder. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
