The Gate Had Closed · The Departure Board

The past perfect is the past of the past: had + third form, used when there are two past moments and one was already finished. When I got to the gate, it had closed. Students say When I arrived, the train left (which means they watched it leave) and had went. The first half is travel; the second half is a real study from 2014: people alone in an empty room for fifteen minutes, with a button that gave an electric shock. The screen is a departure board with two lanes: the front lane is the moment you’re telling; the event that was already over slides to the back lane.

THE RECORDINGS

Three: the empty room and the button (IV), boredom and the plastic cup (VII), and the brain at rest (IX). Each plays with pause and 0.75×; the transcript opens after listening. Both studies are small: say so.

DID YOU SEE IT?

Scene III: most students think “left” and “had left” mean the same. Make them act it out: point at the train leaving, or at the empty track. The corrector can’t hear this one: you can.

DON’T OVERUSE IT

Scene V, item 3: with one moment only, the past perfect is wrong. That is the real error after this lesson.

YOUR WORST TRIP

Scene VIII: tap the first sentence you heard; then your student tells the story. Ask “What had happened before that?” at least twice.

THE ARENA (IX) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the plastic cup study, your student defends a side: should everybody spend fifteen minutes a day with nothing to do? One minute for their side, then Swap sides and one minute for the other. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk (“had went” → “had gone”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
