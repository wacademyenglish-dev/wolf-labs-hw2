Actually, Anyway, Mind You · The Mixing Desk

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Real people don’t talk in straight lines.

The grammar: The Rule: Six little words, six jobs.

Short class (about 50 min): I You and Conversation · III A Real Conversation · V The Mixing Desk · VI The Rule · VII On Air · X The Listeners’ Poll · XI Off Air. Skip if you’re short on time: VIII, IX (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 64 min.

Open with: “Do you listen to podcasts or watch talk shows? Which kind, and when?”

Real people don’t talk in straight lines. They correct each other, jump to a side topic, get interrupted, come back, and say what they really think. Six little words do that steering, each with one job: actually (fix a fact, or surprise), by the way (a quick side topic), anyway (back to the topic, or close it), mind you (the other side, a small but), as I was saying (back after an interruption), to be honest (the honest truth). On screen, a mixing desk with six faders, one per marker: the right line pushes the fader up and lights the channel. On air, eight seats in the audience light up, one per good cue.

THE VIDEO

Scene III: a real, unscripted conversation about mistakes (5:46), sealed until you press play, in four chapters that stop by themselves. The questions are about listening: who talks, what about, and which little words they use to start, turn and finish. Accept any marker they really heard (so, well, actually, anyway, I mean). After the four chapters, the last step of scene III is ONE screen: the whole video stays on the left (tap a time to hear that moment again), with the same six-fader mixing desk as scene V under it; on the right, six True or Lie lines about what Neil and Beth say. Every right answer pushes a fader up. On a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, Jo asks one last question (“Do you make many mistakes?”): the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud, with a marker (to be honest, mind you, actually, anyway, by the way).

THE TRAP

actually looks almost the same as a Portuguese word that means currently, these days, so students say Actually, I live in Boston to mean now. In English it means in fact. One Portuguese word covers both by the way and anyway, so students mix them. They also say in the way, to be sincere, as I said you, and mind without you. The red line catches the forms; the actually = now meaning is yours to catch.

ON AIR AND THE COUCH

Scene VII: be Rosa, big and warm. Read her line, point at the producer’s card, and wait. Watch anyway used to correct (cues 1 and 7) and by the way used to go back or close (cues 3 and 8). Scene VIII: on A MISTAKE, really interrupt them so they need As I was saying. Scene IX: you are Sam, the guest.

MIND YOU

mind you is more British; American English also says then again or that said.

The words (II): tired, mistakes, suitcase, lucky and resigned, before the video, in memory and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE LISTENERS’ POLL (X) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the last page, a ranking for the next episode: four annoying things people do in a conversation. The student puts them in order and explains the worst and the least annoying with a marker (to be honest, mind you, actually). There is no right order. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Then tap “Show my teacher’s order”, give your reasons, and agree on one top 3 together. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. It prepares XI (is it rude to change the subject?). About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
