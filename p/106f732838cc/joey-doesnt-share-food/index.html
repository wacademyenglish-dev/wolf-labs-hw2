Joey Doesn’t Share Food · Friends · The Laugh Meter

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Six friends, one couch, ten years of bad food decisions.

The grammar: Should: Should and shouldn’t: advice. Then the verb, nothing else.

The class (about 57 min): I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie · II The Words · III The Clip · IV Line by Line · V The Food Court · VI Should · VII The Laugh Meter · VIII Ask the Couch · IX One Minute. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Joey now. In the clip, what should his friends never do? Guess.”

Friends (NBC, 1994–2004) is the American sitcom about six friends in New York: Rachel, Monica, Phoebe, Joey, Chandler and Ross, and the orange couch of their coffee house. Monica is a chef; Joey loves food more than anything, and he never shares it. The clip is a reel of their worst food decisions: a dessert with beef in it, a turkey stuck on a head, a neighbor’s cheesecake, Joey and the chicken, Ross’s sandwich. The grammar: should / shouldn’t + the verb for advice, and the questions Should I…? Should we…? What should I do?

A note for the teacher: the clip is a 7:20 reel; in III the lesson stops it at 3:25, and the frames in IV reach the rest. The five times were checked against its captions on 23/09, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Two Truths and a Lie: Three rounds about Friends (Joey, Monica, the coffee house). The student reads the three sentences, says which one is the lie and why, then taps: the card turns and explains. In the last round the student tells two truths and one lie about their own life, and you guess. A2: short answers are fine (“Number two. He loves food!”).

The words (II): dessert, layers, borrow, delicious and terrible, before the video, in match the columns and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, One Minute): the student picks a topic (advice for a first date, for a tourist in their city, for Joey) and talks for one minute with should and shouldn’t. Then the teacher does one. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a coffee house at night with a big orange couch. I: Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie. III: the clip in three steps. Before: a slider (if a friend eats your fries, should you say something?), and the student says why. Watch: True or lie? and Which word did you hear?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: match each friend to the problem, then two taps (what should Chandler and Joey do?) and the student says the whole sentence. IV: Line by Line. V: The Food Court, the student is the jury for three food crimes. VI, the note on the fridge: Monica writes to the gang; the student reads it out loud, taps the words after should, shouldn’t and Should, then finishes the rule. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Laugh Meter: an orange couch with six cushions, one for each friend with a problem. The student reads the problem and the clue, says the advice out loud, then taps Check: the sentence is written, the cushion lights up and the audience’s laugh meter (a needle on a half circle) goes up, from quiet to roaring. VIII, Ask the Couch: three friends with a problem (a noisy neighbor, English, a tourist). The student picks how to answer (direct, gentle, funny…) and the talk appears: read it out loud, you are the friend. IX: one minute.

THE COMMON ERRORS

should to take, shoulds, should takes, should gave, should relaxing, don’t should / doesn’t should, Do I should…?, I should share my food? (Should I…?).

THE FOOD COURT (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student is the jury for three food crimes: Rachel’s dessert with beef, Ross’s sandwich at work, and Joey’s fries on a date. They read the evidence, choose a verdict (or say their own) and say why with should / shouldn’t. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own life. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (should to, shoulds); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie, V The Food Court, VII The Laugh Meter, VIII Ask the Couch and IX One Minute.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Clip and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Should.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
