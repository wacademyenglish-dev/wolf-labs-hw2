Half as Much · The Phone Week

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Bea, a designer, used her phone six hours a day: longer than her sleep. For one week she tries half as much. Day one and day seven are audio diaries.

The grammar: calmer than / more relaxed than · a bit / much / far + comparative · (not) as … as · twice / half as … as · “the less I check it, the less I want it” as a ready chunk.

The lesson (about 50 min, ~45 without the extra): I Your Gears · II Bea’s Week · III Bigger, Better, Half As Much · IV Five Voices · V Before and After · VI The Debate · VII Fix Bea’s Notes (extra) · VIII Your Challenge.

Open with: “How many hours a day do you think you spend on your phone?”

A CONVERSATION LESSON

The student talks most of the time. Nothing is multiple choice. Ask the follow-up, take the other side, and listen for more better, more calm and as … than. This is the B1 sister of Nine Times Longer (B2, same gears, harder grammar).

WHAT EACH SCENE DOES

  • I · Your Gears (talk, 6 min). Gear A = what they do on the phone, gear B = what changes (sleep, mood…). Up or down? Three questions follow.
  • II · Bea’s Week (listen and predict, 7 min). Play day one. The student taps every prediction they believe and says why. Play day seven: the last step is ONE screen. The audio and its transcript stay on the left; on the right, six True or Lie lines about Bea’s week (sleep, books, evenings, calm, the phone, her rule). Every right answer confirms a card; on a Lie the student says the true line. When all six are done, Bea asks what she should use less: the student taps an answer and says the sentence out loud (half as much, much calmer, the less … the less …).
  • III · Bigger, Better, Half As Much (rule, 8 min). The grammar stays on screen. In the comparer, pick a word (calm, tired, bored…) and how big the change is (a bit, much, far): the same fact appears in two sentences, “calmer than” and “not as … as”. Ask: “Is it true for you this week?”
  • IV · Five Voices (listen and retell, 7 min). The student retells with one comparison; you tap “Good comparison” or “Not yet” (only you see it). Then a question about them.
  • V · Before and After (speaking, 7 min). Bea’s chart: phone, sleep, books, steps, coffee. The student describes each change (half as much, twice as much, as much as), then gives their own number.
  • VI · The Debate (8 min). Read each idea and take the other side (your card says how). The last one: they say an idea and you answer.
  • VII · Fix Bea’s Notes (extra, 5 min). Six lines; five wrong, “The less I check it, the less I want it” is right.
  • VIII · Your Challenge (6 min). They choose one thing to do half as much next week and write what will change. Ask about it in the next class.

THE COMMON ERRORS

more better · more calm / more happy · not as good than · two times more · the double · calmer that.

THE SURPRISE QUESTION

This lesson already has its conversation moments (I Your Gears, VI The Debate, VIII Your Challenge). The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) adds one personal question at any moment: wait five seconds in silence, let them choose, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly (much better, twice as much, not as … as); at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Your Gears · V Before and After · VI The Debate.
  • Listen: II Bea’s Week · IV Five Voices.
  • Focus: III Bigger, Better, Half As Much · VII Fix Bea’s Notes.
  • Write: VIII Your Challenge (and the homework).
