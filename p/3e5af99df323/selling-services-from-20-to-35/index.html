From 20% to 35% · Selling Services · Lesson 22

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the review of Unit 22 (numbers on a slide: increase / decrease · from … to · by). No new grammar. The student pulls everything from memory.

The story: Chapter 4 of the course story “Leo’s First Big Client”: Leo answers the cheaper competitor with one slide and three numbers from Brava Foods (fictional), and Rita, the CFO of Orla Beverages (fictional), says “Send me the contract”… then Sara texts: “Call me NOW. It’s about Orla.” The student types six results he hears, finds the Brazilian traps, presents the slide to you (you are the CFO and ask about every number), debates “3 or 10 numbers?” and writes his own slide in three sentences.

About 57 min: I · Your Numbers (5) · II · Easy Reader: The Numbers (8) · III · Listen to the Numbers (8) · IV · By, From, To (9) · V · Your 3-Slide Pitch (10) · VI · Mini-Debate: 3 or 10 Numbers? (6) · VII · Your Slide (8) · VIII · I Can (3).

Open with: “What number is important in your job? Did your sales go up or go down this year?”

Presenting results: increase / decrease / go up / go down / grow + from … to … (start and end) + by … (the difference); by half. Pronunciation: fifteen × fifty, hundred with no -s. Brazilian traps: “increased in 15%”, “from 20% for 35%”, the decimal comma (1,5 million: in English 1.5 = one point five; the comma is for thousands), and “grew up 20%”. In III ask him to type numbers as digits. In V you are the CFO: ask a follow-up after every number (“In how many months?”, “And the cost?”).

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

… increased / went up from … to … · … decreased / went down by …% · 1.5 = one point five · 1,500 = one thousand five hundred · Would you like the same results?

YOUR NUMBERS

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

EASY READER: THE NUMBERS

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

LISTEN TO THE NUMBERS

Listen and Type: the audio plays up to 3 times, like the test. The student types, then Check compares word by word: green is right, red is missing or misspelled. Read the tip out loud.

BY, FROM, TO

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

YOUR 3-SLIDE PITCH

Project P-04: three slides, a title and one number each. The slides build on screen as the student types. Then a 1-minute presentation; you ask one question.

MINI-DEBATE: 3 OR 10 NUMBERS?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

YOUR SLIDE

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask him to bring next class one real slide from his company and present it in one minute with from… to… and by….

THE TECH WORDS · SÓ PARA O PROFESSOR

O aluno vende serviços B2B. Você não precisa ser especialista; precisa saber do que ele está falando. Não ensine estes termos: deixe que ele explique o trabalho dele (é conversa real).

  • BPO (Business Process Outsourcing): Terceirização de processos: a empresa contratada EXECUTA uma rotina do cliente (folha de pagamento, contabilidade, fiscal, contas a pagar). O cliente para de fazer aquilo dentro de casa.
  • BTO (Business Transformation Outsourcing): Terceirização com transformação: além de executar, a contratada MUDA o processo (tecnologia, automação, integração de sistemas). Vende-se como “melhorar”, não só “fazer por você”.
  • ERP: Sistema de gestão que integra finanças, compras, estoque, RH etc. Exemplos (marcas): SAP, Oracle, TOTVS. Um BPO/BTO não vende o ERP: trabalha COM ele ou o integra.
  • Closing time · close the books: Tempo de fechamento contábil do mês (em dias). Um BPO contábil promete reduzir esse número.
  • Revenue: Receita / faturamento.
  • Decimal point: Em inglês o decimal é com ponto (1.5 = “one point five”) e a vírgula separa milhares (1,500). O contrário do Brasil.
  • Percentage point: Ponto percentual: de 20% para 35% são 15 “percentage points” (não 15%). Bom saber se o cliente for de finanças.
  • Late payments: Pagamentos em atraso (a fornecedores). Indicador clássico de contas a pagar.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

went up in 10% (by 10%), from 20% for 35% (from 20% to 35%), 1,5 million (1.5 million), Sales grew up 20% (grew by 20%). A1+: one correction per answer is enough.
