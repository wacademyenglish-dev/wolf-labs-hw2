I Ended Up · The Saturday Clock

The thing you said you were going to do, and what you did instead. Three verbs tell one day: I was going to (the plan), I kept + -ing (the loop), I ended up + -ing (what really happened). Students usually own WAS GOING TO and have never produced the other two. Every task gets a clock: a dashed hand for the plan, an amber arc that runs around for the loop, and a coral hand where the day ended.

THE TEST IS A CONVERSATION STARTER

Scene I: ten sentences, often / sometimes / never. Ask for one real example on a few of them. The score is not a diagnosis; say so. What matters is the ONE sentence your student marks as most true. It comes back in Scene VIII.

THE CLOCK

Scene III is the heart. Your student says the KEPT line, you tap what you heard; the arc runs. Then the ENDED UP line; the coral hand lands. Then all three lines as one story. Ask for a sixth task from their own Saturday.

THE LAST WORD IS WILL

Scene VIII: six questions, one push-back each (only you see it). Question 3 often changes the room. The last answer is the only WILL of the hour: one small thing they will do today.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I kept to check, I ended up to do, I ended up slept, I kept look, I was going to starting.

THE HOT SEAT (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Your student becomes Sam, the writer who started a book ten years ago and still hasn’t finished it. You are the radio host. Ask the four questions in character, wait five seconds in silence after each one, and let them build the story with the plan (was going to), the loop (kept -ing) and what really happened (ended up -ing). Use the PUSH line (only you see it) at the end. Recast quietly while they talk (kept checking, not “kept to check”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
