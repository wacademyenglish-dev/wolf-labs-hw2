On the Record · Superman · The Front Page

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A hero who stopped a war, and a reporter who asked why.

The grammar: He Said, She Asked: He said he had stopped a war. She asked if he had asked the President.

The class (about 56 min): I Warm-up and The Daily Planet · III The Trailer · IV Line by Line · V The City Council · VI He Said, She Asked · VII The Front Page · VIII What Did They Say? · IX The Hot Seat. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the story now. In the trailer, what did Lois ask Superman? Guess.”

Superman (James Gunn, DC Studios, 2025) stars David Corenswet as Superman, Rachel Brosnahan as Lois Lane and Nicholas Hoult as Lex Luthor. Superman stopped a war in another country without asking anybody; Lois, a reporter at the Daily Planet, interviews him, the government has questions, and Lex says he isn’t a man at all. The grammar: reported speech: said (no person) / told + person, one step back in time (is → was, will → would, can → could, stopped → had stopped), the pronouns that change, reported questions with asked if / asked where + normal order, and reported orders with told + person + (not) to.

A note for the teacher: the trailer has action and a few explosions, nothing graphic on the lesson screens. The times follow its captions (24/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and The Daily Planet (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · The Daily Planet: the front page of the newspaper where Clark and Lois work, with three stories. The student picks one, says what they think happened, reads the start of the story and reacts. The stories are already in reported speech (he said that…, she asked him if…).

The words (II): serious, interview, illegally, consult and consequences, before the video, in mystery meaning and hot seat. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Hot Seat): the student is Superman after the press conference; the teacher asks what people said and asked (the reporters, Lex, Lois, the Secretary, his parents). Then swap. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an art deco skyline with a golden globe on the newspaper building. I: Warm-up and The Daily Planet. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: Lois asks Superman “Did you consult with the President?” and the student picks how he answers (three reactions, each one with a short conversation); the real answer comes in the trailer. Watch: True or lie? Five sentences in reported speech, each with a ▶ time before 0:55 (Lois asks if she can interview him; the Secretary said he would look into his actions; Superman said he had stopped a war…); if it is a lie, the student says the truth. After: who said it? Five lines from the trailer; the student taps the line, then the name (Lois, Superman, Lex). IV: five frames on the film strip. V, The City Council: three proposals from the trailer; the student votes Yes, Propose a change or No, then reports the vote. VI, He Said, She Asked: a page from the Daily Planet on a dark card. The student taps what people said, asked and told (six places), then finishes the rule board: one step back, asked if, asked where, told + not to. The board stays in the corner on the next page. VII, The Front Page: a dark newspaper page with a big headline and six columns. Each quote waits in its column: the student reads it, reports it out loud, taps Check, and the right sentence prints in the column; the big headline changes to the newest story. At the end, a red PRINT IT stamp. VIII, What Did They Say?: six words (said, told me, asked me if, asked me where, told me not to, would); the student tells the story of the day and taps each word when they use it. IX: the hot seat.

THE COMMON ERRORS

said me, told he would, told to Lois, said he stops / will / can / has (no step back), asked did he consult, asked where did he live, asked if had he, told Lois don’t print, not to printing.

THE CITY COUNCIL (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student sits on the Metropolis city council and votes three proposals that come from the trailer: Superman must ask the government before he helps another country; the Daily Planet should print Lex’s story on the front page; Superman should answer the city’s questions on live TV once a month. For each one: Yes, Propose a change, or No, and say why with what people said and asked (Lois asked him if…, Lex said that…). There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the vote harder. The last step, Report the vote, is one minute of reported speech about the council itself (I said that…, you asked me if…). Recast quietly while they talk (said me, asked did he, told him don’t); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and The Daily Planet, V The City Council, VII The Front Page, VIII What Did They Say? and IX The Hot Seat.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI He Said, She Asked.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
