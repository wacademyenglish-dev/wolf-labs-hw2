Turn It On · The Dark Apartment

In the first minutes your student will say turn on it or pick up her. Nobody has ever corrected it, because a listener understands anyway. The hour bets on two ideas: the small word is the verb, and it, them, him, her, me go in the middle. The screen is an apartment at night, seen from above: every pronoun in the right place lights a room.

THE SMALL WORD, NOT THE LIST

Don’t turn scene IV into a vocabulary list. Ask why each verb goes with its small word: What does OFF usually mean?

THE TRAP IS ONE INCH WIDE

Scene V proves it in three steps. Don’t explain before step 3.

NEVER OPENS

look for, look after, deal with, run into never split; the starters in VIII use them. No rule says which; these few are learned by heart.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

turn on it, pick up her, send back it, call back me, and after an hour of IT in the middle, I’m looking it for.

YOUR CALL, IN THE DARK (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Three small dilemmas at home: the lights go out, the neighbor’s music at midnight, a friend’s cat for two weeks. There is no right answer: let them choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. The starters carry the verbs that never open (look for it, look after it, deal with it) and the ones that do (turn it down, turn it off). Recast quietly while they talk (turn it down, never turn down it); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
