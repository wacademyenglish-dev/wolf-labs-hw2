The Evidence Suggests · The Red Pen

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A sleep scientist on stage says a sound can make your sleep deeper.

The grammar: The Rule: A reviewer never says “it proves”. They keep a distance.

Short class (about 56 min): I You and Your Sleep · III The Talk · V The Manuscript · VI The Rule · VII The Referee Report · VIII The Reviewer’s Call · IX The Editor Calls · X The Decision. Skip if you’re short on time: IV Line by Line (marked EXTRA on your screen). Full lesson: about 56 min.

Open with: “How did you sleep last night? What time did you go to bed?”

A sleep scientist on stage says a sound can make your sleep deeper. The student is the reviewer, as in a journal’s peer review: how carefully does the speaker treat the evidence? At C1, the student learns to keep a distance from a claim, the way a journal does: The evidence suggests that poor sleep is linked to disease. It has been claimed that the device works at home. The study purports to show… Three distances: from the evidence, from other people, and with your own caution (there is reason to believe, would seem to, apparently, reportedly, supposedly).

THE COMMON ERRORS

The empty subject disappears: Is widely assumed that, Has been claimed that, Has reason to believe. This is the error to hunt all hour. Also: the evidences, the study pretends to show (pretend = act as if something false is true), purports that, suggests to sleep, would seems.

ON SCREEN

The red pen: a manuscript with tracked changes. The strong claim is struck in red, the careful version is inserted in green, and a “Reviewer 2” note sits in the margin. In scene V the student places six lines on a ruler of four strengths (STRONG, LIKELY, POSSIBLE, A JUMP): tap a line, then its box. Then the green lines appear and the student reads them like a reviewer. In VI the student matches each frame to what it tells the reader. In VII the intern rewrote six lines with distance and each one has a mistake: the student finds it, says the fix and taps Check; the stamp reads ACCEPT WITH MAJOR REVISIONS. In IX the student taps how to answer the editor (two ways per question), reads the model, and says their own answer.

THE TALK

Scene III: the talk (about six and a half minutes), sealed until the first play, in five chapters. Before the play: a ruler (do you believe it?) and one question to tap. In each chapter: three true-or-lie lines, each with a ▶ at its minute; if it is a lie, the student says the truth. After: the ruler again and two reviewer questions to tap. At 2:14 he is careful (what many scientists believe); at 5:27 he is not (actually primes your mind). He builds the technology himself: a good question for a reviewer.

A NOTE

B2 lessons in the catalog teach everyday hedging (tend to, seem to, arguably) and is said to. None of those is a target here: this hour is academic distance, one level up.

The words (II): efficient, productivity, crisis, deprived and recover, before the video, in mystery meaning and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV, extra): every line of the video; tap one to hear it again, and the lines with yellow words open the explanation.

THE REVIEWER’S CALL (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VIII is now a dilemma in three cases: a study with only thirteen people, a scientist who sells his own device, and a newspaper headline that says “proves”. The student chooses as the reviewer and says why with a distancing frame. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves (hunt the missing IT and THERE).

The Surprise question button (only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I You and Your Sleep, VII The Referee Report, VIII The Reviewer’s Call and IX The Editor Calls.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Talk and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: V The Manuscript and VI The Rule.
  • Write: X The Decision.
