If It Rains, Take This · A2 Unit 9 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new A2 lesson on if + present simple with an imperative (If it rains, close the door. If he barks, don’t worry.), the comma when if comes first, and no will after if.

The story: Nina (invented) is away for a week. The student looks after her flat, her plants and her dog, Biscuit. She left a note with if instructions and a cake in the fridge.

About 40 min: I · Nina’s Instructions (9) · II · Problem → Instruction (9) · III · Day 3: The Call (7) · IV · Your Instructions (9) · V · Would You Rather? (6).

Open with: “Do you have a pet or plants? Who looks after them when you travel?”

Real instructions for house-sitting: very useful for travel and for students who have pets. The big trap is “If it will rain”. Keep the student’s own home in the writing card: it is more fun and personal.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

if + present simple · then an instruction (imperative) · don’t worry if… · the comma when if comes first · no will after if · giving clear instructions

NINA’S INSTRUCTIONS

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

PROBLEM → INSTRUCTION

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

YOUR INSTRUCTIONS

The writing card: short lines with the model on the side. One correction per line is enough.

WOULD YOU RATHER?

Would you rather: the student taps A or B and says why, with because. Then you answer too, and agree or disagree.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

If it will rain (If it rains), If the dog bark (barks), If you are hungry eat (comma: If you’re hungry, eat), No worry (Don’t worry), If he will call (If he calls). A2: one correction per answer is enough.

DAY 3: THE CALL (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

A short hot seat in the middle of the lesson, with the roles turned around: your student is Nina, on holiday, and you are the house-sitter calling on day 3. Ask the four questions (Biscuit on your bed, water on the floor, a “cousin” at the door, sad plants); the student answers on the spot with if + present and an instruction. Wait five seconds in silence after each question, then use the PUSH line (only you see it). Your secret: a birthday on Saturday; the student decides about the party on screen. No right answer. Recast quietly while they talk (If it will… → If it…); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 7 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · Nina’s Instructions.
  • Focus: II · Problem → Instruction.
  • Write: IV · Your Instructions.
  • Out loud: III · Day 3: The Call and V · Would You Rather?.
