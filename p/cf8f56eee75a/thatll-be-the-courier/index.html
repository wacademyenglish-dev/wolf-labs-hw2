That’ll Be the Courier · C1 Unit 2 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on will / would for deduction: that’ll be the courier, she’ll be at work now, he’ll have left by now, he won’t have read it yet, that would be about 1965, he’d be about twenty.

The story: Max and Nina (invented) work from home: the bell rings, the phone rings, someone doesn’t reply. Then the student calls three assistants to find three busy people.

About 48 min: I · A Normal Afternoon (9) · II · Future or Guess? (9) · III · Hot Takes (8) · IV · Three Phone Calls (15) · V · Your Guesses (7).

Open with: “When the doorbell rings and you’re not expecting anyone, what do you think first?”

Students use must for every guess. Natives often use will: relaxed and confident, based on what they know. The afternoon at home shows it in tiny real moments (bell, phone, old photo); the phone calls make the student guess with will have and would over and over.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

that’ll be… (a guess about now) · she’ll be at work now · will have + participle (a guess about the past) · won’t have … yet · that would be about 1965 · he’d be about twenty

A NORMAL AFTERNOON

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

FUTURE OR GUESS?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

THREE PHONE CALLS

You are each assistant. Your card (only you see it) has the answers. When the student asks a question, tap it: the card fills in. If the student freezes, tap Say it easier. Then you are the boss: the student reports with she’ll be / he’ll have left / she’d be. Listen for the three slips on your screen and tap the one you heard.

YOUR GUESSES

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

he will left, she must arrived, he would have twenty (age: would be), it will be the courier tomorrow (for now: that’ll be), she won’t read it yet (won’t have read), he will has left. C1: one correction per answer is enough.

HOT TAKES (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

In the middle of the lesson, your student rates four unpopular opinions about bells, phones and messages from 1 to 5 and defends the number with confident guesses (they’ll have seen it, that’ll be a scam). There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, then take the opposite number and use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Listen: I · A Normal Afternoon.
  • Focus: II · Future or Guess?.
  • Out loud: III · Hot Takes, IV · Three Phone Calls and V · Your Guesses.
