The Lizard and the Needle · The Four Levers

Non-fiction, and the language of a mechanism. The weight-loss pen everybody talks about copies a hormone your gut makes, GLP-1. Your own GLP-1 is cut by an enzyme in about two minutes; the medicine started with a molecule found in the venom of a desert lizard, described in 1992. Four verbs say what one thing does to another, and each one changes what comes after the person: make / let + person + verb (no to); help + person + (to) verb; stop + person + from + -ing. On screen, a panel of four levers: the right one lights up; a wrong sentence makes it spring back. The mold strip under it shows the slot after the person.

THIS IS CONTENT, TOO

The student learns how the medicine works by saying what does what. The facts are simplified but true. It is not medical advice, and the panel in scene VIII has no right answer. Nobody’s body is the topic: keep it on the mechanism and on habits.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

It makes me to feel full · They let me to eat · It stops me to eat · It stops me from eat · It helps me eating less · letted. The question that fixes almost everything: Which lever is it, and what comes after the person?

THE RECORDINGS

A researcher (II), a person who uses the pen (V) and a doctor (VII). Each plays with pause and 0.75×; the questions and the transcript open after listening. In VII the doctor is careful with blame (“it does not mean those people failed”): ask why that matters.

THE CHAIN

Scene IV: six links from the plate to the desert. Links 1 and 2 accept two levers (make or help). Link 6 is in the past: let, not letted.

THE PANEL

Scene VIII: the student picks a side; you read each person’s line in character and push back. Tap every lever you hear in the answer; two different levers close the round. Three rounds close the panel. Tap a classic mistake when you hear it, and the student says it again.

THE FOOD COUNCIL (VI) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Scene VI is now a council vote, in the middle of the lesson. Your student sits on the city’s food council and votes three proposals (schools, offices, supermarkets): Yes, Propose a change, or No, and says why with a lever (make, let, help, stop). The Panel (VIII) is about the pen; this one is about everyday food rules, so it is not medical. There is no right answer. Wait five seconds in silence, vote the other way, and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast mistakes quietly (“stops me to eat” → “stops me from eating”); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. The last step: they write one rule of their own in one minute.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
