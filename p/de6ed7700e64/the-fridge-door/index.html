The Fridge Door · Seven Days of Notes

An easy reader in five chapters, and the whole story happens on one door. Marco is on the night shift all week: he gets home at 8:00 AM and Bea leaves for work at 7:30 AM, so for seven days everything they say to each other is written on a note under a magnet. Notes go up and come down on the screen, day by day, except one, from Monday, under the red magnet. In chapter four you read it: ARE YOU STILL HAPPY HERE? In chapter five it is off the door, turned over: I AM STILL HERE. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

Already in a yes, for something that happened sooner than expected (say that second half once, early: it answers half the questions of the hour). Not … yet in a no and in a question, with yet at the end. Still for no change, in a yes and in a no. The place: already and still in the middle (in front of a normal verb, after am / is / are), and still in front of the not: he is still not home. Present simple and be only; no present perfect.

THE TRAP

In many languages one word does the work of both already and yet, so learners say he is not already home and is he already home? when they are waiting. Scene IX is only this. Round 2 is the one almost everyone gets wrong: let it happen, then read the explanation out loud.

THREE NOTES (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Friday, after Chapter Three: three notes are still on the door (the bread, the window and the one under the red magnet). Your student lives there: which one first? They tap 1, 2, 3 and say why with the starters (It is still… / It isn’t… yet). There is no right order. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). At the end, open Show my teacher’s order and agree on one order together. Recast mistakes quietly; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Read: the five chapters (no audio: read together, line by line).
  • Out loud: I Before the Door (write down what you hear, say nothing), VII Three Notes (your student ranks the three notes left on Friday), XI Your Own Door (six real sentences; a sentence about nobody and nothing doesn’t count).
  • Focus: III and V (the door moves with each right answer), IX Yes, or No?
  • Write: XII, their own fridge door, with the red wavy line for not already, already? when waiting, yet in a yes, not still, and the word in the wrong place. It goes to their My notes.

CHAPTER FOUR

Somebody will ask what the Monday note says before chapter four. Don’t tell. Say it is five words and move on: the wait is half of what makes it work.
