I Was Not Alone · The Floor Plan

Your student looks after Mrs. Reed’s house and her dog Rex for one night. In the morning she comes back: four cups, one still warm, a coat on the hook, an open window, and a dog that didn’t bark. She lives alone and nobody has a key. The house is a blue plan in the dark: every room lights up only when the sentence with was or were is right. No timer, no points.

THE GRAMMAR

was for I, he, she, it and one thing; were for you, we, they and more than one. There was / there were follow the thing after them. wasn’t / weren’t. The question puts the verb first: Were you alone? A feeling is one word: I was scared (not I was with fear). In scene II the student says seven sentences in the present; in scene III the night is over and the same seven go into the past.

THE STRANGEST THING (VIII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the night card and the morning card, your student ranks three clues: the four cups, the black coat, the quiet dog. Which one was the strangest? Answer first yourself, as a model; then let them tap their order, wait five seconds in silence, and ask the PUSH line (only you see it): a yes / no question. “Show my teacher’s order” shows yours. Recast quietly while they talk (they was → they were); at the end, ask them to fix one sentence themselves. About 5 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. There are three; use one when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Five Snapshots, V Four Cups, VI Night Card, Morning Card, VIII The Strangest Thing (ranking), IX Three in the Morning.
  • Listen: II Nine O’Clock (the opening line), VII Short and Long (was not / wasn’t), X Mrs. Reed Asks (her questions are recorded; the transcript opens after the audio).
  • Focus: III She Is Back (the plan lights up), IV The Rule.
  • Write: XI Your Night, with the red wavy line for they was, there was four, You were alone?, with fear and not was. It goes to their My notes.

THE INFORMATION GAP (SCENE VI)

The student has the night card. Your morning card appears only on your side, after the student asks. Wait for a real question with Was…? or Were…?, tap “Asked”, read the morning line, then tap “Answered · show it”. Rooms that changed light up on the plan. Don’t show your card.

THE ENDING

The hour ends without an answer. Mrs. Reed says: “I live alone. Nobody has a key.” The last sentence is four words: I wasn’t alone. Stop there.

IN THE LIVE ROOM

The plan, the cups and the cards move on both screens. Every audio plays and pauses; 0.75× makes it slower. G opens this guide.
