Any Allergies? · English for Healthcare · Lesson 4 of 16

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: the medical history and admission questions: allergies and reactions, current medication, operations, smoking and alcohol, family history, pregnancy when relevant. Grammar: Do you…? (habits), Are you taking…? (now), Have you ever had…? (life).

The story: A Fluent Nurse shadowing video (admission phrases to repeat). Then the student builds full questions from notes and fills in a fictional patient’s admission form.

About 41 min: I · Shadow a Nurse (8) · II · Build the Questions (10) · III · The Hot Seat (7) · IV · The Admission Form (12) · V · I Can (4).

Open with: “Which question do you ask EVERY patient, no matter what?” (Usually: allergies.)

The video is “shadowing”: pause after each phrase if needed and ask the student to repeat with the same intonation. The admission form uses a fictional patient (Joan Harper) and a fictional hospital.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

Can you tell me your full name and date of birth? · Do you have any allergies? What happens…? · Are you taking any medication at the moment? · Have you ever had an operation? · Do you smoke? How much alcohol…? · Does anyone in your family…?

SHADOW A NURSE

Fluent Nurse Medical English, “Speak Like a Nurse | Patient Admission Shadowing Practice”, only 0:00–1:52 (captions checked on 15/10/2026).

BUILD THE QUESTIONS

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THE ADMISSION FORM

A practice form in English, filled in with a FICTIONAL person’s data (never the student’s real documents in class). The student reads the profile card and fills in the form; Check the form shows each field right or wrong, with the correct answer.

I CAN

The student reads each “I can” line out loud and taps the ones they can do. You confirm and stamp the unit. Lines left open go to the homework, and to your next class. Ask the student to write the admission questions of their own service, in order.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

Do you take medicine now? (Are you taking any medication at the moment?), Did you ever have surgery? (Have you ever had…?), Are you allergic of…? (allergic to). B1+: one correction per answer is enough.

THE HOT SEAT (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After building the questions, the roles flip: the student is the experienced head nurse, and you are Theo, a shy trainee on his first day. Ask his four questions in order (why vitamins, a patient who says “No allergies… I think”, the alcohol question, the pregnancy question). Your secret (only you see it): Theo hates the personal questions, so insist until the student gives you the exact words. Wait five seconds in silence after each question, then use the PUSH line (a famous actor in a hurry). Recast quietly while they talk (Are you taking …? Have you ever had …? Is there any chance …?); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
