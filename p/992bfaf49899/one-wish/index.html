If I Had One Wish · Obsession · The Wish Contract

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: A very old story: every wish has a price.

The grammar: If I Had, I Would: If I had one wish, I would… If I were you, I wouldn’t.

The class (about 51 min): I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie · III The Trailer · IV Scene by Scene · V If I Had, I Would · VI The Wish Contract · VII Bargain for a Wish · VIII What Would You Do? · IX One Minute. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the old stories now. In the trailer, what would you do if you were Bear?”

Obsession is a 2026 horror film that starts like a love story. In a strange little shop, Bear buys one wish for one coin, breaks a willow branch, and wishes that his friend Nikki loved him more than anyone in the world. The next day she does, too much, and she can’t stop. When he calls the shop to cancel the wish, the answer is no: it lasts as long as he lives. The grammar: the second conditional for imagined situations: If + past, would + base verb (If I had one wish, I would…), the question What would you do if…?, advice with If I were you, and If I could….

A note for the teacher: ⚠ the trailer has jump scares and screaming; warn the student before you play it. Nothing scary is on the lesson screens. The trailer has no captions on YouTube now, so the five frames are estimated, and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; don’t correct the grammar yet. 2 · Two Truths and a Lie: three rounds (the genie’s lamp, the monkey’s paw, the wish shop in the film). The student reads the three sentences, says which one is the lie and why, then taps. In the last round, they say two truths and one lie about their own life, and you guess.

The words (II): drink, weird, dating, spells and cancel, before the video, in memory and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, One Minute): the student picks a topic (if I had one wish, if I could live in any time in history, advice for Bear) and talks for one minute with If…, I would… Then the teacher does one. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is a dusty curiosity shop at night with an old coin and a willow branch. I: Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: three cards (the shop, the deal, you are Bear), then a button reveals the twist (every wish has a price); the student says what they would wish. Watch: Which word did you hear? Five lines, each with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: Choose Your Response. Nikki says “I love you so, so, so, so much.” The student picks how Bear answers (tell the truth, say nothing, call the shop), and the screen shows the conversation. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, If I Had, I Would: a message from Bear on a dark phone screen. The student taps the verbs of the imagined situation (had, would take, knew, would be, were, would run), then finishes the rule board (past after if, would + verb, If I were you, What would you do). The board stays in the corner on the next pages. VI, The Wish Contract: a dark parchment with six clauses (the wish, the girl, the advice, the cancellation, the past, the signature). For each clue the student reads it, says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the clause is signed and the small print appears in red ink, the price. With six, a wax seal. VIII, What Would You Do?: a wheel of six impossible situations (a million dollars, invisible for a day, any language…) and a counter: the teacher taps Got it! for each If…, I would… sentence. IX: one minute.

THE COMMON ERRORS

If I would have, If she would love me, If I had…, I will…, If she loves me, I would…, If I was you (for advice), wouldn’t to make, would you to do it, would changed, wouldn’t loves, What you would do…?, If I can change…, I would….

BARGAIN FOR A WISH (VII) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

After the Wish Contract, your student walks into the shop to buy their own wish. You are the shop owner; your secret limit is on your screen only (if they say no twice, accept one year of Saturdays). Ask the four questions; they answer in role with If … would. Wait five seconds in silence before you help, and use the PUSH line to make the deal harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, they close the deal on the screen (or walk away) and fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Two Truths and a Lie, VI The Wish Contract, VII Bargain for a Wish, VIII What Would You Do? and IX One Minute.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: V If I Had, I Would.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
