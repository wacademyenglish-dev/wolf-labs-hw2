The House That Waited · The Odyssey · The House Plan

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: Ten years at war, ten years at sea, and a house that waits.

The grammar: There Is, There Are: There is one thing. There are two or more. His, her, their.

The class (about 55 min): I Warm-up and Put It in Order · II The Words · III The Trailer · IV OK or Not OK? · V Line by Line · VI There Is, There Are · VII The House Plan · VIII Your House · IX The Debate. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know the story now. In the trailer, is there a ship? Is there a monster? Guess.”

Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey (Universal, 2026) tells Homer’s poem, almost three thousand years old: Odysseus, the king of Ithaca, played by Matt Damon, takes ten years to come home after the war. At home there is his wife Penelope, his son Telemachus, his old dog Argos, and many men who want his wife and his kingdom. The grammar: there is / there are (there is a table, there are four cups), Is there…? / there isn’t, and his, her, their.

A note for the teacher: the five frames follow the timing of the trailer’s lines (read on 23/09), and each moment is told in our own words. A1 rule: no portraits; the people of the house are objects (her loom, his bed, their cups).

Warm-up and Put It in Order (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). A1: short answers are fine. 2 · Put It in Order: four cards of Odysseus’s story, shuffled. The student taps them in order (“First… then…”); then the cards show the real order, and the student tells the story with there is / there are.

The words (II): remember, kingdom, throne, power and understand, before the video, in match the columns and the word in your life. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (V) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, The Debate): “a home is the people, not the house”. The student chooses a side and uses there is / there are; the teacher defends the other side. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an old stone house on the island at night. I: Warm-up and Put It in Order. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a slider (my home is the best place in the world), and the student says why. Watch: Which word did you hear? and Pick the word you hear, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment. After: three taps (what does Odysseus remember? who says “My dad is coming home”? what is gone?), then the student says the sentence. IV: five frames on the film strip. V, There Is, There Are: five sentences with one mistake each (Have a big table…, there is four cups, his loom, they cups, Have a king…?). The student says the five fixes out loud, taps Check, and the rule board appears and stays in the corner on the next pages. VI, The House Plan: the palace of Ithaca seen from above in dark stone, four rooms (the great hall, Penelope’s room, Telemachus’s room, the courtyard). The student is the art director: tap a sentence (“It is her loom”), then its room, and the object goes into the room. A wrong room sends the card back with a tap. Then the student walks the teacher through the house: “In the great hall, there is …”. VII, Your House: two counters. The student walks through their own house (kitchen, bedroom, street) and says what there is; the teacher taps Got it! for each sentence. Then one thing of the mother and one of the father (her, his). VIII: the debate.

THE COMMON ERRORS

Have a table in the hall, there is four cups, there are a table, a old dog, it is his loom (for Penelope), the bed of him, they cups, There is a king?, there no is.

OK OR NOT OK? (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after the trailer: strangers live in Odysseus’s house, eat his food and wait in his hall. Now your student judges three things in their own house: ten guests, a friend eating their food, a dog on the bed. Two colored buttons, OK or Not OK, and an almost-ready sentence with there is / there isn’t and his / my. There is no right answer. Answer first yourself, then wait five seconds in silence and use the PUSH line (only you see it). Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one short personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Put It in Order, IV OK or Not OK?, VII The House Plan and VIII Your House.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and V Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI There Is, There Are.
  • Write: IX The Debate.
