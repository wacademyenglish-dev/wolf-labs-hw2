Not a Normal Day · A2 Unit 1 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new A2 lesson on present simple (habits: usually, every evening, never) and present continuous (now: tonight, right now), and state verbs without -ing (know, want, like).

The story: The power is out at Green Towers (invented building) at 7 p.m. The neighbors talk in the group chat: their normal evening and what they’re doing by candlelight.

About 41 min: I · The Group Chat (9) · II · Usually or Right Now? (9) · III · What Do You Miss? (6) · IV · But Tonight… (8) · V · Your Not-Normal Day (9).

Open with: “What do you usually do at 7 p.m.?”

The power cut makes the contrast natural: what people usually do at 7 p.m., and what they’re doing tonight. Ask the student to say WHY each sentence is a habit or a moment. Watch for “I’m knowing” and missing am / is / are (“I watching”).

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

present simple for habits · present continuous for now · usually, every evening, never · now, tonight, right now · negatives: doesn’t / isn’t · state verbs: know, want, like

THE GROUP CHAT

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

USUALLY OR RIGHT NOW?

One situation at a time. The student says which side it goes on and WHY, then taps. The color confirms and one line explains. The two columns build a board the student can photograph at the end.

BUT TONIGHT…

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

YOUR NOT-NORMAL DAY

Small talk: personal questions with the easier version a tap away. After each answer, wait: the student asks “And you?”.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

I watching (I’m watching), I’m knowing (I know), she cook (she cooks), He don’t answer (doesn’t), Usually I am watch (I usually watch). A2: one correction per answer is enough.

WHAT DO YOU MISS? (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

A short ranking in the middle of the lesson, between the two practice screens: the power is out, and your student puts five things in order (Wi-Fi, a hot shower, the fridge, the lights, the TV). There is no right order. Let them tap, wait five seconds in silence, then they defend number 1 and number 5 with the habit and the now (I usually… every evening / Tonight I’m…). Your own order appears so you can disagree; use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make it harder. Recast quietly while they talk; at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About 6 minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
