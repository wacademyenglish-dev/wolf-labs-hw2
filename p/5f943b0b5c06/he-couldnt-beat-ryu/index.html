He Couldn’t Beat Ryu · Street Fighter · The Final Round

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

The story: From a coin in an arcade to a final round tonight.

The grammar: Could: Could and couldn’t: what you were able to do. Then the verb, nothing else.

The class (about 57 min): I Warm-up and Pick a Side · II The Words · III The Trailer · IV Line by Line · V The Jury · VI Could · VII The Final Round · VIII When You Were Ten · IX Questions. No extra scenes: every scene is part of the class.

Open with: “You know Ryu and Ken now. In the trailer, who could win the tournament? Guess.”

Street Fighter is the fighting game from Japan (Capcom, 1987): two players, two life bars, one winner in each round. In 2026 it becomes a film again (Paramount and Legendary): the best fighters in the world come to one big tournament, and Ryu and Ken, old friends and old rivals, meet again. The lesson’s own story (said as such): twenty-five years ago Ken couldn’t beat Ryu in the final; tonight the student fights him. The grammar: could / couldn’t + the verb for ability in the past, the question Could you…?, and was / were able to for one success.

A note for the teacher: the trailer has fights but no blood; the lesson plays the whole trailer (2:52); near the end there are two swear words (the captions hide them). The five frames follow the official captions (read on 25/09), and each moment is told in our own words.

Warm-up and Pick a Side (I): two parts. 1 · Warm-up: three questions about the student’s life, one at a time (big on the screen, with “Ask more” under each and one short line about the film under the photo). Let them talk; A2: short answers are fine. 2 · Pick a Side: Ryu or Ken, best friends and best rivals. The student picks one and says why he could win. Then tap “The teacher pushes back” and let them answer. Twice. Push for could / couldn’t / was able to.

The words (II): champion, street, fighter, ready and perfect, before the video, in predict the story and the missing word. Five minutes: let the student guess first. In the video (with CC on), these words turn yellow; the explanation comes after the video, in Line by Line. Line by Line (IV) lists the lines with the yellow words: tap a line, hear it again, and the student says it like the character.

The ending (IX, Questions): five questions about the student as a child (what could you do well at school, what couldn’t you eat, could you go out alone at fifteen, who could beat you at games, what were you able to do after practice). Then the student asks the teacher two. Speaking only.

ON SCREEN

The cover is an empty fighting arena under red and blue spotlights. I: Warm-up and Pick a Side. III: the trailer in three steps. Before: a twenty-second timer to say the names of the fighters the student knows. Watch: One word is wrong (say the right one) and Which word did you hear?, each line with a ▶ time: tap it and the video jumps to that moment (all before 1:56, where the swearing starts). After: guess who (four clues, one at a time; the answer is Ken) and a slider (could Ken beat Ryu this time?). IV: Line by Line. V: The Jury, the student judges three choices from the trailer. VI, the scoreboard: five sentences with a mistake (could to fight, couldn’t won, didn’t could, Did you could, was able win). The student says the right word for each, taps Check, and the rule appears. The rule stays in the corner on the next pages. VII, The Final Round: a fighting-game screen with two life bars (YOU and RYU), a clock and the round in the middle. The student reads the fighter and the clue, says the sentence out loud, then taps Check: the sentence is written, the move’s name flashes and Ryu’s bar goes down by one sixth. Six hits: K.O. VIII, When You Were Ten: six cards to open (swim, ride a bike, cook, stay up late, play a game, speak English); the student answers and tells the story, and taps each form (could, couldn’t, was able to, Could you…?) when they use it. IX: questions.

THE COMMON ERRORS

could to fight, could fought, coulds / could kicks, could doing, didn’t could, Did you could…?, You could see…?, when he was young, he can’t, was able win, he were able.

THE JURY (V) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Line by Line, the student is the jury for three choices from the trailer: Ken, who went to TV after he couldn’t beat Ryu; Ryu, who doesn’t want to fight now; and the stranger who finds him and brings him back. They read the evidence, choose a verdict (or say their own) and say why with could / couldn’t / was able to. There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to bring it to their own life. Recast mistakes quietly while they talk (could to, didn’t could); at the end, ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves. About six minutes.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I Warm-up and Pick a Side, V The Jury, VII The Final Round, VIII When You Were Ten and IX Questions.
  • Listen: II The Words, III The Trailer and IV Line by Line.
  • Focus: VI Could.
  • Write: the homework (the lesson ends speaking).
