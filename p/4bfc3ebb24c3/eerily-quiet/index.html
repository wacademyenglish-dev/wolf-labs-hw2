Eerily Quiet · The Gothic Writing Room

A gothic writing workshop by candlelight. The student hears the opening of Poe’s The Tell-Tale Heart (1843), takes apart how fear is built, turns the dial on eight adjectives, edits a beginner’s page and writes the first page of their own tale. The grammar is adverb + adjective collocations: the adverb chooses the adjective (eerily quiet, bitterly cold, dimly lit, faintly familiar). With extreme adjectives: utterly / absolutely / completely, never very terrified. With gradable ones: very / extremely / deeply / highly, never absolutely scary. The point: The house was very quiet and The house was eerily quiet are the same scene, with a different fear.

THE VIDEO · ONLY TWO PIECES

Scene II: the 1953 UPA animation, narrated by James Mason. Only the opening (0:58–2:08) and the night scene (3:20–4:12). The crime and the body are left out on purpose. Each piece stops by itself.

THE DIAL · THE EDITOR · THE READING

Scene IV: eight adjectives; the student says the sentence with the adverb that sounds natural, then taps it. Scene V: a weak page full of “very”; the student rewrites each line out loud before seeing the editor’s version. Scene VII: a dramatic reading of Poe’s opening; tap one note for the student.

THE STORY BEHIND THE STORIES

Scene VI: from Samhain to the cold summer of 1816 (Byron’s ghost story challenge and Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein) to Poe. Read it out loud, then the four questions.

THE MISTAKES

very terrified, absolutely scary, strongly cold, highly sorry, strongly unlikely, eerie quiet.

YOUR HALLOWEEN NIGHT (III) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Right after Poe: three Halloween nights to choose from (footsteps upstairs in an old house, a free night in a “haunted” hotel, a scary bedtime story for a nephew). There is no right answer, and nobody gets hurt in any of them. Let your student choose, wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Ask for two collocations per answer (eerily quiet, utterly terrified); recast quietly while they talk, and at the end ask them to fix one or two sentences themselves.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.

WOLF IN THIS LESSON

  • Out loud: I What Scares You, III Your Halloween Night, V The Dial, VI The Editor, VIII Read It Like Poe.
  • Listen: II The Tell-Tale Heart, VII Why We Tell Them.
  • Focus: IV The Rule, IX Quick Check.
  • Write: X, the first page of their own gothic tale. It goes to their My notes.
