What I Really Meant · C1 Unit 3 Lesson

PREPARE IN 1 MINUTE

What this is: a new C1 lesson on cleft sentences used to correct and clarify: It wasn’t X that…, it was Y; What I meant was…; What I didn’t say was…; All I said was…; The thing I regret is…; It’s not that…, it’s that…

The story: The host of the “Slow Kitchen” podcast (invented) said “Italian grandmothers are overrated” and it went viral out of context. Episode 112 is her correction.

About 44 min: I · The Correction Episode (10) · II · Where Is the Spotlight? (9) · III · Set It Straight (8) · IV · Out of Context (7) · V · The Interview (10).

Open with: “Has anybody ever repeated something you said, but out of context? What happened?”

Other lessons of this unit use clefts for emphasis in general; this one is about CORRECTING: a viral misquote, a journalist who gets it wrong. Insist on calm intonation: the cleft does the work, not the volume. In the hot seat, be a slightly provocative journalist.

THE LANGUAGE OF THIS LESSON

It wasn’t X that…, it was Y · What I meant was… · What I didn’t say was… · All I said / did was… · The thing I regret is… · It’s not that…, it’s that…

THE CORRECTION EPISODE

Read: the student reads the text out loud. First one question before reading, then questions about the MEANING. Only then Look closer: let the student find the pattern, and tap Show the pattern to highlight it.

WHERE IS THE SPOTLIGHT?

Notice: two sentences side by side. The student explains the difference in their own words (Portuguese is fine here). Then Show answer. After the pairs come the rule cards and the traps: the student says the right form before you tap.

SET IT STRAIGHT

Use: the student reads a sentence and the clue, and says it the new way. Show the answer after the attempt; accept natural variations. Tap Right for each good one.

THE INTERVIEW

The hot seat: now the STUDENT asks and you answer, as yourself, short. Each card says what to ask about; Help me ask shows a model question. Encourage reactions and one follow-up.

THE MISTAKES TO LISTEN FOR

What I meant it was (What I meant was), It was Leo which (who), All what I said (All I said), What I need are (is), It’s not that…, is that (it’s that). C1: one correction per answer is enough.

OUT OF CONTEXT (IV) AND THE SURPRISE QUESTION

Before the interview, your student faces three everyday misunderstandings (a team chat, a friend’s dinner, an anonymous survey), chooses how to fix each one, and then says the correction itself with a cleft sentence (What I meant was…, It wasn’t… that…, it was…, It’s not that…, it’s that…). There is no right answer: wait five seconds in silence, then use the PUSH line (only you see it) to make the choice harder. Recast quietly while they talk, and at the end ask for one or two self-corrections.

The Surprise question button (bottom right, only you see it) opens one personal question at any moment. Use it when the lesson has been only exercises for a while.
